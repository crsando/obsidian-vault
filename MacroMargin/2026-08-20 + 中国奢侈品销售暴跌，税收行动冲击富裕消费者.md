### 中文译文

## 中国奢侈品销售暴跌，税收行动冲击富裕消费者

作者：彭博新闻社
2026年8月20日 下午2:00 GMT+8
*更新于2026年8月20日 下午3:54 GMT+8*

### **要点**

- 全球奢侈品牌在中国面临日益严重的销售下滑，7月份25个最大奢侈品牌的销售额下降超过10%。
- 此次下滑恰逢中国努力遏制资本外流并追回税收收入，这抑制了中国富裕人群的消费欲望。
- 路威酩轩集团旗下的路易威登和迪奥、开云集团旗下的古驰均出现两位数的销售下滑，而爱马仕则从增长转为下滑。
全球奢侈品牌正面临中国日益加深的销售下滑，因为中国对离岸财富征税的行动从股票市场波及到[赌场](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/macau-casinos-hit-by-slump-as-china-crackdown-rattles-market)，并抑制了该国最富有消费者的支出。

据彭博社调查的三家追踪行业数据的研究公司称，7月份中国25个最大奢侈品牌的销售额下降超过10%。这比6月份的放缓更为严重，标志着今年早些时候的强劲业务出现急剧逆转。

上述人士称，路威酩轩集团旗下的[路易威登](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/LVMM%20FP%20Equity)和[迪奥](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CDI%20FP%20Equity)，以及开云集团旗下的[古驰](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/0179201D:US)、葆蝶家和[巴黎世家](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/225674Z:FP)均出现两位数的销售下滑，而[爱马仕](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/RMS%20FP%20Equity)则从增长转为下滑。这些公司还表示，香奈儿和[普拉达](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1913%20HK%20Equity)的增长也显著放缓。

![Hermès, Cartier and Bulgari shops in Shanghai on Aug. 18.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481589b2bc17100256b45/3c281cc9-0b14-81c6-965e-d39ddb0dbe22-5a7b3d0ef98202e31a74f3cd3d2db4f3.png)

8月18日，上海的爱马仕、卡地亚和宝格丽门店。摄影师：Hector Retamal/AFP/Getty Images

对于全球奢侈品巨头而言，销量下滑为其最重要的市场之一增添了前景不确定性。中国曾是数十年奢侈品增长的引擎，但如今对最富有消费者的争夺加剧，且在该国[经济放缓](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/china-s-economy-extends-slowdown-by-weakening-across-the-board)背景下，中产阶级消费者已削减支出。

此次下滑恰逢中国全面遏制资本外流并[追回税收收入](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-12/china-s-global-tax-hunt-is-sowing-chaos-and-confusion)的努力，包括加强跨境股票交易管控，以及要求公民就境外资产和投资收益缴纳数十亿美元税款。这些措施抑制了中国富裕人群的消费意愿，可能使不到一年前在人工智能驱动的股市繁荣下开启的[奢侈品复苏](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-31/china-s-shoppers-are-buying-luxury-again-as-stock-market-rebounds)势头受挫。

“运营商开始报告称，由于财富效应减弱和高收入消费者面临更严格的税收环境，其VIP客户变得更加谨慎，”上海咨询公司Foresight Performance Partners联合创始人Jacques Roizen表示。“奢侈品高管在审视7月表现时存在合理担忧。”

LVMH、开云、爱马仕、香奈儿和普拉达未立即回应置评请求。

中国的新措施相当于十年来其跨境金融体系的最大调整，进一步收紧了富裕家庭长期用于保值和分散财富的渠道。这一打击行动已促使去年[MSCI中国指数](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MXCN%20Index)28.3%的涨幅被抹去，该指数今年下跌8.9%，是全球主要市场中表现最差的之一。香港[恒生指数](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HSI%20Index)在2025年强劲上涨后也失去动力。

周四，LVMH股价下跌1%，开云和爱马仕在巴黎均下跌约0.6%。

市场低迷进一步削弱了消费者信心。在房地产价值低迷的情况下，中国富人越来越多地将资金转向股票和其他金融资产，使其对市场波动更加敏感。在[博彩中心澳门](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/macau-casinos-hit-by-slump-as-china-crackdown-rattles-market)，赌场6月和7月收入降幅大于预期，豪赌客下注减少且到访频率降低。

![The Grand Lisboa casino resort in Macau.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481589b2bc17100256b45/3c281cc9-0b14-81b4-a2dd-ed2e1a3e94ab-192b04858d0591af6b5dc862a115ddf2.png)

澳门葡京赌场度假村。摄影：Eduardo Leal/彭博

“我们观察到过去两年资本市场表现与奢侈品销售之间存在一些相关性，”上海市场数据与研究公司百观（Baiguan）的首席执行官Robert Wu表示。“过去，这种相关性不那么明显，因为大量财富存储在房地产中。”

37岁的上海金融产品销售人员Stella Lin表示，经济低迷已足以让可自由支配支出暂停。她的股票投资组合（占其投资资本一半以上）的价值暴跌，最近几个月她没有购买任何奢侈品。此前，她在暑假期间挥霍，参加VIP活动，购买名牌手袋和衣服。

“我已经处于亏钱的心情中，”林说，她过去每年至少花$15,000购买奢侈品。“过去两个月我都不想逛任何高档商店。什么时候再花钱？你得问股市什么时候能好转。”

像林这样的客户的谨慎给中国消费行业的前景增添了阴霾。上个月零售销售增长放缓至0.6%，而珠宝和汽车等大件商品受到的冲击最为严重，销售额暴跌逾10%。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481589b2bc17100256b45/3c281cc9-0b14-8133-b041-e82d92c05b10-f81ae69e11d2083a616c663a7367ba52.png)

亿万富翁伯纳德·阿尔诺（Bernard Arnault）的[LVMH](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MC%20FP%20Equity)也在处理与本土饮料公司Molly Tea的商标纠纷的余波。尽管路易威登赢得了涉及标志性四瓣花卉图案的商标侵权案，但这一事件引发了社交媒体的强烈反弹，文化挪用的指控被疯传。

当然，奢侈品牌在7月暑假期间还面临极端高温、强降雨和出境游激增，这些都导致了客流量和销售疲软。

远见绩效合作伙伴（Foresight Performance Partners）的Roizen表示，8月将是对消费者信心的关键考验，因为中国情人节——通常是该国最强劲的奢侈品购物时刻之一——将在本周到来。

“如果品牌即使在那种有利条件下仍无法实现正增长，我会认为这是真正放缓的有力证据，”他说。

## Luxury Sales Plunge in China as Tax Push Hits Rich Shoppers

By Bloomberg News
August 20, 2026 at 2:00 PM GMT+8
*Updated on August 20, 2026 at 3:54 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways**

- Global luxury brands are facing a deepening sales slump in China, with sales at the 25 biggest luxury labels dropping more than 10% in July.
- The slump coincides with China's efforts to stem capital outflows and reclaim tax revenues, which has dampened the spending appetite of wealthy Chinese.
- Luxury brands such as LVMH's Louis Vuitton and Dior, as well as Kering SA's Gucci, recorded double-digit sales drops, while Hermès swung from gains to declines.
Global luxury brands are facing a deepening sales slump in China, as the country’s campaign to tax offshore wealth sends ripples from stock markets to [casino floors](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/macau-casinos-hit-by-slump-as-china-crackdown-rattles-market) and dampens spending by the country’s richest consumers.

Sales at the 25 biggest luxury labels in China dropped more than 10% in July, according to three research firms surveyed by Bloomberg that track industry data. That’s worse than the slowdown witnessed in June, and marks a sharp reversal from the brisk business seen earlier this year.

LVMH’s [Louis Vuitton](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/LVMM%20FP%20Equity) and [Dior](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CDI%20FP%20Equity), as well as Kering SA’s [Gucci](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/0179201D:US), Bottega Veneta and [Balenciaga](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/225674Z:FP) all recorded double-digit sales drops, while [Hermès](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/RMS%20FP%20Equity) swung from gains to declines, the people said. Growth for Chanel and [Prada](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1913%20HK%20Equity) also decelerated significantly, the firms said.

Hermès, Cartier and Bulgari shops in Shanghai on Aug. 18. Photographer: Hector Retamal/AFP/Getty Images

For global luxury giants, the declines add uncertainty to the outlook in one of their most important markets. China was once the engine of decades of luxury growth, but competition for the wealthiest shoppers has intensified and middle-class consumers have cut back on their spending amid the country’s [economic downturn](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/china-s-economy-extends-slowdown-by-weakening-across-the-board).

The slump coincides with China’s sweeping efforts to stem capital outflows and [reclaim tax revenues](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-12/china-s-global-tax-hunt-is-sowing-chaos-and-confusion), including tighter controls on cross-border stock trading and demands for citizens to pay billions of dollars in levies on offshore assets and investment gains. The measures have dampened the spending appetite of wealthy Chinese, threatening to unravel a [luxury recovery](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-31/china-s-shoppers-are-buying-luxury-again-as-stock-market-rebounds) that began less than a year ago on the back of an AI-fueled stock market boom.

“Operators are beginning to report more caution among their VIP clients against the waning wealth effect and a tighter tax environment for high-income consumers,” said Jacques Roizen, co-founder of Shanghai-based consultancy Foresight Performance Partners. “There’s legitimate concern among luxury executives when looking at July’s performance.”

LVMH, Kering, Hermès, Chanel and Prada didn’t immediately respond to requests for comment.

China’s new measures amount to the biggest shake-up of its cross-border financial system in a decade, further restricting channels long used by affluent households to preserve and diversify their wealth. The clampdown has contributed to erasing last year’s 28.3% rally in the [MSCI China Index](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MXCN%20Index), which is down 8.9% this year — one of the worst performers among major global markets. Hong Kong’s [Hang Seng Index](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HSI%20Index) has also lost steam after strong gains in 2025.

Shares of LVMH were down 1%, while Kering and Hermès were both trading about 0.6% lower in Paris Thursday.

The market downturn has further eroded consumer confidence. With property values depressed, wealthy Chinese have increasingly shifted their money into stocks and other financial assets, making them more sensitive to market swings. In [gambling hub Macau](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/macau-casinos-hit-by-slump-as-china-crackdown-rattles-market), casinos reported steeper-than-expected revenue declines in June and July, with high-rollers betting less and visiting less frequently.

The Grand Lisboa casino resort in Macau. Photographer: Eduardo Leal/Bloomberg

“We observe some correlation between the capital market performance and luxury sales in the last two years,” said [Robert Wu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/robert-wu-03778a19b/), CEO of Shanghai-based market data and research firm Baiguan. “In the past, such correlation was less obvious because a lot of wealth was stored in real estate.”

Stella Lin, a 37-year-old financial product salesperson in Shanghai, said the downturn has been enough to halt discretionary spending. The value of her stock portfolio — accounting for more than half of her invested capital — has plunged, and she hasn’t bought any luxury goods in recent months. Previously, she splurged during summer holidays, attending VIP events and buying designer handbags and clothes.

“I’m already in a money-losing mood,” said Lin, who used to spend at least $15,000 a year on luxury products. “I haven’t felt like shopping in any fancy stores in the past two months. When to spend again? You need to ask when the stock market can improve.”

Caution from clients like Lin adds to a gloomy outlook for China’s consumer sector. Retail sales growth slowed to 0.6% last month, while big-ticket items including jewelry and cars were among the worst hit, with sales plunging more than 10%.

Billionaire Bernard Arnault’s [LVMH](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MC%20FP%20Equity) is also dealing with the fallout of a trademark dispute with local beverage company Molly Tea. While Louis Vuitton won the trademark infringement case involving its signature four-petal floral motif, the episode sparked a social media backlash, with accusations of cultural appropriation going viral.

To be sure, luxury brands also faced extreme heat, heavy rainfall and a surge in outbound travels during the July summer holidays, which contributed to weaker foot traffic and sales.

August will be a crucial test of consumer confidence, with the Chinese Valentine’s Day — typically one of the country’s strongest luxury shopping occasions — falling this week, said Roizen of Foresight Performance Partners.

“If brands cannot produce positive growth even with that tailwind, I would regard that as a strong evidence of a genuine slowdown,” he said.
