### 中文译文

## 中国风投基金在三年干旱后竞相募资

![A string of successful listings, including of AI model developer Zhipu in January, has helped revive investor interest in China’s technology sector © Lam Yik/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b181cc90b14818a8e63ec1f973a3960/3b181cc9-0b14-81db-8a93-fe81e56ec09b-487049153d0f7c4498eddb25b756f3db.png)

一系列成功的上市，包括1月份AI模型开发商智谱的上市，帮助重燃了投资者对中国科技行业的兴趣 © Lam Yik/Bloomberg

中国的风险投资公司正在竞相募集新基金，并利用投资者对中国科技行业重新燃起的热情，此前三年的募资规模创下历史新低。

根据私募配售和咨询公司Asante Capital的数据，这些公司正寻求为至少60只新的美元计价基金募集约350亿美元，其中包括约40只风险投资基金。

投资者表示，鉴于中国公司在成本上竞争激烈，并[提供更便宜的模型访问](https://www.ft.com/content/9c8ff45b-7c20-4c2e-93c9-c52339ffdcee)，支持中国AI是对美国市场押注的一种“对冲”。

据已接触过这些基金的投资者称，HSG、IDG资本、经纬中国和未来资产等公司正在推销新基金或计划很快开始募资。真格基金和启明创投近期完成了基金募集。

一系列成功的科技公司上市，加上[中国AI公司的突破](https://www.ft.com/content/c6ecd8ce-c441-4d7c-aea6-fae3e28fb6ff)，如月之暗面和DeepSeek，以及机器人技术的进步，帮助重燃了投资者的兴趣。

但专家表示，募资活动的激增并不标志着中国风险投资黄金时代的回归，那时全球投资者竞相进入那些受欢迎且超额认购的基金。

“这还不是中国风险投资的繁荣，”Asante合伙人兼亚太区主管Ricardo Felix表示。“相反，这是在三年创纪录低点之后，中国风险投资美元基金募资的有选择性重启。”

此次复苏之前，中国[风险投资](https://www.ft.com/venture-capital-investment)经历了长期低迷，原因是国内经济放缓、美国对敏感行业投资的限制，以及中国监管机构收紧新股上市监管后退出机会枯竭。

多位基金管理和有限合伙人（向风投基金提供资金）表示，当前的募资环境比过去三年有了显著改善，过去三年许多美元基金难以筹集到新资金。

根据投资数据平台Preqin的数据，2022年有1105只专注于中国的基金共筹集了1500亿美元。相比之下，去年有97只基金筹集了136亿美元。

一位不愿透露姓名的基金经理表示：“市场已经有三四年缺乏资金了。突然间，所有基金经理都涌进来了。问题是投资者是否有足够的兴趣来养活这么多人。”

红杉中国（HSG，前身为红杉资本中国）已告知有限合伙人，计划今年推出一只专注于早期初创企业的基金。据多位知情人士透露，该公司还准备筹集一只人民币计价的基金，但具体金额尚未确定。红杉中国拒绝置评。

未来资本（Future Capital）正在募集一只新基金，将投资于[人工智能](https://www.ft.com/artificial-intelligence)硬件集团和利用人工智能进行科学研究的初创企业，包括药物发现。知情人士表示，虽然最终金额尚未确定，但预计略高于2亿美元。该公司拒绝就该数字置评。

两位知情人士称，IDG还计划推出一只人民币基金，并寻求今年为一只成长基金筹集约20亿美元。该公司没有回应置评请求。

多位投资者表示，人民币基金日益依赖地方政府投资集团，这些集团资金更为雄厚，且未受到部分机构投资者分配税收上调的影响。

上市AI模型开发商MiniMax和智谱的早期投资方已从其首次公开募股中获益，并于今年募集了新基金。其中包括MiniMax的投资方云启资本、未来资本和IDG，以及智谱的早期投资者启明创投和Luminous Ventures。红杉中国也同时投资了这两家公司。

“LP（有限合伙人）对中国管理人正变得更加挑剔。”未来资本合伙人Murong Yang表示，“他们越来越认为，投资一家领先的大语言模型（LLM）公司——并在该领域成为共识之前坚定地持续投资——是管理人具备判断力与渠道、能够在AI时代参与竞争的证据。”

Felix表示，首次公开募股市场的复苏给投资者带来了一些喘息空间，尽管许多基金尚未清算其投资并将收益分配给有限合伙人。

“这支撑了‘中国科技公司可与美国竞争，且估值仍然更低’的投资论点。”他说。

MiniMax的人工智能智能手机应用。该公司的早期投资方已从今年其首次公开募股中获益。© Raul Ariano/Bloomberg

募资目标明显低于2020年和2021年的高峰期。一位有限合伙人表示，他们“鼓励管理人在投资时更加专注”。

许多资金雄厚的美国投资者因[拜登政府时期的限制措施](https://www.ft.com/content/64ef2042-9ece-4b0c-ad02-184c3454f43b?syn-25a6b1a6=1)而袖手旁观，这些限制针对的是半导体和量子计算等敏感的中国科技领域。

尽管一些美国投资者继续通过排除受限行业的架构向中国主题基金注资，但许多此前支持中国风投公司的公共养老基金和大型捐赠基金已经退缩。

同时，欧洲和中东投资者表现出更大的兴趣，因为他们寻求分散投资，减少对美国资产的依赖。但Felix表示，不确定他们是否“能填补”美国基金留下的需求缺口。

多位投资者将当前环境描述为“买方市场”，使他们能够与基金管理人协商更有利的条款，例如在未来融资轮中获得更大的共同投资权利，这有助于降低管理费，以及要求普通合伙人投入更多自有资本。

投资者还指出，小型专业基金正在兴起，这些基金通常由一两名管理人运营，募集资金通常不到1亿美元，瞄准机器人供应链或AI代理等细分领域。

Nebulon Ventures是一家目标募资6000万美元的新基金，由曾任职于百度风投的Yongteng Wen领导。他把自己定位为新一代投资者，出生于1990年代，与中国AI初创社群关系密切，并定位为投资更年轻的创始人。

在现有基金仍试图部署繁荣时期募集的剩余资本之际，募资活动出现复苏。

“过去四年，这些管理人一直相当保守，现在他们拥有大量资本需要配置，”一位有限合伙人表示。“资本正在激烈争夺少数几个高信心交易，尤其是在人工智能领域。”

Matrix Partners和Nebulon Ventures未回应置评请求。

## Chinese VC firms rush to raise funds after three-year drought

A string of successful listings, including of AI model developer Zhipu in January, has helped revive investor interest in China’s technology sector © Lam Yik/Bloomberg

China’s venture capital firms are rushing to raise new funds and capitalise on investors’ renewed enthusiasm for the country’s technology sector after three years of record-low fundraising.

Firms are seeking to raise an estimated $35bn for at least 60 new dollar-denominated funds, including about 40 venture capital funds, according to data from private placement and advisory firm Asante Capital.

Investors described backing Chinese AI as a “hedge” against US market bets, given Chinese companies had been competing aggressively on cost and [offering cheaper access to models](https://www.ft.com/content/9c8ff45b-7c20-4c2e-93c9-c52339ffdcee).

HSG, IDG Capital, Matrix Partners China and Future Capital are among the firms marketing new funds or planning to start fundraising soon, according to investors who have been pitched on the funds. ZhenFund and Qiming recently closed funds.

A string of successful technology listings, alongside [breakthroughs from Chinese AI companies](https://www.ft.com/content/c6ecd8ce-c441-4d7c-aea6-fae3e28fb6ff) such as Moonshot AI and DeepSeek, as well as advances in robotics, has helped revive interest.

But experts said the surge in fundraising activity did not signal a return to the heyday of Chinese venture capital when global investors competed to access popular, heavily oversubscribed funds.

“This isn’t a China VC boom yet,” said Ricardo Felix, partner and head of Asia-Pacific at Asante. “Instead, this is a selective reopening of US dollar fundraising for Chinese VC after three years of record-low levels.”

The revival follows a prolonged downturn in Chinese [venture capital](https://www.ft.com/venture-capital-investment), driven by a slowing domestic economy, US restrictions on investment in sensitive sectors and a drought in exit opportunities after Chinese regulators tightened controls on new listings.

Several fund managers and limited partners, who provide capital to venture funds, said the current fundraising environment represented a significant improvement from the past three years, when many dollar funds struggled to raise new money.

In 2022, 1,105 China-focused funds raised a total of $150bn, according to investment data platform Preqin. By contrast, 97 funds raised $13.6bn last year.

“The market has been starved of capital for three to four years,” said a fund manager who asked not to be named. “Suddenly, all the fund managers are rushing in. The question is whether there is enough interest from investors to feed so many people.”

HSG, formerly Sequoia China, has told limited partners it plans to launch a fund this year focused on early-stage start-ups. The firm is also preparing to raise a renminbi-denominated fund, although the exact amount is yet to be determined, according to several people familiar with the plans. HSG declined to comment.

Future Capital is raising a new fund that will invest in [AI](https://www.ft.com/artificial-intelligence) hardware groups and start-ups using AI scientific research, including drug discovery. While the final amount was yet to be determined, it was expected to be just over $200mn, said a person with knowledge of the matter. The firm declined to comment on the figure.

IDG was also planning to launch a renminbi fund, said two people, and was seeking to raise about $2bn for a growth fund this year. The firm did not respond to a request for comment.

Several investors said the renminbi funds were increasingly reliant on local government investment groups, which have deeper pockets and have not been affected by tax rises on distributions for some institutional investors.

Early backers of listed AI model developers MiniMax and Zhipu have benefited from their initial public offerings and raised new funds this year. They include MiniMax backers Yunqi Partners, Future Capital and IDG, and early Zhipu investors Qiming Venture Partners and Luminous Ventures. HSG also backed both companies.

“LPs are becoming more discerning towards Chinese managers,” said Murong Yang, partner at Future Capital. “Increasingly, they see backing a leading LLM [large language model] company — and continuing to invest with conviction before the sector became consensus — as evidence that a manager has the judgment and access to compete in the AI era.”

Felix said the initial public offering market’s revival had given investors some breathing space, even though many funds had yet to liquidate their investments and distribute returns to limited partners.

“It has supported the investment thesis that China technology companies can compete with the US, while valuations remain cheaper,” he said.

MiniMax’s AI smartphone app. Early backers of the company have benefited from its initial public offering this year © Raul Ariano/Bloomberg

The fundraising targets are significantly lower than in the peak years of 2020 and 2021. One limited partner said they were “encouraging managers to be more focused in their investment”.

Many deep-pocketed US investors are sitting out because of [Biden-era restrictions](https://www.ft.com/content/64ef2042-9ece-4b0c-ad02-184c3454f43b?syn-25a6b1a6=1) on backing sensitive Chinese technology sectors such as semiconductors and quantum computing.

While some US investors continue to commit capital to China-focused funds through structures that exclude restricted sectors, many public pension funds and large endowments that previously supported Chinese VC firms have stepped back.

At the same time, European and Middle Eastern investors have shown greater interest as they seek diversification away from US assets. But Felix said it was uncertain whether they “could fill the demand gap” left by US funds.

Several investors described the current environment as a “buyers’ market”, allowing them to negotiate more favourable terms with fund managers, such as greater rights to co-invest in future funding rounds, which help reduce management fees, and expectations that general partners commit more of their own capital.

Investors also pointed to the rise of smaller specialist funds, which are often run by one or two managers, typically raise less than $100mn and target niche areas such as robotics supply chains or AI agents.

Nebulon Ventures, a new fund targeting $60mn, is led by Yongteng Wen, formerly at Baidu Ventures. He is marketing himself as a new generation of investor, born in the 1990s, with close ties to China’s AI start-up community and positioned to invest in younger founders.

The fundraising revival comes as existing funds are still trying to deploy remaining capital raised from the boom years.

“Over the past four years, these managers have been pretty conservative and now they have a lot of capital to deploy,” said one limited partner. “Capital is competing aggressively for a limited number of high-conviction deals, especially in AI.”

Matrix Partners and Nebulon Ventures did not respond to requests for comment.
