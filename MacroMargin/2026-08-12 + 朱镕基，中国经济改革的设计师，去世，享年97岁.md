### 中文译文

## 朱镕基，中国经济改革的设计师，去世，享年97岁

这位前总理引导中国走向西方市场原则，并推动中国加入世界贸易组织

James T. Areddy

2026年8月12日 上午6:52 美国东部时间

![Chinese Premier Zhu Rongji delivering a work report in Beijing's Great Hall of the People in 1999](https://images.wsj.net/im-130282?width=700&size=0.755&pixel_ratio=2)

朱镕基1999年在北京人民大会堂。新华社/美联社

据官方新华社报道，曾引导中国走向西方市场原则并推动中国加入世界贸易组织的前总理朱镕基因病于周三去世，享年97岁。

当朱镕基在1990年代登上国家政治舞台时，中国经济正处于青春期——增长迅速但急需管理。共产党将他从上海提拔上来，他曾担任上海市长，1991年被任命为副总理。朱镕基亲自负责中央银行、金融监管、农业政策以及庞大的国有企业体系，他认为这些企业臃肿、低效且腐败。

他抑制了27%的通货膨胀，将货币贬值三分之一，并裁减了数千万员工。这些以市场为导向的行动为中国赢得了全球对经济政策的尊重，但也让中国人民感到不安。朱镕基在江泽民领导下成为中国的非正式经济沙皇——江泽民也是上海人物，在1989年天安门广场镇压后的政治动荡中进入国家领导层——朱镕基于1998年升任总理，任职至2003年。

朱镕基逻辑严密且好斗，他将中国接入全球贸易、银行和通信体系。他的做法对共产党的保守派构成威胁，朱镕基期望美国政府帮助中国加入世界贸易组织，在那里国际规则将占主导地位。这一进程在政治上损害了朱镕基在国内的地位，耗时15年，直到2001年中国才获准加入。

1997年邓小平葬礼上的中国官员。新华社/美联社

### **为增长奠定基础**

朱镕基将国际市场原则与国内现实相结合，形成了一种他的官员称之为“中国特色社会主义市场经济”的体系。这为中国赢得了任何主要经济体中最长时间的增长记录奠定了基础，尽管这个国家仍由受过毛泽东式孤立主义教育的共产党领导。

通过集中金融监管，朱镕基还为中国创造了环境，得以建立一些世界上最大的银行和股票、债券及大宗商品市场，外加一种受管控且国际认可的货币——毛泽东可能会为印有自己头像的货币感到自豪。

在[1999年接受《华尔街日报》的采访](https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB923347230657319106?mod=article_inline)中，这位高个子的技术官僚露出顽皮的微笑，形容自己是一个“脾气不好的普通中国人”。

由于他愿意摒弃共产党的意识形态，朱镕基有时被称为中国的米哈伊尔·戈尔巴乔夫。事实上，朱镕基是巩固了国家权力，而不是放弃它，但这一比较在一个方面确实成立：与这位苏联领导人一样，朱镕基在国外获得的赞誉往往多于国内。打破党传统的“铁饭碗”承诺（即从摇篮到坟墓地支持其人民）是对公开共产主义者的冒犯。

“如果说邓小平是改革的设计师，江泽民是执行其愿景的总承包商，那么朱镕基就是那把锤子，”美国前财政部长亨利·保尔森在《与中国打交道》一书中写道。

“他自己不乏宏大的想法，但最重要的是他把事情办成了。他坦率、务实、直截了当。我从未怀疑过他的意图，他的下属也是如此，”保尔森写道。

![Chinese Premier Zhu Rongji signs a guest book at Nasdaq-Amex MarketSite, with Frank Zarb, CEO of NASDAQ, in 1999.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ba81cc90b1481d391edd60cd7350aa5/3ba81cc9-0b14-813e-ad64-db40d8b1138a-510cf86ee9ddfffc134544b33e41dda3.jpg)

朱镕基的市场化行动为中国赢得了全球经济政策制定方面的尊重。Richard Drew/Associated Press

### **漫长的政治流放**

朱镕基在湖南省会长沙附近长大，由叔父抚养成人，因为他从未见过父亲，母亲在他10岁前去世。但家庭富裕，所以即使在他身边中国与日本的战争以及后来的内战肆虐，朱镕基依然接受了教育。他考入了北京著名的清华大学，并于1951年从电机工程系毕业前已是党员。

在20世纪50年代末，党内指责他未能坚持毛主义正统。他被下放从事农活，直到1978年才恢复政治地位，当时邓小平在毛泽东逝世后使中国摆脱了共产主义正统。

“尽管我生命中的那段经历对我来说是痛苦的，但它也是有益的。它教会了我更多，让我接触了更多社会阶层，”朱镕基在1999年对《华尔街日报》表示。

![Chinese Premier Zhu Rongji and his wife Lao An riding a magnetic levitation train.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ba81cc90b1481d391edd60cd7350aa5/3ba81cc9-0b14-810c-8224-f22f10f11b1a-c35d5c48de5ae4039c2bf123e81d975a.jpg)

2000年，朱镕基与夫人劳安在日本乘坐磁悬浮列车。Press Pool

在中国改革开放初期，朱镕基在北京的一个经济委员会工作，随后调任上海，邓小平在那里培养官员以引领全国经济转型。

1989年，朱镕基担任上海市长，当时他做出了务实的公开呼吁，以平息这座商业城市中类似天安门广场的示威活动。“我们需要的是上海人民最大限度的团结，而不是社会混乱和生产下降，那会给人民生活带来困难，”他在那年5月对当地电视观众说。上海的抗议活动逐渐平息，不像北京，两周后邓小平命令人民解放军开枪。

### **治理通胀**

1991年，朱镕基升任副总理，负责管理一个当时正以13%增速飞速前进的经济，用他自己的话说，“就像飞机起飞一样”。一项关键任务是遏制27%的通胀率，朱镕基通过亲自掌控金融行业并解雇包括央行行长在内的不称职官员，完成了这一任务。

后来，他将矛头对准那些冒充国有企业的臃肿政府垄断企业，让数千万人下岗，并积极吸引外国投资者。他公开指责其他政策制定者存在“逃税、骗税、骗汇和走私”行为，并斥责当局的统计数据刮起“浮夸和造假之风”。

![Zhu Rongji (right) and a Chinese delegate (left) smiling at a shipyard in Saint Nazaire, France.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ba81cc90b1481d391edd60cd7350aa5/3ba81cc9-0b14-8142-a9f9-f2346c3acd8a-8112a1d783368d6647df03554ba4f0fa.jpg)

1991年，朱镕基身着黑色大衣在法国。图片来源：Frank Perry/AFP/Getty Images

朱镕基亲自挑选了一批较低层级的官员，其中包括后来执掌央行超过15年的周小川。上世纪90年代中期，中国允许[摩根士丹利](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/MS)成立第一家中美投资银行合资企业——中国国际金融公司，朱镕基的儿子朱云来（Levin Zhu Yunlai）获得了高管职位，至今仍活跃在金融业。

朱镕基的努力几乎没有让他赢得中国传统权力结构的青睐，但他是改革阵营的旗手。他最大的挑战是说服华盛顿的克林顿政府相信中国会在全球贸易中公平竞争，以争取华盛顿支持中国加入世贸组织。他对参议员马克斯·鲍卡斯（Max Baucus）说：“我需要你们从外部推动，以便我从内部推动。”鲍卡斯是民主党人，后来成为美国驻华大使。

朱镕基退休后远离公众视线，但中国出版商出版了他过去的多卷演讲集，其中一些此前从未公开，这些内容使人们得以一窥这位经济沙皇如何帮助打造了世界最大的贸易国。

## Zhu Rongji, Engineer of China’s Economic Overhaul, Dies at 97

The former premier steered China toward Western market principles and championed its entry into the World Trade Organization

By

James T. Areddy

Aug. 12, 2026 6:52 am ET

Zhu Rongji in Beijing’s Great Hall of the People in 1999. Xinhua/Associated Press

Zhu Rongji, a former premier who steered China toward Western market principles and championed its entry into the World Trade Organization, died Wednesday after an illness, according to the official Xinhua News Agency. He was 97 years old.

When Zhu rose to national prominence in the 1990s, China’s economy was adolescent—growing rapidly but direly in need of stewardship. Plucked by the Communist Party from Shanghai, where he was mayor, and named a vice premier in 1991, Zhu took personal charge of central banking, financial regulation, agricultural policy and a sprawling system of state-owned enterprises that he considered wastefully bloated, ineffective and corrupt.

He tamed 27% inflation, slashed the currency’s value by one-third and laid off tens of millions of people. Those market-oriented actions earned China global respect for economic policymaking but unsettled the Chinese people. Crowned China’s unofficial economy czar under President Jiang Zemin—another Shanghai figure who moved into national office in the political tumult following the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown—Zhu was elevated in 1998 to premier, serving in that position until 2003.

Logical and combative, Zhu plugged China into global systems of trade, banking and communications. His approach posed threats to the Communist Party’s old guard and Zhu looked to the U.S. government to help him get China into the World Trade Organization, where international rules would prevail. The process, which damaged Zhu politically at home, took 15 years until China gained admission in 2001.

Chinese officials at the funeral of Deng Xiaoping in 1997. Xinhua/Associated Press

### **Setting the stage for growth**

Zhu fused international market principles with domestic realities in a construct his officials called a “socialist market economy with Chinese characteristics.” It set the stage for China to claim credit for the longest stretch of growth for any major economy, despite still being run by a Communist Party schooled in Maoist isolationism.

By centralizing financial regulation, Zhu also created an environment for China to build some of the world’s largest banks and markets in stocks, bonds and commodities, plus a controlled yet internationally recognized currency Mao might have been proud to have his face on.

Flashing a mischievous smile in [a 1999 interview with The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB923347230657319106?mod=article_inline), the tall technocrat described himself as an “ordinary Chinese with a bad temper.”

For his willingness to toss out Communist Party ideology, Zhu was sometimes called China’s Mikhail Gorbachev. In fact, Zhu shored up state power instead of letting it go but the comparison rang true in one respect: Like the Soviet leader, Zhu often won more accolades overseas than at home. Breaking the party’s traditional “iron rice bowl” promise to support its people from cradle to grave was an affront to avowed Communists.

“If Deng \[Xiaoping\] was the architect of reform and Jiang Zemin the general contractor carrying out his vision, then Zhu Rongji was the hammer,” former U.S. Treasury Secretary Henry Paulson wrote in “Dealing with China.”

“He had no shortage of big ideas himself, but above all he got things done. He was frank, practical and to the point. I never doubted for a second what he wanted, nor did his subordinates,” Paulson wrote.

Zhu Rongji's market-oriented actions earned China global respect for economic policymaking. Richard Drew/Associated Press

### **A long political exile**

Zhu grew up near Hunan province’s capital Changsha and was raised by uncles, since he never knew his father and his mother died before he turned 10. But the family was wealthy, so even as China’s war with Japan and its later civil war raged around him, Zhu got an education. He won a spot at Beijing’s prestigious Tsinghua University and was a party member before he graduated from its electrical engineering department in 1951.

In the late 1950s, the party accused him of failing to uphold Maoist orthodoxy. He was sent to do farm work and didn’t regain his political status until 1978, when Deng pivoted China away from Communist orthodoxy after Mao’s death.

“Though that part of my life was a painful experience for me, it was also a useful one. It taught me more, and allowed me exposure with more strata of society,” Zhu told the Journal in 1999.

Zhu Rongji with his wife Lao An on a magnetic levitation train in Japan, in 2000. Press Pool

During the early years of China’s period of overhaul and opening up, Zhu worked in an economic commission in Beijing and then landed in Shanghai, where Deng was grooming officials to spearhead a national economic transformation.

Zhu was Shanghai mayor in 1989, when he made a practical public appeal to diffuse Tiananmen Square-like demonstrations in the business-minded city. “What we need is the greatest possible unity among all the people of Shanghai rather than social chaos and a decline in production, which would create difficulties in people’s lives,” he told the local TV audience that May. Protests in the city fizzled, unlike in Beijing, where Deng two weeks later ordered the People’s Liberation Army to shoot.

### **Tackling inflation**

In 1991, Zhu was elevated to vice premier and took charge of an economy that was soon barreling ahead at a 13% growth pace, “like a plane taking off,” he said. A critical duty was arresting 27% inflation, which Zhu accomplished by putting himself in control of the financial sector and firing hapless officials including the country’s central-bank chief.

He later took aim at bloated government monopolies posing as state companies, furloughing tens of millions and courting foreign investors. He called out fellow policymakers for “tax evasion, tax fraud, obtaining foreign currency through deception and smuggling” and charged that authorities produced statistics from “a wind of embellishment and falsification.”

Zhu Rongji, wearing a black coat, in France in 1991. Frank Perry/AFP/Getty Images

Zhu handpicked lower-level leaders, including Zhou Xiaochuan for the central bank, who would later run it for more than 15 years. When China permitted [Morgan Stanley](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/MS) to create the first Sino-U.S. investment-banking joint venture in the mid-1990s, China International Capital, Zhu’s son Levin Zhu Yunlai gained a senior position and remains active in the financial industry.

Little of Zhu’s effort endeared him to China’s traditional power structure, but he was the reform camp’s standard-bearer. His biggest challenge was convincing the Clinton administration in Washington that China would play fair in global commerce so Washington would support its bid to join the WTO. “I need you to push from the outside so I can push from the inside,” he told Sen. Max Baucus, a Democrat who later became the U.S. ambassador to China.

Zhu stayed out of the public eye after he retired but Chinese publishers have released volumes of his old speeches, some of them previously unknown, that give some insight into how the economy czar helped create the world’s biggest trading nation.
