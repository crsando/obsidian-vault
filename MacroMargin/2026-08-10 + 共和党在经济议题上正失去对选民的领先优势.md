来源：福克斯新闻于2026年7月17-20日对1003名登记选民进行的民调；误差幅度为±3个百分点。

- 持续的生活成本担忧正侵蚀共和党作为更善于处理经济问题的政党的形象，中期选举即将来临。
- 自2010年以来，福克斯新闻民调首次发现，选民认为民主党在经济方面比共和党更好。
- 在多项调查中，仅有约四分之一的受访者认为当前经济状况为优秀或良好，而约四分之三的受访者认为经济状况一般或糟糕。
共和党民调专家米切尔·布朗（Mitchell Brown）在战场州主持的选民焦点小组中发现了一个新现象：“他们对电费金额精确到每一美元都了如指掌，”他说。他表示，在医疗费用或牛肉价格上也同样如此。

在布朗看来，这表明选民对经济的看法[对他的政党在今年中期选举中构成明显威胁](https://www.wsj.com/economy/trump-voters-economy-2026-6c76d508?mod=article_inline)，其他共和党战略家也有同样的担忧。随着选民的负面印象日益固化，一些共和党和独立分析人士表示，特朗普总统及其共和党盟友几乎没有时间重新夺回一项关键资产：他们作为更擅长处理经济问题的政党的形象。

共和党担心的是，那些在2024年因相信特朗普会降低生活成本而支持该党的选民，可能会在今年11月留在家中或倒戈。

“要让共和党的运势真正改变，所需发生的一系列事件非常重大。”范德堡大学政治学家约翰·赛兹（John Sides）表示。

对经济的悲观情绪与股市的飙升形成鲜明对比，后者因[对人工智能的热情](https://www.wsj.com/economy/the-ai-boom-is-transforming-the-american-economy-beyond-recognition-c7825b31?mod=article_inline)占据主导而大涨。消费者支出[保持稳健](https://www.wsj.com/economy/u-s-economic-growth-slowed-to-1-5-in-second-quarter-003c464f?mod=article_inline)，即使面临高油价，部分受到今年减税政策的提振。

基于主要指标，对特朗普第二任期内经济的最佳描述可能是“马马虎虎”。截至6月的六个季度，国内生产总值年化增长1.9%，相比之下，前总统乔·拜登（Joe Biden）任内最后一年为2.4%。失业率几乎未变，从2025年1月的4%小幅上升至[7月仍处于低位的4.1%](https://www.wsj.com/economy/jobs/july-jobs-report-unemployment-0d7c08a7?mod=article_inline)，尽管就业增长已明显放缓。

通胀是最痛处：据劳工部数据，6月[消费者价格同比上涨3.5%](https://www.wsj.com/economy/cpi-inflation-report-june-2026-afb89992?mod=article_inline)，而2025年1月的通胀率为3%。这侵蚀了工资增长——6月平均时薪同比上涨3.5%。

美国人对经济的感受明显比这些数字所显示的更糟。多项调查显示，只有约四分之一的受访者将经济评为“优秀”或“良好”，而约四分之三的人表示经济状况“一般”或“糟糕”。

这种低落情绪的一个结果是：选民对共和党管理经济能力的看法发生了重大逆转。在许多民调中，更多选民认为民主党是[更称职的经济管理者](https://www.wsj.com/politics/elections/this-democrat-has-never-lost-a-race-can-he-flip-north-carolinas-senate-seat-fcaf6f80?mod=article_inline)，而非共和党——这是近年来罕见的现象。

自2010年以来，福克斯新闻民调首次发现，选民认为民主党在经济问题上比共和党做得更好。在今年4月的这项调查中，民主党领先4个百分点，7月领先9个百分点，而2022年和2023年，共和党被视为更优秀的经济管理者，领先幅度高达15个百分点。

作为对国会共和党人的警示，共和党策略师大卫·温斯顿在6月援引了共和党在处理通胀、就业和整体经济方面的地位出现类似逆转的情况。“如果民主党能够像一些民调显示的那样，在经济上保持领先，并在独立派选民中赢得两位数的优势，那么蓝色浪潮是可能的，”他写道。

美国繁荣行动组织（Americans for Prosperity Action）是一个倡导有限政府的超级政治行动委员会，该组织在民调和挨家挨户接触摇摆州选民时发现，大约五分之一的倾向共和党的选民因经济担忧而可能呆在家里或倒戈。该组织表示，通过与民主党政策形成对比，60%的这类选民会回到共和党阵营。

尽管经济问题仍然是共和党面临的严峻挑战，但民主党尚未完全利用这一优势。他们还有其他问题：民主社会主义者正[与中间派争斗](https://www.wsj.com/politics/elections/progressive-victory-in-michigan-senate-primary-deepens-democratic-divide-b3b7fe16?mod=article_inline)，争论党的经济议程，民调显示选民对民主党看法不佳。例如，在福克斯新闻的民调中，69%的选民不认可民主党在国会的工作表现。

特朗普政府表示，与伊朗的战争及其[对汽油价格的影响](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/trump-blasts-big-oil-companies-for-making-too-much-money-e113c41b?mod=article_inline)正在推动人们对经济的负面看法，但这种影响将是暂时的。

“当特朗普总统促成与伊朗的成功解决方案时，油价以及整体通胀将再次大幅下降，”白宫发言人库什·德赛表示。“特朗普的经济议程在提高实际工资、创造就业和确保关键投资方面有着经过验证的记录。”

多年来，经济学家和分析人士一直困惑，为什么一个按传统指标看起来相对稳健（即使称不上出色）的经济，却引发了如此悲观的公众情绪。

通货膨胀衡量的是物价在一年内上涨了多少，但令许多人恼火的是，物价比疫情前高出这么多。根据得克萨斯大学奥斯汀分校政治学家克里斯托弗·维齐恩的说法，选民似乎是在以近期为参照来评价经济，而不是看其绝对表现。

[高房贷利率](https://www.wsj.com/economy/housing/mortgage-rates-jump-to-a-one-year-high-9ea35b8a?mod=article_inline)和房价让许多美国人买不起房。股市的繁荣也未能平息不满。消费者分析公司CivicScience的首席执行官约翰·迪克表示，这可能是因为越来越多的美国人觉得财富增长大多流向了顶层人群。

为国会共和党领袖提供建议的温斯顿表示，他相信选民的印象仍然可能改变。他说：“人们与价格时刻接触。所以，如果10月份人们走进杂货店和加油站时说，‘我现在能应付生活成本了’，那就是决定性时刻。”

此外，民调显示，经济不满转化为对民主党候选人投票的迹象有限。总体来看，民调显示，选民表示将在国会选举中支持民主党而非共和党的比例约为6个百分点——这与2018年同期民主党的领先优势相同，当时选民对特朗普经济管理的看法要积极得多。

代表特朗普曾三次获胜的华盛顿州农村选区的民主党众议员玛丽·格卢森坎普·佩雷斯表示：“人们可能对拿下众议院的难度有点过于乐观了。是的，特朗普非常非常不受欢迎。但这并不意味着民主党必然受欢迎。”

与此同时，选民可以将许多生活成本问题归因于白宫采取的行动。与伊朗的战争导致汽油价格高企。关税也加剧了价格压力。

赛兹表示：“我们面临的特定经济状况实际上未必是前政府遗留下来的。它们源于本届政府做出的非常具体的决定。”

Source: Fox News poll of 1,003 registered voters conducted July 17-20, 2026; margin of error +/- 3 pct. pts.

- Persistent cost-of-living worries are eroding the GOP’s image as the party better suited to handle the economy ahead of the midterm elections.
- For the first time since 2010, Fox News polls find voters citing Democrats as better on the economy than Republicans.
- In several surveys, only about one-quarter of respondents rate the economy as excellent or good, while about three-quarters say it is in fair or poor shape.
Mitchell Brown, a Republican pollster, has noticed something new in the focus groups he runs with voters in battleground states: “They know exactly, to the dollar, the amount of their power bill,” he said. The same, he says, happens with health costs, or the price of beef.

To Brown, it is a sign that views of the economy [pose a clear danger](https://www.wsj.com/economy/trump-voters-economy-2026-6c76d508?mod=article_inline) for his party in this year’s midterms, a concern echoed by other Republican strategists. With voters’ negative impressions now hardening, some Republican and independent analysts say President Trump and his GOP allies have little time to regain a key asset: their image as the party better suited to handle the economy.

For the GOP, the fear is that voters who backed the party in 2024 believing Trump would ease the cost of living will stay home or defect this November.

“The set of events that would need to happen to produce a real change in the Republican Party’s fortune is pretty big,” said John Sides, a political scientist at Vanderbilt University.

The downbeat mood on the economy stands in contrast to a stock market that has soared as [enthusiasm over artificial intelligence](https://www.wsj.com/economy/the-ai-boom-is-transforming-the-american-economy-beyond-recognition-c7825b31?mod=article_inline) has taken hold. Consumer spending [has remained solid](https://www.wsj.com/economy/u-s-economic-growth-slowed-to-1-5-in-second-quarter-003c464f?mod=article_inline), even in the face of high gasoline prices, buoyed in part by this year’s tax cuts.

The best characterization of the economy in Trump’s second term, based on the main measures, might be “so-so.” Gross domestic product expanded at a 1.9% annual rate over the six quarters ended in June, which compares with 2.4% during former President Joe Biden’s final year in office. The unemployment rate has barely budged, edging up from 4% in January 2025 to [a still-low 4.1% as of July](https://www.wsj.com/economy/jobs/july-jobs-report-unemployment-0d7c08a7?mod=article_inline), although job growth has slowed markedly.

Inflation is the sorest point: [Consumer prices were 3.5% higher](https://www.wsj.com/economy/cpi-inflation-report-june-2026-afb89992?mod=article_inline) in June than a year earlier, according to the Labor Department, versus a January 2025 inflation rate of 3%. That ate away at wage gains—average hourly earnings were up 3.5% on the year in June.

Americans’ feelings about the economy are decidedly worse than those numbers suggest. In several surveys, only about one-quarter of respondents rate the economy as excellent or good, while about three-quarters say it is in fair or poor shape.

One result of the sour mood: a significant reversal in views of the GOP’s ability to manage the economy. In many polls, more voters see the Democratic Party as [a better economic steward](https://www.wsj.com/politics/elections/this-democrat-has-never-lost-a-race-can-he-flip-north-carolinas-senate-seat-fcaf6f80?mod=article_inline) than the Republican Party—a rare finding in recent years.

For the first time since 2010, Fox News polls find voters citing Democrats as better on the economy than Republicans. Democrats led by 4 points on the question in April and by 9 points in July, whereas the GOP was viewed as the better economic manager by as much as 15 points in 2022 and 2023.

In a cautionary note to Republicans in Congress, GOP strategist David Winston in June cited similar reversals in the GOP’s standing—on its handling of inflation, jobs and the overall economy. “If Democrats could sustain leads on the economy and win double digit margins among independents as some polls show, a Blue Wave is possible,” he wrote.

In its polling and door-knocking to engage swing-state voters, Americans for Prosperity Action, a limited-government super PAC, has found that about one in five Republican-leaning voters is at risk of staying home or defecting, due in large part to economic worries, the group has said. Drawing a contrast with Democratic policy, however, brings 60% of those voters back to the GOP, the group says.

But while the economy remains a strong challenge for Republicans, Democrats haven’t yet fully taken advantage. They have other problems: Democratic socialists [are battling centrists](https://www.wsj.com/politics/elections/progressive-victory-in-michigan-senate-primary-deepens-democratic-divide-b3b7fe16?mod=article_inline) over the party’s economic agenda, and polling shows voters hold dim views of the party. In the Fox News poll, for example, 69% of voters disapproved of the job Democrats are doing in Congress.

The Trump administration says that the war with Iran and its [impact on gas prices](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/trump-blasts-big-oil-companies-for-making-too-much-money-e113c41b?mod=article_inline) are driving negative views of the economy, but that the effect will be transitory.

“Oil prices—along with overall inflation—will plummet again when President Trump forces a successful resolution with Iran,” said Kush Desai, a White House spokesman. “The Trump economic agenda has a proven track record of raising real wages, creating jobs, and securing key investments.”

Economists and analysts have puzzled for several years over why an economy that looks relatively solid by traditional metrics, if not stellar, has produced such a sour public mood.

Inflation measures how much prices have risen over a year, but what irks many people is how much higher prices are than they were before the pandemic. Voters appear to rate the economy relative to the recent past, rather than its outright performance, according to University of Texas at Austin political scientist Christopher Wlezien.

[High mortgage rates](https://www.wsj.com/economy/housing/mortgage-rates-jump-to-a-one-year-high-9ea35b8a?mod=article_inline) and home prices have put buying a home out of reach for many Americans. Nor has the boom in stock prices assuaged discontent. That might be because a growing number of Americans feel that gains in wealth are mostly accruing to those at the top, according to John Dick, chief executive of consumer analytics company CivicScience.

Winston, who advises congressional GOP leaders, said he believes that voter impressions can still change. “People are in constant contact with prices,” he said. “So, if in October people start walking into grocery stores and gas stations and say, ‘I can now handle the cost of living,’ that is the decisive moment.”

Moreover, polling shows only limited signs that the economic discontent is turning into votes for Democratic candidates. As a group, polls show that voters say they will back a Democrat for Congress over a Republican by about 6 points—the same lead Democrats had at this point in 2018, when views of Trump’s management of the economy were far more positive.

“People are maybe a little bit too optimistic about how easy it is going to be to take the House,” said Rep. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez, a Democrat who represents a largely rural Washington state district that Trump won three times. “Yes, Trump is very, very unpopular. That doesn’t mean that Democrats are necessarily popular.”

At the same time, voters can map many of their cost-of-living concerns to actions taken by the White House. Gasoline prices are high as a result of the war with Iran. Tariffs have also added to price pressures.

“The specific economic circumstances we’re dealing with are not really necessarily an inheritance from a previous administration. They are due to very specific decisions that this administration has made,” said Sides.
