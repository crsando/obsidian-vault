### 中文译文

## 成本飙升之际，沙特阿拉伯就国家支持的战争保险进行谈判

![Underwriters have become warier of ships linked to Saudi Arabia in recent months following attacks by Yemen’s Houthi rebels, with these vessels treated more like high-risk Israeli and US assets © Khaled Ziad/AFP/Getty Images](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A26c5a071-813a-4c70-abd1-c769d3ea2f1c?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

*近几个月以来，由于也门胡塞叛军的袭击，承保人对与沙特阿拉伯相关的船只愈发警惕，这些船只得像高风险以色列和美国资产一样对待。© Khaled Ziad/AFP/Getty Images*

[****李·哈里斯****](https://www.ft.com/lee-harris) 伦敦报道

12小时前发布

据知情人士透露，沙特阿拉伯已与伦敦经纪商就一项国家支持的战争和政治风险保险计划进行会谈，该计划可为该地区船只提供保障，因冲突威胁到该王国的贸易。

近几个月来，由于伊朗战争和胡塞武装对沙特阿拉伯的升级打击，保险公司提高了价格并限制了对船舶、基础设施以及石油和化学品等货物的承保范围。

在某些情况下，保险公司拒绝向试图通过包括红海在内的关键咽喉要道进行贸易的船只出售战争险，自霍尔木兹海峡航运受限以来，红海的延布港对该王国的石油出口变得至关重要。

据知情人士透露，沙特财政部已探讨一项计划，根据该计划，船舶和海洋货物资产可通过所谓的共保体获得更便宜的保险，该共保体将为每次承保事件（如船舶被扣押或导弹袭击）提供高达SAR 700mn（$186mn）的商业保障。

这些人士表示，虽然第一层保险保障将由保险公司和再保险公司提供，但通过国有的沙特进出口银行提供的兜底，每个受保实体还可获得数亿美元的额外资金。

在讨论的计划的一个版本中，再保险公司 Saudi Re 和 Riyadh Re 将牵头一组参与的再保险公司，其中也可能包括国际公司。

沙特保险监管局、沙特进出口银行、Saudi Re 和 Riyadh Re 未回应置评请求。

知情人士补充称，有关该计划的谈判仍在进行中，包括条款和沙特政府承担的风险规模，但谈判可能无法达成协议。

此次讨论之前，美国总统唐纳德·特朗普今年早些时候宣布一项计划，对试图通过该海峡的船只提供保险补贴，该计划获得了保险公司 Chubb 和 AIG 的支持。据英国《金融时报》此前报道，该机制旨在提供高达 400 亿美元的保险，但启动数月后[尚未承保任何一美元](https://www.ft.com/content/eabadd1a-a712-4b44-99bf-bb50eb753811?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。

近几个月来，在也门胡塞叛军发动袭击后，承保人对[与沙特阿拉伯有关联的船只](https://www.ft.com/content/52aa89e9-f014-498e-8415-2c435a1c858d?syn-25a6b1a6=1)变得更加警惕，这些船只被视为更像高风险的以色列和美国资产。

政府通常在巨大损失或严重持续威胁使商业保险对企业而言过于昂贵或完全无法获得时，设立保险池。

例如，英国和美国分别在爱尔兰共和军（IRA）轰炸行动和纽约 2001 年 9 月 11 日袭击之后，设立了国家支持的恐怖主义再保险计划。

沙特近期探讨的该计划旨在降低保险成本，帮助船东和其他企业获得更全面的保障，包括战争、政治暴力和恐怖主义保险，这些险种通常单独出售。

“该地区的保险保障肯定仍可买到，我不认为这是一个危机市场，”政治风险咨询公司Enmetena Advisory创始人马克西米利安·赫斯表示。但他补充道，一个保险池可能有助于“确保保险仍以良好的商业费率提供，从而不会对[项目]的经济性和最终投资决策产生过大的实质性影响。”

“该地区直接战争与直接恐怖主义之间的界限模糊，”他说。“胡塞武装的袭击属于战争事件还是恐怖事件？为了确保你现在得到充分保障，你可能需要更广泛的保障范围。”

*尼古拉斯·帕拉西补充报道*

## Saudi Arabia holds talks over state-backed war insurance as costs jump

*Underwriters have become warier of ships linked to Saudi Arabia in recent months following attacks by Yemen’s Houthi rebels, with these vessels treated more like high-risk Israeli and US assets © Khaled Ziad/AFP/Getty Images*

[**](https://www.ft.com/lee-harris)[**Lee Harris**](https://www.ft.com/lee-harris)[**](https://www.ft.com/lee-harris) in London

Published 12 hours ago

Saudi Arabia has held talks with London brokers about a state-backed scheme for war and political risk insurance that could provide cover for ships in the region, according to people familiar with discussions, as conflict threatens the kingdom’s trade.

Insurers have raised prices and restricted coverage for vessels, infrastructure and cargo such as oil and chemicals in recent months owing to the Iran war and the Houthis’ [escalating campaign](https://www.ft.com/content/17583c3f-f8f4-44f8-bb2e-277a8304f252?syn-25a6b1a6=1) against Saudi Arabia.

In some cases, insurers have refused to sell war cover to ships seeking to trade through vital chokepoints including the Red Sea, whose port of Yanbu has become critical to the kingdom’s oil exports since the throttling of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

The Saudi Ministry of Finance has explored a plan under which ships and marine cargo assets could obtain cheaper insurance through a so-called pool, which would offer as much as SAR 700mn ($186mn) in commercial cover for each insured event, such as the seizure of a ship or a missile attack, according to people familiar with talks.

While the first layers of insurance coverage would be provided by insurers and reinsurers, the people said, hundreds of millions more dollars would be available for each insured entity through a backstop provided through the state-owned Saudi Export-Import Bank.

In one version of the plans discussed, reinsurers Saudi Re and Riyadh Re would lead a group of participating reinsurers, which could also include international firms.

The Saudi Insurance Authority, Exim Bank, Saudi Re and Riyadh Re did not respond to requests for comment.

Talks about the programme, including over terms and the amount of risk shouldered by the Saudi government, were ongoing and could fail to result in an agreement, the people added.

The discussions come after an effort announced earlier this year by US President Donald Trump to subsidise insurance for ships seeking to transit the strait, which secured backing from insurers Chubb and AIG. The facility had aimed to provide as much as $40bn in cover but months after launching had [not written a single dollar of cover](https://www.ft.com/content/eabadd1a-a712-4b44-99bf-bb50eb753811?syn-25a6b1a6=1), the FT previously reported.

Underwriters have become warier of [ships linked to Saudi Arabia](https://www.ft.com/content/52aa89e9-f014-498e-8415-2c435a1c858d?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in recent months following attacks by Yemen’s Houthi rebels, with these vessels treated more like high-risk Israeli and US assets.

Governments typically set up insurance pools after huge losses or serious ongoing threats make commercial insurance too costly or wholly unavailable for businesses.

For example, both the UK and the US set up state-backed terrorism reinsurance schemes following the IRA’s bombing campaign and New York’s September 11 2001 attacks respectively.

The scheme recently explored by Saudi would aim to bring down insurance costs and help shipowners and other businesses obtain more comprehensive coverage, including for war, political violence and terrorism, which are often sold separately.

“Cover is certainly still attainable in the region, I don’t see this as a market in crisis,” said Maximilian Hess, founder of Enmetena Advisory, a political risk consultancy. But he added that a pool could help “ensure that insurance is still available at good commercial rates that don’t have too much of a meaningful impact on [project] economics and final investment decisions.

“The lines between direct war and direct terrorism in the region are blurred,” he said. “Are the Houthi attacks a war incident or terror? To ensure that you’re adequately covered now, you might want broader coverage.”

*Additional reporting by Nicolas Parasie*
