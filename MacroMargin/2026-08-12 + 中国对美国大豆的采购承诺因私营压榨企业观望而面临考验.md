### 中文译文

## 中国对美国大豆的采购承诺因私营压榨企业观望而面临考验

中国大规模采购美国大豆的努力——这是去年贸易休战协议下的一项承诺——可能因国内私营贸易商参与不足而变得复杂。

近几周，在习近平主席与唐纳德·特朗普总统预计于[9月举行的峰会](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-06/trump-says-he-expects-to-host-china-s-xi-in-us-around-sept-24)之前，大豆采购有所加快。但这一采购狂潮主要由中国大型国有农业贸易商主导，私营部门基本持观望态度，因为这宗贸易在商业上不可行。

中国承诺购买包括大豆在内的美国农产品，是习近平与特朗普10月达成的贸易休战协议的关键内容，该协议旨在结束一场威胁全球前两大经济体的不断升级的争端。双方下月计划在华盛顿举行的会谈中将审议该协议的进展。

交易商称，尽管私营部门未参与大豆采购并不意味着中国无法实现白宫提出的每年2500万吨的目标，但考虑到国有供应链中仓储和压榨能力有限，这使任务变得更加棘手。

物产中大期货有限公司分析师刘浩文表示，私营压榨企业大多在观望并随机应变，如果美国大豆供应不能及时到位，第四季度国内豆粕供应可能会收紧。

仅中粮集团和中储粮就完成了特朗普与习近平2025年峰会时提出的购买[1200万吨](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-20/china-reaches-12-million-tons-of-soy-purchases-to-meet-us-pledge)美国大豆的初步目标。致电这两家公司均未获接听。

上周，中国国有企业又[预订了一批](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/china-books-13-more-us-soy-cargoes-nearing-quarter-of-target)美国大豆货物，使2026-27年度总采购量达到约600万吨。这相当于白宫称中国到2028年每年将购买2500万吨目标的四分之一。北京方面从未公开确认具体目标。

私营进口商通常从8月起随着收获季节临近开始大量预订美国货物，但美国产品仍面临更高进口关税，以及[脆弱](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/china-hits-us-pecans-with-steep-levies-before-xi-trump-summit)的中美关系，意味着这宗贸易并不吸引人。

与此同时，来自巴西的船货正变得更昂贵。交易商称，中国商业压榨厂对11月至1月的需求大体上保持开放，因为巴西大豆的利润率进入负值区间。

五昌中大（音译）的刘姓分析师表示，中国充足的大豆和豆粕库存将缓解任何直接的供应冲击。但这一缓冲可能不会持续太久，而且确保额外供应的窗口正在收窄。

### English Original

## China Pledge on US Soybeans Tested as Private Crushers Hold Back

China’s drive to buy large volumes of US soybeans — a commitment under last year’s trade truce — risks being complicated by a lack of participation from the country’s private traders.

Purchases of soybeans have accelerated in recent weeks, ahead of an expected [September summit](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-06/trump-says-he-expects-to-host-china-s-xi-in-us-around-sept-24) between President Xi Jinping and President Donald Trump. But the buying splurge is being spearheaded by China’s giant state-owned agricultural traders, with the private sector largely sitting on the sidelines because the trade isn’t commercially viable.

China’s pledge to buy US agricultural products including soybeans was a key plank of the trade truce struck by Xi and Trump in October to end an escalating dispute threatening the world’s top two economies. Progress on that agreement will be reviewed at their planned talks in Washington next month.

While the lack of private participation in soybean purchasing doesn’t mean China can’t meet a 25-million-ton annual target outlined by the White House, it makes for a trickier task given limited storage and crushing capacity in the state supply chain, traders said.

Private crushers are largely waiting and playing by ear, and soybean meal supplies could tighten up domestically in the fourth quarter if more US soybean supplies aren’t readily available, said Liu Haowen, analyst with Wuchan Zhongda Futures Co. Ltd.

Cofco and Sinograin alone completed the initial goal of buying [12 million tons](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-20/china-reaches-12-million-tons-of-soy-purchases-to-meet-us-pledge) of US beans made at Trump and Xi’s 2025 summit. Calls to both companies were not answered.

State-owned Chinese firms booked [another flurry](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/china-books-13-more-us-soy-cargoes-nearing-quarter-of-target) of US soybean cargoes last week, bringing total purchases for the 2026-27 season to about 6 million tons. That’s about a quarter of the 25 million tons the White House said China will buy annually through 2028. Beijing never publicly affirmed specific targets.

The private importers typically start booking American shipments in significant volumes from August as the harvest approaches, but higher import tariffs still in place on US products — and [fragile](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/china-hits-us-pecans-with-steep-levies-before-xi-trump-summit) China-US ties — means the trade isn’t attractive.

At the same time, shipments from Brazil are getting more expensive. Commercial crushers in China have left their demand for November to January largely open, as margins for Brazilian beans go into negative territory, traders said.

Any immediate supply shock would be cushioned by China’s ample inventories of soybeans and soybean meal, Wuchang Zhongda’s Liu said. But that buffer may not last long, and the window to secure additional supplies is narrowing.
