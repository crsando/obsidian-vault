## 中国可再生能源改革促使企业进军电力交易

彭博新闻 2026年8月25日 11:24 AM GMT+8

### **要点**（彭博 AI）

### **要点**

- PCG Power 计划下月推出一个交易平台，以进入中国日益增长的电力市场。
- 包括136号文件在内的政策改革，正通过迫使风电和光伏项目在公开市场出售电力，创造交易机会。
- 该公司目标是2027年交易量达到100亿千瓦时，并已与英国 Octopus Energy Group 成立合资企业，以引入交易经验。
屋顶太阳能开发商 [PCG Power](https://www.pcgpower.com/en) 计划下月推出一个交易平台，以进入中国日益增长的电力市场。

削弱可再生能源利润的政策改革正在创造交易机会，董事长李文轩在北京接受采访时表示。该公司还提供储能服务，并为大型工业用户管理电力采购，其目标是2027年交易量达到100亿千瓦时，足以为一个欧洲小国供电一年。

虽然中国是全球最大的电力消费国，但在通过供需匹配定价的自由市场交易方面，中国落后于美国和欧洲。政府正寻求在本十年末建立统一的全国市场来改变这一现状。

PCG更直接的触发因素是去年生效的一项政策，即“136号文件”，该文件强制风电和光伏项目在公开市场上出售电力，而不是由电网保量收购。

“136号文件终结了保量和保价，但同时打开了市场化运营的大门，”李说。“如果我们能把发电运营好，资产不仅能保值，还能获得升值空间。”

### **Octopus合资企业**

这家总部位于杭州的公司成立于2022年，已为大型工业客户开发了约3吉瓦的光伏项目，其中大部分为屋顶光伏。今年早些时候，它与英国Octopus Energy Group成立了一家合资企业——即英国Octopus Energy宣布与PCG电力成立中国合资企业——李表示，这将带来“来自全球电力市场的交易经验”。

几十年来，中国依靠自上而下的方式管理电力市场，政府定价，电网运营商决定哪些电厂发电以及何时发电。这一体系卓有成效，有助于保持价格稳定，并协调了支撑国家快速增长的大规模基础设施建设。

近年来，北京转向减少监管，这是习近平主席领导下推动市场信号引导经济的更广泛努力的一部分。

进展一直缓慢。[去年](https://www.nea.gov.cn/20260618/41e848b9ec6543998757db3864e01ab2/c.html)，根据国家能源局的数据，超过60%的电力消费通过市场化交易交付。但绝大多数都是定期合同，而非能更高效匹配供需的次日交易。

短期交易显示出增长迹象。国家能源局表示，全国超过一半的省份现在已有市场，上半年所有电力交易中有12%为[现货交易](https://www.nea.gov.cn/20260727/560b4ea8e0254484a08ea551ce54bde2/c.html)。

然而，增长并不自动意味着盈利。鉴于中国的规模，李预计将有数千家企业进入市场。但只有那些拥有最佳预测能力并掌握不断演变的市场规则的企业才能成功。

“每个人都看到了巨大的市场，但技术难度极高，”李说。“也许只有10%的公司能真正盈利。”

## China’s Renewable Energy Overhaul Spurs Push Into Power Trading

By Bloomberg NewsAugust 25, 2026 at 11:24 AM GMT+8

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

### **Takeaways**

- PCG Power plans to launch a trading platform next month to tap into China’s growing market for electricity.
- Policy reforms, including Document 136, are creating opportunities in trading by forcing wind and solar projects to sell their electricity on the open market.
- The company is targeting transactions of 10 billion kilowatt-hours in 2027 and has formed a joint venture with the UK’s Octopus Energy Group to bring in trading experience.
Rooftop solar developer [PCG Power ](https://www.pcgpower.com/en)plans to launch a trading platform next month to tap into China’s growing market for electricity.

Policy reforms that have undercut profits on renewables are creating opportunities in trading, Chairman Li Wenxuan said in an interview in Beijing. The company, which also supplies energy storage and manages electricity purchases for large industrial users, is targeting transactions of 10 billion kilowatt-hours in 2027, enough to power a small European country for a year.

Although China is the world’s largest electricity consumer, it lags the US and Europe when it comes to free-market transactions that set prices by matching supply with demand. The government is looking to change that by establishing a unified national market by the end of the decade.

PCG’s more immediate trigger is a policy that went into effect last year, known as Document 136, which forces wind and solar projects to sell their electricity on the open market instead of guaranteed purchases from the grid.

“Document 136 ended guaranteed volume and guaranteed pricing, but at the same time it opened the door to market-based operations,” Li said. “If we can operate generation well, the asset doesn’t just retain investment value, it gains upside.”

### **Octopus Venture**

The Hangzhou-based company was founded in 2022 and has developed about 3 gigawatts of mostly rooftop solar for large industrial customers. Earlier this year, it formed a UK’s Octopus Energy Announces China JV With PCG Power with the UK’s Octopus Energy Group, which will bring in “trading experience from power markets around the world,” Li said.

For decades, China has relied on a top-down approach to its power market, with government setting prices and grid operators deciding which plants generate electricity and when. The system has paid dividends, helping to keep prices stable and coordinate the massive build-out of infrastructure that has underpinned the country’s rapid growth.

In recent years, Beijing has pivoted to less regulation, part of a broader push under President Xi Jinping to allow market signals to guide the economy.

Progress has been slow. [Last year](https://www.nea.gov.cn/20260618/41e848b9ec6543998757db3864e01ab2/c.html), more than 60% of power consumption was delivered via private transactions, according to the National Energy Administration. But the vast majority of those were term contracts and not the next-day deals that would more efficiently match supply and demand.

Short-term trading is showing signs of growth. More than half the country’s provinces now have markets, and 12% of all power transactions were of the [spot variety](https://www.nea.gov.cn/20260727/560b4ea8e0254484a08ea551ce54bde2/c.html) in the first half, the NEA said.

Growth doesn’t automatically mean profits, though. Given China’s size, Li expects thousands of players to enter the market. But only those with the best forecasting abilities and grasp of evolving market rules will succeed.

“Everyone sees a huge market, but the technical difficulty is extremely high,” Li said. “Maybe only 10% of companies will truly be profitable.”
