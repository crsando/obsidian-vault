## 高盛称欧盟限制措施或影响中国对欧盟27%的出口

### [科技](https://www.bloomberg.com/technology)

彭博新闻社 2026年8月24日 晚上8:07 GMT+8

### **要点** 由彭博人工智能提供

- 据高盛集团称，欧盟针对中国的现有及拟议贸易措施可能覆盖中国对欧盟年度名义出口的约27%。
- 欧盟的限制措施，包括对插电式混合动力汽车征收新关税以及扩大欧盟碳边境调节机制，可能威胁中国的市场份额增长，但成本竞争力和关键材料的杠杆作用应能缓冲影响。
- 高盛经济学家预计欧盟政策将更加严厉，但不会采取可能引发北京强烈反制的措施，因为欧盟在关键材料上依赖中国，且中国具有成本优势和产品质量提升。
据高盛集团称，欧盟针对中国的现有及拟议贸易措施可能覆盖中国对欧盟年度名义出口的约27%，显示双方经济之间[对峙](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/china-criticizes-eu-over-extraterritorial-reach-in-jd-com-probe)的利害关系。

高盛经济学家陈新泉（Xinquan Chen）和Chelsea Song在周日的一份报告中表示，欧盟的限制范围“并不直接转化为出口损失，实际影响取决于最终政策细节和实施情况。” “更广泛的限制将威胁中国的市场份额增长，尽管成本竞争力、关键材料的杠杆作用以及欧洲的商业利益应能缓冲影响。”

尽管在去年贸易战后与美国的关系趋于稳定，但在10月解决创纪录贸易失衡的最后期限之前，北京与布鲁塞尔的对抗正在加剧，欧盟领导人越来越将这一失衡视为战略挑战。

鉴于欧盟去年吸收了中国的海外销售额的15%，且近几个月出货量不断上升，高盛经济学家表示：“这已成为中国出口前景日益增长的政策风险。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b14810ead6dceedf9df74be/3c681cc9-0b14-8129-b998-f136cfd2de87-92956991541323756d1614c3430ecc08.png)

根据高盛的说法，欧盟的主要新提议之一是针对插电式混合动力汽车征收新关税。此外，欧盟碳边境调节机制（CBAM）的扩展，即欧盟CBAM义务计算器（EUCBAM 1.0.4），经济学家表示：“代表重大升级，中国面临的敞口最大。”

这家美国银行表示，CBAM旨在对在较弱环境规则下生产的较便宜商品征税，其范围可能会扩大到基本钢铁和铝产品之外，额外纳入580亿美元的出口。高盛表示，该提议意味着电气和运输设备以及机械设备将主要面临风险，并估计这三个行业去年对中国对欧盟名义出口增长8.5%的贡献率为4.9个百分点。

高盛表示，这种威胁可能还很遥远，实际实施预计最早要到2028年。它表示：“下游产品的实际税率可能仅占最终出口价格的一小部分。”

尽管高盛经济学家警告称，与中国与欧盟的贸易摩擦对中国出口前景构成“下行风险”，但他们对于未来的危险相对乐观。

他们表示，中国的成本优势和产品质量的提升“仍然是一个重要的缓冲”。高盛称，由于欧盟按重量计算90%以上的稀土元素依赖中国，欧洲官员“有动力保留进入中国市场的渠道，并避免重大报复”。

经济学家们表示：“因此，我们预计欧盟政策将变得更加严格，但不会采取可能引发北京强烈反应的措施。”

## EU’s Curbs Threaten 27% of Chinese Exports to Bloc, Goldman Says

### [Technology](https://www.bloomberg.com/technology)

By Bloomberg NewsAugust 24, 2026 at 8:07 PM GMT+8

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

- The European Union's existing and proposed trade measures against China could cover about 27% of its annual nominal exports to the bloc, according to Goldman Sachs Group Inc.
- The EU's curbs, including fresh tariffs on plug-in hybrids and an extension of the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, may threaten China's market-share gains, though cost competitiveness and leverage in critical materials should cushion the impact.
- Goldman's economists expect EU policy to become tougher, but to stop short of measures likely to trigger a severe response from Beijing, due to the EU's reliance on China for certain materials and China's cost advantages and product quality improvements.
The European Union’s existing and proposed trade measures against China could cover about 27% of its annual nominal exports to the bloc, according to Goldman Sachs Group Inc., showing the stakes involved in the [standoff](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/china-criticizes-eu-over-extraterritorial-reach-in-jd-com-probe) between the two economies.

The range of the EU’s curbs “does not translate directly to export loss, with actual impact contingent on final policy specifics and implementation,” Goldman economists Xinquan Chen and Chelsea Song said in a report Sunday. “Broader restrictions would threaten China’s market-share gains, though cost competitiveness, leverage in critical materials and Europe’s commercial interests should cushion the impact.”

While ties with the US have stabilized after last year’s tariff war, Beijing’s standoff with Brussels is intensifying ahead of an October deadline to address record trade imbalances that EU leaders have increasingly cast as a strategic challenge.

With the bloc absorbing 15% of China’s sales abroad last year and shipments on the rise in recent months, “this has become a growing policy risk for China’s export outlook,” Goldman’s economists said.

Among the EU’s main new proposals are fresh tariffs on plug-in hybrids, according to Goldman. What’s more, an extension of the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, or EU CBAM Obligation Calculator (EUCBAM 1.0.4), “represents a major escalation, with China facing the largest exposure,” the economists said.

Designed to tax cheaper goods produced under weaker environmental rules, the CBAM may be expanded beyond basic steel and aluminum products to include an additional $58 billion of exports, the US bank said. The proposal means electrical and transport equipment, along with machinery, would be primarily at risk, according to Goldman, which estimates the three sectors contributed 4.9 percentage points of China’s 8.5% nominal export growth to the EU last year.

The threat is likely far off, Goldman said, with the effective implementation expected no earlier than in 2028. “And the actual tax rate on downstream products may represent only a small share of the final export price,” it said.

While warning of a “downside risk” to China’s export outlook from its trade frictions with the EU, Goldman’s economists are relatively sanguine about the dangers ahead.

China’s cost advantages and improvements in product quality “remain a significant buffer,” they said. And with the EU relying on China for more than 90% of its rare earth elements by weight, European officials “have incentives to preserve access to the Chinese market and avoid significant retaliation,” according to Goldman.

“We therefore expect EU policy to become tougher, but to stop short of measures likely to trigger a severe response from Beijing,” the economists said.
