### 中文译文

## 中国芯片行业迎来突破时刻

1979年春天，美中建交后不久，一个美国官方贸易代表团抵达上海。当时中国一直在尝试制造集成电路——计算机的基本单元，而冷战时期的出口禁令使这一努力更加困难。政策制定者们渴望得到建议。在一家国营工厂，美国高管们目睹工人们（主要是年轻女性）借助镊子和显微镜将微小的硅线摆放到位，然后手工焊接。参观结束后，中方官员急切地向来访者征求改进其过时工艺的建议。

在随后的几十年里，中国眼睁睁看着日本、韩国和台湾地区冲到了半导体行业的前沿。这些较小的邻居大约在同一时间开始制造芯片，但当它们取得爆炸性成功时，中国仍然落后。2015年，中国政府发布了雄心勃勃的《[中国制造2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-10-31/almost-10-years-later-china-s-made-in-2025-has-succeeded)》蓝图，旨在掌握尖端产业，但当年中国约85%的需求依赖外国半导体，进口芯片的花费超过进口石油。

由于毛泽东时代动荡和冷战孤立造成的深层技术赤字似乎难以克服。仅靠砸钱不够。《中国制造2025》承诺投入约1500亿美元的政府资金来培育国内芯片产业，地方政府也自掏腰包。五年过去，前景黯淡，许多项目毫无进展，腐败调查正在进行，旗舰企业之一[紫光集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-01-17/chinese-chip-giant-weighs-ipos-land-sales-to-slash-debt-burden)也陷入破产。

然而在过去几年里，中国让怀疑者大跌眼镜。[华为技术有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH)的人工智能芯片现已足够先进，能够[训练复杂的大语言模型](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-14/china-s-zhipu-unveils-new-ai-model-trained-on-huawei-s-chips)、操作自动驾驶汽车并指挥人形机器人的动作。中国企业正在开发本土制造一些[芯片制造工具](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/china-duv-chipmaking-how-machine-advances-could-reduce-reliance-on-asml-nvidia)的方法，并提升从国外购买的旧设备的性能，这在很大程度上受到美国阻止销售最新外国设备的刺激。7月，存储芯片制造商[长鑫存储技术有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity)（CXMT）在[股价暴涨466%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/china-memory-champion-cxmt-set-to-debut-after-9-8-billion-ipo)后，超越大型石油生产商和国有银行，成为在中国上市的最高市值公司。这是今年全球第三大IPO，仅次于[SpaceX](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SPCX%20US%20Equity)和[SK海力士](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/000660%20KP%20Equity)。

这些进步反映出，必要的要素正在汇聚，以打造一个可行的本土半导体产业。历史上，取得突破式成功的芯片制造商都得益于两个因素的结合：一场有效的国家产业政策运动，以及至少一个强大的、忠实的客户，推动他们实现创新突破。

![Undated photograph of Texas Instruments’ semiconductor product plant in Dallas. Source: Richardson Public Library](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14817784ecca1458831b1e/3bf81cc9-0b14-81fc-ba53-e2cd8f87fd3e-ef062b7d10b5ece23cfaa19df0c7d75a.png)

未注日期的德州仪器达拉斯半导体产品工厂照片。图片来源：理查森公共图书馆

以[德州仪器公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TXN%20US%20Equity)为例，它是该行业第一家占主导地位的芯片制造商，在20世纪60年代和70年代经历了黄金时代。它的崛起恰逢美苏太空竞赛，巨额联邦合同和拨款保障了其财务，并为其雄心勃勃的研发项目提供资金。对德州仪器同样关键的是，它是[国际商业机器公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/IBM%20US%20Equity)早期大型主机电脑的主要供应商。IBM在还不确定德州仪器能否真的交付时就承诺了大额订单，两家公司的工程师合作了数月，以克服制造晶体管的那些技术障碍。

接下来是[英特尔公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/INTC%20US%20Equity)。1980年，当IBM开始秘密项目打造其第一台个人电脑时，它选择了这家加州公司而非德州仪器作为供应商。这使英特尔在个人电脑时代抢占先机，帮助其在接下来几十年里稳坐全球领先芯片制造商的地位。在80年代后半期，英特尔还成为一项联邦补贴计划的主要受益者之一，该计划帮助美国芯片制造商在与新的低成本日本对手的残酷竞争中生存下来。

![The IBM Personal Computer system, introduced in 1981. Photographer: SSPL/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14817784ecca1458831b1e/3bf81cc9-0b14-813c-9a55-dbddb2423f96-c6b9a2c798956398af6ddbf9f469ae4a.png)

1981年推出的IBM个人电脑系统。摄影师：SSPL/Getty Images

最近，英特尔的光芒被[台积电](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSM%20US%20Equity)所掩盖，后者是在台湾政府及其数十年专注而坚定的产业政策支持下建立起来的。在与[苹果公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AAPL%20US%20Equity)、[英伟达公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity)等企业建立了紧密的共生关系，并帮助它们开发了一代又一代新产品之后，台积电已[近乎垄断](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2024-10-21/tsmc-s-dominance-is-starting-to-worry-more-than-just-rivals)全球先进芯片制造。

这两个因素——政府有重点的支持以及与创新客户的合作——目前正推动中国芯片制造商的发展。为应对美国对某些高科技产品的出口禁令，北京一直在调整其半导体发展目标，当前的首要任务是攻克几个可能阻碍中国人工智能雄心的特定技术瓶颈。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14817784ecca1458831b1e/3bf81cc9-0b14-81d9-b5f8-e8ada2ece8c9-9f7c79113b6d1c184f41fc0792d26829.png)

与此同时，中国科技巨头华为和[腾讯控股有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity)已达到一定的规模和科技复杂程度，能够作为核心客户和研发伙伴提供有力支持，帮助本土芯片制造商克服技术障碍。这些中国公司此前更倾向于与领先的国际芯片制造商合作开发最先进的产品，但在美国强制隔离的新时代，它们已不再有这种选择。

对于像长鑫存储（CXMT）这样的公司来说，这些有利因素汇聚在一起，提供了千载难逢的大展宏图的机会。CXMT创始人朱一明毕业于北京精英学府清华大学，职业生涯初期在硅谷度过，但他的第一家芯片初创公司的经历截然不同。据一份大学校友杂志的报道，2005年创办[兆易创新（GigaDevice Semiconductor Inc.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3986%20HK%20Equity)时，朱一明费尽心力向投资者凑集100万美元，反复打电话恳求清华校友出资。公司资源匮乏，使得兆易创新只得将雄心局限于设计专用闪存产品，并外包生产。自建晶圆厂不在预算之内。

![CXMT headquarters in Hefei, China. Photographer: CFOTO/Future Publishing/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14817784ecca1458831b1e/3bf81cc9-0b14-815b-b0b0-ea2432382526-f6417ac692bcec2e820ee15af8d1eb60.png)

中国合肥的长鑫存储总部。摄影师：CFOTO/Future Publishing/Getty Images

到2016年长鑫存储成立时，闸门已经打开。从一开始，朱一明的第二家初创企业就获得了数十亿美元的政府资金，并肩负着自建先进工厂、与内存芯片全球领导者[三星](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/005930%20KS%20Equity)、SK海力士和美光正面竞争的任务。该公司亏损了十年，直到今年才实现盈利并弥补累计亏损，目前在该领域全球排名第四。乔治城大学安全与新兴技术中心非常驻研究员约翰·沃维（John VerWey）表示，中国优先发展对新手来说更容易生产的内存芯片，是在效仿日本30年前成功打进半导体行业的路径。

长鑫存储正竞相开发“高带宽存储器”（high-bandwidth memory），这是一种内存芯片，是英伟达AI系统的关键组成部分，但中国尚无法商业化生产。据其招股说明书，该公司的客户包括[阿里巴巴](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9988%20HK%20Equity)、字节跳动、[联想](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/992%20HK%20Equity)、腾讯和[小米](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1810%20HK%20Equity)，它们对于帮助国内供应商实现这一能力有着切身利益。

总部位于香港的科技研究公司 Counterpoint Research 联合创始人 Neil Shah 指出，尽管 CXMT 与行业领导者之间仍存在“巨大的技术代差”，但该公司正借助国内生态系统积极缩小差距。他表示：“制裁起初威胁要扼杀增长，但最终推动公司进行微创新以实现自给自足。”CXMT 的研发推进还得益于时机巧合：人工智能热潮引发了[全球存储芯片短缺](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-boom-memory-chip-shortage/)，这不仅充实了公司资金，还保证了未来一段时间的需求。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14817784ecca1458831b1e/3bf81cc9-0b14-8130-b7bb-c40c238fe907-5b6a4584801ac47d8cff29f08a3f1db6.png)

当然，这些项目仍有许多可能出错的地方。国内联盟可能破裂或陷入内斗。路透社最近报道，CXMT 曾突然下令一家华为关联供应商的工程师停止在其研发设施工作，原因是对该芯片制造商提价感到不满。

与此同时，美国政府继续努力遏制中国在该领域的进步，美国官员警告称，这些技术不仅将用于民用目的，还将用于升级中国军力。2018 年，CXMT 的竞争对手[福建晋华集成电路有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1684194D:CH)——该公司起初看起来更有前景，拥有更多启动资金和更大的建设计划——因美国指控其[合谋窃取](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-11-01/u-s-says-china-state-owned-co-stole-micron-trade-secrets)[美光科技公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MU%20US%20Equity)的商业机密而脱轨。这家中国芯片制造商于 2024 年被判无罪，但尚未恢复元气。中国周期性的反腐行动又增加了不确定性。国有的紫光集团前董事长去年因挪用国家资金被判处死缓。

在某种程度上，经过几十年的国际合作伙伴关系后，中国的半导体行业正在变得更加封闭。在美中科技竞争紧张的背景下，重要的研发项目正秘密进行。希望一窥公司内部运作的外部人士，只能满足于类似 1979 年那次参观——一次罕见、严格编排的管窥。但中国自主推进技术进步的能力比以往任何时候都强。经过多年完善产业政策、参与国际交流以及提升供应链上下游的技术能力，中国已经拥有足够完整的生态系统，即使在孤立的情况下也可能继续创新。

### English Original

## China’s Chip Industry Is Having a Breakout Moment

In the spring of 1979, shortly after the US established diplomatic relations with the People’s Republic of China, an official American trade delegation landed in Shanghai. China had been attempting to build integrated circuits, the basic units of computers, an endeavor made more difficult by Cold War-era export bans. Its policymakers were eager for input. At a state-run factory, US executives watched as workers, mainly young women, maneuvered the tiny silicon wires into place with the help of tweezers and microscopes, then bonded them by hand. After the tour, the Chinese officials pumped the visitors for suggestions on improving their outdated processes.

In the decades that followed, China watched as Japan, South Korea and Taiwan hurtled to the front lines of the semiconductor industry. Those smaller neighbors had started chipmaking about the same time, but while they attained explosive success, China continued to lag behind. In 2015, the year the Chinese government published its ambitious [Made in China 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-10-31/almost-10-years-later-china-s-made-in-2025-has-succeeded) blueprint for mastery of leading-edge industries, the nation relied on foreign-made semiconductors to cover about 85% of its needs, spending more to import chips than petroleum.

The deep technological deficit, resulting from the chaotic Mao Zedong years and Cold War isolation, seemed too large to overcome. Throwing money at the problem wasn’t enough. Made in China 2025 committed roughly $150 billion in government funds toward fostering a domestic chip industry, with local municipalities kicking in some of their own money. Five years in, the outlook was grim, with a number of the projects going nowhere, graft investigations underway and one of the flagship companies, [Tsinghua Unigroup Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-01-17/chinese-chip-giant-weighs-ipos-land-sales-to-slash-debt-burden), collapsing into bankruptcy.

Yet in the past couple of years, China has surprised the skeptics. [Huawei Technologies Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH)’s artificial intelligence chips are now advanced enough to [train sophisticated large language models](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-14/china-s-zhipu-unveils-new-ai-model-trained-on-huawei-s-chips), operate self-driving cars and direct the movements of humanoid robots. Chinese companies are developing methods to build some [chipmaking tools](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/china-duv-chipmaking-how-machine-advances-could-reduce-reliance-on-asml-nvidia) domestically and to goose the performance of older ones purchased abroad, spurred in no small part by US efforts to block sales of the newest foreign gear. And in July memory-chip maker [ChangXin Memory Technologies Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity), or CXMT, soared past hulking oil producers and state-run banks to grab the title of most valuable publicly listed company in China after its [shares surged 466%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/china-memory-champion-cxmt-set-to-debut-after-9-8-billion-ipo) following an initial public offering. It was the third-largest IPO in the world this year, behind those of [SpaceX](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SPCX%20US%20Equity) and [SK Hynix Inc](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/000660%20KP%20Equity).

These advances reflect how the necessary ingredients are coming together to deliver a viable homegrown semiconductor industry. Historically, chipmakers that have attained breakout success benefited from the confluence of two factors: an effective national industrial policy campaign and at least one powerful, committed customer that helped push them to achieve innovative breakthroughs.

Undated photograph of Texas Instruments’ semiconductor product plant in Dallas. Source: Richardson Public Library

Take [Texas Instruments Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TXN%20US%20Equity), the industry’s first dominant chipmaker, which enjoyed a golden era through the 1960s and ’70s. Its rise coincided with the US-Soviet space race, with hefty federal contracts and grants securing its finances and funding its ambitious research and development program. Also crucial for TI was its role as a key supplier to [International Business Machines Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/IBM%20US%20Equity)’s early mainframe computers. IBM committed to massive orders before it was even clear if TI could actually deliver, and the two companies’ engineers worked together for months to overcome the technical obstacles of fabricating the transistors.

Next was [Intel Corp](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/INTC%20US%20Equity). When IBM began a stealth project to build its first personal computer in 1980, it chose the Californian company over TI as its supplier. This head start into the PC era helped secure Intel’s status as the world’s leading chipmaker for the next several decades. In the second half of the ’80s, Intel also became one of the major beneficiaries of a federal subsidy program to help American chipmakers survive the brutal competition against new, low-cost Japanese rivals.

The IBM Personal Computer system, introduced in 1981. Photographer: SSPL/Getty Images

More recently, Intel has seen its star eclipsed by [Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSM%20US%20Equity), which was built with the help of Taiwan’s government and its decades of focused and determined industrial policy. After forging close symbiotic relationships with [Apple Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AAPL%20US%20Equity), [Nvidia Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity) and other companies, and helping them develop generations of new products, TSMC has gained a [near-monopoly](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2024-10-21/tsmc-s-dominance-is-starting-to-worry-more-than-just-rivals) on global advanced-chip manufacturing.

Both of these factors—focused governmental support and collaborations with innovative customers—are now buoying China’s chipmakers. In response to US bans on certain high-tech exports, Beijing has been refining its goals for semiconductor development, with the priority today to surmount several specific technological bottlenecks that could hinder the nation’s AI aspirations.

At the same time, Chinese tech giants Huawei and [Tencent Holdings Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity) have reached a level of scale and technological sophistication at which they can provide meaningful support as anchor customers and R&D partners to help local chipmakers overcome technical obstacles. These Chinese companies had previously preferred to work with leading international chipmakers to develop their most advanced products, but in the new era of US-enforced isolation, they no longer have that option.

For a company such as CXMT, the aligning of these stars offers a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity to go big. CXMT founder Zhu Yiming, a graduate of Beijing’s elite Tsinghua University who spent the early part of his career in Silicon Valley, had a very different experience with his first chip startup. When he started [GigaDevice Semiconductor Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3986%20HK%20Equity), in 2005, Zhu struggled to cobble together $1 million from investors, repeatedly calling to implore fellow Tsinghua alumni to pitch in, according to a profile in a university alumni magazine. Its meager resources moved GigaDevice to limit its ambitions to designing specialized flash-memory products, with outsourced production. Building its own fab wasn’t in the budget.

CXMT headquarters in Hefei, China. Photographer: CFOTO/Future Publishing/Getty Images

By the time CXMT was founded, in 2016, the floodgates had opened. From the outset, Zhu’s second startup had billions of dollars in governmental funding, plus a mandate to build its own advanced factories and go head-to-head with the global leaders in memory chips: [Samsung](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/005930%20KS%20Equity), SK Hynix and Micron. The company lost money for a decade, turning profitable and recouping its accumulated losses only this year, as the global No. 4 in its field. John VerWey, a nonresident fellow at Georgetown University’s Center for Security and Emerging Technology, says that, in prioritizing memory chips, which are easier to produce for newcomers, China was emulating the path Japan successfully took 30 years earlier to secure a foothold in the semiconductor industry.

CXMT is now racing to develop “ high-bandwidth memory,” a type of memory chip that’s a crucial part of Nvidia’s AI systems but that China isn’t yet able to produce commercially. The company’s customers—which include [Alibaba](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9988%20HK%20Equity), ByteDance, [Lenovo](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/992%20HK%20Equity), Tencent and [Xiaomi](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1810%20HK%20Equity), according to its prospectus—have a vested interest in helping domestic suppliers achieve this capability.

Neil Shah, co-founder of tech research firm Counterpoint Research in Hong Kong, notes that though there’s still a “massive technical generation gap” between CXMT and industry leaders, the company is aggressively narrowing the distance with the help of the domestic ecosystem. “While sanctions initially threatened to stifle growth, they ultimately pushed the company to drive micro-innovations for self-sufficiency,” he says. CXMT’s R&D push is also benefiting from fortuitous timing: The AI boom has created a [global memory-chip shortage](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-boom-memory-chip-shortage/) that’s filling the company’s coffers and guaranteeing demand for some time to come.

Of course, there are still many ways these projects could go awry. Domestic alliances could break down or become mired in infighting. Reuters recently reported that CXMT had abruptly ordered engineers from a Huawei-affiliated vendor to cease work in its R&D facility, amid unhappiness from Huawei over the chipmaker’s hiking its prices.

Meanwhile the US government is continuing its efforts to thwart China’s advancement in the field, with American officials warning that these technologies will be used not only for civilian purposes but also to upgrade China’s military. In 2018 the prospects of CXMT rival [Fujian Jinhua Integrated Circuit Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1684194D:CH)—which started out looking more promising, with more startup capital and bigger construction plans—were derailed when the US indicted it on allegations of [conspiring to steal trade secrets](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-11-01/u-s-says-china-state-owned-co-stole-micron-trade-secrets) from [Micron Technology Inc](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MU%20US%20Equity). The Chinese chipmaker was acquitted of the charges in 2024 but has yet to regain its footing. China’s periodic corruption purges add another element of uncertainty. A former chairman of state-owned Tsinghua Unigroup received a suspended death sentence last year for embezzling state funds.

In some ways, China’s semiconductor industry is becoming more cloistered after decades of greater international partnership. Amid the tense US-China technological rivalry, important R&D projects are proceeding in secret. Outsiders hoping for a glimpse of a company’s inner workings would have to settle for the equivalent of that 1979 tour—a rare, tightly choreographed peek. But the country is far more capable of advancing technologically on its own than it’s ever been. After many years of honing its industrial policy, engaging in international exchanges and bolstering its technical competencies up and down the supply chain, China has a complete enough ecosystem that it may be able to continue innovating, even in isolation.
