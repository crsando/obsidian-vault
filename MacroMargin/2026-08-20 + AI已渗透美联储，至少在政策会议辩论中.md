### 中文译文

## AI已渗透美联储，至少在政策会议辩论中

人工智能的焦虑无处不在——从担心饭碗的工人到警告金融泡沫的投资者。如今，它开始主导美联储的讨论。

美联储最近一次政策会议的纪要显示，当政策制定者思考经济的几乎任何方面时，这项技术已变得多么突出。在专门讨论当前形势和经济前景的15段官员发言中，AI被提及不少于18次。

“关于AI的讨论不仅冗长，而且范围非常广。”货币政策分析公司（Monetary Policy Analytics）的经济学家Derek Tang表示。“AI现在从不同角度影响他们——他们对通胀的预测、对就业的预测、金融稳定。它似乎现在无处不在。”

早在ChatGPT于2022年首次向大众推出之前，AI的影响就一直是经济学家争论的话题。讨论往往集中在技术提高工人和公司生产率的潜力上——用相同甚至更少的资本和劳动力做更多的事。这可以转化为更高的增长，而不会带来更高的通胀负担。

美联储官员也在思考这种可能性，但他们最近的公开演讲以及7月会议的纪要显示，他们也担心AI可能给经济带来需要应对的短期冲击，尽管生产率回报的时间和程度仍高度不确定。

### **双重使命的影响**

美联储的任务是实现两个关键要素，它们共同创造健康的经济：物价稳定和最大就业。尽管人们对于AI可能导致大规模裁员忧心忡忡，但美联储官员对AI可能对通胀意味着什么越来越担忧。

近年来，一系列冲击推高了价格压力，包括关税和美国-伊朗战争导致的油价飙升。这些冲击重新点燃了通胀，而通胀在疫情后飙升后已有所降温。

如今，通胀率高于美联储 2% 的目标已超过五年，官员们开始怀疑 AI 投资是否会引发新一轮价格压力。这从 AI 建设推高芯片和软件价格，进而抬高智能手机等消费品价格中可见一斑。

“多位与会者评估认为，AI 建设对消费者价格的影响迄今仅限于部分类别，”会议纪要称，“然而，其他几位与会者认为，AI 投资已通过推高总需求对价格产生更广泛的影响，或估计这种情况可能很快就会发生。”

这场辩论触及了近几个月来困扰美联储官员的核心问题。一些人认为当前的通胀压力是暂时的。这将允许央行维持利率稳定，因为价格压力最终会自行消退。另一些人，包括上个月支持加息的 3 名持异议官员，则看到了更普遍通胀的证据。

AI 也给劳动力市场带来了相互矛盾的压力。一方面，它正在摧毁一些入门级白领工作，甚至包括计算机编程中更高级的职位。但数据中心建设也在某些领域造成了专业工人的短缺。这正是达拉斯联储主席洛里·洛根今年夏天早些时候在得克萨斯州西部谈到的问题，当时埃尔帕索周边数据中心建设正导致电工、水管工和建筑工人[短缺](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-05/fed-s-logan-finds-more-inflation-drivers-in-west-texas)。

“少数与会者评估认为，AI 相关发展迄今对就业的净影响似乎有限，一些工人被取代，另一些人则受益于 AI 建设创造的工作岗位，”会议纪要写道。

### **为时过早**

然而，许多政策制定者一致认为，现在判断 AI 能否实现其巨大经济潜力还为时过早。

与 1990 年代个人电脑和互联网迅速改变经济时一样，AI 似乎无处不在，但唯独没有出现在生产力数据中。然而，一些最大的乐观主义者包括特朗普政府的经济官员，以及于 5 月加入央行的美联储主席凯文·沃什。

“人工智能将是一股显著的反通胀力量，提高生产率并增强美国的竞争力，”他去年在《华尔街日报》的一篇专栏文章中写道。“年生产率增长每提高1个百分点，就能在一代人的时间内使生活水平翻一番。”

沃什在美联储的许多同事都表示希望这种繁荣能够实现。但他们也表现出了谨慎。

### **金融稳定**

“几位与会者表示，与人工智能相关的投资可能会在未来几年提高生产率和潜在产出的增长，”会议纪要称。“然而，这些与会者指出，关于潜在生产率提升的时间和幅度，仍然存在相当大的不确定性。”

几位官员随后警告称，美联储应为一个情景做好准备，即人工智能的承诺令人失望，“导致股票大幅重新定价，并对消费者支出产生负面影响”，会议纪要显示。

官员们警告称，市场定价的急剧转变也可能使[面临](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-13/wall-street-opens-spigots-for-ai-debt-and-stock-offerings)人工智能相关贷款的金融机构承压。

“几位与会者强调，人工智能领域的资本支出越来越多地通过借贷来融资，包括非银行投资者或地区银行提供的信贷，”会议纪要称。

此外，官员们还在会议上指出，人工智能在长期困扰美联储的领域——网络安全——内创造了新的风险水平。今年4月，美国财政部[紧急召集](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-10/anthropic-model-scare-sparks-urgent-bessent-powell-warning-to-bank-ceos)美国最大银行负责人开会，时任美联储主席杰罗姆·鲍威尔也出席了会议，讨论新人工智能工具带来的网络安全风险。

### English Original

## AI Has Infiltrated the Fed, at Least in Policy Meeting Debates

Artificial intelligence angst is everywhere — from workers worried about their jobs to investors warning of a financial bubble. Now it’s beginning to dominate discussions at the Federal Reserve.

Minutes from the US central bank’s most recent policy meeting show how prominent the technology has become when policymakers contemplate almost any aspect of the economy. In 15 paragraphs devoted to officials’ discussion of current conditions and the economic outlook, AI was referenced no fewer than 18 times.

“The discussion on AI was not just long but also very broad,” said Derek Tang, an economist at Monetary Policy Analytics. “AI is now affecting them from different angles — their forecast for inflation, their forecast for employment, financial stability. It seems to be in all the corners now.”

The impact of AI has been a topic of debate among economists since before ChatGPT first launched to the masses in 2022. The conversation has often centered on the technology’s potential to make workers and companies more productive — doing more with the same, or even less, capital and labor. That could translate to higher growth without the burden of higher inflation.

Fed officials have been contemplating this possibility, too, but their public speeches recently, and the minutes from the July meeting, show they’re also worried that AI could deliver a near-term shock for the economy to weather, even as the timing and extent of the productivity payoff remains highly uncertain.

### **Dual Mandate Impact**

The Fed is tasked with delivering two key components that together create a healthy economy: stable prices and maximum employment. For all the hand wringing over how AI might lead to mass layoffs, Fed officials are sounding increasingly concerned about what it could mean for inflation.

A series of shocks have driven up price pressures in recent years, including tariffs and the oil-price surge from the US-Iran war. Those reignited inflation that had been cooling after surging following the pandemic.

Now, with inflation above the Fed’s 2% target for more than five years, officials are wondering whether AI investment might be spurring another wave of price pressures. That’s evident in how the AI build-out is driving up prices for chips and software, which in turn is raising prices of consumer goods like smartphones.

“Several participants assessed that the effects of the AI build-out on consumer prices had so far been limited to select categories,” the minutes said. “However, several other participants viewed investment in AI as already having broader effects on prices by pushing up aggregate demand or assessed that it would likely do so relatively soon.”

The debate gets to the heart of what has been perplexing Fed officials in recent months. Some see the current inflation pressures as temporary. That would allow the central bank to hold interest rates steady because price pressures will eventually fade on their own. Others, including three officials who dissented in favor of a rate hike last month, see evidence of more pervasive inflation.

AI is also putting conflicting pressures on the labor market. On one hand it’s destroying some entry-level, white-collar jobs, and even more advanced positions in computer programming. But data center construction is also generating a shortage of specialized workers in some areas. That’s something Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan spoke about earlier this summer in west Texas, where the construction of data centers around El Paso is leading to a [shortage](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-05/fed-s-logan-finds-more-inflation-drivers-in-west-texas) of electricians, plumbers and construction workers.

“A few participants assessed that AI-related developments appeared to have had a limited net effect on employment so far, with some workers being displaced and others benefiting from jobs created by the AI build-out,” the minutes read.

### **Too soon to know**

Many policymakers agree, however, that it’s far too early to know whether AI will deliver on its great economic potential.

As in the 1990s, when the personal computer and the Internet were rapidly changing the economy, AI seems to be showing up everywhere but in the productivity data. Yet some of the biggest optimists include economic officials in the Trump administration, as well as Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, who joined the central bank in May.

“AI will be a significant disinflationary force, increasing productivity and bolstering American competitiveness,” he wrote in a Wall Street Journal op-ed last year. “A 1-percentage-point increase in annual productivity growth would double standards of living within a single generation.”

Many of Warsh’s colleagues at the Fed have expressed hope that such a boom materializes. But they’ve also shown caution.

### **Financial stability**

“Several participants suggested that AI-related investments would likely increase the growth of productivity and of potential output in the coming years,” the minutes said. “These participants remarked, however, that considerable uncertainty remained regarding both the timing and magnitude of potential productivity gains.”

Several officials then went on to warn the Fed should be prepared for a scenario in which AI’s promises disappoint, “leading to a significant repricing of stocks, with consequent negative effects on consumer spending,” according to the minutes.

A sharp shift in market pricing could also strain financial institutions that are [exposed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-13/wall-street-opens-spigots-for-ai-debt-and-stock-offerings) to AI-related lending, officials warned.

“A few participants highlighted the increased degree to which capital spending in the AI sector was being financed by borrowing, including credit provided by nonbank investors or regional banks,” the minutes said.

Not to be forgotten, officials also pointed out at the meeting that AI has created a new level of risk within an area that has long worried the Fed: cybersecurity. In April, the Treasury [urgently summoned](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-10/anthropic-model-scare-sparks-urgent-bessent-powell-warning-to-bank-ceos) the leaders of the largest US banks to a meeting also attended by then Fed Chair Jerome Powell to discuss risks to cyber security posed by new AI tools
