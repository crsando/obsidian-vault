## 消息人士称，美国在伊朗战争中已使用“几乎所有”远程精确导弹

纽约/华盛顿，8月4日（路透社）——据三位知情人士透露，美国陆军在与伊朗持续五个月的[战争](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/)中，已消耗了大量精确远程导弹库存，这引发了人们对美军未来冲突战备状态的担忧。

这些导弹主要是陆军的对地武器，即陆军战术导弹系统（ATACMS）和精确打击导弹（PrSM）。据其中两位消息人士称，美国已使用了“几乎所有”这些武器。

美军ATACMS和精确打击导弹（PrSM）即将耗尽的程度此前未见报道。

远程弹药——每枚成本超过100万美元——是美军武器库中的重要组成部分，能够从安全距离进行精确打击。美国提供的ATACMS在[乌克兰战争](https://www.reuters.com/world/ukraine-russia-war/)中发挥了关键作用，使乌克兰军队能够攻击俄罗斯境内目标。PrSM是更新、更先进的一代，将取代射程较短的ATACMS。

精确远程导弹的急剧减少意味着，如果美国总统唐纳德·特朗普重新对伊朗发动大规模攻击，他可能不得不更多依赖风险更高的有人驾驶轰炸任务。

消息人士拒绝透露美国每种弹药还剩多少。

特朗普于2月与以色列共同发动了对伊朗的战争，并预测冲突将持续很短时间。

但随着战争拖延，这三位知情人士担心导弹供应的减少可能限制美国威慑对手（包括俄罗斯和中国）的能力。

第四位知情人士表示，负责中东美军的中央司令部虽然几乎用尽了战前拥有的陆基导弹，但已能够从世界其他地方的美国军用物资中重新补充。

接受本文采访的消息人士要求匿名。

在被问及库存数据时，白宫发布了特朗普的声明，称美国“拥有的弹药比世界上任何人都多得多”，并且“远超我们的需求”。

“我们的国防公司此刻正在生产比以往任何时候都多的弹药，此外还在以创纪录的水平扩建工厂和设备，”特朗普说。

分析人士一致认为，包括炮弹和几种导弹在内的某些弹药正以创纪录水平生产，但也警告说，供应可能无法满足长期战争的需求。

制造ATACMS和PrSM以及反弹道导弹萨德系统的洛克希德·马丁公司，没有立即回应关于特朗普声明或供应水平的问题。制造战斧导弹和爱国者拦截弹这两种重要美国武器的雷神公司，也没有立即回应。

五角大楼首席发言人肖恩·帕内尔在回应置评请求时表示：“美国军队是世界上最强大的，拥有在总统选择的时间和地点执行任务所需的一切。我们已在各作战司令部执行了多次成功行动，同时确保美军拥有深厚的武器库，以保护我们的人民和利益。”

过去一周，这些供应数据在联邦政府内部流传，特朗普政府内部就美国还能继续打击伊朗多久而不将库存降至可能限制军方应对其他地区危机能力的水平进行了紧张讨论。

### 武器供应警告

其中一位消息人士称，ATACMS和PrSM库存的减少反映了特朗普政府决定避免使用更危险的方式攻击伊朗目标，例如使用有人驾驶飞机投弹。

由于这些武器使军方能够远距离攻击目标，分析人士称，它们在与拥有强大防空能力的对手（如中国）作战时很有价值。根据战略与国际研究中心（CSIS）3月份的一份报告，这些武器已被用于打击伊朗境内的目标。

根据该报告，PrSM的库存一开始就较低，因为它是一种相对较新的弹药，但美国军方已为2027年订购了大量这种导弹。美国陆军表示，ATACMS正在逐步淘汰，生产正转向更新的PrSM导弹。

两位消息人士称，数周以来，军方领导人一直警告总统，包括对弹道导弹有效的爱国者拦截弹在内的防御性武器库存正在减少。上周，多家媒体报道称，特朗普决定不对伊朗发动另一次大规模进攻，部分原因是其军事顾问对美国库存提出了警告。

一名美国官员否认了这些说法，称特朗普没有推进另一次攻击是因为来自海湾国家的压力。

中东冲突引发了关于特朗普在未经国会授权的情况下对伊朗发动敌对行动的权力的激烈辩论。目前还没有向国会提交宣战或授权使用武力的请求。

### 防御性武器库存也在减少

上周，CSIS发布的一份报告估计，2月至7月间，大约65%的爱国者拦截弹已被消耗，美国库存中的THAAD弹道导弹拦截弹数量比战争开始时至少减少了38%。爱国者和THAAD都是探测并摧毁来袭导弹的系统，是美国武器库中最有效的系统之一。

两位消息人士称，虽然路透社尚未看到供应数据，但这些数字与美国内部数据相符。

其中一位消息人士称，自战争开始以来，美国还消耗了其全球战斧巡航导弹库存的略少于一半。此类导弹通常从舰船发射。

路透社无法独立核实该数字。

战斧导弹是一种海军武器，从驱逐舰、巡洋舰和潜艇上发射，长期以来一直是海军在不危及飞行员的情况下打击重兵防御目标的主要手段。

雷神公司是 RTX 的一个部门 [RTX.N（在新标签页打开）](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/RTX.N)，已与五角大楼达成初步多年期协议，旨在提高战斧导弹产量，同时增加其他弹药的产量，因为华盛顿正急于重建库存。

## US has used 'virtually all' of its long-range precision missiles during Iran war, sources say

- US has used 'virtually all' PrSM and ATACM munitions, two sources say
- US also burned through a little less than half its global Tomahawk supply, one source says
- Some officials worry the low stockpiles will affect US readiness should another conflict break out
NEW YORK/WASHINGTON, Aug 4 (Reuters) - The U.S. Army has used up much of its stockpile of highly accurate long-range missiles during its five-month [war with Iran](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/), according to three people familiar with the data, raising concerns about the military's readiness for future conflicts.

The missiles are principally the Army's surface-to-surface weapons, known as Army Tactical Missile Systems (ATACMS) and Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM). The U.S. has used "virtually all" of these weapons, according to ‌two of the sources.

The degree to which the military is running out of ATACMS and Precision Strike Missiles has not been previously reported.

The long-range munitions – which cost more than $1 million each – are an important part of the military's arsenal, allowing accurate strikes from a safe distance. U.S.-supplied ATACMS have played a key role in the [war in Ukraine](https://www.reuters.com/world/ukraine-russia-war/), allowing Ukrainian forces to attack targets inside Russia. The PrSM is a newer, more advanced generation that will replace the ATACMS, which have a shorter range.

The dramatic rundown in precision, long-range missiles means U.S. President Donald Trump may have to rely more on riskier, piloted bombing missions if he relaunches large-scale attacks on Iran.

The sources declined to say how many of each munition the U.S. had left.

Trump launched the Iran war jointly with Israel in February, predicting that the conflict would last a short time.

But as the war drags on, the three people familiar with the matter expressed worry that the falling missile supplies could limit the U.S. ability to deter adversaries, including Russia and China.

A fourth person ⁠familiar with the matter said that while Central Command — which oversees U.S. forces in the Middle East — has nearly used up the land-based missiles it had before the war began, it has been able to reload from U.S. military supplies elsewhere in the world.

The sources interviewed for this story spoke on the condition of anonymity.

Asked for comment on the stockpile data, the White House issued a statement from Trump, saying the U.S. had “far more munitions than anyone in the world" and "far more than we need."

"Our defense companies are, at this moment, making more munitions than they have ever made before, in addition to expanding their plants and equipment at record levels," Trump said.

Analysts agree that certain munitions, including artillery shells and several types of missiles, are being produced at record levels but caution that supplies might fall short of what is needed for a prolonged war.

Lockheed Martin, which makes the ATACMS and PrSMs, along with the anti-ballistic missile THAAD system, did not immediately respond to questions about Trump's statements or about supply levels. Raytheon, which makes Tomahawk missiles and Patriot interceptors, two important U.S. weapons, also did not immediately respond.

Responding to a request for comment, chief Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell said: “America’s military is the most powerful in the world and has everything it needs to execute at the time and place of the President’s choosing. We have executed multiple successful operations across combatant commands while ensuring the U.S. military possesses a deep arsenal of capabilities to protect our people and our interests.”

The supply figures have circulated inside the federal government over the last week during tense conversations inside the Trump administration about how much longer the U.S. can continue ‌striking Iran without ⁠drawing down the stockpile to levels that would limit the military's ability to respond to crises elsewhere.

### WARNINGS OVER WEAPONS SUPPLIES

One of the sources said the drawdown of the ATACMS and PrSM stockpiles reflected a decision by the Trump administration to avoid riskier ways of attacking Iranian targets, such as by using piloted aircraft to drop bombs.

Because these weapons allow the military to attack targets from a distance, analysts say they would be valuable in a war against an adversary with strong air defenses, such as China. They have been used to strike targets inside Iran, according to a March report by the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS).

According to the report, PrSM stockpiles were low to start with, since it is a relatively new munition, but the U.S. military has ordered a large number of them for 2027. The Army has said that ATACMS are being phased out and that production is shifting ⁠to the newer PrSM missiles.

Military leaders have for weeks warned the president that stockpiles of defensive weapons — including Patriot interceptors, which are effective against ballistic missiles — were dwindling, said two of the sources. Last week, several media outlets reported Trump had decided not to launch another massive offensive inside Iran in part because his military advisers had warned about the U.S. stockpile.

A U.S. official disputed those accounts, saying Trump chose not to move forward with another attack because of pressure from Gulf states.

The Middle East conflict has sparked intense debate about Trump's authority to prosecute hostilities against Iran without congressional authorization. No ⁠request for a declaration of war or an authorization to use military force has been submitted to Congress.

### DEFENSIVE WEAPON STOCKS ARE ALSO DIMINISHED

Last week, CSIS published a report estimating that between February and July about 65% of Patriot interceptors had been expended and that the number of THAAD ballistic missile interceptors in U.S. stockpiles was at least 38% lower than at the start of the war. Patriots and THAADs are systems that detect and destroy incoming missiles and are among the most effective in the country's arsenal.

While Reuters has not seen the supply ⁠figures, those numbers match internal U.S. data, two of the sources said.

The U.S. also burned through a little less than half of its global supply of Tomahawk cruise missiles, which are generally launched from ships, since the start of the war, one of the sources said.

Reuters could not independently verify that number.

The Tomahawk is a Navy weapon, launched from destroyers, cruisers and submarines, and has long served as the sea service's principal means of striking heavily defended targets without risking pilots.

Raytheon, a unit of RTX [(RTX.N), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/RTX.N), has reached a tentative multi-year agreement with the Pentagon aimed at boosting Tomahawk production, alongside increases in other munitions, as Washington races to rebuild stockpiles.
