### 中文译文

## 中国AI芯片制造商将从北京的技术推动中获益

中国的AI芯片设计公司预期在本次财报季实现销售丰收，这得益于北京推动本土企业采用国产组件并降低对美国技术的依赖。

[寒武纪科技公司（Cambricon Technologies Corp.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688256%20CH%20Equity)公布上半年营收大幅增长，销售额同比增长一倍多，达到59.9亿元人民币（合8.87亿美元），根据其[最新](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-08-08/688256_20260808_TUNG.pdf)中期业绩。新锐的[上海天数智芯半导体有限公司（Shanghai Iluvatar CoreX Semiconductor Co.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9903%20HK%20Equity)预计同期销售额将增长两倍。总部位于北京的[摩尔线程科技有限公司（Moore Threads Technology Co.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688795%20CH%20Equity)上月表示，预计2026年前六个月的营收将[增长](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-07-17/688795_20260717_J7VN.pdf)多达149%。

中国企业在北京要求本地科技公司寻找[英伟达（Nvidia Corp.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity)尖端产品的替代品后，正加大订单。多年来，华盛顿一直限制中国获取先进芯片，但最近批准了英伟达H200的销售，这是美国有史以来获准在华销售的最强大人工智能处理器。然而，美国官员上月表示，自那以来实际运往中国的英伟达处理器数量“[微不足道](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/small-amount-of-nvidia-ai-chips-shipped-to-china-with-us-license)”。

私营的[华为技术有限公司（Huawei Technologies Co.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH)和[阿里巴巴集团控股有限公司（Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BABA%20US%20Equity)旗下的芯片部门平头哥（T-Head）也有望扩大其国内市场份额，而此时英伟达和美国芯片制造商[超威半导体公司（Advanced Micro Devices Inc.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AMD%20US%20Equity)实际上已被排除在市场之外。华为正大幅提高2026年最先进人工智能芯片的产量，目标是生产约60万颗其旗舰910C昇腾芯片，彭博新闻社去年[报道](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-29/huawei-to-double-output-of-top-ai-chip-as-nvidia-wavers-in-china)称。

因此，根据彭博行业研究6月对中国科技高管进行的调查，中国企业预计在未来12个月内将把AI加速器预算的46%用于购买本地制造的芯片，而目前这一比例为30%。大量受访者评估了寒武纪和[海光信息技术股份有限公司（Hygon Information Technology Co.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688041%20CH%20Equity)生产的AI加速器。

从长远来看，中国芯片制造商将受益于北京雄心勃勃的数据中心建设计划，该计划[旨在](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-09/china-prepares-295-billion-plan-to-fund-nationwide-ai-buildout)未来五年内投入2万亿元人民币（合2950亿美元）。这一庞大项目将至少80%的技术（如AI芯片）依赖包括华为在内的本地供应商。根据摩根士丹利的估计，这将推动中国AI芯片自给率到本十年末达到70%，高于2025年的42%。

英伟达掌门人黄仁勋一直[主张](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2025-05-22/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-s-techno-optimism-has-a-point)在中国使用其公司芯片，以确保美国在尖端技术上的领先地位。据晨星分析师Phelix Lee称，他的论点提醒北京注意这样一种前景：过度依赖外国技术，并可能在该国追求全球AI领导地位的过程中造成脆弱性。

“大多数人私下都会承认，能获得英伟达芯片始终是理想之选，但在无法获得的情况下，我看到的趋势是越来越多的中国芯片被采用，”李说。

中国制造的芯片在单芯片性能方面仍落后于英伟达[数年](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-25/huawei-touts-chipmaking-breakthrough-to-shorten-gap-with-tsmc)。这在一定程度上是美国主导的一项长达多年的运动的结果，该运动限制最先进的芯片制造设备流入中国，包括荷兰公司[阿斯麦（ASML Holding NV）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/ASML%20NA%20Equity)生产的顶级极紫外光刻系统，而行业领导者[台积电（Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2330%20TT%20Equity)正是用这类设备制造全球最强大的AI加速器。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b581cc90b1481e98409f4970031af08/3b581cc9-0b14-81df-a03f-cae23cc4e426-73875bac05900b141aa009d916027bda.png)

随着客户开始从使用芯片训练AI模型转向将其整合到业务中，中国芯片制造商凭借以有竞争力的价格生成token的能力赢得订单，摩根士丹利包括Charlie Chan在内的分析师在7月底的一份研究报告中表示。

“采购决策越来越多地由部署经济学驱动，而非绝对的峰值芯片性能，”Chan写道。

中国芯片制造商也认识到，他们可以用规模来弥补单芯片计算能力的不足。上个月在上海举行的中国顶级AI峰会上，从华为到摩尔线程以及[沐曦集成电路（上海）有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688802%20CH%20Equity)等中国芯片制造商展示了能够集成数万颗芯片的服务器机架。

“他们试图安抚的是，我们有足够的芯片，我们有基础设施将这些芯片连接起来，从而在数量上超过你，”晨星公司的Lee表示。“这也表明，只要给予足够时间，中国最终将把差距缩小到比今天小得多的水平。”

## Chinese AI Chipmakers Set to Gain From Beijing’s Tech Push

China’s AI chip designers are expecting bumper sales this earnings season off the back of Beijing’s push to get local firms to use homegrown components and reduce their reliance on American tech.

[Cambricon Technologies Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688256%20CH%20Equity) reported a sharp increase in revenue during the first half of the year, with sales more than doubling from a year ago to 5.99 billion yuan ($887 million), according to its [latest](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-08-08/688256_20260808_TUNG.pdf) interim results. Up-and-coming [Shanghai Iluvatar CoreX Semiconductor Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9903%20HK%20Equity) expected to see sales triple over the same period. Beijing-based [Moore Threads Technology Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688795%20CH%20Equity) said last month it expected revenue for the first six months of 2026 to [grow](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-07-17/688795_20260717_J7VN.pdf) as much as 149%.

Chinese firms are ramping up orders after Beijing ordered local tech firms to find alternatives to [Nvidia Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity)’s cutting-edge products. Washington has for years restricted China’s access to advanced chips, but it recently cleared sales of Nvidia’s H200, the most powerful artificial intelligence processors ever allowed by the US to be sold in the country. However, US officials said last month that the actual number of Nvidia processors shipped to China since then has been “[trivial](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/small-amount-of-nvidia-ai-chips-shipped-to-china-with-us-license).”

Privately-held [Huawei Technologies Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH) and [Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BABA%20US%20Equity)’s chip unit T-Head are also expected to boost their domestic market shares at a time when Nvidia and US chipmaker [Advanced Micro Devices Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AMD%20US%20Equity) are virtually locked out of the market. Huawei is sharply ramping up production of its most advanced artificial intelligence chips in 2026, aiming to produce about 600,000 of its marquee 910C Ascend chips, Bloomberg News [reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-29/huawei-to-double-output-of-top-ai-chip-as-nvidia-wavers-in-china) last year.

As a result, Chinese companies expect to spend 46% of their AI accelerator budget in the next 12 months to acquire locally-made chips, compared to 30% now, according to a survey of Chinese technology executives by Bloomberg Intelligence in June. AI accelerators produced by Cambricon and [Hygon Information Technology Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688041%20CH%20Equity) were being evaluated by a large pool of respondents to the survey.

In the longer term, Chinese chipmakers are set to benefit from Beijing’s ambitious data center buildout that [aims](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-09/china-prepares-295-billion-plan-to-fund-nationwide-ai-buildout) to spend 2 trillion yuan ($295 billion) over the next five years. The massive project will rely on local suppliers including Huawei for at least 80% of technology such as AI chips. That would push China’s self-sufficiency level in AI chips to 70% at the end of this decade, up from 42% in 2025, according to estimates from Morgan Stanley.

Nvidia boss Jensen Huang has consistently [advocated](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2025-05-22/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-s-techno-optimism-has-a-point) for the use of his firm’s chips in China to ensure America’s lead in cutting-edge technology. His arguments alerted Beijing to the prospect of over-relying on foreign technology and creating vulnerabilities in the nation’s quest for global AI leadership, according to Morningstar analyst Phelix Lee.

“Most people would privately agree that having access to Nvidia would always be ideal, but in the absence of that, the trend I’m seeing is more Chinese chips being used,” Lee said.

China-made chips, when it comes to single-unit performance, are still [years behind](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-25/huawei-touts-chipmaking-breakthrough-to-shorten-gap-with-tsmc) those built by Nvidia. It’s partly the result of a yearslong US-led campaign that restricts the most advanced chipmaking machines including the state-of-the-art extreme ultraviolet lithography systems made by Dutch company [ASML Holding NV](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/ASML%20NA%20Equity) from flowing to China, the kind of gear that industry leader [Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2330%20TT%20Equity) uses to make the world’s most powerful AI accelerators.

As customers start to shift from using chips for training AI models to integrating them into their businesses, Chinese chipmakers are winning orders from their ability to generate tokens at competitive pricing, Morgan Stanley analysts including Charlie Chan said in a research note at the end of July.

“Purchasing decisions are increasingly driven by deployment economics rather than absolute peak silicon performance,” Chan wrote.

Chinese chipmakers also have concluded that they can compensate for a lack of computing power in a single chip with scale. At China’s premier AI summit held in Shanghai last month, Chinese chipmakers from Huawei to Moore Threads and [MetaX Integrated Circuits Shanghai Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688802%20CH%20Equity) showcased their server racks capable of integrating tens of thousands chips.

“What they are trying to reassure is that we have enough chips and we have the infrastructure to link all these chips together so we can outnumber you,” Morningstar’s Lee said. “It also demonstrates how China, given enough time, will eventually narrow the gap to much narrower levels than today.”
