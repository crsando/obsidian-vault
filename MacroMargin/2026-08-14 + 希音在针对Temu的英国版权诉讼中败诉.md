### 中文译文

## 希音在针对Temu的英国版权诉讼中败诉

快时尚集团希音（Shein）在一起针对其主要竞争对手Temu的英国版权诉讼中败诉，该案有可能重塑在线平台对第三方商家在其网站上销售商品的法律责任。

周四在伦敦高等法院作出的这一判决驳回了希音的指控，即Temu允许其平台上的商家使用从希音网站窃取的照片为数千件服装商品做广告，构成了“工业规模”的版权侵犯。

希音还辩称，Temu并非被动的中介，而是积极助长了商家滥用其受版权保护的图片。

培根法官（Mrs Justice Bacon）在裁决中表示，Temu“并未授权其网站用户侵犯版权”，也不“知晓”或没有“理由相信”案件核心的服装照片“侵犯了希音的版权”。

Temu还赢得了对希音的反诉赔偿请求，称希音错误地发出了通知，迫使Temu从其网站删除商品列表——希音对这些主张提出了异议。任何损害赔偿金的数额将在后续审判中确定。

该案是这两家创立于中国的零售商之间国际法律纠纷的最新篇章，也是对在线平台在商家如何使用其网站方面的法律责任的一次关键考验。

该案触及了一个更广泛辩论的核心，即平台对其用户发布内容的责任——这一辩论也在美国法院涉及科技集团的案件中上演。Temu在辩护中辩称，它是一个中立的平台，第三方商家应对其网站上的发布内容负责。

希音凭借开创了一种将中国工厂的服装直接运送到西方消费者的商业模式，已成为全球零售巨头。在疫情期间经历爆炸式增长后，中国集团拼多多控股（PDD Holdings）推出了Temu，后者采用类似策略，销售更广泛的无品牌商品。

在周四的裁决之后，Shein表示不同意法院对“一个复杂且不断演变的法律领域所作的狭隘解释”。Temu拒绝置评。

案件被缩减至少数几个样本，其中包括一家供应商销售的“草莓睡裙”，该商品因销售不佳从Shein下架，随后以相同照片发布在Temu上。

法官写道，Shein在该案中关于该连衣裙的版权归属立场“发生了重大变化”，且“最终形成了一套极其复杂的法律陈述”。

### English Original

## Shein loses UK copyright lawsuit against Temu

Fast-fashion group Shein has lost a UK copyright lawsuit against its arch rival Temu, in a case that had the potential to reshape online platforms’ legal responsibility over third-party merchants selling goods on their websites.

The judgment, handed down in London’s High Court on Thursday, rejected Shein’s claim that Temu perpetrated “industrial-scale” copyright violations by allowing merchants on its platform to advertise thousands of clothing items using photographs lifted from Shein’s website.

It also argued that Temu was not a passive intermediary but instead actively enabled vendors to misuse its copyrighted images.

In her ruling, Mrs Justice Bacon said that Temu “did not authorise copyright infringement by users of its website”, nor did it “know” or have “reason to believe” that the photographs of clothing at the heart of the case “infringed Shein’s copyright”.

Temu also won a counterclaim for damages against Shein over what it argued were wrongfully issued notices that forced it to remove product listings from Temu’s website — claims that Shein had disputed. The size of any damages award will be determined at a later trial.

The case, which is the latest episode in an international legal tussle between the two China-founded retailers, was a key test of online platforms’ legal responsibility over how vendors use their websites.

It hit at the centre of a broader debate around platforms’ liability for content posted by their users — one that has also played out in US courts in cases involving tech groups. In its defence, Temu argued that it operates as a neutral platform and that third-party merchants were responsible for posts on its website.

Shein has become a global retail powerhouse by pioneering a business model based on delivering clothes from Chinese factories directly to western consumers. After booming growth during the pandemic, Chinese group PDD Holdings launched Temu, which adopted a similar strategy to sell a broader range of unbranded goods.

Following Thursday’s ruling, Shein said it disagreed with the court’s “narrow interpretation of a complex and evolving area of law”. Temu declined to comment.

The case was narrowed down to a handful of samples, including a supplier selling a “Strawberry Nightdress” that was delisted from Shein due to poor sales and later posted on Temu with the same photos.

The judge wrote that Shein’s position on copyright ownership in relation to the dress had “changed significantly” during the case and the “end-result was a set of submissions of quite extraordinary complexity”.
