### 中文译文

## 教授涉嫌参与中国人才计划遭美国联邦资助禁令

根据彭博新闻社查阅的此前未公开文件，一位教授在两年多时间里负责管理美国联邦资助的计算机科学研究项目，却未能披露其同时参与了中国政府的人才计划。

这位教授名叫李涛，2015年至2017年在美国国家科学基金会（NSF）担任项目官员，职责包括监督硬件、软件和算法等领域的科研拨款审批。根据NSF监察长办公室的一份报告，在此期间，李涛参与了两项中国人才计划。美国联邦调查局（FBI）曾警告，此类计划可能诱使参与者窃取或盗用外国知识或技术。

此外，根据另一批详细说明美国国家安全关切的文件，李涛还参与了向至少其他七名科学家发放美国研究资助，这些科学家同样被指控未披露其参与一项名为“ Dragon Star ”的中国人才计划。

据一位曾参与会议的前中央情报局分析师称，2019年，联邦调查局首次向NSF领导层通报了对李涛涉嫌参与“ Dragon Star ”计划的关切。监察长办公室根据这一线索展开调查，但从FBI通报到调查结束、NSF依据调查结果采取行动，历时约七年。报告称，司法部于2024年拒绝了对李涛的刑事起诉移交。

根据彭博新闻社查阅的通讯记录，NSF于5月禁止李涛在六年内获得联邦资助。该机构并未公开宣布这一禁令，但政府网站sam.gov上有李涛被取消资格的记录。

当NSF作出决定时，李涛已开始了新的工作：在中国中部一所军事附属大学，他目前在那里领导一个人工智能研究中心。

通过电子邮件联系到李涛时，他表示，给予参与人才计划的其他科学家的资助是基于“实绩”授予的，并且“得到了整个评审小组的推荐和同意”。

李涛在给彭博新闻社的电子邮件中表示：“在我担任NSF项目主任期间，我与同事们一起认真履行了我的职责——包括提案审查、资助项目管理以及年度报告评估——从未收到过任何投诉或反对意见。”

有关美国对李的调查以及随后禁止其获得联邦资助的决定，以及他目前在中国的状况，此前从未被报道过。

彭博社查阅了三十多份可追溯至十年前的文件，这些文件是美国国家科学基金会（NSF）监察长在调查李时汇编的。这些文件由众议院中国问题特别委员会提供给彭博社，该委员会正在对政府禁止科学家一事进行更广泛的调查。彭博社还查阅了其他文件，其中阐述了政府对李和中国人才计划的国家安全担忧。

“此案是迄今披露的最严重的研究安全失误之一，”密歇根州共和党人、众议院中国问题委员会主席约翰·穆勒纳尔（John Moolenaar）表示。“这严峻地提醒我们，对手将利用任何存在的漏洞，包括在负责保护联邦研究的机构内部。”

![John Moolenaar Photographer: Tom Williams/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bb81cc90b1481bf9119ce77dbd46d57/3bb81cc9-0b14-81e4-b434-fd55ea19a098-d125633be21151a27548b841de51cee2.png)

*约翰·穆勒纳尔 摄影师：汤姆·威廉姆斯/盖蒂图片社*

中国驻华盛顿大使馆发言人刘畅在一份声明中表示，尽管他“不了解”有关李的“具体情况”，但“中国一贯反对将学术研究和科技交流政治化、武器化。”

美国司法部没有回应置评请求。监察长办公室的一位发言人称，该办公室不对调查发表评论。美国国家科学基金会拒绝置评。

美国国家科学基金会将李列入黑名单之前，特朗普首届政府曾采取一项有争议的行动，旨在应对一些美国官员所称的中国窃取或以其他方式侵占美国科学进步成果的协同努力。‘中国行动计划’在几起针对学者和研究人员的知名案件失败后适得其反，批评者称该行动具有歧视性，并对科学界造成寒蝉效应。该计划在2022年经过审查后被废除。

此前针对学者或研究人员的一些法庭案件指控他们接受美国纳税人的研究资金，同时涉嫌未披露其参与中国人才计划的情况。根据监察长的报告，李也未披露其参与人才计划的情况。但对他指控的不同寻常之处在于，他同时担任一个职位，该职位帮助决定哪些科学家获得美国研究资金。

据其前雇主佛罗里达大学的存档教授页面显示，李在来美国读研究生之前，于1993年在中国西北工业大学获得计算机工程学士学位。该校属于中国的“国防七子”，[与中国人民解放军联系紧密的精英大学](https://www.aspi.org.au/report/china-defence-universities-tracker/)。2001年，出于国家安全考虑，美国商务部对该校实施了技术出口限制。

彼时，李已移居美国，并于2004年在得克萨斯大学奥斯汀分校获得电气工程博士学位，这一信息来自存档的传记和一位大学发言人。

随后，他加入了位于盖恩斯维尔的佛罗里达大学电气与计算机工程系，并最终成为终身教授，据美国国家科学基金会（NSF）的文件和一位大学发言人介绍。

据他的传记，从2006年到2009年，李获得了微软公司和国际商业机器公司（IBM）的多个奖项，此外还获得美国国家科学基金会颁发的一项著名奖项，该奖项面向“有潜力在研究和教育领域成为学术榜样的人”。微软发言人证实了李的获奖情况，而IBM代表则表示，公司曾颁发此类奖项，但由于时间久远，无法核实李是否为获奖者。

根据美国国家科学基金会监察长提供的数据，自2010年以来，李共获得了该机构490万美元的研究经费，涉及15笔资助。

2015年8月，李暂时离开佛罗里达大学，成为美国国家科学基金会项目主任，负责管理计算机科学研究美国资助的授予流程。根据NSF网站，项目官员负责筛选提案、确定同行评审人、分析其意见，并就NSF是否应资助这些项目向部门主管提出建议。

根据提出国家安全关切的文件，他参与了NSF约8000万美元资助的授予过程。

据监察长报告，当时美国国家科学基金会不知道的是，李已经在参与中国的“龙星计划”。报告称，李在2012年至2016年间参与了龙星计划。

多年后，FBI反间谍人员评估认为，“龙星”（Dragon Star）是一个专门针对NSF研究设立的中国人才计划。这一结论出自彭博社看到的、描述美国对这些项目担忧的文件。

特别是，“龙星”计划“展示了人才招募如何从美国联邦科研资助体系内部扩大北京的技术转移机制”。[据此](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2020-10/Overseas_Chinese_Students_and_Scholars_in_Chinas_Drive_for_Innovation.pdf)美中经济与安全审查委员会（US-China Economic and Security Review Commission）2020年10月的一份报告，该委员会是由国会设立的两党机构。

“从表面上看，这无疑是中国的技术转移策略对他们有利的典型案例，”乔治城大学安全与新兴技术中心执行主任海伦·托纳（Helen Toner）表示。

应彭博社请求，托纳审阅了李（Li）研究论文的主题，称其研究范围“相当广泛”，涵盖在线人工智能应用，以及可能在中美AI竞争中更具战略意义的领域，包括数据中心效率、硬件架构和量子机器学习等论文。

李对美国政府将“龙星”定性为人才计划表示异议，在另一封电子邮件中称其为“暑期学术教学项目”。

根据监察长报告，李在2017年加入了另一项中国政府人才计划——“长江学者奖励计划”（Changjiang Scholars Program），但未告知NSF。报告称，他于当年年底离开NSF，恢复在佛罗里达大学的学术职务。

李表示，NSF曾邀请他再任一届，但出于家庭原因他婉言谢绝。他还通过电子邮件向彭博社发送了一份由NSF官员签署的证书，认可他在2017年12月之前对该机构的贡献。

次年，第一届特朗普政府启动了“中国行动计划”，旨在起诉与中国相关的国家安全威胁，包括黑客入侵、窃取商业机密以及暗中参与中国人才招聘项目的美国科学家。

据杰夫·斯托夫（Jeff Stoff）称，2018年底，联邦调查局反间谍特工仔细审查美国大学里的中国活动时，发现了“龙星”计划以及李在其中扮演的角色。斯托夫曾在美国中央情报局任职，为这项调查提供研究支持。

调查人员随后发现，在李任职期间获得资金的人中，有一位是“龙星”计划的创始者。据彭博社查阅的文件显示，这位科学家曾在十多年前担任美国国家科学基金会（NSF）的项目主任，并且与李一样，在向“龙星”计划参与者拨款方面发挥了作用。

据斯托夫称，到2019年初，联邦调查局特工和斯托夫向NSF领导层通报了他们的发现。根据该机构监察长办公室的报告，此后该办公室开始了调查，但报告未指明具体开始日期。

他说，“龙星”计划相关事件的披露促使斯托夫和其他机构的专家决定创建一个由NSF监察长办公室牵头的外国影响力工作组。

斯托夫说：“我们只研究了涉及NSF一个下属部门的风险。我们过去不知道，现在仍然不知道，其他资助研发的机构可能存在的这类活动的规模与范围。”斯托夫目前运营着总部位于弗吉尼亚州的非营利组织“研究安全与诚信中心”。

监察长办公室的一位发言人表示，该工作组“是因涉及外国影响美国研究事业案件数量不断增加而设立的”。

联邦调查局（FBI）拒绝置评。

与此同时，李（Li）正在处理其他问题。

根据监察长报告，在佛罗里达大学，一名博士生指控李强迫他在一篇由美国国家科学基金会（NSF）资助的研究论文中伪造数据，该论文是他们为一次计算机科学会议合著的。据警方报告，这一指控包含在该学生Huixiang Chen（陈）的遗书中，他于2019年6月被发现死于校园实验室。

李否认有不当行为，并在陈去世几天后亲自在会议上展示了该论文。

据发言人称，佛罗里达大学于2019年6月立即开始审查。两个学术机构——美国计算机协会（ACM）和电气与电子工程师协会（IEEE）——组成的联合委员会也对相关指控展开调查，并最终撤回了该论文。据彭博社查阅的一封信件，ACM禁止李在旗下出版物发表文章15年，而IEEE一位发言人称，该协会已“永久开除”其会员资格。

根据美国国家科学基金会（NSF）监察长报告，2021年2月15日，佛罗里达大学在继续调查期间将李停职。报告称，两个月后，该大学与NSF共同终止了李的三项NSF资助。报告还称，次日，李离开美国前往中国，“而不是面对”该大学调查的结果。

在彭博社查阅的另一份2021年10月报告中，佛罗里达大学调查人员表示，他们认定李存在研究不端行为，包括“明知故犯”地伪造论文结果。对于有关他在佛罗里达大学任职期间的指控，李未回应提问。

李表示，他离开佛罗里达后立即返回中国的“首要原因”是照顾生病的父亲。

佛罗里达大学和美国计算机协会的调查结果被纳入美国国家科学基金会监察长建议禁止资助的报告中。该报告还指控李在美国国家科学基金会工作期间，至少16次未申报前往中国，并在中国高校和计算机论坛上未经授权讲课。

“总而言之，你的行为极其恶劣，没有任何减轻情节。”彭博社查看了美国国家科学基金会写给李的一封信，信中这样写道。

当美国国家科学基金会决定禁止李获得联邦资助时，中国记录和研究发表数据显示，他已成为湖南科技大学的一名人工智能方向教授。该校未回应置评请求。

这所大学由湖南省和国家国防科技工业局共建，后者是一个负责军工科研与开发的文职机构。

李目前担任该校元宇宙创新研究院院长，该研究院是一个于2022年成立的人工智能研究中心。该研究院号称拥有“由长江学者、海外杰出青年科学家、IEEE会士和海外归国博士组成的高水平教学科研团队”，并宣传多个项目以持续引进全球人才。

李未回应关于其在湖南工作的问题。

2024年7月17日，该机构宣布，李（Li）曾在一个由中国共产党组织召集的活动上发表演讲，提议将湖南省省会长沙打造为人工智能中心。

公告称，在由中国人民政治协商会议长沙市委员会主办的活动中，李“特别强调了加快人工智能体与人形机器人实验室建设（包括大语言模型驱动的研究）的紧迫性”。

据彭博社对公开信息的搜索，李回国后还发表了至少11篇学术论文，其中一篇附有他的个人简介。

### English Original

## Professor’s Alleged Role in China Programs Leads to Ban From US Funding

A professor managed US funding of computer science research for more than two years while failing to disclose that he was simultaneously involved in Chinese government programs, according to previously unreported documents reviewed by Bloomberg News.

The professor, Tao Li, was a program officer at the National Science Foundation from 2015 to 2017, a job that included overseeing the awarding of grants for research in areas such as hardware, software and algorithms. During that time, Li participated in two Chinese talent plans, according to a report by the NSF’s Office of Inspector General. The FBI has warned that such plans can incentivize members to steal or misappropriate foreign knowledge or technology.

In addition, Li was involved in awarding US research grants to at least seven other scientists who also allegedly failed to disclose their participation in one of the Chinese talent plans, called Dragon Star, according to a separate set of documents detailing US national security concerns with Li and such plans.

The FBI first flagged its concerns about Li’s suspected involvement in Dragon Star to NSF leadership in 2019, according to a former CIA analyst who said he participated in the meeting. The inspector general’s office began an investigation based on the tip, but it would be about seven years from the time of the FBI briefing before the probe concluded and the NSF took action based on its findings. The Justice Department declined a referral for a criminal prosecution in 2024, according to the report.

The NSF in May banned Li from federal funding for six years, according to communications reviewed by Bloomberg. The agency didn’t announce the ban, though there is an entry on the government website sam.gov for Li’s debarment.

By the time the NSF made its decision, Li had already embarked on a new pursuit: at a military-affiliated university in central China, where he now runs an artificial intelligence research center.

Reached by email, Li said the grants given to the other scientists who participated in the talent plan were awarded “based on the merits,” and were “recommended and concurred by the entire panel” involved in the process.

“During my tenure as an NSF Program Director, I diligently fulfilled my responsibilities—including proposal reviews, funding award management, and annual report evaluations—alongside my colleagues, never once facing any complaints or objections,” Li said in an email to Bloomberg.

The details surrounding the US’s investigation into Li and subsequent decision to ban him from federal funding, as well as his current status in China, haven’t been previously reported.

Bloomberg reviewed more than 30 documents dating back a decade that the NSF inspector general had compiled for its investigation into Li. The documents were provided to Bloomberg by the House Select Committee on China, which is doing a broader investigation into government bans on scientists. Bloomberg also reviewed other documents laying out the government’s national security concerns about Li and Chinese talent programs.

“This case represents one of the most egregious research security failures uncovered to date,” said House China committee Chairman John Moolenaar, a Michigan Republican. “It is a stark reminder that our adversaries will exploit vulnerabilities wherever they exist, including within the institutions responsible for safeguarding federal research.”

*John Moolenaar Photographer: Tom Williams/Getty Images*

Liu Chang, spokesperson for the Chinese Embassy in Washington, said in a statement that while he is “not aware of the specifics” regarding Li, “China consistently opposes the politicization and weaponization of academic research and scientific and technological exchanges.”

The US Justice Department didn’t respond to requests for comment. A spokesperson for the inspector general’s office said it doesn’t comment on investigations. The NSF declined to comment.

The NSF’s debarring of Li follows a controversial effort by the first Trump administration to tackle what some US officials have said is a concerted effort by China to steal or otherwise misappropriate American scientific advances. The “China Initiative” backfired after several high-profile cases against academics and researchers collapsed and critics claimed the effort was discriminatory and scientifically chilling. The initiative was scrapped in 2022 following a review.

Some of the prior court cases brought against academics or researchers accused them of accepting US taxpayer money for research, while allegedly failing to disclose their involvement in Chinese talent plans. Li also failed to disclose his involvement in talent plans, according to the inspector general’s report. But what makes the allegations against him unusual is that he simultaneously held a position in which he helped to decide which scientists got US research money.

Before he came to the US for graduate school, Li earned his undergraduate degree in computer engineering in 1993 from China’s Northwestern Polytechnical University, according to an archived professor page from his former employer, the University of Florida. It’s among China’s “Seven Sons of National Defense,” [elite universities with deep ties](https://www.aspi.org.au/report/china-defence-universities-tracker/) to the People’s Liberation Army. In 2001, the US Commerce Department imposed restrictions on exporting technology to the school due to national security concerns.

By then, Li had already moved to the US, where he obtained a doctorate in electrical engineering from the University of Texas at Austin in 2004, according to the archived biography and a university spokesperson.

He then joined the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Florida in Gainesville, where he went on to become a tenured professor, according to NSF documents and a university spokesperson.

From 2006 to 2009, Li won a handful of awards from Microsoft Corp. and International Business Machines Corp., according to his biography, in addition to a prestigious prize from the NSF for junior researchers “who have the potential to serve as academic role models in research and education.” A Microsoft spokesperson confirmed Li’s awards, while an IBM representative said the company gave such prizes but wasn’t able to verify if Li was a recipient given the time that’s passed.

Li has won a total of $4.9 million in research funding from the NSF since 2010, according to data from the agency’s inspector general, citing 15 awards.

In August 2015, Li took a temporary leave from the University of Florida to become an NSF program director, where he managed the process for awarding US funding for computer science research. Program officers screen proposals, identify peer reviewers, analyze their input and make recommendations to the division director about whether NSF should fund them, according to NSF’s website.

He was involved in the process of awarding about $80 million in grants at NSF, according to the documents flagging national security concerns.

What the NSF didn’t know at the time was that Li was already participating in China’s Dragon Star Plan, according to the inspector general’s report. Li was involved in Dragon Star from 2012 to 2016, the report says.

Years later, FBI counterintelligence agents assessed that Dragon Star was a Chinese talent plan specifically created to target NSF research, according to a document viewed by Bloomberg describing US concerns with such programs.

The Dragon Star program in particular “showcases how talent recruits can expand Beijing’s technology transfer apparatus from within the US federal research funding system,” [according to](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2020-10/Overseas_Chinese_Students_and_Scholars_in_Chinas_Drive_for_Innovation.pdf) an October 2020 report by the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission, which is a bipartisan body created by Congress.

“On its face, this certainly looks like a textbook example of China's technology transfer playbook working to their favor,” said Helen Toner, executive director at Georgetown University’s Center for Security and Emerging Technology.

Toner reviewed the subjects of Li’s research papers at Bloomberg’s request and said his pursuits appeared “quite wide-ranging,” including topics such as AI use online as well as areas that could be more strategic in the US-China AI competition. Those include papers on data center efficiency, hardware architectures and quantum machine learning.

Li disputed the US government's characterization of Dragon Star as a talent plan, saying in another email that it is “a summer academic teaching program.”

Li joined a second Chinese government talent plan called the Changjiang Scholars Program in 2017 without telling NSF, according to the inspector general’s report. He left his role at NSF at the end of that year and resumed his academic duties at the University of Florida, the report says.

Li said that NSF offered him the opportunity to serve another term but that he declined due to family reasons. He also emailed Bloomberg a certificate signed by an NSF official recognizing his contributions to the agency through December 2017.

*National Science Foundation (NSF) certificate recognizing Tao Li. Source: Tao Li/NSF*

The following year, the first Trump administration launched the “China Initiative,” an effort intended to prosecute China-related national security threats, including hacking, trade-secret theft and US-based scientists secretly involved in Chinese talent recruitment programs.

In late 2018, as FBI counterintelligence agents pored over Chinese activities at US universities, they discovered the Dragon Star program and Li’s involvement in it, according to Jeff Stoff, a China analyst who provided research support to the investigation while he was at the CIA.

Investigators then found that one of the people awarded funds during Li's tenure was the Dragon Star program’s original founder. That scientist had previously served as an NSF program director more than a dozen years before and, like Li, had played a role in awarding money to fellow Dragon Star participants, according to documents reviewed by Bloomberg.

By early 2019, FBI agents and Stoff briefed NSF leadership on their findings, according to Stoff. The agency’s inspector general’s office began an investigation afterward, according to its report, though it doesn’t specify a start date.

The Dragon Star revelations contributed to a decision by Stoff and experts at other agencies to create a foreign influence working group led by the NSF inspector general’s office, he said.

“We had only looked at threats involving just one subdivision of NSF. We did not know, and still don’t know, the scale and scope of this type of activity that may be taking place at other agencies funding R&D,” said Stoff, who now runs the Center for Research Security & Integrity, a Virginia-based nonprofit.

*FBI headquarters in Washington. Photographer: Graeme Sloan/Bloomberg*

A spokesperson for the inspector general’s office said the working group “was founded in response to the rising number of cases involving foreign influence affecting the US research enterprise.”

The FBI declined to comment.

Meanwhile, Li was dealing with other issues.

At the University of Florida, a doctoral student accused Li of forcing him to falsify data in an NSF-funded research paper they co-authored for a computer science conference, according to the inspector general’s report. The allegation was contained in a suicide note written by the student, Huixiang Chen, who was found dead in a campus lab in June 2019, according to police reports.

Li denied wrongdoing and presented the paper himself at the conference just days after Chen’s death.

*Huixiang Chen, a doctoral student at the University of Florida, accused Li of forcing him to falsify data in a research paper they co-authored. Li denied wrongdoing. Photographer: Brad McClenny/Reuters*

The University of Florida immediately began a review in June 2019, according to a spokesperson. A joint committee of two academic bodies, the Association for Computing Machinery and the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers, also opened investigations into the allegations and eventually retracted the paper. ACM banned Li from publishing in their venues for 15 years, according to a letter reviewed by Bloomberg, and IEEE “permanently expelled” him from its membership, a spokesperson said.

On February 15, 2021, the University of Florida placed Li on administrative leave while it continued to investigate, according to the NSF inspector general’s report. Two months later, the university and NSF mutually terminated three of Li’s NSF awards, the report said. Li left the US for China the next day “rather than face the outcome” of the university’s investigation, according to the report.

In a separate October 2021 report reviewed by Bloomberg, University of Florida investigators said they concluded Li had engaged in research misconduct, including by “knowingly and intentionally” fabricating results in the paper. Li didn’t respond to questions about the allegations concerning his tenure at the University of Florida.

Li said “the primary reason” he returned to China immediately after leaving Florida was to care for his ailing father.

The findings of the University of Florida, and ACM, are included in the NSF inspector general’s report recommending debarment. The report also alleges that Li, while working at the NSF, took at least 16 undisclosed trips to China where he provided unauthorized lectures at universities and computer forums.

*The University of Florida campus entrance in 2017. Photographer: Jeff Greenberg/Getty Images*

“In total, your actions were egregious. There are no mitigating factors,” reads a letter from the NSF addressed to Li that was reviewed by Bloomberg.

By the time the NSF moved to ban Li from federal funding, he had become an AI-focused professor at the Hunan University of Science and Technology, Chinese records and research publication data show. The university didn’t respond to requests for comment.

The university is co-supervised by Hunan Province and China’s State Administration of Science, Technology and Industry for National Defense, a civilian agency that oversees military-industrial research and development.

Li is currently dean of the university’s Metaverse Innovation Research and Development Institute, an AI research center launched in 2022. The institute boasts a “high-level teaching and research team led by Changjiang Scholars, overseas outstanding young scientists, IEEE Fellows, and overseas returned PhDs” and advertises multiple programs to continue recruiting global talent.

Li didn’t respond to questions about his work in Hunan.

On July 17, 2024, the institute announced Li had given a speech at an event convened by a Chinese Communist Party group in which he proposed to transform Changsha, the capital of Hunan Province, into an AI hub.

In his remarks at the event hosted by the Changsha Municipal Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference, Li “specifically emphasized the urgency of accelerating the construction of an artificial intelligence agent and humanoid robot laboratory including large language model-driven research,” the announcement says.

Li has also published at least 11 academic papers since returning to China, including one that features a brief biography of him, according to a Bloomberg search of publicly available information.

*Tao Li Bio on ScienceDirect in 2026. Source: ScienceDirect*

The biography notes his PhD from the University of Texas. But it makes no mention of his roles at the University of Florida or the National Science Foundation.
