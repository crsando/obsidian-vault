### 中文译文

## 美国威胁加大对伊朗经济施压后，霍尔木兹海峡交通进一步放缓

伦敦/巴拿马城，8月14日（路透社）——周五，在又有两艘船只在该海峡遇袭、且美国表示可无限期维持[对伊朗的海上封锁](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-can-keep-naval-blockade-iranian-ports-indefinitely-pentagon-chief-says-2026-08-13/)之后，霍尔木兹海峡的过境似乎几乎陷入停顿。

一位伊朗高级消息人士周三表示，在6月协议基础上进行谈判以结束战争方面[没有进展](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-says-no-progress-reviving-interim-peace-deal-with-us-2026-08-12/)。由于6月协议中重新确认的停火破裂，伊朗恢复了对被其指控未经许可试图通过海峡的船只的袭击。

阿联酋阿布扎比国家石油公司表示，周四晚间，其两艘船只在通过海峡时遭到袭击。阿联酋政府指责伊朗，伊朗方面没有立即置评。

根据船舶跟踪公司Kpler的分析，周五有两艘船只通过了这条狭窄水道：一艘运粮船进入伊朗水域，一艘空载干散货船驶向相反方向。

数据显示，另有一艘空载液化石油气产品油轮正通过海峡驶入海湾。周五没有可见的原油运输。

周四有[九艘船](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/hormuz-shipping-traffic-capped-amid-competing-claims-us-iran-2026-08-14/)通过海峡，高于周三的五艘，但低于8月份平均12艘的水平。

一些船只可能关闭应答器，未被察觉地通过，但这些数字远低于2月美国和以色列对伊朗发动战争前每天穿越霍尔木兹海峡的130多艘船。

风险情报公司Verisk Maplecroft的中东首席分析师托比约恩·索尔韦特（Torbjorn Solvedt）表示：“除了威胁该地区的能源基础设施外，伊朗限制船只通过海峡的能力是其在谈判中的主要筹码。”

### 美国承诺下周将对伊朗采取新措施

美国国防部长皮特·赫格塞斯对记者表示，美国军方有能力在该地区保持海军存在，以执行对伊朗的报复性封锁，此举已对伊朗造成严重的经济损害。

“美国海军可以无限期维持这样的封锁，因为我们会像现在这样轮换船只进出，并将继续这样做，”赫格塞斯在访问巴拿马途中对记者说。

财政部长斯科特·贝森特表示，美国计划对伊朗施加更多金融损害。

“敬请关注下周的更多公告，因为我们即将采取的措施在经济孤立一个国家的历史上从未见过，”他在Newsmax的“罗布·施密特今晚”节目采访中表示。

![An F/A-18E Super Hornet launches from the flight deck of Nimitz-class aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b1481b3b52bcbdc7d6ac8b3/3bc81cc9-0b14-8119-a995-f13da85dbc6c-f4bcdf3ca4946d361eeaa7d15a74678e.jpg)

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普在国内面临结束一场不得人心战争的压力，高油价拉低了他的支持率，并可能削弱其政党在11月中期选举中对国会的控制。

基准布伦特原油期货和美国西德克萨斯中质原油期货均小幅上涨，分别约为每桶87美元和81美元。

印度[对俄罗斯原油的依赖](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/russian-share-indias-oil-imports-surges-record-high-july-2026-08-14/)在7月飙升至创纪录水平，而亚洲炼油厂（海湾燃料的主要客户）本周[购买了美国原油](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/asian-refiners-buy-more-us-crude-hormuz-remains-blocked-traders-say-2026-08-14/)，以确保未来供应。

特朗普一再宣称美国对霍尔木兹海峡拥有“完全控制权”，战前全球五分之一的石油和液化天然气经由该海峡运输。

伊朗对此予以否认，并表示在其条件得到满足之前，不会允许该水道重新开放。这些条件包括取消经济制裁和释放被冻结的伊朗资产。

据伊朗半官方塔斯尼姆通讯社报道，周四，伊朗议会一委员会批准了伊朗关于霍尔木兹海峡的计划。该计划包括一项条款，禁止美国、以色列及其他“敌对”国家的资产和设备过境。

美国于6月中旬解除对伊朗航运和港口的封锁一个月，但此后又重新实施，切断了德黑兰的主要硬通货来源，并加剧了此前战时对其能源基础设施打击造成的损失。

华盛顿此前表示，一旦伊朗和阿曼（分处海峡两侧）就恢复商业航运达成协议，美国将解除对伊朗的封锁。

### 石油供应萎缩

特朗普还一再威胁要升级军事打击并“狠狠打击伊朗”，尽管他迄今拒绝部署地面部队、夺取战略岛屿或轰炸海水淡化厂。本周早些时候，特朗普[表示他将依赖经济手段](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trump-says-us-is-low-keying-it-with-iran-stresses-economic-pain-according-axios-2026-08-09/)而非军事行动。

美国加强了对伊朗及其他据称帮助其采购武器的个人和实体的经济制裁，但这一施压行动并未将德黑兰拉回谈判桌。

周四，有报道称也门伊朗支持的胡塞武装用无人机袭击了沙特阿美的一家炼油厂，这再度引发了对地区战争扩大的担忧。

全球经济学家预测全球增长将大幅下滑，部分领域可能陷入衰退，并警告称，如果战争不能尽快结束，影响将进一步扩大。

## Hormuz traffic slows further after US threatens more economic pressure on Iran

LONDON/PANAMA CITY, Aug 14 (Reuters) - Transit through the Strait of Hormuz appeared to grind to a near standstill on Friday after two more ships were attacked there and the United States said it could maintain a [naval blockade of Iran](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-can-keep-naval-blockade-iranian-ports-indefinitely-pentagon-chief-says-2026-08-13/) indefinitely.

A senior Iranian source said on Wednesday that there had been [no progress](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-says-no-progress-reviving-interim-peace-deal-with-us-2026-08-12/) in talks to build on a June agreement to end the war. Since the ceasefire renewed in the June deal broke down, Iran has resumed attacks on ships it accuses of trying to transit the strait without its permission.

The United Arab Emirates' Abu Dhabi National Oil Company said two of its vessels were attacked transiting the strait on Thursday evening. The UAE government blamed Iran, which made no immediate comment.

Two vessels passed through the narrow waterway on Friday, according to analysis from ship tracking firm Kpler: a grain ship entering Iranian waters and an empty dry bulk ship going in the other direction.

A separate empty liquefied petroleum products tanker was sailing into the Gulf via the strait, the data showed. There were no crude oil shipments visible on Friday.

[Nine vessels](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/hormuz-shipping-traffic-capped-amid-competing-claims-us-iran-2026-08-14/) passed through the strait on Thursday, up from five on Wednesday but below the average of 12 for August.

Some ships may pass through undetected with their transponders off, but the figures are nowhere near the more than 130 ships that traversed the Strait of Hormuz daily before the war launched by the U.S. and Israel on Iran in February.

"Alongside the threat to energy infrastructure in the region, Iran's ability to restrict shipping through the strait is its main source of leverage in negotiations," said Torbjorn Solvedt, principal Middle East analyst with risk intelligence company Verisk Maplecroft.

### US PLEDGES NEW MEASURES AGAINST IRAN IN COMING WEEK

U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth told reporters the U.S. military has the capability to maintain a naval presence in the region to enforce its retaliatory blockade of Iran, which has inflicted severe economic damage on the country.

"Indefinitely the United States Navy can maintain a blockade like that because we'll rotate ships in and out, as we have, and we'll continue to," Hegseth told reporters during a trip to Panama.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the United States planned to inflict more financial damage on Iran.

"Watch this space for more announcements coming next week because we are going to apply measures like have never been seen in the history of economic isolation on a country," he said in an interview on Newsmax's "Rob Schmitt Tonight" programme.

U.S. President Donald Trump is under pressure at home to end an unpopular war, with high fuel prices dragging down his approval ratings and potentially eroding his party's control of Congress in midterm elections in November.

Benchmark Brent futures and U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude futures were both up slightly at about $87 and $81 a barrel respectively.

India's [reliance on Russian crude](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/russian-share-indias-oil-imports-surges-record-high-july-2026-08-14/) surged to a record in July, while refineries in Asia, a major customer for fuel from the Gulf, [bought U.S. crude](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/asian-refiners-buy-more-us-crude-hormuz-remains-blocked-traders-say-2026-08-14/) this week to ensure future supplies.

Trump has repeatedly asserted that the U.S. has "total control" over the strait, through which a fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas flowed before the start of the war.

Iran rejects that, and has said it will not allow the waterway to reopen until its conditions are met. These include removing economic sanctions and releasing frozen Iranian assets.

On Thursday, an Iranian parliamentary committee approved Iran's plan for the strait. It includes a provision banning the transit of U.S.-, Israeli- and other "hostile" countries' assets and equipment, the semi-official Tasnim news agency reported.

The U.S. lifted its blockade of Iran's shipping and ports for a month in mid-June but has since reimposed it, cutting off Tehran's primary source of hard currency and compounding earlier losses from wartime strikes on its energy infrastructure.

Washington previously said it would lift the Iranian blockade once Iran and Oman, which sit on either side of the strait, reach an agreement to restore commercial shipping.

### SHRINKING OIL SUPPLY

Trump has also repeatedly threatened to escalate military strikes and "hit Iran hard", although he has thus far resisted deploying ground troops or seizing strategic islands and bombing desalination plants. Earlier this week, Trump [suggested he would rely on economic means,](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trump-says-us-is-low-keying-it-with-iran-stresses-economic-pain-according-axios-2026-08-09/) rather than military action.

The U.S. has tightened economic sanctions against Iran and other individuals and entities that it says are helping it procure weapons, but the pressure campaign has not brought Tehran back to the negotiating table.

Reports that Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis had targeted a Saudi Aramco refinery with drones on Thursday renewed concerns about a widening regional war.

Global economists have forecast a sharp drop in global growth, and potentially a swing into recession in some areas, warning that the impact would grow if the war was not ended soon.
