### 中文译文

## “不值得费力”：全球私募股权在中国零交易

![Investors says limited partners might have been spooked by Beijing’s intervention in recent deals involving companies with links to China © Adek Berry/AFP/Getty Images](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A3d75a640-1a1e-4103-a5df-a820c2804350?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

全球最大的收购公司今年避免了对中国内地的新投资，随着北京加强对人工智能等敏感领域外资的审查，交易荒进一步加深。

根据英国《金融时报》对Dealogic和PitchBook数据的分析，包括KKR、华平投资和黑石集团在内的十家全球最大私人资本公司，在2026年前七个月未在中国进行任何新的公开披露的股权投资。

相比之下，去年仅有3笔私募股权投资，2024年有2笔，这表明外国私人资本在进入中国增长最快的公司方面面临困难。

根据这两家数据提供商的数据，就在2021年，这些公司还在中国进行了大约十几笔交易，包括早期融资。

投资者表示，即使美中紧张关系有所缓和，投资私人基金的有限合伙人可能还是被北京最近对涉华公司交易的干预吓到了。

北京在4月阻止了Meta以20亿美元收购总部位于新加坡、由中国创立的AI初创公司Manus。总部位于香港的长江和记实业出售包括巴拿马运河在内的全球港口业务组合给贝莱德牵头的财团的计划，在遭到中国当局批评后被推迟。

“地缘政治仍然是进入的障碍，”行业组织另类投资管理协会亚太区联席主管李凯胜表示。“美国的有限合伙人会说太麻烦了，可能不值得费力。”

他补充说，中国税收执法的转变意味着一些投资者可能会看到“在更靠近本土、风险状况更好的地方获得类似回报”。

CK Hutchison出售包括巴拿马运河在内的全球港口业务组合给贝莱德牵头的财团的计划，在中国提出批评后被推迟。© Daniel A Gonzalez/Anadolu/Getty Images

全球私人资本在投资退出方面一直举步维艰，各公司采用创造性交易（包括二级销售）为投资者带来回报。

在中国，去年同样的10家收购公司对中国内地投资组合公司完成的公开披露的完全撤资为零，原因是人们对经济增长放缓和美国利率上升的担忧。

在中国以外，私募股权公司正在募集创纪录的亚洲聚焦基金。EQT最近关闭了有史以来最大的亚太私募股权基金，规模为156亿美元，而Blackstone在6月宣布关闭一只131亿美元的亚洲基金。

“最近，如果一家（西方）私募基金募集一只大型亚洲基金，通常他们会说日本、印度和澳大利亚是重点，而中国的占比大约为10%，”香港年利达律师事务所私募股权合伙人Bryan Koo表示。“私募股权集团对结构性逆风心知肚明。”

顾问们看到交易流正朝着相反的方向发展，他补充道，并且越来越多地为希望在中国境外达成交易的中国公司提供建议。

贝恩资本大中华区董事长Jonathan Zhu在6月大连举行的世界经济论坛会议上对彭博电视台表示：“我们受过分析经济数据、商业趋势的训练，但显然政治和地缘政治问题以前并不是首要考虑，而现在……它们已经成为首要考虑。”

来自PitchBook和Dealogic的数据涵盖了黑石、凯雷、华平投资、TPG、EQT、贝恩资本、安宏资本、KKR、阿波罗和CVC。这些公司拒绝对此报道发表评论。

尽管如此，交易人士仍表示，他们对于人工智能和半导体等敏感领域之外的交易活动复苏抱有希望。他们指出，美国总统唐纳德·特朗普与中国国家主席习近平在5月举行的会晤帮助缓解了紧张局势。

“情绪已经好转，”一家西方私募股权公司的高管表示，“美中之间存在一种稳定的紧张关系，这为这类讨论提供了更有成效的背景。”

“更乐观地说，我确实认为估值已经下降了很多，”高伟绅律师事务所的Koo表示，“尽管[事实]完全正确，交易缓慢，但我认为仍有交易可做。”

### English Original

## ‘Not worth the squeeze’: global private equity makes zero deals in China

The world’s biggest buyout firms have avoided new investments in mainland China this year, deepening a dealmaking drought as Beijing tightens scrutiny of foreign investment in sensitive sectors such as AI.

Ten of the largest global private capital firms, including KKR, Warburg Pincus and Blackstone, have made no new publicly disclosed equity investments in [China](https://www.ft.com/china) in the first seven months of 2026, according to an FT analysis of figures from Dealogic and PitchBook.

This compares with just three private equity investments last year and two in 2024, in a sign of how foreign private capital is struggling to get access to the country’s fastest-growing companies.

Firms made roughly a dozen deals in China, including early stage funding, as recently as 2021, according to data from the two providers.

Investors said limited partners, which invest in private funds, might have been spooked by Beijing’s intervention in recent deals involving companies with links to China, even as tensions between the US and China have eased.

Beijing [blocked Meta’s $2bn purchase](https://www.ft.com/content/1e4c269a-5258-406c-a308-e55c3d5d640f) of Singapore-based, China-founded AI start-up Manus in April. Hong Kong-based CK Hutchison’s plan to sell a portfolio of global port operations, including on the Panama Canal, to a BlackRock-led consortium has been delayed after it was met with criticism from Chinese authorities.

“Geopolitics continues to be a barrier to entry,” said Kher Sheng Lee, Asia-Pacific co-head of the Alternative Investment Management Association, an industry group. “US LPs will say it’s too much hassle, the juice may not be worth the squeeze.”

He added that [shifting tax enforcement in China](https://www.ft.com/content/e17912c0-7691-42f7-8920-b43268930e41) meant some investors were likely to see “similar returns on a better risk profile closer to home”.

CK Hutchison’s plan to sell a portfolio of global port operations, including on the Panama Canal, to a BlackRock-led consortium has been delayed after Chinese criticism © Daniel A Gonzalez/Anadolu/Getty Images

Private capital globally has been struggling to find exits on investments, with firms [adopting creative deals](https://www.ft.com/content/4675b8bb-4047-458a-be01-90c2aff29219?syn-25a6b1a6=1), including secondary sales, to generate returns for investors.

In China, last year, the same 10 buyout firms had [zero publicly disclosed complete divestments](https://www.ft.com/content/cdc229ae-e7ec-4a8b-b4d6-ffed2f3747f7?syn-25a6b1a6=1) from mainland Chinese portfolio companies, hit by concerns over slowing economic growth and higher US interest rates.

Outside China, [private equity](https://www.ft.com/private-equity) firms are raising record Asia-focused funds. EQT recently closed the largest ever dedicated Asia-Pacific private equity fund, worth $15.6bn, while Blackstone announced in June the closure of a $13.1bn Asia fund.

“Recently if a [western] PE fund raises a big Asia fund, usually they say Japan, India and Australia are the focus and China is like 10 per cent,” said Bryan Koo, private equity partner at law firm Clifford Chance in Hong Kong. “The private equity groups are well aware of structural headwinds.”

Advisers were seeing deal flows going the other way, he added, and increasingly advising Chinese companies that want deals outside China.

Bain Capital’s Greater China chair Jonathan Zhu told Bloomberg TV at a World Economic Forum meeting in Dalian in June: “We are trained to analyse economic data, business trends but obviously political and geopolitical issues previously were not front of mind, and now . . . they have become front of mind.”

The data from PitchBook and Dealogic covers Blackstone, Carlyle, Warburg Pincus, TPG, EQT, Bain Capital, Advent International, KKR, Apollo and CVC. The companies declined to comment on this story.

Dealmakers nevertheless said they remained hopeful for a pick-up in activity outside sensitive sectors such as AI and semiconductors. They pointed to a meeting between US President Donald Trump and China’s Xi Jinping in May that helped ease tensions.

“Sentiment has changed for the better,” said an executive at a western private equity firm. “There is a kind of stable tension between the US and China ,which has been a much more productive backdrop for these kinds of discussions.”

“On a more optimistic note, I do think valuations have gone down a lot,” said Koo of Clifford Chance. “While [it is] absolutely correct that dealmaking is slow, I think there are deals to be done.”
