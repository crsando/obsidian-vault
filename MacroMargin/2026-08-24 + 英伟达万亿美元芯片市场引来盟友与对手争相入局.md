## 英伟达万亿美元芯片市场引来盟友与对手争相入局

### **要点**

- 亚马逊的半导体业务如果作为独立公司运营，年收入将超过250亿美元，销售额以三位数的百分比增长。
- 英伟达正面临来自其最大客户日益激烈的竞争，包括亚马逊和谷歌，这些客户正在开发内部芯片设计并向第三方出售。
- AI芯片市场收入有望突破1万亿美元，英伟达的财报可能会提醒人们该公司占据主导地位，但也会让其地盘对竞争对手更具吸引力。
亚马逊公司(Amazon.com Inc.)的安迪·贾西(Andy Jassy)今年开始在电话会议上发表大胆言论。尽管他的公司仍然是英伟达公司(Nvidia Corp.)的最大客户之一，但它本身也是全球最大的数据中心芯片制造商之一。

如果亚马逊的半导体业务是一家独立企业，其年收入将超过250亿美元，首席执行官7月对分析师如此表示。而且销售额正以三位数百分比的速度增长。

“我们对芯片业务的发展感到非常兴奋，”贾西表示。

这种自信凸显了半导体格局正在发生多么巨大的变化——而且速度有多快。尽管英伟达仍是数据中心处理器（尤其是用于训练人工智能模型的处理器）的主导制造商，但它正面临来自四面八方的日益激烈的竞争，包括其最大的客户。

亚马逊和其他云计算提供商仍然是英伟达芯片的最大买家，它们一直在开发自研芯片设计，甚至向第三方出售。超威半导体公司（AMD）和博通公司等竞争对手的芯片制造商正在录得数百亿美元的数据中心收入。与此同时，大量初创公司正从渴望支持下一个突破的投资者那里筹集数十亿美元资金。

就在过去几天，谷歌与迈威尔科技公司达成协议，以[进一步推进其芯片制造雄心](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/marvell-gives-google-right-to-buy-up-to-12-2-billion-in-shares)，而英伟达的竞争对手 Cerebras 系统公司则宣布推出一款新计算机，号称能够[比任何其他产品更快地处理人工智能提示](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/cerebras-cbrs-says-its-new-computer-boosts-ai-speed-advantage-over-nvidia)。与此同时，Anthropic 公司[已聘请](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/anthropic-taps-google-chip-veteran-as-part-of-push-into-hardware)一位谷歌芯片资深人士，此时该公司正谋划进军芯片领域。

作为全球市值最高的公司，英伟达将难以被超越：其增长速度仍快于许多挑战者。而且该公司已扩展到软件、网络和其他技术领域，以确保其在人工智能经济中保持不可或缺的地位。

但随着人工智能芯片市场收入即将突破1万亿美元，攻势只会更加猛烈。

“英伟达拥有强大的生态系统，”投资公司加贝利的投资组合经理兼分析师亨迪·苏珊托表示。不过，市场的变化——比如亚马逊和谷歌突然向其他公司出售自研芯片——难以预料。他说：“这来得相对较快。”

英伟达周三的财报可能再次提醒人们该公司占据主导地位。分析师估计，上一季度营收同比增长近一倍，达到920亿美元。这比任何竞争对手全年的收入都要多。但大量现金——以及极高的利润率——只会让它的地盘更具吸引力。与此同时，据知情人士透露，英伟达一些最大客户表示，在许多情况下，包含其人工智能芯片的服务器价格上涨超过15%。

这家总部位于加利福尼亚州圣克拉拉的公司没有回应置评请求。

对英伟达的威胁可分为几大类。一方面，现有的芯片竞争对手和越来越多的新贵都在争夺更大的市场份额。另一方面，人工智能实验室和所谓“超大规模企业”——最大的云计算提供商——正在设计自己的芯片。

### **1. 长期竞争对手**

在开发者意识到其图形处理单元——一种因电脑游戏玩家而普及的技术——非常适合训练人工智能模型之后，英伟达登上了半导体行业的顶峰。

在OpenAI的ChatGPT于2022年底问世后，对这些也被称为人工智能加速器的GPU的需求激增。这为英伟达的长期竞争对手带来了机会。

GPU第二大制造商AMD上一季度数据中心营收增长一倍以上，达到67亿美元。其首席执行官苏姿丰预计，到2030年，人工智能加速器的总市场规模将达到1.4万亿美元。

不过，尚不清楚[AMD能在这个市场中占据多大份额](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/amd-sales-outlook-disappoints-investors-after-ai-fueled-rally)。英伟达仍占据加速器市场约90%的份额。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481e6aeddd33b94fe9477/3c681cc9-0b14-810e-8f29-d6d87055abb9-75e9ad0606b81a522f3fe0cb18e2f052.png)

英特尔公司曾在芯片行业超越英伟达，如今尝试[销售自己的加速器](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-09/intel-unveils-new-ai-accelerator-chip-in-bid-to-gain-on-nvidia)，但迄今收效有限。不过，它从人工智能数据中心的更广泛支出热潮中获益，推动其销售额和股价回升。

博通和美满电子为客户定制组件，在人工智能市场中走出了自己的道路。它们正在帮助谷歌和其他所谓的超大规模企业（即最大的云计算公司）开发芯片，以填充快速增长的数据中心。

此外还有中国的竞争者，英伟达正在应对贸易限制。美国的出口管制意味着公司不能向中国买家出售其最优质的产品。尽管英伟达大力游说试图推翻这些规定，但这些规则反而激发了中国的国产替代品研发热情。英伟达表示，像[华为技术有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH)这样在美国被列入黑名单的企业，正在快速发展出直接可竞争的产品。

### **2. 新兴挑战者**

人工智能行业的一个转折点正在帮助英伟达的挑战者获得助力。数据中心正从人工智能模型的训练阶段（即向模型灌输数据的阶段）转向所谓的推理阶段。在这个阶段，软件开始对现实世界的输入做出响应并给出回应。换句话说，AI模型已经构建完成，现在正在投入使用。

英伟达的GPU无疑是训练模型的强大工具，但推理阶段为更广泛的处理器创造了机会。这为新兴芯片企业带来了一场潜在的争夺战。根据Jon Peddie Research的数据，目前有150家公司正在开发200多种不同形式的AI半导体设计。

其思路是定制芯片，以更快或更高效地处理推理。产品还可以针对特定类型的模型进行微调。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481e6aeddd33b94fe9477/3c681cc9-0b14-8151-94bc-f9c8ea09145e-99c4fd33beff381c0e46406194eaaf19.png)

“推理并非一刀切，因此试图用单一芯片强行完成推理是行不通的。”专注这一领域的初创公司d-Matrix的首席执行官Sid Sheth如此表示。

另一个动机是什么？是不希望英伟达（Nvidia）如此独霸市场。该公司的高端加速器芯片每块售价约3万美元。其利润率已扩大到惊人的75%，比最接近的竞争对手高出约20个百分点。

芯片初创公司Positron的首席执行官Mitesh Agrawal表示：“市场更喜欢一点寡头垄断结构，而不是垄断。”

公开市场和私人市场资金充裕，但凡有方案能撼动英伟达的领先地位者均可获得融资。Cerebras于5月进行了半导体行业有史以来规模最大的首次公开募股，在超额认购的首秀中筹集了创纪录的资金。

### 延伸阅读：[AI芯片制造商Cerebras在年内最大IPO后暴涨68%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-14/cerebras-shares-indicated-to-surge-89-after-year-s-top-ipo)

该公司首席执行官Andrew Feldman的说法很简单：他的芯片在响应AI提示时比英伟达的芯片快得多。“这是一个对速度极为在意的市场，”他说。

OpenAI和亚马逊（Amazon）都已与Cerebras达成合作，后者依靠餐盘大小的芯片来提升性能。与此同时，此前默默无闻的初创公司，如Positron、Fractile和Etched，正通过旨在帮助他们推出新型芯片的融资轮吸引快速增长的估值。

这些都不容易。设计半导体不适合缺乏耐心、经验不足或资金短缺的人。芯片的研发可能需要数年时间——耗资数亿美元或更多——而且公司很少能一次成功。

d-Matrix公司的Sheth亲自负责了约20次芯片流片（tape-outs），这是公司开始大规模量产处理器之前的设计最后阶段。“我不认为第21次会更容易，”他说。

### **3. 超大规模云服务商**

超大规模云服务商——之所以如此命名，是因为它们以无与伦比的能力运行计算业务——如今面临一个问题：它们添加数据中心的速度太快，芯片供应跟不上。

这让他们几乎必须寻找英伟达的替代方案，并且他们越来越倾向于自行开发。

这正是博通的用武之地。这家曾以Wi-Fi半导体和其他通信组件闻名的公司，如今已成为内部芯片设计的首选合作伙伴。

博通正与谷歌、Meta平台公司（Meta Platforms Inc.）等合作开发AI芯片，并[预计今年该产品销售额将达到560亿美元](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/broadcom-outlook-disappoints-investors-seeking-ai-fueled-blowout)。这项努力涉及使用专用集成电路（ASIC）来填补英伟达GPU所扮演的角色。

根据彭博行业研究的分析，博通设计部门的AI相关收入如今已增长逾一倍。

但推动供应商多元化的努力意味着，即使有博通也不够。谷歌还与迈威尔（Marvell）合作开发其AI芯片，即其所谓的张量处理单元（TPU）。

### 延伸阅读：[迈威尔授予客户谷歌购买122亿美元股票的权利](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/marvell-gives-google-right-to-buy-up-to-12-2-billion-in-shares)

这也是竞争升级的又一迹象。谷歌今年将首次同时发布两个版本的TPU，这将使其能够将其中一个瞄准快速推理市场。该公司还开始允许客户在谷歌云和数据中心之外使用这些芯片，这种模式更类似于英伟达的销售方式。

### **4. AI实验室**

顶级AI开发者，如OpenAI和Anthropic，正在竞相构建自己的基础设施，这不仅仅是寻求数据中心和设备。与超大规模云厂商一样，他们希望拥有自己的芯片，以便针对各公司的需求进行定制。

Anthropic是Claude AI软件的制造商，已采取积极措施减少对英伟达的依赖。其中包括签署了价值数百亿美元的合同，使用来自谷歌、亚马逊和AMD的AI加速器芯片。ChatGPT背后的公司OpenAI也在采取类似行动。

下一步是建立内部芯片业务。Anthropic尚未透露其打造AI加速器计划的太多细节，但SK海力士已被接洽，商讨为该技术配套供应存储芯片。上个月在一次活动上，综合企业集团[SK集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/034730%20KS%20Equity)董事长崔泰源承认了相关讨论。

### 延伸阅读：[SK董事长称Anthropic请求供应以制造自家芯片](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/sk-chair-says-anthropic-asked-for-supplies-to-make-its-own-chips)

OpenAI为其内部芯片（名为Jalapeno）提供了更清晰的路线图。这款与博通合作开发的处理器将于今年晚些时候部署到数据中心。

起初，该芯片将仅专注于推理——训练仍使用英伟达GPU。但未来版本可能会处理更多任务。博通CEO陈福阳（Hock Tan）曾表示，该芯片将在2028年升级，此后每年更新一次。

值得注意的是，OpenAI半导体业务负责人Richard Ho在6月的一次采访中表示，OpenAI可能会选择让其他企业使用其芯片。

### 了解更多：[OpenAI与博通发布芯片，可更快更便宜地运行模型](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-24/openai-and-broadcom-unveil-ai-chip-to-run-models-faster-cheaper)

他表示，设计自己的芯片对于OpenAI获得性能、成本和速度的最佳组合至关重要。“这是OpenAI为真正全面掌控整个基础设施堆栈而采取的一系列措施的开端。”

与此同时，Meta正计划多种内部半导体设计，以支撑其AI雄心。它已与AI组件领域几乎所有重要厂商签署了协议，甚至包括相对后来的高通公司。但有迹象表明仍处于早期阶段：最近几个月，Meta用于英伟达的实际支出反而上升了。

在华尔街，英伟达的星光略有黯淡。尽管其股价在2026年上涨了15%，但费城证券交易所半导体指数上涨了66%。英特尔和AMD的股价涨幅超过一倍，内存生产商美光科技公司的股价已涨至原来的三倍以上。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481e6aeddd33b94fe9477/3c681cc9-0b14-8198-b056-e96814d6a7f5-4a7fdf0c13644eff58b1ef8809e1668b.png)

但英伟达也采取了自己的行动，以保持在推理领域的竞争力。据报道，去年12月，它同意斥资200亿美元收购专注于这一市场的初创公司Groq的技术和人员。

英伟达的竞争对手面临的另一个挑战是供应。在现代芯片行业，大多数公司设计产品，然后将生产外包给少数几家被称为晶圆代工厂的制造商。

台积电是占主导地位的晶圆代工厂，生产最先进的芯片。这使其成为AI处理器竞赛中的造王者。英伟达与台积电有着长期合作，并可以利用其不断增强的财力巩固其在行业中的核心地位。

彭博行业研究分析师 Kunjan Sobhani 表示：“凭借超过 1000 亿美元的供应承诺，该公司实际上提前多个季度锁定了关键的 AI 基础设施产能。随着 AI 基础设施支出加速，英伟达的规模和购买力本身可以成为竞争优势，使规模较小的竞争对手更难以类似速度获得足够产能。”

而英伟达的行动正在不断加快。该公司如今每年都会彻底重新设计其产品，并且能够提供越来越广泛的其他技术，包括数据中心冷却系统、网络和存储。

英伟达潜在的竞争对手承认，他们尚缺乏与之竞争的规模。Positron 的 Agrawal 表示，Cerebras 已获得一份价值数百亿美元的合同，但其后的初创企业尚未达到这一水平。

他说，甚至很难声称自己是英伟达的竞争对手。Agrawal 表示：“说实话，除非你能声称拥有至少其十分之一的收入，否则没有人是英伟达的竞争对手。”

即便是骄傲的芯片巨头亚马逊，也仍然深度依赖英伟达。尽管这家电商巨头在内部进行了种种努力，但它仍将大约四分之一的资本支出用于英伟达。

## Nvidia’s Trillion-Dollar Chip Market Has Friends and Foes Closing In

### [Technology](https://www.bloomberg.com/technology)AI

*Illustration: 731*

By [Dina Bass](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ACZCnwVoBvY/dina-bass) and [Ian King](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AB1M_8ht4mM/ian-king)August 24, 2026 at 8:00 PM GMT+8

### **Takeaways**

- Amazon's semiconductor operation would generate annual revenue of more than $25 billion if it were a standalone business, with sales growing by a percentage in the triple digits.
- Nvidia is facing increasing competition from its biggest customers, including Amazon and Google, which are developing in-house silicon designs and selling them to third parties.
- The AI chip market is poised to cross $1 trillion in revenue, with Nvidia's earnings report likely to serve as a reminder of the company's commanding lead, but also making its turf more attractive to competitors.
Amazon.com Inc.’s Andy Jassy has begun making an audacious boast during conference calls this year. Though his company remains a top Nvidia Corp. customer, it’s also one of the world’s largest makers of data center chips in its own right.

If Amazon’s semiconductor operation were a standalone business, it would generate annual revenue of more than $25 billion, the chief executive officer told analysts in July. And the sales are growing by a percentage in the triple digits.

“We’re quite excited about what’s happening in our chips business,” Jassy said.

The swagger underscores how much the semiconductor landscape is changing — and how fast. Though Nvidia remains the dominant maker of data center processors, particularly ones that help train artificial intelligence models, it’s facing increasing competition from all sides, including its biggest customers.

Amazon and other cloud-computing providers, still the biggest buyers of Nvidia chips, have been developing in-house silicon designs and even selling them to third parties. Rival chipmakers such as Advanced Micro Devices Inc. and Broadcom Inc. are booking tens of billions of dollars in data center revenue. And a raft of startups are raising billions more from investors keen to back the next breakthrough.

Just in the past few days, Google forged a deal with Marvell Technology Inc. to [further its chipmaking ambitions](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/marvell-gives-google-right-to-buy-up-to-12-2-billion-in-shares) and Nvidia rival Cerebras Systems Inc. announced a new computer that it says can [process AI prompts faster](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/cerebras-cbrs-says-its-new-computer-boosts-ai-speed-advantage-over-nvidia) than any other. Anthropic PBC, meanwhile, [has hired](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/anthropic-taps-google-chip-veteran-as-part-of-push-into-hardware) a Google silicon veteran at a time when it’s plotting its own foray into chips.

Nvidia, the world’s most valuable business, will be hard to catch: It’s still growing faster than many of its challengers. And the company has expanded into software, networking and other technologies to ensure it remains essential to the AI economy.

But with the AI chip market poised to cross $1 trillion in revenue, the onslaught is only going to grow more intense.

“Nvidia has a strong ecosystem,” said Hendi Susanto, a portfolio manager and analyst at investment firm Gabelli. Shifts in the market — like Amazon and Google suddenly selling their own chips to others — have been unpredictable, though. “That came on us relatively fast,” he said.

Nvidia’s earnings report on Wednesday will likely serve as another reminder of the company’s commanding lead. Analysts estimate that revenue nearly doubled last quarter from a year earlier to $92 billion. That’s more money than any of its rivals get in a full year. But the flood of cash — with sky-high profit margins — only makes its turf more attractive. Meanwhile, some of Nvidia Corp.’s biggest customers that the prices of servers containing its artificial intelligence chips are going up more than 15% in many cases, people familiar with the matter have said.

The Santa Clara, California-based company didn’t respond to a request for comment.

The threats to Nvidia can be divided into a few broad categories. On one side, existing chip competitors and a growing group of upstarts are both vying for a bigger share of the market. On the other, you have AI labs and so-called hyperscalers — the largest cloud-computing providers — designing their own chips.

### **1. Longtime Rivals**

Nvidia ascended to the top of the semiconductor industry after developers realized that its graphics processing units — technology popularized by computer gamers — were well-suited for training AI models.

Demand for these GPUs, also known as AI accelerators, exploded after OpenAI’s ChatGPT hit the scene in late 2022. And that opened up an opportunity for Nvidia’s longstanding competitors.

AMD, the second-largest maker of GPUs, saw its data center revenue more than double last quarter to $6.7 billion. And CEO Lisa Su expects the total market for AI accelerators to reach $1.4 trillion by 2030.

Still, it’s [unclear how much](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/amd-sales-outlook-disappoints-investors-after-ai-fueled-rally) of that category AMD will claim. Nvidia retains roughly 90% of the accelerator market.

Intel Corp., which once eclipsed Nvidia in the chip industry, has attempted to [sell its own accelerators](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-09/intel-unveils-new-ai-accelerator-chip-in-bid-to-gain-on-nvidia) — with limited success so far. But it’s benefited from the broader spending spree on AI data centers, fueling a resurgence in its sales and share price.

Broadcom and Marvell, which make customized components for clients, have beaten their own path to the AI market. They’re helping Google and other so-called hyperscalers — the biggest cloud-computing companies — develop chips to fill a fast-growing fleet of data centers.

Then there are competitors in China, where Nvidia is grappling with trade restrictions. US export controls mean the company can’t sell its best products to Chinese buyers. And though Nvidia has lobbied hard to overturn them, the rules have lit a fire under Chinese efforts to develop domestic alternatives. Companies such as [Huawei Technologies Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH) — blacklisted in the US — are making rapid strides toward developing products that are directly competitive, Nvidia has said.

### **2. The Upstarts**

A turning point in the AI industry is helping give Nvidia’s challengers a lift. Data centers are shifting from the training of AI models — a stage that involves bombarding them with data — to a phase known as inference. That’s the point when the software begins responding to real-world inputs and spitting out responses. In other words, the AI models have been built and are now being put into use.

Nvidia’s GPUs are an undeniably powerful tool for training models, but the inference stage is creating opportunities for a wider range of processors. That’s set up a potential land grab by the chip upstarts. There are currently 150 companies working on more than 200 different AI semiconductor designs of one form or another, according to Jon Peddie Research.

The idea is to tailor chips to handle inference faster or more efficiently. Products can also be fine-tuned to accommodate certain kinds of models.

“Inference is not a one-size-fits-all, so brute-forcing inference with a single chip is not going to work,” said Sid Sheth, who is CEO of d-Matrix, a startup focusing on the space.

Another motivation? A desire to keep Nvidia from being quite so dominant. The company charges about $30,000 per chip for its high-end accelerators. Its profit margins have expanded to a remarkable 75%, roughly 20 points wider than those of its nearest rivals.

“The markets prefer a little bit of an oligopoly structure rather than a monopoly,” said Mitesh Agrawal, CEO of chip startup [Positron](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/nvidia-rival-positron-holds-talks-to-raise-funds-at-5-billion-valuation).

The public and private markets are awash with cash for anyone with a plan to make a dent in Nvidia’s lead. Cerebras held the semiconductor industry’s largest-ever initial public offering in May, raising a record amount in an oversubscribed debut.

### Read More: [AI Chipmaker Cerebras Climbs 68% After Year’s Biggest IPO](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-14/cerebras-shares-indicated-to-surge-89-after-year-s-top-ipo)

That company’s CEO, Andrew Feldman, has a simple pitch: His chips are much faster than Nvidia’s at providing an answer to an AI prompt. “This is a market that cares desperately about speed,” he said.

OpenAI and Amazon have both forged deals with Cerebras, which relies on dinner-plate-sized chips to boost performance. At the same time, previously obscure startups like [Positron](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/nvidia-rival-positron-holds-talks-to-raise-funds-at-5-billion-valuation), [Fractile](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/ai-chip-startup-fractile-in-talks-for-6-5-billion-value-after-anthropic-deal) and Etched are attracting rapidly growing valuations in funding rounds aimed at helping them field novel chips.

None of this is easy. Designing semiconductors isn’t for the impatient, inexperienced or short of cash. Chips can take years to develop — at a cost of hundreds of millions of dollars or more — and companies rarely get them right on the first try.

Sheth, of d-Matrix, has personally overseen about 20 chip tape-outs, the final stage in design before a company starts manufacturing a processor in mass quantities. “I don’t think it gets easier the 21st time,” he said.

### **3. The Hyperscalers**

Hyperscaler companies — so named because they run computing operations with unmatched capacity — have a problem. They’re adding data centers too quickly for the chip supply to keep up.

That’s made it a near-necessity to seek alternatives to Nvidia, and increasingly they’re looking to do the job in-house.

That’s where Broadcom fits in. The company, once best known for Wi-Fi semiconductors and other communications components, is now the go-to partner for in-house chip design.

Broadcom is working with Google, Meta Platforms Inc. and others to develop AI chips, and it [expects to sell $56 billion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/broadcom-outlook-disappoints-investors-seeking-ai-fueled-blowout) of the products this year. The push involves using application-specific integrated circuits, or ASICs, to fill a role held by Nvidia’s GPUs.

AI-related revenue at Broadcom’s design division is now more than doubling, according to analysis by Bloomberg Intelligence.

But the drive to diversify suppliers means that even Broadcom isn’t enough. Google is also working with Marvell to develop its AI chips, which it calls tensor processing units, or TPUs.

### Read More: [Marvell Gives Client Google Right to Buy $12.2 Billion in Shares](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/marvell-gives-google-right-to-buy-up-to-12-2-billion-in-shares)

In another sign of escalation, Google will release two versions of its TPUs simultaneously this year for the first time. That will let it target one of them at the market for fast inference. The company also has begun letting customers use those chips outside of Google’s cloud and data centers, a model more akin to the way Nvidia sells.

### **4. The AI Labs**

Top AI developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic are racing to build their own infrastructure, and that goes beyond seeking data centers and equipment. Like the hyperscalers, they want their own chips, which they can tailor for each company’s needs.

Anthropic, the maker of Claude AI software, has taken aggressive steps to decrease its reliance on Nvidia. That’s included lining up tens of billions of dollars in contracts to use AI accelerator chips from Google, Amazon and AMD. OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, is making similar moves.

The next step is to build in-house chip operations. Anthropic hasn’t given much detail about its plans to create an AI accelerator, but SK Hynix Inc. has been approached about supplying the memory chips to accompany the technology. Chey Tae Won, chairman of conglomerate [SK Group](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/034730%20KS%20Equity), acknowledged the discussions during an event last month.

### Read More: [SK Chair Says Anthropic Asked for Supplies to Make Its Own Chips](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/sk-chair-says-anthropic-asked-for-supplies-to-make-its-own-chips)

OpenAI has provided more of a road map for its in-house chip, called Jalapeno. That processor, developed with Broadcom, will be deployed to data centers later this year.

To start, the chip will only focus on inference — with training remaining on Nvidia GPUs. But future versions may handle additional tasks. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan has said that the silicon will be refreshed in 2028 and then annually thereafter.

Notably, OpenAI may opt to let other businesses use its chips, Richard Ho, the company’s semiconductor chief, said in a June interview.

### Read More: [OpenAI, Broadcom Unveil Chip to Run Models Faster, Cheaper](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-24/openai-and-broadcom-unveil-ai-chip-to-run-models-faster-cheaper)

Designing its own chip is critical for OpenAI to get the best combination of performance, cost and speed, he said. “This is the beginning of a series of steps for OpenAI to take real full control over the entire infrastructure stack.”

Meta, meanwhile, is planning a variety of in-house semiconductor designs to underpin its AI ambitions. It has signed agreements with literally everyone of consequence in AI components, even relative latecomer Qualcomm Inc. But there are signs it’s still early days: In recent months, Meta’s spending dedicated to Nvidia actually went up.

On Wall Street, Nvidia’s star has dimmed a bit. Though its shares have climbed 15% in 2026, the Philadelphia Stock Exchange Semiconductor Index has gained 66%. Intel and AMD have more than doubled, and memory producer Micron Technology Inc. is up more than threefold.

But Nvidia has made its own moves to stay competitive in inference. In December, it agreed to pay a reported $20 billion for technology and personnel from Groq, a startup focused on this market.

Another challenge that Nvidia’s contenders face is supply. In the modern chip industry, most companies design their products and then outsource production to a handful of manufacturers known as foundries.

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. is the dominant foundry and produces the most advanced chips. That makes it the kingmaker in the AI processor race. Nvidia has a long-term relationship with TSMC and can use its growing financial muscle to cement its central role in the industry.

“With supply commitments above $100 billion, the company is effectively locking in critical AI-infrastructure capacity multiple quarters in advance,” according to Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Kunjan Sobhani. “As AI-infrastructure spending accelerates, Nvidia’s size and purchasing power can become competitive advantages themselves, making it harder for smaller rivals to secure sufficient capacity at a similar pace.”

And Nvidia is moving ever-faster. The company is now completely overhauling its designs every year, and it’s able to offer a widening range of other technology, including data center cooling systems, networking and storage.

Nvidia’s would-be rivals acknowledge that they lack the scale needed to compete yet. Cerebras has secured a contract worth tens of billions, but the startups in its wake have yet to reach that level, said Positron’s Agrawal.

It’s hard to even claim to be an Nvidia rival, he said. “Genuinely, no one is a competitor of Nvidia until you can claim at least a 10th of their revenue,” Agrawal said.

And even Amazon, the proud chip juggernaut, remains deeply reliant on Nvidia. For all its in-house efforts, the e-commerce giant continues to send about a quarter of its capital expenditures to the company.
