## 为什么世界需要迫使人民币升值

## **为什么世界需要迫使人民币升值**

### 中国正以廉价出口充斥世界。或许只有通过协议令其货币升值才是唯一解决方案。

![Greg Ip](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b14812a893fffba64686747/3ca81cc9-0b14-8195-8fe4-ea4ac7d2d413-4bbfc30fc8d6c772d35c334707ad60d6.png)

2026年8月28日 上午10:00（美国东部时间）

![James Baker speaking to reporters at the Plaza Hotel with finance ministers from Japan, Germany, and the U.S. present.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b14812a893fffba64686747/3ca81cc9-0b14-81fa-a85c-ed449d909c85-a7cea565bcc55a52f5cef50fb52a2940.jpg)

曾参与谈判《广场协议》（Plaza Accord）以助美元贬值的美国财政部长詹姆斯·贝克（James Baker），1985年在纽约广场酒店向记者发表讲话。图片来源：Mario Cabrera/AP

1985年，世界主要经济强国齐聚纽约广场酒店，以应对一种严重失衡、足以危及全球经济的货币。

如今，世界需要另一个“广场协议”，但这次的目标不是压低被高估的美元，而是推高被低估的人民币。

中国庞大且不断增长的贸易顺差，可能掏空其贸易伙伴的工业基础。多年来，世界其他国家一直恳求中国转变经济模式，更多依赖内需而非出口，但无济于事。

以关税强制执行的货币协议，或许是促使中国行动的唯一途径。时机可能已经成熟。对中国的失望情绪正在沸腾，尤其是在欧洲。[法国政府2月的一份报告](https://www.strategie-plan.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/2026/2026-02-09%20-%20Rouleau%20compresseur%20chinois/HCSP-2026-REPORT-THE%20CHINESE%20STEAMROLLER-18fevrier14h30-COUV.pdf)称：“中国的这一波激增如今威胁到……欧洲生产体系的核心。”今年6月，德国总理弗里德里希·默茨（Friedrich Merz）呼吁达成一项针对中国的新广场协议。

本周末在北卡罗来纳州阿什维尔举行的二十国集团央行行长和财长会议，将是开启这一对话的好地方。

#### **盈余的规模**

有两个事实没有争议。首先，中国拥有巨大且不断增长的贸易顺差。[高盛](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GS)预计今年将达到1.2万亿美元。它估计，其“经常账户”盈余——一项涵盖商品、服务和投资收入的贸易指标——将占全球GDP的1%，这一比例在战后历史上没有任何国家达到过。

其次，相对于贸易平衡和购买力等基本面因素所决定的应有水平，人民币汇率被严重低估：据高盛估计低估了19%，据外交关系委员会学者布拉德·塞策尔估计低估了35%。

### 分歧在于两者之间的联系：是后者导致了前者吗？

在国际货币基金组织和大多数正统经济学家看来，人民币低估并非中国经常账户盈余的原因，而是导致盈余的内需不足的一个症状。

经常账户反映了一个国家储蓄与投资之间的差额。按照正统观点，这植根于经济体的根本结构。美国出现经常账户赤字是因为储蓄过少。中国出现经常账户盈余是因为储蓄过多。

IMF将中国的过度储蓄归因于一系列结构性因素，例如社会保障体系不完善，以及财政体系对家庭征税过重，从而抑制了消费。

最近，房地产泡沫破裂削弱了投资，并进一步扩大了经常账户盈余。国际货币基金组织（IMF）表示，解决方案是：“财政刺激应侧重于通过对人投资来持久提振消费”，并遏制房地产市场的崩溃。

另一种观点认为，中国固定其汇率，明确目的是促进出口、抑制进口。

人民币贬值会降低中国商品在国外的价格，提高外国商品在中国的价格，从而扩大经常账户盈余。它还转移“可贸易商品消费者与生产者之间的收入”，与卡内基国际和平基金会有关联的中国问题专家Michael Pettis写道。通过将收入和投资引向出口产业而远离消费者，这抑制了消费并扩大了经常账户盈余。

过去十年，人民币兑美元汇率一直在相对稳定的区间内交易。然而，正如高盛经济学家Kamakshya Trivedi和Hui Shan在最近的一份报告中所指出的，自疫情以来，中国的商品价格有所下降，这是封锁和房地产泡沫破裂的结果，而发达市场的价格却因刺激措施和供应链中断而上涨。这意味着经通胀调整后的人民币汇率已大幅下跌。

![Man in a white Tesla, with other Tesla vehicles on the production floor of a factory in Shanghai.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b14812a893fffba64686747/3ca81cc9-0b14-814a-97df-f0861b079b24-2daf59bec4a5fea2753354e6f308705e.jpg)

最近的一份报告发现，中国电动汽车制造成本低于其他地区。共同社/ZUMA新闻社

这提高了中国的竞争力。高盛将中国制造商（如鞋类的新百伦、电动汽车的特斯拉）与其他地区的成本进行了比较，并将中国企业（如家电制造商海尔）的价格与外国同行（如西门子）进行了比较。中国的价格折扣在电动汽车领域为32%，冰箱为38%，鞋类为53%。理论上，如果人民币估值合理，这种差距不应持续。

#### **解决方案**

中国官员口头上说要提振消费，但实际上很少作为。中国领导人习近平将对家庭的支持贬斥为“福利主义”。他认为，扩大出口、限制进口会让世界更加依赖中国，而中国则减少对世界的依赖。

高盛的经济学家认为，货币升值将在贸易顺差收窄时通过维持增长来补充国内改革。然而，塞策尔(Setser)表示，人民币低估减轻了中国改革的压力，因为它在其他经济部门低迷时维持了出口。

在《广场协议》下，美国及其盟友结合联合干预，配合国内改革（如缩小美国预算赤字），推动美元兑西德马克和日元贬值。这奏效了：美国贸易逆差先扩大，然后急剧缩小。

不过，西德和日本是美国的亲密盟友。中国不是，而且也没有合作意愿。“中国不会接受以汇率为借口进行打压，也不会回到大国协调少数国家命运的老时代，”中国共产党喉舌《环球时报》6月表示。

中国的贸易伙伴也无法简单地大量买入人民币来推高汇率，因为中国严格控制其货币的获取渠道。

一艘集装箱船驶离中国青岛港。法新社/盖蒂图片社

相反，他们可以征收关税，并承诺如果人民币升值就逐步取消关税。2005年，在美国国会威胁加征关税的情况下，中国开始进行重大的人民币升值。

美国已经通过高额关税减少了与中国的贸易逆差。欧洲对此想法逐渐升温。法国报告提议“前所未有的贸易保护措施，相当于对中国征收30%的普遍关税；或欧元兑人民币贬值20%至30%”。

障碍在于特朗普总统喜欢关税，可能不愿意用关税换取货币升值。他也没有表现出对削减赤字的太大兴趣。其他国家意见不一，不愿与中国对抗，并且在遭受特朗普关税打击后，也不愿与特朗普合作。

然而，如果他们能够克服政治分歧，美国及其盟友将会认识到他们面临一个共同的问题，而一项货币协议——无论有没有中国的合作——可能是最干净、扭曲最小且最有效的解决方案。

## Why the World Needs to Force China’s Yuan to Revalue

## **Why the World Needs to Force China’s Yuan to Revalue**

### China is flooding the world with cheap exports. An accord to revalue its currency might be the only solution.

By

Aug. 28, 2026 10:00 am ET

Treasury Secretary James Baker, who negotiated the ‘Plaza Accord’ to help weaken the dollar, spoke to reporters at New York’s Plaza Hotel in 1985. Mario Cabrera/AP

In 1985, the world’s leading economic powers met at New York’s Plaza Hotel to deal with a currency so out of whack it was putting the entire global economy at risk.

Today, the world needs another Plaza Accord, this one not to bring down an overvalued dollar but to push up China’s undervalued yuan.

China’s massive and growing trade surplus threatens to hollow out its trading partners’ industrial bases. For years, the rest of the world has pleaded with China to change its economic model to rely more on domestic demand, and less on exports, to no avail.

A currency accord, enforced with tariffs, might be the only way to get China to act. The time may be ripe. Frustration with China is boiling over, especially in Europe. “This Chinese surge now threatens…the very core of Europe’s productive system,” a [French government report](https://www.strategie-plan.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/2026/2026-02-09%20-%20Rouleau%20compresseur%20chinois/HCSP-2026-REPORT-THE%20CHINESE%20STEAMROLLER-18fevrier14h30-COUV.pdf) said in February. In June, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called for a new Plaza Accord aimed at China.

A meeting by central bank governors and finance ministers from the top 20 economies starting this weekend in Asheville, N.C., would be a good place to start the conversation.

#### **The scale of the surplus**

Two facts aren’t in dispute. First, China has a monstrous and growing trade surplus. [Goldman Sachs](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GS) projects it will hit $1.2 trillion this year. It estimates that its surplus on the “current account”—a trade measure encompassing goods, services and investment income—is headed to 1% of global GDP, a share no country has achieved in postwar history.

Second, its currency is deeply undervalued relative to where fundamental determinants such as the trade balance and purchasing power say it should trade: by 19%, according to Goldman, and by 35%, according to Brad Setser, a scholar at the Council on Foreign Relations.

### The disagreement is over the link: Did the second cause the first?

In the view of the International Monetary Fund and most orthodox economists, the undervalued yuan isn’t the cause of China’s current account surplus, but a symptom of the inadequate domestic demand that leads to the surplus.

The current account reflects the difference between how much a nation saves and invests. In the orthodox view, this is rooted in economies’ underlying structure. The U.S. runs a current-account deficit because it saves too little. China runs a current account surplus because it saves too much.

The IMF attributes China’s excess saving to a range of structural factors, such as an inadequate safety net and a fiscal system that taxes households too heavily and thus depresses their consumption.

More recently, a collapsed property bubble has shriveled investment and further widened the current account surplus. The solution, the IMF says: “Fiscal stimulus should be focused on durably boosting consumption by investing in people” and arresting the property bust.

The alternate view is that China fixes its currency with the explicit aim of boosting exports and suppressing imports.

A cheap yuan lowers Chinese prices abroad and raises foreign prices in China, boosting the surplus. It also shifts “income between consumers of tradable goods and producers of tradable goods,” [writes](https://x.com/michaelxpettis/status/2090731133495300161) Michael Pettis, a China expert affiliated with the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace. By funneling income and investment toward export industries and away from consumers, this depresses consumption and expands the current account surplus.

China’s currency has traded in a relatively stable range against the dollar for the past decade. However, as Goldman Sachs economists Kamakshya Trivedi and Hui Shan show in a recent report, goods prices have fallen in China since the pandemic, a result of lockdowns and the property bust, while rising in developed markets because of stimulus and supply-chain disruptions. This means the inflation-adjusted yuan exchange rate has plummeted.

Electric-vehicle manufacturing costs are lower in China than they are elsewhere, a recent report finds. Kyodonews/ZUMA Press

This has increased China’s competitiveness. Goldman compared costs experienced by manufacturers in China, such as New Balance in shoes and [Tesla](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/TSLA) in electric vehicles, with their costs elsewhere, and prices of Chinese companies, such as appliance manufacturer Haier, to foreign peers such as [Siemens](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/XE/XETR/SIE). China’s price discount is 32% in electric vehicles, 38% in refrigerators and 53% in shoes. In theory, such gaps should not persist if the yuan is fairly valued.

#### **The solution**

Chinese officials pay lip service to boosting consumption while doing little. Chinese leader Xi Jinping [disparages support for households](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/communist-party-priorities-complicate-plans-to-revive-chinas-economy-84a156d7?mod=article_inline) as “welfarism.” He believes boosting exports while restricting imports makes the world more dependent on China and China less dependent on the world.

Goldman’s economists argue that currency revaluation would complement domestic reforms by sustaining growth as the trade surplus shrinks. Yet the undervalued yuan takes the pressure off China to reform because it sustains exports while the rest of the economy is moribund, Setser says.

Under the Plaza Accord, the U.S. and its allies combined joint intervention to push the dollar down against the West German and Japanese currencies with domestic reforms, such as a narrower U.S. budget deficit. It worked: The U.S. trade deficit first widened, then shrank sharply.

West Germany and Japan, though, were close American allies. China isn’t, and is in no mood to cooperate. “China will not accept using exchange rates as a pretext for oppression, nor will it return to the old era of great powers coordinating the fate of a few countries,” the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202606/1364067.shtml), a mouthpiece for the Chinese Communist Party, said in June.

Nor can China’s trading partners simply buy up yuan to force it higher, since China tightly controls access to its currency.

A containership leaving the port of Qingdao, China. AFP/Getty Images

Instead, they could impose tariffs, with a promise to dial them back if China revalues. China began a significant yuan revaluation in 2005 under the threat of tariffs in Congress.

The U.S. has already reduced its trade deficit with China with steep tariffs. Europe is warming to the idea. The French report proposed “unprecedented trade protection, equivalent to a general tariff of 30% vis-à-vis China; or a depreciation of the euro of 20% to 30%” against the yuan.

Standing in the way is that President Trump likes tariffs and might not be willing to trade them for currency appreciation. Nor has he shown much interest in deficit reduction. Other countries are divided and reluctant to antagonize China, and aren’t disposed to cooperate with Trump after being hit with his tariffs.

Yet if they can get past the politics, the U.S. and its allies would recognize they face a common problem, and that a currency accord, with or without China’s cooperation, could be the cleanest, least distorting and most effective solution.
