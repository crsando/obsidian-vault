华盛顿——据美国新的情报报告，俄罗斯总统[弗拉基米尔·普京](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/vladimir-putin)可能在未来几年试图对盟国发动有限攻击，以试探北约的决心。这些报告设想了从网络攻击到小规模地面入侵等可能情景。

美国官员所描述的这些评估出炉之际，军方在潜在未来战争中对抗[俄罗斯](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/russia)或中国所需的某些关键弹药出现短缺。由于俄罗斯入侵后向乌克兰转让武器，以及美国与伊朗的冲突，美国已严重削减了这些武器的库存。其他官员称，面临风险的库存包括远程精确打击导弹和陆军战术导弹系统，以及短程“毒刺”地对空导弹。

美国此前评估认为，在普京仍在[乌克兰](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/ukraine)作战期间，他不会挑衅北大西洋公约组织国家。但美国官员称，这一评估在年初发生了变化，因为普京在乌克兰受到挤压，并且在国内面临取得胜利的压力。基辅的无人机正在对俄罗斯军队和石油工业造成越来越大的损失，而普京的部队在前线取得较小进展，且伤亡惨重。

美国和欧洲领导人日益看到俄罗斯进行试探性攻击以测试北约决心的迹象，包括俄罗斯巡航导弹[落入波兰](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/russian-airstrikes-kill-at-least-eight-in-ukraine-ce1b1756?mod=article_inline)和[无人机飞入罗马尼亚](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/russian-drone-crashes-into-apartment-building-in-nato-member-romania-16a50198?mod=article_inline)。

美国官员称，普京在对乌克兰的持续战争期间攻击北约的目的，如果发生的话，将是分裂该联盟。

“问题在于，在这种情况下美国会如何回应，”美国企业研究所高级研究员希瑟·康利在被问及俄罗斯可能的入侵时表示。“（俄罗斯的）目标一直是挑战北约的可信度，并将美国与其盟友分开。”

这些人士称，随着普京难以在乌克兰获得他想要的成果，美国情报官员一直在分析俄罗斯是否打算在短期内升级对欧洲盟友的侵略行动。美国官员表示，担忧在于普京在被逼入绝境时可能会变得更加危险。

可能的威胁包括对北约国家的网络攻击、派遣无标记武装团体占领领土，或对联盟东翼进行有限入侵。根据美国情报评估，俄罗斯任何行动的时间框架估计在今年秋天至2029年之间。

根据报告，最极端情况——有限地面入侵——的可能性较低，但随着时间推移可能性会增加。此类行动一直被认为极不可能，因为这会触发北约第五条，该条款要求成员国集体防御。但第五条对于北约应对网络或混合威胁并不明确。

“这始终是北约应对低于第五条攻击的挑战，而且到目前为止，北约盟友确实更倾向于不升级，”康利说。例如，当俄罗斯2007年对爱沙尼亚发动一系列网络攻击时，该国选择双边处理危机。

五角大楼和北约欧洲盟军最高司令部发言人拒绝讨论敏感情报。国家情报总监办公室发言人未回应置评请求。

“我们一直在思考并准备应对各种情况。北约正在密切关注，我们准备好在必要时进行威慑和防御，这一点我们一直在展示，”北约发言人马丁·奥唐奈上校说。

新的评估加剧了美国官员对某些关键弹药短缺的现有担忧。官员们表示，除了某些远程和中短程导弹外，其他关键防空储备也处于低位，包括“爱国者”、SM-3和“末段高空区域防御”（萨德）拦截弹，以及“萨德”雷达和防空炮兵部队。

五角大楼首席发言人肖恩·帕内尔称弹药短缺的说法不实。白宫发言人安娜·凯利表示，美国军方拥有“绰绰有余”的弹药，能够“实现特朗普总统的所有战略目标”，同时补充说，总统已敦促国防承包商[生产更多弹药](https://www.wsj.com/articles/trump-summons-munitions-makers-as-worries-grow-over-low-u-s-stockpiles-7af0ddc8?mod=article_inline)。

“我们拥有在总统选择的时间和地点进行打击所需的一切，”帕内尔说。“在多个作战司令部中，我们已经成功执行了行动，同时保持着深厚、随时可用的武器库，以保卫我们的人民和利益。任何将媒体噪音误认为美国软弱的对手，都将以惨痛的方式认识到真相。”

战略与国际研究中心（CSIS）的汤姆·卡拉科表示，在与俄罗斯的潜在冲突中，精确打击导弹（PrSM）和陆军战术导弹系统（ATACMS）——两者都由高机动性火炮火箭系统（海马斯）发射——以及“毒刺”导弹对于击退地面入侵至关重要。

关键弹药的短缺在美国政府内部引发了关于武器库存下降的辩论，与此同时，特朗普正在权衡对伊朗的下一步行动。据《华尔街日报》此前报道，参谋长联席会议主席丹·凯恩将军[最近警告白宫](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-pauses-iran-strikes-as-officials-weigh-dwindling-air-defense-stocks-c7d7ebb0?mod=article_inline)，防空拦截弹数量减少。凯恩对此感到担忧，他认为，低库存不会排除恢复对伊朗重大作战行动的可能性，但会增加风险。

政府以外的分析人士多次警告说，在中东消耗弹药可能会削弱美国威慑全球对手的能力，这些对手不仅包括俄罗斯，还包括中国。

战略与国际研究中心最近的一份报告[涵盖了与伊朗战争中使用的七种关键弹药](https://www.csis.org/analysis/last-rounds-status-key-munitions-iran-war-ceasefire)。例如，更新后的报告估计，美国在伊朗战争中已消耗了1060至1430枚“爱国者”导弹，美国武库中可能仅剩870枚。

“如果只是与伊朗作战的问题，尽管我们已经用掉了一半多一点的拦截弹，我们仍然剩下一半。因此我们可以继续对伊朗作战相当长一段时间，”CSIS报告合著者马克·坎西安表示。“问题在于，五角大楼现在非常担心这会对西太平洋与中国的关系产生什么影响。”

### New assessment updates past forecasts that Russia’s leader wouldn’t provoke the alliance during the Ukraine war

WASHINGTON—Russian President [Vladimir Putin](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/vladimir-putin) could try to test NATO’s resolve with a limited assault on an allied country in the next few years, according to new U.S. intelligence reports that posit possible scenarios ranging from a cyberattack to a small-scale land incursion.

The assessments, described by U.S. officials, come amid a shortfall in certain critical munitions that the military would need to confront [Russia](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/russia) or China in a potential future war. The U.S. has severely degraded its stockpile of these weapons because of transfers to Ukraine after Russia’s invasion, as well as the U.S. conflict with Iran. Stockpiles at risk include long-range Precision Strike Missiles and Army Tactical Missile Systems, as well as short-range Stinger surface-to-air missiles, other officials said.

The U.S. had previously assessed that Putin wouldn’t provoke a North Atlantic Treaty Organization country while he is still fighting in [Ukraine](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/ukraine). But that assessment changed earlier this year, the U.S. officials said, as Putin is squeezed in Ukraine and under pressure at home to secure a victory. Kyiv’s drones are inflicting growing damage on both Russia’s army and oil industry, while Putin’s forces are making smaller gains at the front line amid heavy casualties.

U.S. and European leaders increasingly see signs of Russia conducting probing attacks to test NATO’s resolve, including Russian cruise missiles that [have landed in Poland](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/russian-airstrikes-kill-at-least-eight-in-ukraine-ce1b1756?mod=article_inline) and [drones flown into Romania](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/russian-drone-crashes-into-apartment-building-in-nato-member-romania-16a50198?mod=article_inline).

Putin’s goal in attacking NATO during a continuing war with Ukraine, if it happens, would be to fracture the alliance, the U.S. officials said.

“The question mark is how the United States would respond in that case,” said Heather Conley, senior fellow with the American Enterprise Institute, on a possible Russian incursion. “It has always been a goal [of Russia’s] to challenge the credibility of NATO and to separate the United States from its allies.”

Polish military gathered in July near where a Russian projectile hit a field after crossing into Poland. Kacper Pempel/Reuters

With Putin struggling to secure the gains he wants in Ukraine, U.S. intelligence officials have been analyzing whether Moscow intends to escalate its aggressive actions toward European allies in the near term, the people said. The concern is that Putin may become more dangerous when backed into a corner, the U.S. officials said.

The possible threats range from a cyberattack on a NATO country or sending unmarked armed groups to occupy territory to a limited incursion on the alliance’s eastern flank. The time frame for any move by Russia is estimated to happen between this fall and 2029, according to the U.S. intelligence assessments.

The likelihood of the most extreme scenario—a limited land incursion—is low, but gets higher as time goes on, according to the reports. Such a move has always been considered extremely unlikely because it would trigger NATO’s Article 5, which commits members to collective defense. But Article 5 isn’t as clear about NATO’s response to cyber or hybrid threats.

“This has always been NATO’s challenge to respond to a sub-Article Five attack, and it’s been very true that to this point, NATO allies have preferred not to escalate,” Conley said. When Russia launched a series of cyberattacks on Estonia in 2007, for example, the country chose to manage the crisis bilaterally.

Spokesmen for the Defense Department and NATO’s Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe declined to discuss sensitive intelligence. A spokesperson for the Office of the Director for National Intelligence didn’t respond to a request for comment.

“We are always thinking through and preparing for any number of scenarios. NATO is watching and we are ready to deter and defend as necessary, which we continue to demonstrate,” said Col. Martin O’Donnell, a NATO spokesman.

The site of a recent Russian strike in Zaporizhzhia, Ukraine. Thomas Peter/Reuters

The new assessments compound existing concerns among U.S. officials about a shortfall in certain critical munitions. In addition to certain long- and short-range missiles, other stores of critical air defenses are also low, including Patriot, SM-3 and Terminal High Altitude Area Defense interceptors, as well as Thaad radars and air defense artillery units, officials said.

Chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell called claims of munition shortages false. White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly said the U.S. military had “more than enough” munitions to “serve all of President Trump’s strategic goals,” while adding that the president has urged defense contractors [to produce more munitions](https://www.wsj.com/articles/trump-summons-munitions-makers-as-worries-grow-over-low-u-s-stockpiles-7af0ddc8?mod=article_inline).

“We have everything required to strike at the time and place of the President’s choosing,” Parnell said. “Across multiple combatant commands, we have already executed successful operations while maintaining a deep, ready arsenal to defend our people and our interests. Any adversary who confuses media noise with American weakness will discover the truth the hard way.”

In a potential conflict with Russia, weapons such as the Precision Strike Missile and Army Tactical Missile Systems, both of which are fired by the High Mobility Artillery Rocket System, and Stinger missiles are crucial to repel a land invasion, said Tom Karako of the Center for Strategic and International Studies.

The shortfall of critical munitions has sparked a debate within the U.S. government about declining weapons stocks as Trump weighs next steps in Iran. Gen. Dan Caine, the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, [recently warned the White House](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-pauses-iran-strikes-as-officials-weigh-dwindling-air-defense-stocks-c7d7ebb0?mod=article_inline) of fewer air-defense interceptors, The Wall Street Journal previously reported. The issue is a concern for Caine, who believes that the low inventories wouldn’t preclude a resumption of major combat operations against Iran, but would add to the risk.

Analysts outside the government have repeatedly cautioned that the expenditure of munitions in the Middle East could hamper the U.S.’s ability to deter adversaries around the globe, including not only Russia but also China.

A recent report from the Center for Strategic and International Studies [covered the seven key munitions](https://www.csis.org/analysis/last-rounds-status-key-munitions-iran-war-ceasefire) that had been used in the war with Iran. For example, the updated report estimates that the U.S. has expended 1,060-1,430 Patriot missiles in the Iran war, leaving as few as 870 in America’s arsenal.

“If it’s just a question of fighting Iran, even though we’ve used half of the interceptors, a little more, we still have half of them left. So we could go on against Iran for quite some time,” said Mark Cancian, a co-author of the CSIS report. “The problem is that the Pentagon is now very worried about what this does in the Western Pacific with China.”
