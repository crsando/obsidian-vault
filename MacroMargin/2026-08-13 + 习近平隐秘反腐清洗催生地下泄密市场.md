### 中文译文

## 习近平隐秘反腐清洗催生地下泄密市场

围绕习近平反腐行动的黑箱长期以来助长了中国的政治八卦。如今，一个出售所谓“谁可能是下一个倒台者”的秘密的地下市场正在兴起。

据官方新华社出版的《半月谈》杂志最近的一篇报道，在中国受到严格管控的互联网上，当局正在玩“打地鼠”游戏，以阻止那些神秘用户兜售所谓泄密群组的访问权限，这些群组声称泄露哪些官员正接受审查的细节。

报道称，成员通常需要支付较低的入群费，而进入更私密的聊天室则承诺提供更可靠的消息，费用高达399元（59美元）。线索往往很隐晦：一名干部的简历被毫无解释地发布，或者一名官员的名字用同音字伪装。为了逃避监控，运营者不断将成员转移至新的群组。

《半月谈》称，并非所有信息都可靠——一些账号捏造指控以吸引流量，贵州省警方已关闭了多个此类网站。然而，一些消息似乎源自政府官员，表明内部存在不忠。在《半月谈》引用的一起案例中，贵州一名基层干部获取人事信息，然后与六名亲属合作运营九个微信账号，目的是增加其在线粉丝数。

目前没有迹象表明这种消息交易被用来预测某位特定官员倒台并从中获利，这在世界其他地方是一个新兴业务。尽管如此，随着习近平的反腐清洗触及体制的各个新角落，这种做法凸显了中国官员中普遍存在的焦虑感。现任政治局24名成员中已有3人被罢免，同时一场[大规模军事清洗](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-xi-china-military-officials-purge/)使这个世界最大规模常备军的领导人数量降至1970年代以来的最低水平。

“中国的每一位中高层官员现在都必须假定他们有一定风险被反腐败机构密切审查，”加州大学圣迭戈分校教授、中国精英政治专家史宗瀚（Victor Shih）表示。“他们自然愿意花钱获取某些潜在调查的信息。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bb81cc90b1481308805fe58163cc69b/3bb81cc9-0b14-81f0-a590-e72ddfcbb357-af48280f1c08c899e4e0c9d0b9b29a7e.png)

随着中国政治进入一个特别敏感的时期，这些群组正在蓬勃发展。共产党正开始为明年秋天的领导人大会进行为期一年的倒计时，届时习近平可能获得打破先例的第四任期，并主持人事改组。在这场十年两次的会议之前，官员们将争相争取晋升，这只会加深对内幕消息的需求。

理论上，关于即将进行的调查的消息能使被针对的官员准备辩护，或帮助有抱负的干部培养合适的人脉以塑造职业生涯。对于投资者来说，省级领导人受到审查的早期预警提供了时间，以抛售与受牵连公司相关的资产。

“提前了解晋升、调查或清洗的信息，对试图评估政治风险、管理政府关系以及判断哪些官员关系仍然有效的公司来说，具有商业价值，”亚洲协会政策研究所中国分析中心高级研究员Neil Thomas表示。“干部们自己也迫切需要了解可能影响其职业生涯或政治网络的人事变动。”

国务院新闻办公室、反腐败监督机构中央纪律检查委员会以及互联网监管机构国家互联网信息办公室未回应传真发送的置评请求。

### **党的纪律**

14年前上台后不久，习近平誓言要拿下“老虎”和“苍蝇”——即高层和基层官员。他的目标是在国家向市场经济转型期间，经过数十年他所认为的纪律松弛之后，对共产党进行整顿。

随着这场运动达到前所未有的规模，谣言市场应运而生：中国的纪律检查机关去年调查了98.3万名官员，其中包括181名由中央政府直接管理的高级官员。这两项数字都创下了纪录。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bb81cc90b1481308805fe58163cc69b/3bb81cc9-0b14-81b4-a9ba-e1a2cfbd9bd9-176e53a8595c167b3378572cb393c609.png)

由于这一行动没有显示出放缓迹象，早在付费群出现之前，猜测就越来越多地在中国社交媒体上流传——有时准确性惊人。

例如，2月10日上午，中国最高反腐机构宣布对浙江省原省委书记易炼红展开调查。然而，关于易炼红落马的消息早已在普通聊天群里流传数日。

一篇微信公众号文章称，在此前的几次高峰之后，两天前该平台上对易炼红的搜索量已上升了六倍，这些高峰自2025年4月以来多次出现。推测可能是由于对他相关人员的调查而加剧。

官员缺席政治活动往往是麻烦的第一个迹象。几个月后，当局通常会宣布一名干部涉嫌“严重违纪违法”，在调查进行期间不提供任何细节。

涉及党内官员的调查可能尤其不透明。党员通常首先在共产党的纪律体系内处理。涉及涉嫌犯罪的案件可能会在之后移交给检察官，通常是在党的处分已经宣布之后。

据该报道，付费聊天室似乎更进一步，在官员落马有任何公开迹象之前就将他们置于聚光灯下。彭博新闻无法在线访问此类群组，也无法独立核实这些报道。

![Yi Huiman, China’s former top securities regulator, was probed last year for suspected violation of discipline and law. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bb81cc90b1481308805fe58163cc69b/3bb81cc9-0b14-81b8-a35e-f3bc3fbaede1-7dcdeadca865a487006d70b512752dde.png)

尽管落马官员数量激增，习近平上月仍将反腐行动吹捧为党有能力“清除侵蚀自身健康的病毒”的证据，他在一次[讲话](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-01/xi-positions-china-s-ruling-party-as-global-force-for-progress)中表示这场运动不会停止。

根据中共中央政治局七月会议的通报，定于十月召开的五中全会将聚焦纪律问题，寻求汲取反腐运动的“宝贵经验”。这次闭门会议预计将确认将高级官员从党的中央委员会中除名，并增补候补委员以填补空缺。

它还将提供线索，表明习近平在2027年遴选下一届班子时将看重哪些品质——忠诚、纪律和执行其优先事项的能力可能会成为重要考量。

这种讨价还价很可能只会加剧对据称是官方泄露的信息的需求，而《半月谈》的报道正是对此提出批评。

对于监管机构来说，这些帖子的隐晦性质构成了法律和实际挑战。发布官员公开可得的履历本身并不违法，也不一定构成散布谣言或泄露国家机密。警方可能也缺乏有关机密调查的足够信息，无法判断网上的说法是否虚假。

这使得当局难以压制一个部分由政治体制自身保密性所催生的市场。

亚洲协会政策研究所的托马斯（Thomas）表示：“说法越耸人听闻，就越引人关注。与民主政治体制相比，外界对北京内部运作知之甚少，因此坚持事实很快会变得枯燥乏味。”

## Xi’s Secretive Graft Purges Spawn Underground Market for Leaks

The black box around Xi Jinping’s corruption crackdown has long fueled political gossip in China. Now, an underground market is emerging for selling purported secrets on who might be next to fall.

Across China’s tightly controlled internet, authorities are playing whack-a-mole to stop shadowy users who peddle access to groups claiming to leak details of which officials are under scrutiny, according to a recent report from Banyuetan, a magazine published by the official Xinhua News Agency.

Members are typically charged a small entry fee, with access to more exclusive chat rooms promising better tips fetching as much as 399 yuan ($59), the report said. Clues are often cryptic: A cadre’s resume is posted without explanation, or an official’s name appears disguised by homophones. To evade monitoring, operators constantly shuffle members into new rooms.

Not all the information is reliable — some accounts fabricate allegations to drive traffic, Banyuetan said, with police in the southwestern province of Guizhou closing a number of such sites. Some tips, however, appear to originate from government officials — pointing to disloyalty within the ranks. In one case cited by Banyuetan, a low-ranking cadre in Guizhou obtained personnel information, then worked with six relatives to run nine WeChat accounts with the aim of boosting their online follower count.

There’s no indication the tip trading is used to profit off predicting the downfall of any particular officials, a burgeoning new business elsewhere in the world. Still, the practice underscores the prevailing sense of anxiety among Chinese officials as Xi’s anti-graft purges reach into new corners of the system. Three of the 24 members appointed to the current Politburo have been ousted, while a [sweeping military purge](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-xi-china-military-officials-purge/) has left the world’s largest standing army with the fewest leaders since the 1970s.

“Every mid- and high-level official in China now must assume they bear some risk of being closely examined by anti-corruption authorities,” said Victor Shih, an expert on elite politics in China and professor at the University of California, San Diego. “It’s natural they would pay to obtain information about some of these potential investigations.”

The groups are flourishing as Chinese politics approaches a particularly sensitive period. The Communist Party is embarking on its one-year countdown to next autumn’s leadership congress, where Xi may secure a precedent-defying fourth term and preside over a personnel reshuffle. Officials will jostle for promotions ahead of that twice-a-decade conclave, only deepening the demand for inside information.

In theory, tips on an impending investigation can enable a targeted official to prepare their defense, or help an ambitious cadre cultivate the right relationships to shape their career. For investors, early warnings a provincial leader is under scrutiny provide time to offload any assets associated with exposed companies.

“Advance knowledge of a promotion, investigation, or purge can be commercially valuable to companies trying to assess political exposure, manage government relations, and determine which official relationships remain viable,” said Neil Thomas, senior fellow on Chinese politics at the Asia Society Policy Institute’s Center for China Analysis. “Cadres themselves also have strong incentives to understand personnel shifts that could affect their careers or political networks.”

The State Council Information Office, corruption watchdog Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the internet regulator Cyberspace Administration didn’t respond to faxed requests for comment.

### **Party discipline**

Xi vowed to take down both “tigers” and “flies” — high and low ranking officials — shortly after assuming power 14 years ago. His goal was to clean up the Communist Party after decades of what he saw as disciplinary drift during the nation’s transformation toward a market-oriented economy.

The rumor market has emerged as the campaign reaches unprecedented scale: China’s disciplinary authorities probed 983,000 officials last year, including 181 senior officials who were directly managed by the central government. Both were records.

With that initiative showing no sign of slowing, speculation has increasingly circulated on Chinese social media, long before the paid groups emerged — and sometimes with striking accuracy.

On the morning of Feb. 10, for example, China’s top graft buster announced an investigation into Yi Lianhong, a former Communist Party chief of Zhejiang province. Yet talk of Yi’s downfall had already circulated in regular chat groups for days.

One WeChat article said searches for Yi on the platform had risen sixfold two days earlier, following several previous surges since April 2025. The speculation was probably fueled by investigation of personnel seen as linked to him.

An official’s absence from political events is often the first sign of trouble. Months later, authorities typically announce a cadre is suspected of “serious violations of discipline and law,” offering no details while an investigation proceeds.

Probes involving party officials can be especially opaque. Party members are generally first handled inside the Communist Party’s disciplinary system. Cases involving suspected crimes may later be transferred to prosecutors, often after party sanctions have already been announced.

The paid chat rooms appear to go further by throwing a spotlight on officials before any public sign of their downfall, according to the report. Bloomberg News couldn’t gain access to such groups online or independently verify the reports.

Despite the surging number of disgraced officials, Xi last month touted the anti-corruption drive as evidence the party is capable of “eliminating viruses that erodes its health,” in a [speech](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-01/xi-positions-china-s-ruling-party-as-global-force-for-progress) that indicated the campaign will not stop.

The Fifth Plenum scheduled for October will focus on disciplinary issues, as it seeks to draw on the “valuable lessons” of the anti-corruption campaign, according to the readout of the Politburo’s July meeting. The closed-door meeting is expected to confirm the removal of senior officials from the party’s Central Committee and elevate alternate members to fill vacancies.

It will also offer clues about the qualities Xi will reward when selecting his next cohort in 2027 — with loyalty, discipline and the ability to carry out his priorities likely to loom large.

Such horse trading is only likely to fuel demand for the allegedly official leaks that the Banyuetan report rallied against.

For regulators, the coded nature of the posts presents a legal and practical challenge. Publishing an official’s publicly available resume is not itself illegal, nor does it necessarily constitute spreading a rumor or leaking a state secret. Police may also lack enough information about a confidential investigation to determine whether an online claim is false.

That leaves authorities struggling to suppress a market generated, in part, by the political system’s own secrecy.

“The more sensational the claim, the more attention it attracts,” said Thomas of Asia Society Policy Institute. “Sticking to the facts quickly becomes dull when so little is known about internal maneuvering in Beijing compared with democratic political systems.”
