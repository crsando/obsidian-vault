### 中文译文

## 日本议员在双边关系深冻之际访华

由日本跨党派议员组成的代表团于周一开始对中国进行为期四天的访问，标志着在去年日本首相高市早苗就台湾问题发表言论导致两国关系紧张后，双方朝着重新开展政治接触迈出试探性一步。

据一位熟悉此行安排的人士透露，该议员团预计将会见执政的共产党国际部门的高级官员以及行业代表。

两国议员之间的交流在修复政府间官方关系方面可能影响有限。

自高市去年11月发表言论暗示若北京入侵台湾，日本可能出动军队以来，日本与其最大贸易伙伴之间的摩擦一直居高不下。中国对此回应称日本对地区构成军事威胁，并建议本国公民不要前往日本。

在其他措施中，北京加强了对两用产品（即既有民用又有军用用途的物品）的出口管制。

此次访问的消息最初由日本当地媒体（包括日本电视台）援引多方消息来源报道。参与者包括执政的自民党的桥本岳和中立改革联盟（在野党）的伊佐进一。

桥本不属于高市内阁，他曾在6月访问北京参加供应链活动。

伊佐在X平台上发帖称，北京与东京之间的关系“处于1972年以来最糟糕的状态”，政府、商业、文化和青年交流“完全停滞”。

“这种状况持续下去绝不符合日本或中国的国家利益，”他写道。“在与国会同僚的讨论中，我们得出的结论是，无论如何，两国之间需要对话的窗口，因此我们决定向中方提议访华，以推进意见交流。”

伊萨的帖子证实此次访问是国会议员主动提出，并非如最初报道（包括日本电视台NTV）所称，是应中共中央对外联络部邀请。

“我不认为该代表团能实质改善双边关系，但毫无疑问，这是一个积极信号，表明中国体制内的官员正在谨慎探索缓和路径，”欧亚集团中国与东北亚高级分析师杰里米·陈表示。

“这也可能是北京方面默认其针对东京的施压行动适得其反，而高市早苗至少还将留任一年，”他补充道。

日方多次试图与中国重新接触，但要么难以推进，要么被北京方面淡化处理。

今年1月，一个知名的日本高管团体最终[搁置](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-31/japan-ceos-halt-trip-to-china-as-diplomatic-rift-hits-business)了原定的访华计划，这是13年多来政治僵局首次干扰该团体旨在促进经济交流的年度访问。

上月，日本最高外交官茂木敏充[表示](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/china-japan-top-diplomats-hold-first-talks-since-takaichi-row)，他在马尼拉举行的东南亚国家联盟会议间隙与中国外长偶遇。中国外交部发言人林剑随后表示，中国在峰会期间与日方官员“没有安排”会晤。

今年5月，日本贸易大臣赤�的良成[表示](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-23/japan-s-akazawa-has-brief-talk-with-china-commerce-minister-wang)，他在苏州举行的地区贸易部长会议间隙与中国商务部长王文涛进行了简短交谈，但中方官员后来否认了这一交流。

由日本国际贸易促进协会组织的日本代表团原定于六月访华，因该协会主席、前日本外相河野洋平去世而被推迟。此后，据共同社（7月8日）报道，日本贸易团体将于九月访问中国，行程定于九月下半月。

不过，对于双边关系可能出现一线希望的是，日本驻中国西南部大城市重庆的外交机构在紧张局势加剧后约八个月没有总领事，最终由西海繁弘于8月4日就任该职位。

计划于11月在深圳举行的亚太经合组织领导人峰会也可能提供重置关系的机会，尤其是如果习近平与高市早苗举行双边会晤的话。

7月，中国日本商会会长本间哲郎表示，亚太经合组织峰会也可以成为恢复商业交流的机会。

“虽然我不能确定交流的恢复，但我想推进一切可能的步骤，”本间当时对记者说，日本在华企业将亚太经合组织峰会视为缓和紧张关系的机会。

## Japan Lawmakers Visit China Amid Deep Freeze in Bilateral Ties

A bipartisan delegation of Japanese parliamentarians kicks off a four-day visit to China on Monday, marking a tentative step toward renewed political engagement after ties were strained by Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s remarks over Taiwan last year.

The group of lawmakers is expected to meet high-ranking officials from the ruling Communist Party’s International Department as well as industry representatives, according to a person familiar with the trip’s planning.

The exchange between lawmakers in each nation may only have a limited impact in terms of repairing the official relationship between the governments.

Friction has run high between Japan and its biggest trading partner since Takaichi’s November remarks implying that Japan could dispatch its military in the event Beijing invaded Taiwan. China responded by accusing Japan of posing a military threat to the region and advised its citizens not to travel there.

Among other measures, Beijing bolstered export controls on dual-use products—items with both civilian and military applications.

News of the visit was first [reported](https://news.ntv.co.jp/category/international/3c3347d35cba425d9599f89f5a5b3fbb) by local media in Japan including broadcaster Nippon TV, citing multiple sources. Participants include Gaku Hashimoto of the ruling Liberal Democratic Party and Shinichi Isa of the Centrist Reform Alliance, an opposition party.

Hashimoto, who is not part of Takaichi’s cabinet, also visited Beijing to attend a supply chain event in June.

In a post on X, Isa [described](https://x.com/isashinichi/status/2089989611955311081?s=20) relations between Beijing and Tokyo as being “in the worst state since 1972,” with government, business, cultural and youth exchanges coming “to a complete halt.”

“This situation continuing is by no means in the national interest of either Japan or China,” he wrote. “In discussions with fellow Diet members, we came to the conclusion that, whatever the case, a window for dialogue between the two countries is necessary, and we decided to propose to China that we visit to advance exchanges of views.”

Isa’s post confirmed the trip was an initiative by the Diet members and wasn’t at the invitation of the CPC’s International Department, as had been originally reported, including by NTV.

“I don’t think the delegation will meaningfully improve bilateral ties, but it’s undoubtedly a positive sign that officials within the Chinese system are gently exploring de-escalatory pathways,” said Jeremy Chan, a senior analyst for China and Northeast Asia at the ⁠Eurasia Group.

“It could also be a tacit admission by Beijing that its pressure campaign against Tokyo has backfired and Takaichi will likely remain in office for another year at least,” he added.

Several attempts by the Japanese side to re-engage with China, have encountered difficulty proceeding or have been played down by Beijing.

In January, a prominent group of Japanese executives ended up [putting on hold](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-31/japan-ceos-halt-trip-to-china-as-diplomatic-rift-hits-business) a planned visit to Beijing, the first time in more than 13 years that a political standoff disrupted that group’s annual mission to promote economic exchange.

Last month, Japan’s top diplomat Toshimitsu Motegi [said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/china-japan-top-diplomats-hold-first-talks-since-takaichi-row) he had a chance encounter with his Chinese counterpart on the sidelines of a gathering of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations in Manila. The Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman Lin Jian subsequently said that China had “no arrangements” with Japanese officials at the summit.

In May, Japanese Trade Minister Ryosei Akazawa [said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-23/japan-s-akazawa-has-brief-talk-with-china-commerce-minister-wang) he’d had a brief chat with Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao on the sidelines of a regional meeting of trade chiefs in Suzhou, an exchange Chinese officials later denied took place.

A visit to China by a Japanese delegation organized by Japan’s Association for the Promotion of International Trade, originally scheduled for June, was postponed due to the death of organization chairman and former Japanese Foreign Minister Yohei Kono. It’s since been Japan Trade Group to Visit China in September: Kyodo (July 8) for the second half of September.

Still, in a possible glimmer of hope for the bilateral relationship, Japan’s diplomatic mission in the southwestern Chinese megacity of Chongqing, which had been left without a consul-general for around eight months as tensions flared, finally filled the post when Shigehiro Nishiumi took it up on Aug. 4.

The APEC leaders’ summit scheduled to be held in Shenzhen in November may also offer a chance at resetting ties, especially if a bilateral meeting between Xi and Takaichi takes place.

In July, Tetsuro Homma, chairman of the Japanese Chamber of Commerce and Industry in China, said the APEC summit could also be an opportunity to restore business exchanges.
