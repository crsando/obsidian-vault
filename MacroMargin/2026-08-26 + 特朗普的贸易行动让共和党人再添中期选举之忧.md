### 中文译文

## 特朗普的贸易行动让共和党人再添中期选举之忧

唐纳德·特朗普总统最新的贸易动荡可能加剧共和党在11月中期选举中的危险，在共和党努力保住多数席位之际，给参议院共和党候选人带来了又一个经济难题。

近日，特朗普宣布对部分碎牛肉进口实施关税减免的计划，激怒了艾奥瓦等州的牧场主，并对加拿大发起新的贸易战，令缅因、密歇根、阿拉斯加和俄亥俄等边境州的制造商和企业感到不安。这些州都是参议院战场州，对民主党夺回参议院的战略至关重要。

与此同时，据彭博新闻社分析，加拿大的反制关税对特朗普在2024年大选中赢得的州的影响，是对其民主党对手卡玛拉·哈里斯所赢得的州的影响的两倍，这在一个预计将是对总统及其国家经济管理能力的公投的中期选举周期中，增添了另一层经济痛苦。

![A tractor trailer crosses the Peace Bridge at the Canada-United States border in Fort Erie, Ontario, Canada, on Aug. 20. Photographer: Cole Burston/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b148106bfb6ecacec3bbd2c/3c881cc9-0b14-8125-b389-f229d6a23b91-c4a57e7af87125da03134314e6d62303.png)

“距离选举日只剩10周，这种紧张关系对他们来说就是问题所在，”共和党策略师凯文·马登表示。“我认为最重要的是，在选举前10周，你希望政党围绕一个信息团结起来。”

特朗普竞选时承诺改善生活成本，但在特朗普的贸易战以及他与伊朗的长期冲突中，选民几乎没有看到缓解。民调显示，特朗普的支持率处于历史最低点。

一位了解中期竞选活动的共和党人，因直言不讳而要求匿名，** **表示，特朗普的新贸易举措存在问题，也是总统采取行动使共和党候选人在势均力敌的竞选中更难竞争的最新例子。此人还提到了中东战争和白宫舞厅的建造。

白宫没有回应置评请求。

这些担忧似乎[没有起到什么作用](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/us-weighs-more-trade-measures-against-canada-after-retaliation)来阻止总统。据一位要求匿名的白宫官员透露，政府官员正在讨论对加拿大实施额外贸易惩罚，以报复其反制关税，包括新的征税或其他行动。该官员要求匿名以讨论私下商议内容。

民主党长期以来一直有望赢得众议院多数席位，但如今人们越来越乐观地认为参议院——民主党需要在那里拿下四个席位，包括在亲特朗普的州——也在争夺之中。控制参议院将使民主党有权否决特朗普的行政和司法提名，此外还能反对他的立法提案并调查总统及其盟友。

特朗普为其行动辩护，称对牛肉的关税减免是短期解决方案，并指责加拿大缺乏谈判诚意。在周五贸易谈判破裂后，他与加拿大的冲突升级。双方都施加了新关税，美国[推进](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/us-canada-trade-talks-collapse-triggering-trump-s-50-tariffs)对价值数十亿美元的加拿大商品加征50%的关税，加拿大则[加倍](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/canada-s-carney-matches-trump-tariffs-with-50-tax-on-us-steel-furniture-dairy)其现有反制关税，将美国钢铁和铝产品的关税提高至50%，并对其他美国产品征收新关税。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b148106bfb6ecacec3bbd2c/3c881cc9-0b14-81e2-86bb-d739786c07c3-f329a8a9efce165036f50361bff51177.png)

[据](https://businessdatalab.ca/canada-u-s-trade-tracker/)加拿大商会称，俄亥俄州和密歇根州是加拿大在美国的前五大贸易伙伴中的两个，这两地正在进行两场最激烈的参议院竞选。

民主党可能需要保住密歇根州的参议院席位才能赢得参议院控制权。拿下目前由共和党人乔恩·赫斯特德（Jon Husted）把持的俄亥俄州席位，将是该党在一个近年来一贯偏向保守的州取得的重大胜利。

这些州的商界人士表示，如果这些关税生效，他们将受到影响。

特朗普誓言从明年1月1日起将加拿大汽车关税[提高一倍](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/trump-vows-to-double-tariffs-on-canadian-autos-escalating-fight)，这将震动两国深度融合的汽车产业，因为密歇根州汽车工厂使用的加拿大零部件可能会变得更加昂贵。

作为特朗普的粉丝，密歇根州的迈克尔·内罗斯（Michael Neros）却希望总统缩减部分海外行动，重新聚焦美国。内罗斯经营着一家销售公司，代表家具制造商面向零售商，他表示还没有听说关税对他所在行业产生重大影响，但有点担心关税对该州汽车行业的影响。

“他试图让美国优先。他必须在未来30天内解决这个问题，并在我们进入中期选举之际努力找到更好的方向，”内罗斯在接受采访时说。“就关税而言，就这场海湾战争而言，有几个关键问题需要解决。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b148106bfb6ecacec3bbd2c/3c881cc9-0b14-81f1-b2ad-ee8443b49126-844248323abbecdec2e491bfcfdb2424.png)

房屋建筑行业也可能受到冲击，这可能会加剧那些本已难以负担购房的美国人的沮丧情绪。

“如果突然间传来正经新闻说我们要对木材再加征50%关税，天哪，那会让房主、建筑商和开发商很快就停摆。就是放慢脚步、踩刹车。”密歇根州东南部木材场运营商Chelsea Lumber的共同所有人约翰·丹尼尔斯（John Daniels）表示。

美国客户将在未来几周内决定是趁价格较低时从加拿大工厂大量进货，还是再等等。丹尼尔斯说，很少有买家能够“消化”关税带来的额外成本，因此最终他们可能不得不将这些成本转嫁给房屋建筑商。

缅因州波特兰国际咨询公司蒙特塞拉特集团（Montserrat Group）总裁珍妮·比萨永-卡里（Janine Bisaillon-Cary）表示，当缅因人寻求国际贸易时，加拿大人通常是第一批客户。主要的蓝莓种植商在边境两侧都有业务，而缅因州的海鲜公司则与加拿大进行进出口贸易。

“我想我从未见过缅因州的哪位政治人物说加拿大对我们不重要，”她说，“我会密切关注不同政治人物在关税问题上的立场，因为我认为关税可能对我们的州经济和国民经济非常不利。”

比萨永-卡里拒绝透露她可能在中期选举中投票给谁，但确实表示自己是中间派，最近投票倾向民主党。

民主党人对自己在参议院的机会越来越乐观，他们强调特朗普的行动是共和党人不关心美国民众优先事项的证据。

在密歇根州，民主党参议员候选人阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德（Abdul El-Sayed）在X平台上表示：“特朗普正为了自己的虚荣心升级与加拿大的贸易战。即使在这一轮之前，加拿大的关税就已经让密歇根家庭每年多支出3200美元。情况会变得更糟。”

他的对手、前众议员迈克·罗杰斯尚未发表评论。但一些参议院候选人在争取对生活成本和经营企业挑战感到不满的选民时，已公开反对特朗普的贸易举措。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b148106bfb6ecacec3bbd2c/3c881cc9-0b14-8172-a5a4-ddf26d221a1f-f90a1aa5923e93dca28c792937263db0.png)

缅因州的苏珊·柯林斯是唯一一位在特朗普2024年输掉的州寻求连任的共和党参议员，她谴责了与加拿大的贸易紧张局势，在社交媒体上发帖称这些紧张局势“导致缅因州企业面临更高的成本、风险和不确定性”。

据美国国际贸易管理局数据，2025年缅因州从加拿大进口了41亿美元的商品，向加拿大出口了13亿美元。根据彭博社的分析，该州是受加拿大反制关税影响最大的州。

阿拉斯加通常是共和党的堡垒，但在经济担忧中突然成为参议院选举的摇摆州，紧随其后。

爱荷华州众议员阿什利·欣森——总统的密切盟友，正在另一场民主党希望翻盘的竞选中竞选参议员——与特朗普的牛肉公告保持距离。

“我当然希望看到这些价格下降。我要养两个十几岁的男孩，”欣森在福克斯新闻的《周日早间期货》节目中说。“但我认为这不是实现这一目标的方式。我认为我们需要专注于帮助我们的牧场主扩大牛群。”

爱荷华州共和党主席杰夫·考夫曼表示，他相信牧场主和农村选民会支持特朗普，尽管他的贸易议程如此，因为他们理解特朗普想要实现的目标。

但他补充说，在11月中期选举之前，他“希望看到一些胜利，这样人们就能实际看到成果，而不是只听到纠正贸易失衡的说法。”

## Trump’s Trade Actions Give Republicans Another Midterm Worry

President Donald Trump’s latest trade upheaval is threatening to deepen Republicans’ peril in November’s midterm elections, saddling Senate GOP candidates with another economic headache as the party fights to hold onto its majority.

In recent days, Trump has announced plans for tariff relief on some ground beef imports, enraging ranchers in states such as Iowa, and launched a new trade war with Canada, rattling manufacturers and businesses in border states including Maine, Michigan, Alaska and Ohio. Those states are all Senate battlegrounds crucial to Democrats’ strategy to retake the chamber.

Canadian counter-tariffs, meanwhile, affect states Trump carried in the 2024 election twice as much as those won by his Democratic opponent, Kamala Harris, according to a Bloomberg News analysis, adding another level of economic pain in a midterm cycle expected to serve as a referendum on the president and his economic stewardship of the country.

“That kind of tension with only 10 weeks until election day is just problematic for them,” said Republican strategist Kevin Madden. “I think the main thing, 10 weeks before election, you want a party to be unified around a message.”

Trump ran on a promise to improve the cost of living, but voters have seen little relief amid Trump’s trade wars, as well as his extended clash with Iran. Polling suggests Trump’s approval is at an all-time low.

A Republican with insight on the midterm campaigns, who requested anonymity to speak candidly,** **said Trump’s new trade moves are problematic and the latest example of the president taking actions that make it harder for GOP candidates to compete in closely contested races. The person also cited the Middle East war and the construction of a White House ballroom.

The White House did not respond to a request for comment.

Those concerns have appeared [to do little](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/us-weighs-more-trade-measures-against-canada-after-retaliation) to deter the president. Administration officials are discussing additional trade penalties against Canada in retaliation to its counter tariffs, including new levies or other actions, according to a White House official who requested anonymity to discuss private deliberations.

Democrats have long been favored to win the House majority, but there’s increased optimism the Senate — where the party needs to pick up four seats, including in Trump-friendly states — is in play. Senate control would give Democrats the power to reject Trump’s executive and judicial nominees, in addition to opposing his legislative proposals and investigating the president and his allies.

Trump has defended his actions, casting tariff relief for beef as a short-term fix and accusing Canada of negotiating in bad faith. His clash with Canada escalated after trade talks collapsed on Friday. Both sides slapped on new tariffs, with the US [moving forward](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/us-canada-trade-talks-collapse-triggering-trump-s-50-tariffs) with 50% duties on billions of dollars of Canadian goods and Canada [doubling](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/canada-s-carney-matches-trump-tariffs-with-50-tax-on-us-steel-furniture-dairy) its existing counter-tariffs on US steel and aluminum products to 50% and applying new duties to other US products.

Ohio and Michigan, where two of the fiercest Senate campaigns are playing out, are among Canada’s top five American trading partners, [according to](https://businessdatalab.ca/canada-u-s-trade-tracker/) the Canadian Chamber of Commerce.

Democrats likely need to hold on to the Michigan Senate seat to win Senate control. Picking up the Ohio seat, now held by Republican Jon Husted, would be a major victory for the party in a state that has consistently skewed more conservative in recent elections.

Business owners in those states said they would feel an impact if these tariffs take hold.

Trump’s vow to [double](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/trump-vows-to-double-tariffs-on-canadian-autos-escalating-fight) Canadian auto tariffs starting Jan. 1 would rattle the nations’ deeply integrated automotive industries, as Canadian parts used in Michigan auto plants could become more expensive.

A fan of Trump, Michael Neros of Michigan, nonetheless would like to see the president scale back some of his efforts abroad and refocus on the US. Neros, who operates a sales firm that represents furniture manufacturers to retailers, said he isn’t hearing of major tariff effects on his industry but is a little concerned about their impact on his state’s auto sector.

“He is trying to keep America first. He has to wrap this up over the next 30 days and try to get a better direction as we head into midterms,” Neros said in an interview. “In terms of tariffs, in terms of this Gulf war, there are a couple pivotal points here that need to be addressed.”

The homebuilding industry could also see a hit, which could add to the frustration of Americans already struggling to afford home purchases.

“If all of a sudden, here comes legit news that we have another 50% tariff on lumber, man, that’ll shut things down pretty quickly in terms of homeowners, and builders, developers. Just slowing down and putting the brakes on,” said John Daniels, co-owner of Chelsea Lumber, a lumberyard operator in southeastern Michigan.

US customers will have to decide in the next few weeks whether to load up at the Canadian mills to take advantage of lower prices, or hold off. Few buyers are in a position to “eat” the added costs from tariffs, Daniels said, so ultimately they would have to pass the costs on to homebuilders.

Janine Bisaillon-Cary, president of the Montserrat Group, an international consulting firm in Portland, Maine, said Canadians are usually the first customers when Mainers look to trade internationally. Major blueberry growers have operations on both sides of the border, while Maine seafood companies import and export to Canada.

“I don’t think I have ever seen a politician in the state of Maine saying that Canada wasn’t important to us,” she said. “I will watch carefully what different politicians’ positions are on tariffs, because I think they can be very detrimental to our state and national economy.”

Bisaillon-Cary declined to say who she might vote for in the midterms, but did say she’s a centrist whose voting has leaned Democrat recently.

Democrats, who are increasingly bullish about their chances in the Senate, are highlighting Trump’s efforts as evidence Republicans are not in touch with Americans’ priorities.

In Michigan, Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed said on X: “Trump is escalating a trade war with Canada for his own vanity. Even before this new round, Canadian tariffs were costing Michigan families an additional $3,200 a year. It’s going to get much worse.”

His opponent, former Representative Mike Rogers, has not commented. But some Senate candidates have spoken against Trump’s trade moves as they try to appeal to voters frustrated over the cost of living and challenges of running a business.

Susan Collins of Maine, the only Senate Republican up for reelection in a state Trump lost in 2024, decried the trade tensions with Canada, posting on social media that they “lead to higher costs, risk, and uncertainty for Maine businesses.”

In Maine, $4.1 billion in goods from Canada were imported in 2025, according to data from the US International Trade Administration, and $1.3 billion was exported to Canada. It’s the state most vulnerable to Canada’s counter-tariffs, according to Bloomberg’s analysis.

Alaska, typically a Republican stronghold that has suddenly become a toss-up Senate battle amid economic worries, is next.

Iowa Representative Ashley Hinson — a close ally of the president running for Senate in another race Democrats hope to flip — distanced herself from Trump’s beef announcement.

“I absolutely want to see those prices come down. I’m feeding two teenage boys,” Hinson said on Fox News’ *Sunday Morning Futures*. “But I think this isn’t the way to do that. I think we need to focus on helping our cattlemen grow their herd.”

Iowa Republican Party Chairman Jeff Kaufmann said he believed ranchers and rural voters would stick with Trump despite his trade agenda because they understand what Trump is trying to accomplish.

But he added that, ahead of November’s midterms, he “would like to see a couple of victories, so that instead of hearing about correcting trade disparities people can actually see it.”
