### 中文译文

## 中国警告美国：若对伊朗制裁扩大，将采取报复措施

![Further serious US sanctions on China, which buys 90% of Iran’s oil, would risk exploding Washington’s relationship with Beijing © AP](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A6a1ebb2c-83f2-445a-acbd-fdec1b0d2ab0?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

美国若对中国（购买伊朗90%石油的国家）实施进一步严厉制裁，可能令华盛顿与北京的关系面临破裂风险 © AP

北京方面已警告美国，如果中国企业被纳入特朗普政府与伊朗相关的新一轮二级制裁的任何重大扩展，中国将采取报复措施。

美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特周一公布的[措施](https://www.ft.com/content/67c74e64-5c37-4a5d-ae6c-d3c002f99628?syn-25a6b1a6=1)针对总部位于香港和中国内地的公司，但未将中国大型金融机构列入清单。

美国对中国（购买伊朗90%石油）的制裁若显著增加，可能会在唐纳德·特朗普总统和习近平主席计划在华盛顿会晤仅一个月前，使两国[关系](https://www.ft.com/us-china-relations)面临破裂风险。

此次峰会被视为对世界经济至关重要，预计两位领导人将讨论延长去年10月达成的贸易战休战协议，该协议为期一年，并经特朗普5月对北京的友好访问而得到巩固。

“中国将采取一切必要措施，坚定维护自身权益。”中国外交部发言人周二在回答关于美国对伊朗二级制裁的问题时表示。

“中方已多次表明坚决反对没有国际法依据或联合国安理会授权的非法单边制裁。”该发言人表示。

“当务之急是推动局势降温，尽快恢复对话与谈判。”该发言人补充道。

尽管中国大型国有炼油企业基本遵守对伊朗原油的制裁，但北京允许被称为“茶壶”的私营部门炼油企业从伊朗进口并加工石油。

美国今年早些时候对其中一些炼油企业实施了制裁，但中国命令国内企业不要遵守。

中国建立了一套日益强硬的反制裁机制，包括对协助外国限制中国公司和供应链的外国个人和组织采取反制措施。

美国试图将针对伊朗的二级制裁制度激进地扩大到中国公司，这将面临北京的报复风险。去年中国就曾通过控制对高科技生产至关重要的关键矿产，使美国制造业陷入停顿。

“我们拭目以待，看这只是虚张声势，还是（美国）真的在考虑对中国实施二级制裁，”北京大学资深学者王栋表示。

“如果他们这样做，那么中国当然会坚决捍卫自己的国家利益……去年第一轮时我们就这样做了，”他补充道。

他说，华盛顿正试图“将责任推给他人，并将其自身失败归咎于中国”。

但南京大学国际关系专家朱锋表示，在幕后，中国对鼓励美伊冲突中的交战各方寻求解决方案深感兴趣。

他指出，由于冲突，霍尔木兹海峡这一关键航道的航运持续受限，正在给全球整体经济带来压力。

“从中国的角度来看，我们不想卷入美伊争斗，”他说。“另一方面，我们也非常清楚，如果霍尔木兹海峡的困境持续下去，对中国也会更加危险。中国的主要石油进口来自中东，不是来自伊朗，而是来自沙特阿拉伯和伊拉克。”

近日，习近平会见了约旦国王阿卜杜拉二世，外交部长王毅会见了科威特外长，两次会谈中都讨论了伊朗和加沙战争问题。

“所以中国给德黑兰的信息非常明确——我们希望和平谈判继续下去，”朱说。

## China warns US it could retaliate over Iran sanctions

Further serious US sanctions on China, which buys 90% of Iran’s oil, would risk exploding Washington’s relationship with Beijing © AP

Beijing has warned the US that it will retaliate if Chinese companies are included in any significant expansion of the Trump administration’s new secondary sanctions relating to Iran.

The measures [unveiled on Monday](https://www.ft.com/content/67c74e64-5c37-4a5d-ae6c-d3c002f99628?syn-25a6b1a6=1) by US Treasury secretary Scott Bessent targeted companies based in Hong Kong and mainland China but stopped short of listing large Chinese financial institutions.

Any serious increase in US sanctions on China, which buys 90 per cent of Iran’s oil, would risk exploding the [relationship](https://www.ft.com/us-china-relations) only a month before presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping are scheduled to meet in Washington.

The summit is seen as crucial to the world economy, with the leaders expected to discuss extending a one-year truce in their trade war that they agreed in October and which was cemented by a cordial visit to Beijing by Trump in May.

“China will take all necessary measures to firmly safeguard its rights and interests,” a Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson said on Tuesday in response to questions about US secondary sanctions on Iran.

“China has made clear on many occasions its firm opposition to illicit unilateral sanctions that have no basis in international law or the authorisation of the UN Security Council,” they said.

“The pressing task is to facilitate de-escalation of the situation and return to dialogue and negotiation as soon as possible,” they added.

While China’s large state-owned refiners largely comply with sanctions on Iranian crude, Beijing allows private sector refiners known as “teapots” to import and process oil from the country.

The US imposed sanctions on some of these refiners earlier this year but China ordered domestic companies not to comply.

China has set up an increasingly strident anti-sanctions regime that includes countermeasures against foreign individuals and organisations that assist with foreign restrictions on Chinese companies and supply chains.

Any attempt by the US to aggressively expand a secondary sanctions regime on Iran to Chinese companies would risk retaliation from Beijing, which last year showed that it could bring American manufacturing to a standstill through its control of critical minerals essential to high-tech production.

“We have to wait and see whether this is just bluffing or [the US is] seriously thinking about putting secondary sanctions on China,” said Wang Dong, a senior scholar at Peking University.

“If they do, then of course China will absolutely defend its national interest . . . we already did that during round one last year,” he added.

He said Washington was trying to “shift responsibility on to others and blame China for its own failure”.

But Zhu Feng, an international relations expert at Nanjing University, said that behind the scenes, China was deeply interested in encouraging the warring parties in the US-Iran conflict to find a solution.

He noted that the continued restrictions on shipping through the crucial Strait of Hormuz due to the conflict are straining the entire global economy.

“From a Chinese perspective, we don’t want to get involved in a US-Iranian fight,” he said. “On the other hand, we are also very highly aware that if the Strait of Hormuz dilemma continues, it will also be more dangerous for China. China’s main oil imports come from the Middle East, not from Iran but from Saudi Arabia and Iraq.”

In recent days, Xi has met King Abdullah II of Jordan while foreign minister Wang Yi has met his Kuwaiti counterpart, with Iran and the war in Gaza discussed in both meetings.

“So China’s message to Tehran is very clear — we hope the peace talks will continue,” said Zhu.
