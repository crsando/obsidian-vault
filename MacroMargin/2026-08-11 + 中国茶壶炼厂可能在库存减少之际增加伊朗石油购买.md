### 中文译文

## 中国茶壶炼厂可能在库存减少之际增加伊朗石油购买

山东省的储油罐。

*摄影：法新社/盖蒂图片社*

### **彭博AI要点**

- 山东省中国独立炼油商的地区石油库存减少，可能促使增加对伊朗原油的购买。
- 根据Energy Aspects汇编的数据，山东省库存7月降至3.6亿桶，为八个月来最低水平。
- Energy Aspects估计，上月山东省库存减少了3500万桶，这是该咨询公司自2016年开始编制该数据以来最大的月度降幅。
山东省中国独立炼油商持有的地区石油库存减少，可能促使增加对[伊朗原油](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/iranian-oil-hoard-swells-off-malaysia-on-weak-chinese-demand)的购买，在经历一段销售疲软期后，为德黑兰提供所需的提振。

根据Energy Aspects汇编的数据，山东省——中国多数所谓茶壶炼油厂的所在地——7月库存降至3.6亿桶，为八个月来最低水平。该地区的库存由民营和国有加工企业共同持有。

在北京指示它们最大化[燃料生产](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-02/china-tells-private-refiners-to-keep-up-fuel-output-at-all-costs)以帮助缓冲伊朗战争影响后，中国茶壶炼油厂减少了采购，并严重依赖库存。这些炼油厂是迄今为止伊朗原油的最大买家，通常约占销售量的90%，而需求疲软导致该国石油在海上油轮上的库存不断膨胀。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b981cc90b1481619e75f0609fa9c110/3b981cc9-0b14-8161-9404-c40fff0f8965-4248d3aeb80667616e903dd6b4bf2188.png)

Energy Aspects估计，上月山东库存减少了3500万桶，这是该咨询公司自2016年开始编制数据以来的最大月度减少。与此同时，据Kpler估计，截至8月6日，中国全国的库存仍处于12.08亿桶的强劲水平，该估计包括地下战略储备基地。

中国不公布库存数据，第三方提供商的估算往往存在差异。

中国茶壶炼油厂利润率极薄，偏爱伊朗和俄罗斯的折扣原油。与国有炼油厂不同，它们需要政府发放的进口配额，北京在要求提高燃料产量后批准了额外配额。据JLC称，中国因供应过剩和利润率问题而放宽茶壶炼油厂产量要求的政策于6月放宽，当时国内[燃料](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/china-seen-lifting-some-fuel-export-curbs-on-ample-supplies)库存膨胀，加工企业面临不断增加的亏损。

Vortexa Ltd.首席中国分析师Emma Li表示：“我们预计8月份受制裁原油在中国的市场份额将上升，伊朗原油进口预计将反弹。”该咨询公司估计，7月份山东茶壶炼油厂持有的原油库存减少了约2000万桶。

中国去年积极建立[原油库存](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/china-s-oil-imports-may-be-set-to-recover-as-stockpiling-returns)，在伊朗战争扰乱中东供应后为炼油厂提供了缓冲。动用这些库存也减轻了全球市场的一些需求压力，这也是油价没有大幅飙升的原因之一。

据Mysteel OilChem汇编的数据，山东炼油厂自7月初以来提高了开工率，截至8月7日利用率升至50.26%。检修结束提高了开工率，但仍低于季节性正常水平。据JLC称，利润率已改善至3月以来的最强水平，此前自4月底以来大多为负值。

据Kpler称，目前在亚洲水域的油轮上约有3000万桶伊朗原油，这可能有助于补充茶壶炼油厂的库存。据交易员称，本月早些时候，9月交货的伊朗轻质原油报价比ICE布伦特原油每桶折价约4美元，较约5美元的折价收窄。俄罗斯ESPO原油最近报价为较布伦特原油折价约1美元，达到与该基准平价的水平。

## China’s Teapots May Boost Iran Oil Buying as Stockpiles Dwindle

Storage tanks in Shandong province.

*Photographer: AFP/Getty Images*

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- Shrinking regional oil stockpiles held by China’s independent refiners in Shandong province may spur increased buying of Iranian crude.
- Inventories in Shandong dropped to 360 million barrels in July, the lowest level in eight months, according to data compiled by Energy Aspects.
- Energy Aspects estimates stockpiles in Shandong declined by 35 million barrels last month, the biggest monthly draw since the consultant started compiling the figures in 2016.
Shrinking regional oil stockpiles held by China’s independent refiners in Shandong province may spur increased buying of [Iranian crude](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/iranian-oil-hoard-swells-off-malaysia-on-weak-chinese-demand), offering Tehran a needed boost after a period of slow sales.

Inventories in Shandong — home to most of China’s so-called teapot refiners — dropped to 360 million barrels in July, the lowest level in eight months, according to data compiled by Energy Aspects. Stockpiles in the region are held by both private as well as state-run processors.

China’s teapots dialed back purchases and relied heavily on stockpiles after Beijing instructed them to maximize [fuel production](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-02/china-tells-private-refiners-to-keep-up-fuel-output-at-all-costs) to help cushion the impact of the Iran war. The refiners are by far the biggest buyers of [Iranian crude](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/dozens-of-idling-iranian-oil-tankers-show-us-blockade-is-working), typically accounting for about 90% of sales, and softening demand led to a swelling hoard of the country’s oil on tankers at sea.

Energy Aspects estimates stockpiles in Shandong declined by 35 million barrels last month, the biggest monthly draw since the consultant started compiling the figures in 2016. Across China, meanwhile, the nation’s inventories still stands at a robust level of 1.208 billion barrels as of August 6, according to Kpler estimates which includes underground strategic reserves sites.

China doesn’t publish inventory data and estimates from third-party providers can often vary.

China’s teapots, which operate on razor-thin margins, favor discounted barrels from Iran and Russia. Unlike state refiners, they require government-issued import quotas, and Beijing granted additional allowances after ordering higher fuel production. The China to Ease Teapot Output Mandate Due to Surplus, Margins: JLC was eased in June as domestic [fuel](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/china-seen-lifting-some-fuel-export-curbs-on-ample-supplies) inventories swelled and processors grappled with mounting losses.

“We expect sanctioned crude to gain market share in China during August with Iranian crude imports expected to rebound,” said Emma Li, lead China analyst at Vortexa Ltd. The consultant estimates crude inventories held by teapots in Shandong fell by about 20 million barrels in July.

China aggressively built up its [crude stockpiles](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/china-s-oil-imports-may-be-set-to-recover-as-stockpiling-returns) last year, providing a buffer for refiners after the Iran war disrupted supplies from the Middle East. Tapping that inventory also took some demand pressure off the global market, and is one of the reasons why oil prices haven’t surged much higher.

Shandong refiners have ramped up operations since early July, with utilization rising to 50.26% as of Aug. 7, according to data compiled by Mysteel OilChem. The end of maintenance has boosted run rates, although they remain below seasonal norms. Margins have improved to their strongest since March after being mostly negative since late April, according to JLC.

There are close to 30 million barrels of Iranian crude currently on tankers in Asian waters, according to Kpler, which could help replenish teapot stockpiles. Iranian Light for delivery in September was offered at discounts of about $4 a barrel to ICE Brent earlier this month, narrowing from about $5, according to traders. Russian ESPO crude was recently offered at a discount of around $1 below Brent to parity with the benchmark.
