### 中文译文

## 消息人士称，中国芯片设计公司Kiwimoore计划赴港IPO，估值约20亿美元

2025年6月10日，中国香港，香港交易所（HKEX）的标志在其办公室内展出。路透社/Tyrone Siu/资料图片

北京/香港，8月12日（路透社）——据两位知情人士透露，中国芯片设计公司Kiwimoore已秘密提交香港首次公开发行（IPO）申请，目标估值约为20亿美元。

Kiwimoore未回应路透社的置评请求。

由于信息保密，消息人士拒绝透露姓名。

以下是详情：

- 消息人士称，Kiwimoore计划于2027年上半年在香港上市。
- 其中一位消息人士称，该公司在7月的一轮融资中筹集了约7亿元人民币（合1.0379亿美元），估值为80亿元人民币。
- 消息人士称，Kiwimoore已聘请中信证券和农银国际牵头其香港IPO。这两家银行未回应路透社的置评请求。
- 据公司官网介绍，成立于2021年初的上海Kiwimoore公司生产用于连接计算集群内人工智能加速器的网络技术。
- 在美国限制向中国出口包括芯片在内的先进技术后，该公司已将自身定位为利用北京推动建立自给自足的人工智能芯片供应链的契机。
($1 = 6.7446人民币元)

报道：唐子怡（北京）和吴凯恩（香港）；编辑：凯特·梅伯里

### English Original

## China chip designer Kiwimoore plans Hong Kong IPO at $2 billion valuation, sources say

The logo of Hong Kong Exchanges & Clearing (HKEX) is pictured in its office, in Hong Kong, China, June 10, 2025. REUTERS/Tyrone Siu/File Photo

BEIJING/HONG KONG, Aug 12 (Reuters) - Chinese chip designer Kiwimoore has confidentially filed for a Hong Kong initial public offering, targeting a valuation of about $2 billion, said two people with knowledge of the matter.

Kiwimoore did not respond to a Reuters request for comment.

The sources declined to be named because the information was confidential.

Here are the details:

- Kiwimoore plans to list in Hong Kong in the first half of 2027, the sources said.
- The company raised about 700 million yuan ($103.79 million) in a July funding round with a valuation of 8 billion yuan, said one of the people.
- Kiwimoore has tapped CITIC Securities and ABC International to lead its Hong Kong IPO, said the sources. The two banks did not respond to Reuters requests for comment.
- Founded in early 2021, Shanghai-based Kiwimoore makes networking technology used to link artificial-intelligence accelerators inside computing clusters, the company's website said.
- The company has positioned itself to capitalise on Beijing's push to build a self-sufficient AI chip supply chain after the U.S. restricted exports of advanced technologies, including chips, to China.
($1 = 6.7446 Chinese yuan renminbi)

Reporting by Ziyi Tang in Beijing and Kane Wu in Hong Kong; Editing by Kate Mayberry
