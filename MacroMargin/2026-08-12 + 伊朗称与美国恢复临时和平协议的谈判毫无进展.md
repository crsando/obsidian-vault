### 中文译文

## 伊朗称与美国恢复临时和平协议的谈判毫无进展

迪拜，8月12日（路透社）——据一位伊朗高级消息人士称，伊朗和美国在就海湾战争永久结束达成协议的努力上仍然僵持不下，该消息人士称，在恢复[六月达成的临时协议](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/g7-leaders-demand-ceasefire-lebanon-welcome-iran-deal-2026-06-17/)并确定实施时间表的谈判中毫无进展。

这些言论是对迅速解决危机希望的进一步打击，此前周二该地区航运遭受袭击，推高了油价。

六月达成的协议宣布“立即永久终止所有战线的军事行动”，但很快瓦解，美国总统唐纳德·特朗普于7月7日称协议“已结束”，伊朗外交部一周后宣布协议“暂停”。

美国指责伊朗未能履行协议中重新开放重要霍尔木兹海峡航运路线的承诺。德黑兰表示，华盛顿背弃了其承诺，包括解除对伊朗港口的封锁和释放被冻结的伊朗资产。

这位[伊朗消息人士](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-us-not-discussing-ceasefire-extension-senior-iranian-official-says-2026-08-12/)表示：“通过调解人讨论的问题之一是美国重返临时协议并确定履行承诺的时间表。在这个问题上绝对没有进展。”

华盛顿没有立即发表评论。

自2月28日美国和以色列对伊朗发动袭击以来，已有数千人在冲突中丧生，主要是在伊朗和黎巴嫩。

伊朗袭击了包括阿曼、约旦、科威特、以色列、阿拉伯联合酋长国和沙特阿拉伯在内的国家的美国资产和基础设施。

### 双方加大言辞攻势

6月的临时停火协议设定了60天的期限，经双方同意可延长。在此期间，伊朗和美国预计将达成一项最终协议，限制德黑兰的核计划并解除美国的制裁。

伊朗消息人士周三驳斥了土耳其阿纳多卢通讯社的一篇报道，该报道援引巴基斯坦政府消息人士的话称，双方已同意延长这60天的期限。

“没有讨论延长的问题，因为从伊朗的角度来看，并没有一个已经开始的期限，因此也就没有延长一说。美国在临时协议达成48小时后就违反了协议，几天后又退出了协议，”该消息人士对路透社表示。

过去两天，伊朗和美国都加强了言辞交锋。

伊朗最高安全官员[穆赫辛·雷扎伊](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/who-is-irans-mohsen-rezaei-hardliner-appointed-key-security-role-2026-08-10/)周二表示，除非美国接受伊朗结束[战争](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/)的条件，否则霍尔木兹海峡将继续关闭。

此前，特朗普周一提出新要求，称伊朗应为50年来战争、袭击和抗议中丧生的人支付赔偿。

在整个冲突期间，特朗普时而威胁升级，时而声称和平协议即将达成。

周二访问俄亥俄州后，[特朗普对记者表示](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/trump-says-his-plane-faced-greater-risk-flight-change-2026-08-12/)：“伊朗情况很好，绝对很好。”

“我们有一个国家，50年来一直是中东的恶霸，如果你细想的话，其实是51年……而他们不再是中东的恶霸了。”

### 针对航运的袭击

霍尔木兹海峡是伊朗和阿曼之间的一条狭窄水道，也是海湾的入口，战前承担了全球五分之一石油和液化天然气流量。

自2月战争爆发以来，基准布伦特原油期货价格飙升，触及每桶126美元的峰值，比战前水平高出约75%，波动不定的交易反映出市场对海湾石油供应中断以及和谈信号变化的担忧。

周二，另一起[针对航运的袭击](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/three-killed-attacks-ships-red-sea-gulf-sources-say-2026-08-11/)加剧了这些担忧。

在红海南端，周二，伊朗支持的胡塞武装在曼德海峡袭击一艘小型货船，造成四名船员死亡，这是自战争爆发以来该组织对航运发动的首次致命袭击。

与此同时，美国军方表示，一架美国海军MH-60直升机发射了两枚地狱火导弹，以瘫痪一艘悬挂巴拿马国旗货船的操舵装置。

美国中央司令部称，该船无视多次警告，继续侵犯伊朗港口的海上封锁。海事消息人士告诉路透社，该船在驶入阿曼湾时在巴基斯坦海岸附近被击中。

周三，布伦特原油期货上涨35美分，涨幅0.4%，至每桶89.26美元，有望连续第六天上涨。

报道：Parisa Hafezi、Tala Ramadan和Elwely Elwelly；撰稿：Alex Richardson；编辑：Timothy Heritage

## Iran says no progress on reviving interim peace deal with US

DUBAI, Aug 12 (Reuters) - Iran and the United States remain at loggerheads over efforts to agree a permanent end to the war in the Gulf, according to a senior Iranian source who said there had been no progress in talks to revive the [interim deal agreed in June](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/g7-leaders-demand-ceasefire-lebanon-welcome-iran-deal-2026-06-17/) and define a timeframe to implement it.

The comments were a further blow to hopes of a swift resolution to the crisis, following attacks on shipping in the region on Tuesday that pushed oil prices higher.

The deal agreed in June declared an "immediate and permanent termination of military operations on all fronts", but quickly unravelled, with U.S. President Donald Trump saying it was "over" on July 7 and Iran's foreign ministry declaring it "suspended" a week later.

The U.S. accuses Iran of failing to honour an agreement under the deal to reopen the vital Strait of Hormuz shipping route. Tehran says Washington has reneged on its commitments, including lifting a blockade of Iranian ports and releasing frozen Iranian assets.

"One of the issues that is being discussed via mediators is the U.S. returning to the interim agreement and defining a timeframe for implementing the commitments. There has been absolutely no progress on this issue," the [Iranian source](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-us-not-discussing-ceasefire-extension-senior-iranian-official-says-2026-08-12/) said.

There was no immediate comment from Washington.

Thousands of people have been killed in the conflict, mainly in Iran and Lebanon, since the U.S. and Israel launched attacks on Iran on February 28.

Iran has struck U.S. assets and infrastructure in countries including Oman, Jordan, Kuwait, Israel, the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia.

### BOTH SIDES STEP UP RHETORIC

The June interim ceasefire agreement set a 60-day period, extendable by mutual consent, within which Iran and the U.S. were expected to reach a final deal limiting Tehran's nuclear programme and lifting U.S. sanctions.

The Iranian source dismissed a report on Wednesday from Turkey's Anadolu news agency that quoted Pakistani government sources as saying they had agreed to extend that 60-day period.

"There is no talk of an extension because, from Iran's perspective, there is no period that began and therefore nothing to extend. The United States violated the interim agreement 48 hours after it was reached and withdrew from it a few days later," the source told Reuters.

Both Iran and the United States have stepped up rhetoric in the past two days.

Iran's top security official, [Mohsen Rezaei](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/who-is-irans-mohsen-rezaei-hardliner-appointed-key-security-role-2026-08-10/), said on Tuesday that the Strait of Hormuz would remain closed unless the U.S. accepted Iran's conditions to end [the war](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/).

That followed a new demand from Trump on Monday that Iran should pay compensation for people killed in 50 years of wars, attacks and protests.

Throughout the conflict, Trump has alternated between threats of escalation and claims that a peace deal is imminent.

[Speaking to reporters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/trump-says-his-plane-faced-greater-risk-flight-change-2026-08-12/) after a visit to Ohio on Tuesday, Trump said: "Iran is going fine, going just absolutely fine."

"We have a country that has been the bully of the Middle East for 50 years, really 51 if you think about it ... and they're no longer the bully of the Middle East."

### ATTACKS ON SHIPPING

The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman and the entrance to the Gulf, handled a fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas flow before the war.

Benchmark Brent crude futures have surged since the start of the war in February, hitting a peak of $126 a barrel, roughly 75% above pre-war levels, with choppy trade reflecting concerns over disruptions to Gulf oil supplies and shifting signals on peace talks.

Those concerns were compounded by separate [attacks on shipping](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/three-killed-attacks-ships-red-sea-gulf-sources-say-2026-08-11/) on Tuesday.

At the southern end of the Red Sea, four crew members were killed in an attack by Iran-backed Houthis on a small cargo ship in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on Tuesday, the first deadly strike on shipping by the group since the start of the war.

The U.S. military, meanwhile, said a U.S. Navy MH-60 helicopter fired two Hellfire missiles to disable the steering gear of a Panama-flagged cargo ship.

The ship ignored repeated warnings to stop violating a naval blockade on Iranian ports, the U.S. Central Command said. Maritime sources told Reuters the vessel was hit off Pakistan while sailing into the Gulf of Oman.

Brent futures rose 35 cents, or 0.4%, to $89.26 a barrel on Wednesday, heading for a sixth day of gains.
