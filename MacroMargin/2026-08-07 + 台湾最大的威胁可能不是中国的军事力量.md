### 中文译文

## 台湾最大的威胁可能不是中国的军事力量

面对中国的威胁。 *摄影师：Cheng Yu-Chen/AFP/Getty Images*

在海空领域，中国正在[稳步打击](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/policy-briefs/taiwans-evolving-response-chinas-grey-zone-actions)台湾的防御。但大陆的军事实力并非唯一的威胁。北京正针对台湾的[政治韧性](https://www.gisreportsonline.com/r/time-running-short-for-taiwan/)进行打击，试图让民众相信抵抗统一是徒劳的——远在任何导弹发射之前。

赖清德总统任期进入第二年，台湾正面临日益加剧的内部压力。他领导的民进党在分裂的议会中难以达成团结，国防预算被[推迟](https://asiasociety.org/policy-institute/national-security-without-consensus-taiwans-domestic-politics-through-2028)，政治内斗使得生活成本等选民优先事项被搁置。这一切都符合北京的叙事：民主是混乱的。

军事威慑在台北的生存算盘中至关重要。但仅靠无人机、战斗机和导弹无法保卫台湾来之不易的民主。周三，台湾启动了为期10天的年度[汉光](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-launches-major-war-games-test-command-flexibility-2026-08-05/)演习，并[首次](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3362965/how-taiwan-preparing-pla-sea-blockade-its-biggest-war-games)测试了应对[海上封锁](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3263763/lower-risks-harder-respond-why-beijing-may-prefer-blockade-conflict-taiwan?module=inline&pgtype=article)的能力。今年[最大规模的军事演习](https://blinks.bloomberg.com/news/stories/TJA1YCGQOFSW)涵盖了部队和预备役人员，也包括[平民](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/07/20/2003861071)将面临的移动网络速度和通信中断模拟情景。

来自北京的压力正在加剧。上个月，中国人民解放军在台湾海峡[进行了](https://www.newsweek.com/china-stages-military-drills-in-key-trade-waters-after-rubio-warning-12227056)为期两天的实弹演习，以提醒台北军事天平仍倾向于大陆。6月，中国派遣船只进行多天巡逻，[宣示](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-11/china-presses-taiwan-by-querying-foreign-ships-for-first-time)台湾东部海域的主张。官员首次盘问了该地区的外国商船，表明北京正在将管辖范围扩大到惯常边界之外。

中国的强制策略是强大的工具，但成本更低的选项是削弱台湾的自我治理能力。更难防御的是对公众信心的侵蚀。北京正在发动[信息](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/how-taiwan-fights-disinformation-war)战，影响选民并侵蚀对政府的信任。赖清德并未助益自己的事业。他在台湾主权问题上不妥协的言论激发了一些支持者，但也[加剧](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-14/china-rejects-lai-remarks-linking-democracy-with-sovereignty)了中国的反应，使得国内共识更加困难。

表面上，台湾仍是经济成功的故事。2026年上半年，经济增长速度创约50年来最快，[国内生产总值](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/taiwan-s-growth-tops-forecasts-even-as-ai-buildout-worries-mount)达13.72%。台湾科技实力的明珠——[台湾积体电路制造公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSM:US)——仍然是全球芯片产业不可或缺的一部分。

但这种繁荣并不保证安全。中国共产党正试图加强议会中[亲北京的声音](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2026/04/03/2003854927)。政治僵局阻碍了赖清德的执政能力，反对党国民党的立法委员阻挠预算并拖延关键举措。

这些宣传活动似乎正在奏效，年轻人尤其容易受到影响。一项[2025年研究](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/jun/06/frequent-tiktok-users-in-taiwan-more-likely-to-agree-with-pro-china-narratives-study-finds)发现，花大量时间在[TikTok](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/tiktok)上的台湾人更可能认同亲中叙事，例如统一不可避免或应为和平牺牲民主等观点。

这就是为什么建设[民间韧性](https://www.gisreportsonline.com/r/time-running-short-for-taiwan/)与军事威慑同样重要。台湾有理由增加国防开支、延长义务兵役期，并投入发展旨在威慑入侵的能力。但它需要进一步投资媒体素养，以反制外部影响力行动，并强化民众对民进党保障台湾人民安全能力的认知。

赖清德也可以借助日渐深化的台湾身份认同。虽然年轻人容易被中国宣传所动摇，但民调也显示，认同自己是台湾人而非中国人的人数更多——尤其是那些在民主环境中长大、对大陆几乎没有情感依恋的人。

中国知道它不必一夜之间赢得人心——它可以等待并打持久战。因此，台湾最大的挑战是确保这一代人仍然认为民主值得为之奋斗。

## Taiwan’s Biggest Threat May Not Be China’s Military

Facing China’s threat. *Photographer: Cheng Yu-Chen/AFP/Getty Images*

In the sea and skies, China is [steadily hammering](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/policy-briefs/taiwans-evolving-response-chinas-grey-zone-actions) away at Taiwan’s defenses. But the mainland’s military prowess isn’t the only threat. Beijing is targeting the island’s [political resilience](https://www.gisreportsonline.com/r/time-running-short-for-taiwan/) to convince citizens that resistance to unification is futile — long before a missile is ever fired.

Two years into President Lai Ching-te’s presidency, Taiwan is facing mounting internal strains. His Democratic Progressive Party is struggling to build unity in a divided parliament, defense budgets have been [delayed](https://asiasociety.org/policy-institute/national-security-without-consensus-taiwans-domestic-politics-through-2028), and political infighting has meant voter priorities such as the cost of living are taking a backseat. All of this plays into Beijing's narrative: Democracy is messy.

Military deterrence is essential in Taipei’s calculus for survival. But drones, fighter jets and missiles alone won’t defend the island’s hard-won democracy. On Wednesday, Taiwan launched the annual 10-day [Han Kuang](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-launches-major-war-games-test-command-flexibility-2026-08-05/) exercises and, [for the first time](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3362965/how-taiwan-preparing-pla-sea-blockade-its-biggest-war-games), tested its ability to counter a [maritime blockade](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3263763/lower-risks-harder-respond-why-beijing-may-prefer-blockade-conflict-taiwan?module=inline&pgtype=article). The [largest military drills](https://blinks.bloomberg.com/news/stories/TJA1YCGQOFSW) of the year include scenarios for troops and reservists, but also for [civilians who will](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/07/20/2003861071) face simulated disruptions to mobile internet speeds and communications.

The pressure from Beijing is intensifying. Last month, the People’s Liberation Army [conducted](https://www.newsweek.com/china-stages-military-drills-in-key-trade-waters-after-rubio-warning-12227056) two days of live-fire drills in the Taiwan Strait to remind Taipei that the military balance remains in its favor. In June, China sent ships on a multi-day patrol to [press claims](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-11/china-presses-taiwan-by-querying-foreign-ships-for-first-time) in the waters east of the island. Officials questioned foreign commercial boats in the area for the first time, a sign that Beijing is expanding its authority beyond its usual boundaries.

China’s coercive tactics are powerful tools, but a less costly option is to weaken the island’s ability to govern itself. It’s harder to defend against chipping away at public confidence. Beijing is waging an [information](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/how-taiwan-fights-disinformation-war) war to influence voters and erode trust in the government. Lai isn’t helping his cause. His uncompromising language on Taiwan's sovereignty has energized some supporters but also [hardened](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-14/china-rejects-lai-remarks-linking-democracy-with-sovereignty) China's response, making consensus at home even more challenging.

On the surface, Taiwan remains an economic success story. Growth expanded at the fastest pace in some 50 years in the first half of 2026, with [gross domestic product](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-31/taiwan-s-growth-tops-forecasts-even-as-ai-buildout-worries-mount) clocking in at 13.72%. The crown jewel behind the island’s technological prowess — [Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSM:US). — remains indispensable to the global chip industry.

But this prosperity doesn’t guarantee protection. The Chinese Communist Party is attempting to strengthen [pro-Beijing voices](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/editorials/archives/2026/04/03/2003854927) in the parliament. The political deadlock has hampered Lai’s ability to govern, with lawmakers from the opposition Kuomintang blocking budgets and delaying key initiatives.

The campaigns appear to be working, and youth are particularly vulnerable. A [2025 study](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2025/jun/06/frequent-tiktok-users-in-taiwan-more-likely-to-agree-with-pro-china-narratives-study-finds) found that Taiwanese people who spend large amounts of time on [TikTok](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/tiktok) are more likely to agree with pro-China narratives, such as the idea that unification is inevitable or that democracy should be sacrificed for peace.

This is why building [civilian resilience](https://www.gisreportsonline.com/r/time-running-short-for-taiwan/) is as important as military deterrence. Taiwan has rightly increased defense spending, extended compulsory military service and invested in capabilities designed to deter an invasion. But it needs to invest further in media literacy to counter foreign influence operations, and shore up perceptions of the DPP’s ability to keep Taiwanese people safe.

Lai could also lean into the deepening sense of Taiwanese identity. While the young may easily be swayed by Chinese propaganda, polls have also shown that more people identify as Taiwanese than Chinese — in particular those who have grown up with democracy, and have little emotional attachment to the mainland.

China knows it doesn’t have to win the confidence of citizens overnight — it can afford to wait and play the long game. Taiwan’s greatest challenge then, is to ensure that this generation thinks democracy is still worth fighting for.
