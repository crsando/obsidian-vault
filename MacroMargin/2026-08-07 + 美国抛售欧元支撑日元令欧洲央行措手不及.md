### 中文译文

## 美国抛售欧元支撑日元令欧洲央行措手不及

![The ECB was made aware of the US move to sell euros to buy yen on Friday after the trade had been executed © Kirill Kudryavtsev/AFP/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b581cc90b148174a00dca56318bd721/3b581cc9-0b14-8118-942a-e38951cd72e7-213aaa6d13ac760afed252595670474e.png)

欧洲央行在周五交易执行后才得知美国出售欧元购买日元的举措 © Kirill Kudryavtsev/AFP/Getty Images

华盛顿上周晚些时候出售欧元以提振日元的行动令欧洲央行措手不及，美国在这项历史性的货币干预之后才通知其法兰克福的同行。

据多名知情人士透露，欧洲央行在周五交易执行后才得知美国出售欧元买入日元的举措。

其中一名知情人士称，欧洲央行行长克里斯蒂娜·拉加德和美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特在周六讨论了这一干预行动。

缺乏协调凸显了近30年来首次联合[华盛顿-东京努力](https://www.ft.com/central-bank-intervention)以提振日元的异常性质。通常情况下，美国在这种行动中本应使用美元。

知情人士称，一些欧洲央行高级官员认为，美国决定在此次交易中使用欧元，是对西方货币当局之间长期合作惯例的前所未有的违背。

自第二次世界大战以来，西方中央银行和财政部一直强调互信与协商，以往货币市场的干预行动通常以协调方式进行。

一位熟悉欧洲政策制定者讨论的人士对英国《金融时报》表示，由纽约联邦储备银行代表美国财政部执行的华盛顿抛售欧元操作“非常引人注目”且“令人遗憾”。

“这种情况以前从未发生过，”他们补充道，并表示西方央行之间数十年的紧密合作可能已受到威胁，这种合作曾促进了金融稳定和经济增长。

美国财政部发言人告诉英国《金融时报》，美国财政部在动用外汇稳定基金进行干预时，并未与外国当局协调该基金内部储备配置的决定。

“有关外汇稳定基金配置的决定由美国财政部做出，同时考虑财政部和美联储对市场流动性、估值及其他相关因素的评估，”发言人表示。

“根据该授权，财政部上周在ESF内重新配置了储备资产，”发言人补充道。

特朗普政府的一名高级官员指出，“与欧洲央行不同，我们尊重与国际同行私下讨论的保密性。”

欧洲央行和纽约联邦储备银行拒绝置评。

美国当局抛售欧元，是因为抛售美元可能被视为削弱美国货币并破坏贝森特强势美元政策的举动。

经济学家和分析人士还猜测，华盛顿方面加入日本干预行动，是为了在日本长期借贷成本接近19年高点之际，阻止东京方面抛售美国国债。

分析师根据日本央行（Bank of Japan）的初步数据表示，仅日本一国可能就在两天内花费了约13.8万亿日元（合870亿美元）来支撑日元。

“日本似乎在仅两个交易日内投入的资金就已超过其此前[创纪录的干预行动](https://www.ft.com/content/62d340a5-0806-40c4-ab30-a13823a00983?syn-25a6b1a6=1) [4月和5月的干预规模为11.73万亿日元]，这凸显了当局对日元快速贬值的担忧，”瑞穗银行（Mizuho）分析师中岛正行（Masayuki Nakajima）表示。

美国和日本的干预推动日元兑美元汇率大幅走高，从本月早些时候的近1美元兑164日元——1986年以来的最低水平——升至约1美元兑157日元。此后日元已回落至1美元兑158日元。交易员警告称，人们仍然担心日本央行遏制通胀上升的行动不够快。

尽管日本央行在7月最近一次会议上维持利率不变，行长植田和男（Kazuo Ueda）表示：“我们认为，与之前相比，现在更有必要关注通胀上行风险。”

交易员认为，日本央行在9月下次会议上加息的可能性为50%。

日本采取这些举措之际，长期美国国债收益率大幅攀升，因交易员正在计入美联储可能不会从当前水平加息的风险上升。尽管美国6月通胀有所缓解，但仍远高于美联储自己设定的2%目标。

*戴维·基奥恩（David Keohane）在东京补充报道*

### English Original

## US euro sale to prop up yen blindsided ECB

The ECB was made aware of the US move to sell euros to buy yen on Friday after the trade had been executed © Kirill Kudryavtsev/AFP/Getty Images

Washington’s sale of euros to boost the yen late last week blindsided the European Central Bank, with the US only informing its counterparts in Frankfurt after the historic currency intervention.

The ECB was made aware of the US’s move to sell euros to buy yen on Friday after the trade had been executed, according to several people familiar with the matter.

ECB president Christine Lagarde and Treasury secretary Scott Bessent spoke about the intervention on Saturday, one of the people said.

The lack of co-ordination highlights the unusual nature of the first joint [Washington-Tokyo effort](https://www.ft.com/central-bank-intervention) to boost the yen in almost 30 years. Typically, the US would have been expected to use dollars in such an operation.

Some senior ECB officials viewed the US decision to use euros in its trade as an unprecedented breach of longstanding conventions on co-operation between western monetary authorities, the people said.

Since the second world war, western central banks and finance ministries have stressed mutual trust and consultation, with previous interventions in currency markets usually happening in a co-ordinated manner.

Washington’s sales of euros, carried out by the New York Federal Reserve on behalf of the US Treasury, were “very striking” and “sad”, one person familiar with discussions among European policymakers told the FT.

“This has never happened before,” they added, saying that decades of close co-operation between western central banks that fostered financial stability and economic growth may have come under threat.

A Treasury spokesperson told the FT that the US finance ministry did not co-ordinate decisions on the allocation of reserves within the department’s Exchange Stabilization Fund, which it used to undertake the intervention, with foreign authorities.

“Decisions regarding the allocation of the Exchange Stabilization Fund are made by the US Treasury, taking into account assessments by the Treasury and the Federal Reserve of market liquidity, valuations and other relevant considerations,” the spokesperson said.

“Consistent with that authority, the Treasury reallocated reserve assets within the ESF last week,” the spokesperson added.

A senior Trump administration official noted that “we respect the confidentiality of private discussions with our international counterparts, unlike the ECB”.

The ECB and New York Fed declined to comment.

The American authorities sold euros as sales of dollars could have been perceived as a move to weaken the US currency and undermine Bessent’s strong-dollar policy.

Economists and analysts have also speculated that Washington joined Japan in intervening to keep Tokyo from selling Treasuries at a time when long-term American borrowing costs are running near 19-year highs.

Analysts suggest, based on provisional data from the Bank of Japan, that Japan alone may have spent approximately ¥13.8tn ($87bn) over two days to support the yen.

“Japan appears to have committed more funds in just two trading days than during its previous [record intervention campaign](https://www.ft.com/content/62d340a5-0806-40c4-ab30-a13823a00983?syn-25a6b1a6=1) [of ¥11.73tn in April and May], underscoring the authorities’ concern over the rapid depreciation of the yen,” said Mizuho analyst Masayuki Nakajima.

The interventions by the US and Japan pushed the yen sharply higher from almost ¥164 to the dollar earlier this month, its lowest level since 1986, to about ¥157. It has since weakened to ¥158. Traders have warned that concerns linger that the BoJ is not moving quickly enough to contain rising inflation.

While the BoJ held interest rates steady at its most recent meeting in July, governor Kazuo Ueda said: “We believe there is a greater need than before to pay attention to upside risks to inflation.”

Traders are putting a 50 per cent chance that the BoJ will increase interest rates at its next meeting in September.

The moves in Japan have come alongside a sharp jump in long-dated Treasury yields, as traders have been pricing in an increased risk that the Fed may not raise interest rates from current levels. While inflation in the US eased in June, it remains far above the Fed’s own 2 per cent target.

*Additional reporting by David Keohane in Tokyo*
