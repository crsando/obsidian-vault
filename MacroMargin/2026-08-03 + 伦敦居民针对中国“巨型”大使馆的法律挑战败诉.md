### 中文译文

## 伦敦居民针对中国“巨型”大使馆的法律挑战败诉

![The Royal Mint Court complex, where the new Chinese embassy will be located © Dan Kitwood/Getty Images](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A0556f790-179a-4269-9636-137e07c5067f?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

新中国大使馆将坐落于皇家铸币局法院综合大楼 © Dan Kitwood/Getty Images

法官驳回了附近居民旨在阻止这一政治敏感项目建设的法律挑战后，中国在伦敦新建“巨型”大使馆的计划可以继续推进。

高等法院周五驳回了当地居民提起的诉讼，这些居民曾试图阻止该项目建设，部分理由是这一综合大楼可能被用作针对中国异见人士的基地。

英国官员今年[批准了中国的计划](https://www.ft.com/content/52c04197-4f6c-4a71-9c0f-82fc5eaecf24?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，即将在伦敦塔附近建造中国在欧洲最大的外交使团。

此举被视为试图改善伦敦与北京的关系。规划许可于1月获批，早于基尔·斯塔默爵士自2018年以来英国首相首次访华之行。

该项目反对者誓言尽管高等法院败诉仍要继续斗争。对华议会联盟执行主任卢克·德普尔福德表示，居民将寻求上诉。

中国政府于2018年购买了前皇家铸币局旧址上的5.4英亩土地，包括地块边缘四栋住宅楼中约100套公寓的永久产权。这些住宅楼位于拟建大使馆的周边范围之外。

居民们提出了一系列反对意见，认为拟建大使馆使他们面临潜在恐怖袭击的风险，并会因抗议活动造成干扰。

在本月的听证会上，居民律师还质疑了政府决定隐瞒平面图和炸弹风险相关信息。

但住房、社区和地方政府部（Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government）坚称，官员们在过程中已“认真且谨慎地”考虑了居民的关切。

政府在回应诉讼请求的辩护中还表示，与中国的“外交和贸易关系”在规划申请中“不构成实质性考量”。

利文法官（Mrs Justice Lieven）与丁格曼斯法官（Lord Justice Dingemans）共同审理，于周五裁定部长们行为合法。

利文法官称，居民们提出的一些论点基于“怀疑和猜测”。

她还表示，爆炸评估的核心结论已公开披露：“并未因这些文件未完整披露而对原告造成不公。”

法官还补充道，“毫无疑问”，政府已经考虑到申请规划许可的是中国政府。

但德普尔福德（de Pulford）表示：“北京认为这一判决是最终决定，但并非如此。”

他补充说：“如果他们指望我们屈服，允许我们自己的首都成为[中国共产党]的权力中心，那他们就完全误判了反对的力量。”

## London residents lose legal challenge against Chinese ‘mega’ embassy

The Royal Mint Court complex, where the new Chinese embassy will be located © Dan Kitwood/Getty Images

Plans for China’s new “mega” embassy in London can go ahead after judges threw out a legal challenge from nearby residents fighting to stop the politically sensitive development.

The High Court on Friday dismissed the case brought by locals who had sought to block its construction partly on the basis that the complex could be used as a base from which to target Chinese dissidents.

UK officials this year [approved China’s plans](https://www.ft.com/content/52c04197-4f6c-4a71-9c0f-82fc5eaecf24?syn-25a6b1a6=1) to build what is set to become the country’s largest diplomatic mission in Europe, near the Tower of London.

The move was seen as an attempt to improve London’s relations with Beijing. Planning permission was granted in January before Sir Keir Starmer made the first trip to China by a British prime minister since 2018.

Opponents of the project vowed to fight on despite the High Court defeat. Luke de Pulford, executive director of the Inter-Parliamentary Alliance on China, said the residents would seek to appeal.

The Chinese government bought 5.4 acres on the site of the former Royal Mint in 2018, including the freehold to about 100 flats across four residential blocks at the edge of the plot. The residential buildings lie outside the proposed perimeter for the embassy.

The residents raised a series of objections, arguing that the proposed embassy put them at risk of potential terrorist attacks and would result in disruption from protests.

At a hearing this month, lawyers for the residents also challenged the government’s decision to withhold information about floor plans and bomb risks.

But the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government insisted officials had considered the residents’ concerns “conscientiously and carefully” as part of the process.

In its defence to the claim, the government also said that “diplomatic and trading relations” with China were “not a material consideration” in the planning application.

Mrs Justice Lieven, sitting with Lord Justice Dingemans, found on Friday that ministers acted lawfully.

Lieven said some of the arguments advanced by residents were based on “suspicion and conjecture”.

She also said core findings of a blast assessment had been disclosed publicly: “There was no unfairness to the claimant by reason of these documents not having been disclosed” in full.

The judge added there was “no doubt” that the government had taken into account that it was the Chinese government that had applied for planning permission.

But de Pulford said: “Beijing thinks this judgment is the final word. It isn’t.”

He added: “If they expect us to roll over and allow our own capital to become a [Chinese Communist Party] power centre, they have completely misjudged the strength of opposition.”
