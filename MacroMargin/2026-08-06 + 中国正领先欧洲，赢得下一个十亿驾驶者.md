> 📌 **核心摘要**
>
> 中国电动汽车正凭借低成本制造和电动化优势，抢先欧洲车企赢得印度、拉美、东南亚等新兴市场的“下一个十亿驾驶者”。
> • 中国作为汽车增长市场高峰期已过，劳动力减少、城市人口趋稳，本土车企转向出口。
> • 比亚迪、吉利、奇瑞等中国车企正以速度和价格优势抢占全球电动汽车市场。
> • 欧洲车企在中国采用“在中国，为中国”战略，但面对全球新兴市场威胁仍过于自满。
> • 未来汽车增长主要来自印度、拉美、东南亚、中东和北非，欧洲车企有传统品牌优势。
> • 梅赛德斯-奔驰的Smart、宝马的Mini和大众的奥迪成功将中国工厂转为出口枢纽。
> • 欧洲车企应将欧洲设计与中国低成本制造结合，才能赢得下一个十亿司机。

### 中文译文

## 中国正领先欧洲，赢得下一个十亿驾驶者

中国电动汽车正在主导亚洲市场。

*摄影师：Lauren DeCicca/Getty Images*

一位审慎的将军总是在为下一场战争做准备，而不是上一场。这恰恰说明了外国汽车制造商在中国错在哪里。

从布鲁塞尔和柏林政策讨论的基调来看，目前只有两个汽车市场值得考虑：欧洲和中国本身。比亚迪股份有限公司（BYD Co.）、吉利汽车控股有限公司（Geely Automobile Holdings Ltd.）以及上汽集团（SAIC Motor Corp.）旗下的名爵（MG）在欧洲联盟的电动汽车销量迅速增长，这似乎正将这两大阵营推向贸易战，正如我的同事 [Juliana Liu 所写](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-02/china-s-trade-defiance-will-come-at-a-cost?srnd=undefined)。

与此同时，欧洲汽车制造商在中国的规模可观的业务正全力以赴，重新在一个被更新的本土电动汽车分散注意力的国内市场中保持自身的相关性。梅赛德斯-奔驰集团（Mercedes-Benz Group AG）首席执行官康林松（Ola Kallenius）上个月对投资者表示，公司一直在开发“量身定制的车型并深化本地化”。大众汽车集团（Volkswagen AG）则更为激进，花费三年时间推翻其以往自上而下的工业战略，转而采用更多车辆在中国设计、为中国打造的策略——“[在中国，为中国](https://www.volkswagen-group.com/en/press-releases/in-china-for-china-five-intelligent-fully-connected-vehicles-and-a-new-ai-based-adas-system-celebrate-their-debut-at-volkswagen-group-night-in-shanghai-19208)。”

他们需要正视现实。中国作为汽车增长市场的高峰期已经过去——但它仍有光明的出口前景。中国本土汽车公司没有在国内市场上为竞争已白热化的驾驶者而争斗，而是过去几年里争相向地球上的每个角落销售电动汽车。欧洲汽车制造商则因担心掏空自己的本土出口机器，追赶的脚步过于迟缓。

想想看：自2021年以来，中国的劳动力减少了约1300万人，相当于解雇了荷兰的全部劳动力。其城市人口的增长似乎正稳定在9.5亿人左右。自去年年底以来，汽车销量一直在下滑，消费[长期疲软](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-20/china-is-solving-the-wrong-consumption-problem?srnd=undefined)。汽车制造商历来用来提升利润的秘方——日益壮大的向上流动消费者的渴望——却明显缺席。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b1481bdbb86f2acf90222b5/3b481cc9-0b14-81f7-be86-dfd566f8d6ef-c91e53a2165fe190e3075fff83360ec4.png)

然而，接下来的数十亿驾驶者的忠诚还有待争取。发达国家与中国在全球约[四十亿人口](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/global-middle-class-interview)的中产阶级中占比不超过一半。未来几年增长最快的汽车市场可能出现在其他地区，主要由印度、拉丁美洲、东南亚、中东和北非带动。

欧洲汽车制造商在这些地区拥有强大的传统品牌。大众可以说几乎一手开创了巴西汽车市场，而Stellantis NV的菲亚特和吉普至今仍在那里占据主导地位。在中东和印度，豪华车就是梅赛德斯-奔驰、宝马集团和大众旗下奥迪的代名词。东南亚仍由日本企业主导，但它们在电动化方面的迟缓代表着一个机遇。

欧洲汽车产业一直比美国更依赖出口，面对中国竞争对手在这些地区构成的威胁，它一直太过自满。

问题在于，高管们在经济利益与作为欧洲制造业旗手的角色之间左右为难。要瞄准新兴市场对价格敏感的消费者，最佳方式是利用其中国工业基地，以比亚迪、吉利和奇瑞汽车所达到的速度和价格，交付新型电动化车型。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b1481bdbb86f2acf90222b5/3b481cc9-0b14-81dd-8617-f324355a81e9-c3d375e935657c35509a3f99d84a6b4d.png)

然而，他们却固守五年前最擅长的做法。Stellantis NV首席运营官Herlander Zola在5月对投资者表示，在巴西，公司将专注于捍卫其汽油动力入门级菲亚特和Ram皮卡的市场份额，希望中国汽车制造商不会试图在这些细分市场竞争。只有大众汽车最近开始谈论利用其中国子公司作为面向全球南方的“出口枢纽”，似乎已经意识到威胁的严重性。

整个行业最好看看自家企业内部哪些做法行之有效。在中国排名前十的欧洲汽车品牌中，只有梅赛德斯-奔驰的Smart、宝马的Mini和大众的奥迪成功将当地工厂转变为出口枢纽。它们都大力押注电动化，并与当地合作伙伴保持了深厚联系。

这与当前的政治推动方向几乎截然相反——后者试图在电动化的全球市场与一群用保护主义捍卫内燃机的富裕国家之间筑起一道鸿沟。但这是唯一一条能提供可持续未来的道路：将欧洲的设计专长与电池技术以及赢得下一个十亿司机所需的低成本制造相结合。对汽车制造商来说，改变方向还不算太晚。

### English Original

## China Is Beating Europe to the Next Billion Drivers

Chinese EVs are dominating the Asia market.

*Photographer: Lauren DeCicca/Getty Images*

A circumspect general is always preparing for the next war, not the last one. That shows you where foreign automakers have been going wrong in China.

To judge by the tone of the policy debate in Brussels and Berlin, there are only two car markets worth thinking about at the moment: Europe, and China itself. The rapid expansion of EV sales in the European Union by BYD Co., Geely Automobile Holdings Ltd., and SAIC Motor Corp.-owned MG appears to be pushing the two blocs toward a trade war, as my colleague [Juliana Liu has written](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-02/china-s-trade-defiance-will-come-at-a-cost?srnd=undefined).

Meanwhile, the still-substantial Chinese arms of European automakers are working flat-out to make themselves relevant again to a domestic market distracted by newer home-made electric vehicles. Mercedes-Benz Group AG has been developing “tailored models and deeper localization,” Chief Executive Officer Ola Kallenius told investors last month. Volkswagen AG has been even more aggressive, spending three years tearing up its former top-down industrial strategy in favor of one where more vehicles are designed and built “[in China, for China](https://www.volkswagen-group.com/en/press-releases/in-china-for-china-five-intelligent-fully-connected-vehicles-and-a-new-ai-based-adas-system-celebrate-their-debut-at-volkswagen-group-night-in-shanghai-19208).”

They need to face up to reality. China’s best years as a growth market for autos are behind it — but it still has a bright future in exports. Rather than fight over domestic drivers for whom competition has become cutthroat, China’s own car companies have spent the past few years rushing to sell electric vehicles to every other spot on the planet. European carmakers, wary of hollowing out their own home-grown export machines, have been far too slow in catching up.

Consider: China’s labor force has shrunk by about 13 million people since 2021. That’s equivalent to laying off every worker in the Netherlands. Growth of its urban population seems to be leveling off at around 950 million people. Auto sales have been going backward since late last year, and consumption is [perennially weak](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-20/china-is-solving-the-wrong-consumption-problem?srnd=undefined). The secret sauce that carmakers have historically depended on to spice up their profits — the aspirations of a growing crowd of upwardly-mobile consumers — is conspicuous by its absence.

The loyalties of the next few billion drivers are up for grabs, however. Developed countries and China make up no more than about half of the roughly [four billion people](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/global-middle-class-interview) in the global middle class. The fastest-growing auto markets over the years ahead are likely to be in other regions, dominated by India, Latin America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East and North Africa.

European carmakers have strong legacy brands there. VW can claim to have more or less created Brazil’s auto market, where Stellantis NV’s Fiat and Jeep remain dominant today. Premium cars in the Middle East and India are synonymous with Mercedes-Benz, BMW AG, and VW’s Audi. Southeast Asia is still dominated by Japanese companies, but their sluggishness on electrification represents an opportunity.

Europe’s car industry, always more export dependent than the US, has been far too complacent about the threat posed by Chinese rivals in these places.

The problem is that executives are torn between their economic self-interest and their role as standard-bearers for European manufacturing. The best way to target cost-conscious consumers in emerging markets would be to use their Chinese industrial bases to deliver new, electrified models at the speed and price that BYD, Geely and Chery Automobile Co. are achieving.

Instead, they’re sticking with what they did best five years ago. In Brazil, Stellantis NV will focus on defending its market share in gasoline-powered entry-level Fiats and Ram pick-ups, in the hope that Chinese carmakers won’t try to compete in those segments, Chief Operating Officer Herlander Zola told investors in May. Only VW, which has recently taken to talking about using its Chinese unit as an “export hub” for the global south, seems to have grasped the scale of the threat.

The industry would do well to look at what’s working within their own businesses. Of the top 10 European car brands in China, only Mercedes-Benz’s Smart, BMW’s Mini, and VW’s Audi have shown success turning their local factories into export hubs. All have leaned heavily into electrification, and maintained deep associations with local partners.

That’s pretty much the opposite approach to the political push underway to build a moat between an electrifying global market and a group of rich countries using protectionism to defend internal combustion engines. But it’s the only one that offers a sustainable future that pairs Europe’s design expertise with battery technology and the low-cost manufacturing needed to capture the next billion drivers. It’s not too late for carmakers to change course.
