> 股市看涨但就业疲软，美国正经历失业型繁荣。
> •股市今年上涨13%，盈利增长强劲，但就业增长近乎停滞。
> •7月总就业人数萎缩，过去12个月中第五次，私人部门月均新增就业仅5万。

### 中文译文

## **失业型繁荣已经到来**

> 股市认为经济正在加速。别指望就业会跟上。

![A job seeker receives pamphlets from a recruiter at a job fair.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bb81cc90b1481e8aa5dd007b1c2c73d/3bb81cc9-0b14-8141-870b-efe616f1c004-6addeed278282dbf5a0b8e25933defa4.jpg)

今年夏初在加利福尼亚州卡森市的一场招聘会。贾斯汀·沙利文/盖蒂图片社

数字支付公司Stripe的首席执行官帕特里克·科里森最近在X上对其150万粉丝进行了关于经济前景的民意调查。

约有1.3万人参与了调查。他们平均预计未来五年经济增长将翻一番，达到平均4.3%，而就业将下降约8%。

可以将其视为“AI乐观派”情景：AI大幅提高经济产出，同时让数千万工人失业。

这一预测成真的可能性几乎为零；就业几乎从未如此大幅崩溃过，也从未在产出飙升时崩溃过。但重要的不是幅度，而是方向。如果说“失业型繁荣”曾经显得牵强，那么现在不再如此，因为我们似乎正处于其中。

诚然，我们的主要增长指标看起来并不繁荣。过去一年，实际（经通胀调整的）国内生产总值增长平均为2.1%，与前一年持平；第二季度年化增长率仅为1.5%，表现糟糕。

但股市呈现出一幅截然不同的图景。今年以来，股市一路横冲直撞，穿过关税、战争、油价上涨和令人失望的GDP，上涨了13%。就在上周，[股市跃升至创纪录高位](https://www.wsj.com/finance/stocks/u-s-stocks-jump-as-bessent-says-iran-deal-may-be-days-away-6855d729?mod=article_inline)，此前财政部长斯科特·贝森特表示，美国和伊朗可能在一两天内达成协议，开放霍尔木兹海峡。该海峡仍然关闭，但股市保持了创纪录水平。

对于股市为何与GDP脱节，有几种可能的解释。一是这是一个泡沫。二是它预示着未来，即增长将加速。

在泡沫中，股价通常比盈利上涨更快，从而推高估值（即市盈率）。但去年，盈利增长快于股价。根据FactSet的数据，剔除因投资收益而夸大了业绩的[亚马逊](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/AMZN)和[Alphabet](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GOOGL)，截至目前第二季度盈利增长了惊人的32%。因此市盈率有所下降。

当然，这在很大程度上是因为人工智能，它推动了对云存储和计算机芯片的需求。（[关税退税](https://www.wsj.com/economy/trade/trump-tariff-refunds-company-earnings-1af269a2?mod=article_inline)也可能起到了一定作用。）人工智能本身很可能处于泡沫之中。但这场繁荣不仅仅是科技或人工智能的故事。据美国银行称，标普500指数成分股公司的盈利增长率中值已从两年前的8%加速至13%。该银行计算得出，剔除能源和金融公司后的该指数总收入，经通胀和汇率调整后，上涨约8%，远高于GDP。

**标普500指数盈利增长中位数**

*来源：美国银行*

少数与人工智能相关的股票曾一度推动市场。但现在不再是了：对商业周期状态敏感的银行股和小盘股，今年涨幅远高于大型科技股的“华丽七雄”。

德意志银行最近发现，“基础广泛”一词在财报电话会议中反复出现。“尽管我理解人工智能对GDP的影响，但考虑到行业和地域的分散性，这不可能成为我们所看到的贷款增长的唯一驱动因素，”PNC金融服务公司首席执行官比尔·德姆查克表示。

那么，总体情况是，整个经济中的公司，而不仅仅是那些涉足人工智能的公司，都看到了稳健的需求，而这正在转化为非凡的利润和收入。

但并不是招聘，这让我们想到了无就业繁荣中无就业的部分。上周我们获悉，[7月份总就业人数萎缩](https://www.wsj.com/economy/jobs/july-jobs-report-unemployment-0d7c08a7?mod=article_inline)，这是过去12个月中的第五次。过去一年，私人部门就业增长平均每月略高于5万人。这种趋势在衰退之外几乎前所未有。

![Construction continues at Amazon Web Services' PHX65 data center campus, with a large crane and excavator visible.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bb81cc90b1481e8aa5dd007b1c2c73d/3bb81cc9-0b14-816d-a0d0-c6e18209b5cf-c69e367ea65e90e9dc5fc944f15127e3.jpg)

亚利桑那州马里科帕县正在建设中的亚马逊数据中心。Eduardo Barraza/ZUMA Press

就业增长的放缓并未推高失业率，失业率徘徊在略高于4%的水平。原因是随着婴儿潮一代继续退休以及移民减少，劳动力正在萎缩。

人口因素可能压制了失业率，但劳动力需求也不温不火。据Indeed称，职位空缺深陷疫情后低点。如果雇主在争夺人才，我们会从薪酬中看到这一点，然而年度收入增长仅为3.2%，为疫情前以来的最低水平。

很多事情都可能干扰经济按照股市似乎希望的方式发展。尽管工资疲软，但由于许多家庭减少储蓄，消费者需求依然稳固。而私人投资依赖于发行数千亿美元的债务来为数据中心融资。这两件事都不可能永远持续下去。

但它们现在还能继续下去。尤其是如果人工智能能够实现乐观主义者所设想的一小部分成就。

### English Original

## The Jobless Boom Has Arrived

## **The Jobless Boom Has Arrived**

> The stock market thinks the economy is accelerating. Don’t expect jobs to follow.

A job fair in Carson, Calif., earlier this summer. Justin Sullivan/Getty Images

Patrick Collison, chief executive of digital payments company Stripe, recently [polled](https://x.com/patrickc/status/2084389116012339220?s=20) his 1.5 million followers on X about their economic outlook.

Some 13,000 took the survey. On average, they expect economic growth to double over the next five years, to an average of 4.3%, and employment to fall by around 8%.

Think of this as the “AI-optimist” scenario, in which AI vastly increases economic output while making tens of millions of workers redundant.

The odds of this forecast coming true are close to nil; employment almost never collapses by that much, and never when output is skyrocketing. But the magnitudes aren’t what matters, it’s the direction. And if a jobless boom once seemed far-fetched, it no longer does, because we seem to be in one right now.

Admittedly, our main growth measure doesn’t look boom-y. Real (inflation-adjusted) gross domestic product growth averaged 2.1% over the last year, in line with the previous year; and a lousy 1.5%, annualized, in the second quarter.

But the stock market delivers a completely different picture. It has bulldozed its way through tariffs, war, higher oil prices and disappointing GDP to rise 13% so far this year. Just last week, [stocks jumped to a record](https://www.wsj.com/finance/stocks/u-s-stocks-jump-as-bessent-says-iran-deal-may-be-days-away-6855d729?mod=article_inline) after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. and Iran could strike a deal to open the Strait of Hormuz in a day or two. The strait is still closed, but stocks have held on to their records.

There are several potential explanations for why the stock market has disconnected from GDP. One is that it’s a bubble. Another is that it tells us something about the future, namely that growth is going to accelerate.

In a bubble, stock prices typically go up faster than earnings, inflating valuations (i.e. the price-earnings multiple). But in the last year, earnings have risen faster than prices. Exclude [Amazon.com](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/AMZN) and [Alphabet](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GOOGL), whose results were inflated by investment gains, and earnings were up a stunning 32% in the second quarter so far, according to FactSet. The multiple has thus declined.

Much of this, of course, is because of AI, which is driving demand for cloud storage and computer chips. ([Tariff refunds](https://www.wsj.com/economy/trade/trump-tariff-refunds-company-earnings-1af269a2?mod=article_inline) may also play a part.) AI itself may well be in a bubble. But the boom isn’t just a tech or AI story. The median earnings growth of S&P 500 companies has accelerated to 13% from 8% two years ago, according to Bank of America. The bank calculates that total revenue for the index excluding energy and financial companies, and adjusted for inflation and currency changes, is up around 8%, much more than GDP.

**Median earnings growth, S&P 500**

*Source: Bank of America*

A handful of AI-linked stocks were once driving the market. Not any more: Banks and small stocks, which are both sensitive to the state of the business cycle, are up much more than big tech’s “Magnificent Seven” this year.

Deutsche Bank recently found the phrase “broad-based” cropping up repeatedly in earnings calls. “While I appreciate the impact AI is having on GDP, that can’t be the only driver of the loan growth that we’re seeing given the industry dispersion and the geographic dispersion,” Bill Demchak, chief executive of [PNC Financial Services](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/PNC), said.

The big picture, then, is that companies across the economy, not just those exposed to AI, are seeing solid demand, and that is translating into extraordinary profit and revenue.

But not hiring, which brings us to the jobless part of the jobless boom. We learned last week that [total payrolls shrank in July](https://www.wsj.com/economy/jobs/july-jobs-report-unemployment-0d7c08a7?mod=article_inline), the fifth time in the past 12 months. Private payroll growth has averaged a little over 50,000 per month for the last year. These sorts of trends are virtually unprecedented outside recessions.

An Amazon data center under construction in Maricopa County, Ariz. Eduardo Barraza/ZUMA Press

The slide in job growth hasn’t pushed up the unemployment rate, which hovers just above 4%. The reason is that the labor force is shrinking as baby boomers continue to retire and immigration dries up.

Demographic factors may be holding down unemployment, yet demand for labor is also tepid. Job openings are mired at postpandemic lows, according to Indeed. If employers were competing for talent, we’d see it in pay, yet annual earnings growth is just 3.2%, the lowest since before the pandemic.

Lots could interfere with the economy unfolding the way the stock market seems to hope. Consumer demand has remained solid despite weak wages because so many households have been saving less. And private investment depends on the issuance of hundreds of billions of dollars of debt to finance data centers. Neither of those things can go on forever.

But they can keep going for now. Especially if AI accomplishes even a fraction of what the optimists think.
