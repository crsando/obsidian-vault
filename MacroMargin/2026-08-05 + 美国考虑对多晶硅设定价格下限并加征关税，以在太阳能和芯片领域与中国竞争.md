## 美国考虑对多晶硅设定价格下限并加征关税，以在太阳能和芯片领域与中国竞争

![A tour of the Qcells North America factory in Cartersville, Georgia](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b381cc90b1481ca8c00fa91b27831dc/3b381cc9-0b14-8164-8662-f1f41ea11ba6-57b8c68b21cf31a5abfab82d0210d30c.jpg)

太阳能电池板在美国佐治亚州卡特斯维尔的Qcells北美工厂组装。2026年6月8日。路透社/Alyssa Pointer [购买许可权，打开新标签](https://www.reutersconnect.com/item/a-tour-of-the-qcells-north-america-factory-in-cartersville-georgia/dGFnOnJldXRlcnMuY29tLDIwMjY6bmV3c21sX1JDMlRQTEFROEI0Mg%3D%3D/?utm_medium=rcom-article-media&utm_campaign=rcom-rcp-lead)

- 总结
- 公司
- 贸易举措瞄准太阳能与芯片制造关键材料
- 相关措施或利好本土生产商，但将推高太阳能开发商与芯片采购商的成本
- 路透社报道后，美国上市太阳能制造商股价集体上涨
8月4日（路透社）——据四位知情人士透露，特朗普政府正准备对多晶硅及相关产品设定价格下限并征收关税，此举将使这种对太阳能电池板和半导体至关重要的材料成为美国在人工智能和能源领域与中国竞争的核心。

预计将于本月晚些时候做出的这一决定，旨在保护赫姆洛克半导体公司和瓦克化学公司[（WCHG.DE），打开新标签](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/WCHG.DE)旗下的美国多晶硅工厂，使其免受中国在芯片供应链中日益增长的野心影响。

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普的商务部对多晶硅进行的为期一年的国家安全调查，也对不断增长的国内太阳能制造业产生重大影响，而此时这位美国领导人已撤回了联邦政府对可再生能源的支持。

路透社首次报道了根据1962年《贸易扩展法》第232条进行的调查结果的细节，包括特朗普政府计划采取一种混合制度，将最低进口价格与对多晶硅及其衍生产品征收的关税相结合。据知情人士透露，这些人士要求匿名。

“中国通过补贴和长期产能过剩建立其多晶硅主导地位，这推动价格低于可持续水平并削弱了竞争对手，”无党派组织“保卫民主基金会”高级研究员克雷格·辛格尔顿表示。“价格下限与关税相结合可以为美国生产商提供生存空间，但该政策需要谨慎校准，以便在国内硅片和电池产能跟上之前，可信赖的投入品仍然可用，”他补充道。

白宫官员在唐纳德·特朗普总统决定前不愿置评。商务部未回应置评请求。

中国驻美国大使馆批评了这项贸易调查。

“中国敦促美国尽快停止第232条关税措施，并通过平等对话妥善解决各方的关切，”一位发言人表示。

路透社报道发布后，Corning [(GLW.N), 打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/GLW.N)、Toyo [(TOYO.O), 打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/TOYO.O)、T1 Energy [(TE.N), 打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/TE.N) 以及其他在美国上市的太阳能制造商（包括 First Solar [(FSLR.O), 打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/FSLR.O)）股价上涨。Corning 在报道发布前已上涨5.2%，随后涨幅升至10%，收涨9.4%；First Solar 涨幅从1.8%升至4.7%；T1 Energy 涨幅从4.9%攀升至9.9%；Canadian Solar [(CSIQ.O), 打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/CSIQ.O) 涨幅从3%升至5.6%；而 Toyo 在报道前上涨约1%，涨幅一度高达5%，最终收涨1.7%。

多晶硅是一种超高纯度的硅，处于半导体和太阳能制造供应链的起点。制造商将硅片制成太阳能电池，然后将其组装成用于太阳能项目的面板。

国内半导体制造是特朗普的优先事项，它依赖太阳能，因为太阳能行业对多晶硅的更大需求有助于支持芯片所需材料的生产。半导体工业协会表示，芯片行业占全球多晶硅需求的2.4%。

日本Toyo Co Ltd首席战略官Rhone Resch表示：“他们发现实际上确实需要太阳能。”Toyo在休斯顿附近经营一家太阳能电池板工厂，并计划投资3.57亿美元建设附近的太阳能电池设施。

两位消息人士称，商务部的计划将允许投资于美国硅片和电池生产的进口商抵消与贸易保护相关的成本。中国约占全球太阳能制造能力的80%。

根据冷战时期的第232条法规，总统可以在调查确认威胁存在后，限制被视为对国家安全构成威胁的进口。

特朗普已经完成调查，并对汽车、钢铁、铝、铜和木材征收关税。预计他很快将公布对进口无人机和工业机器人类似调查的结果，这将为对这些商品征收关税扫清道路。

该政府还在继续推进备受关注的半导体、药品、关键矿产和风力涡轮机调查，以重建其全球关税体系。

自2022年国会设立税收激励措施以来，美国太阳能制造业有所扩张。然而，大部分增长集中在电池板组装，导致制造商依赖进口硅片和电池，而这些产品需要更长的投资周期。

包括Toyo、Qcells [(000880.KS), 打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/000880.KS)、康宁、Canadian Solar和T1 Energy在内的多家美国太阳能工厂所有者，已宣布或启动位于太阳能供应链上游的设施，部分是为了满足与联邦清洁能源补贴相关的严格“美国制造”要求。

美国太阳能制造商协会（Solar Energy Manufacturers for America）和两党议员在提交给美国商务部的意见中表示，在国内多晶硅、硅片和电池产能扩大之前，需要从非中国生产商进口，例如韩国的OCI Holdings [(010060.KS), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/010060.KS)和Wacker，后者在马来西亚和德国设有工厂。

Hemlock在密歇根州经营一家工厂，是康宁（Corning）与日本信越半导体（Shin-Etsu Handotai）的合资企业。总部位于慕尼黑的Wacker在田纳西州运营一家工厂。

“没有多晶硅，价值链的后续环节（硅片和芯片或硅片和太阳能电池）就无法实现，”Wacker在一份声明中表示。

康宁拒绝置评。

### '附带损害'

美国政府必须在支持国内生产商和推高芯片及太阳能电池板成本之间走钢丝，这些产品在数据中心繁荣时期需求旺盛。

代表太阳能开发商和半导体买家的行业团体警告美国政府，关税可能提高太阳能发电厂的成本，并推高消费电子和汽车等产品的价格。

甚至一些制造商也担心这会对他们产品需求造成的影响。

"如果成本变得太高，市场可能会开始出现项目失败的情况。" Heliene首席执行官Martin Pochtaruk在接受采访时表示。Heliene是一家在明尼苏达州运营工厂的面板制造商。他称太阳能行业在针对芯片的贸易调查中所扮演的角色是"附带损害"。

## US weighs polysilicon price floor, tariffs to counter China in solar and chips

Solar panels are assembled at the Qcells North America factory in Cartersville, Georgia, U.S. June 8, 2026. Aug 4 (Reuters) - The Trump administration is preparing to set a price floor and impose tariffs on polysilicon and related products, according to four people familiar with the plan, putting a material critical to solar panels and semiconductors at the center of U.S. efforts to compete with China on artificial intelligence and energy.
8 月 4 日（路透社）——据四位知情人士透露，特朗普政府正准备对多晶硅及相关产品设定价格下限并征收关税，此举将使这种对太阳能电池板和半导体至关重要的材料成为美国在人工智能和能源领域与中国竞争的核心。

The decision, expected to come later this month, aims to protect U.S. polysilicon factories owned by Hemlock Semiconductor and Wacker Chemie [(WCHG.DE), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/WCHG.DE) ‌from growing Chinese ambitions in the chip supply chain.
预计将于本月晚些时候做出的这一决定，旨在保护赫姆洛克半导体公司和瓦克化学公司（WCHG.DE）旗下的美国多晶硅工厂，使其免受中国在芯片供应链中日益增长的野心影响。

President Donald Trump's year-long national security investigation into polysilicon by the Commerce Department also has major implications for the growing domestic solar manufacturing industry at a time when the U.S. leader has rolled back federal support for renewable energy.

Details of the probe's findings under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 are being reported for the first time by Reuters, including the Trump administration's plans to pursue a hybrid system combining a minimum import price with tariffs on polysilicon and derivative products, according to the sources familiar with the deliberations, who spoke on condition of anonymity.

“China built its polysilicon dominance through subsidies and chronic overcapacity that pushed prices below sustainable levels and weakened competitors," said Craig Singleton, senior fellow at the non-partisan Foundation for Defense of Democracies. "A price floor paired with tariffs could give U.S. producers room to survive, but the policy needs careful calibration so ⁠trusted inputs remain available while domestic wafer and cell capacity catches up,” he added.

A White House official would not comment ahead of President Donald Trump's decision. The Commerce Department did not respond to a request for comment.

China's Embassy in Washington criticized the trade investigation.

"China urges the U.S. to stop the Section 232 tariff measures as soon as possible, and properly resolve the concerns of all parties through equal dialogue," a spokesperson said.

Shares of Corning [(GLW.N), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/GLW.N), Toyo [(TOYO.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/TOYO.O), T1 Energy [(TE.N), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/TE.N) and other U.S.-listed solar manufacturers including First Solar [(FSLR.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/FSLR.O) rallied following the Reuters report. Corning, already up 5.2% before publication, rose to 10% and closed up 9.4%; First Solar rose to 4.7% from 1.8%; T1 Energy climbed to 9.9% from 4.9%; Canadian Solar [(CSIQ.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/CSIQ.O) advanced to a gain of 5.6% from 3%; and Toyo, up about 1% before the story, jumped as high as 5% before ending up 1.7%.

Polysilicon, an ultra-pure form of silicon, sits at the start of the semiconductor and solar manufacturing supply chains. Manufacturers turn silicon wafers into solar cells and then assemble those into panels used in solar projects.

Domestic semiconductor manufacturing, a Trump priority, depends on solar because the solar industry's larger demand for polysilicon helps support production of the material required for chips. The chip industry accounts for 2.4% of global polysilicon demand, the Semiconductor Industry Association said.

"They're finding that they actually do need solar," said Rhone Resch, chief strategy officer at Japan's Toyo Co Ltd, which operates a solar panel factory near Houston and plans to invest $357 million in a ‌nearby solar ⁠cell facility.

Two of the sources said the Commerce plan will allow importers investing in U.S. wafer and cell production to offset the costs associated with the trade protections. China accounts for roughly 80% of global solar manufacturing capacity.

Under the Cold War-era Section 232 statute, the president can restrict imports deemed a threat to national security after an investigation to confirm a threat exists.

Trump has already completed probes and imposed tariffs on autos, steel, aluminum, copper and lumber. He is expected to soon release the findings of similar investigations into imported drones and industrial robots, which would clear the way for tariffs on those goods.

The administration is also continuing work on closely watched probes into semiconductors, pharmaceuticals, critical minerals and wind turbines as it seeks to rebuild its global tariff regime.

U.S. solar manufacturing has expanded since Congress created tax incentives ⁠in 2022. Much of that growth, however, has been concentrated in panel assembly, leaving manufacturers dependent on imported wafers and cells, which require longer investment timelines.

Several U.S. solar factory owners, including Toyo, Qcells [(000880.KS), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/000880.KS), Corning, Canadian Solar and T1 Energy, have announced or started facilities farther up the solar supply chain in part to meet stringent Made-in-America requirements tied to federal clean energy subsidies.

The trade group Solar Energy Manufacturers for America and a bipartisan group of U.S. lawmakers have argued in comments to the Commerce Department that imports from non-Chinese producers like South Korea's ⁠OCI Holdings [(010060.KS), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/010060.KS) and Wacker, with factories in Malaysia and Germany, will be needed until domestic polysilicon, wafer and cell production expands.

Hemlock, which operates a plant in Michigan, is a joint venture between Corning and Japan's Shin-Etsu Handotai [(4063.T), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/4063.T). Munich-based Wacker runs a factory in Tennessee.

"Without polysilicon, the next steps of the value chain (wafer and chips or wafer and solar cells) are not possible," Wacker said in a statement.

Corning declined to comment.

### 'COLLATERAL DAMAGE'

The administration must walk a fine line between supporting domestic producers and inflating the ⁠cost of chips and solar panels, products in high demand amid the data center boom.

Industry groups representing solar developers and semiconductor buyers have warned the administration that tariffs could raise the cost of solar power plants and increase prices for products including consumer electronics and automobiles.

Even some manufacturers are worried about the impact on demand for their products.

"The market might start having projects fall down" if costs become too high, Martin Pochtaruk, CEO of panel maker Heliene, which operates factories in Minnesota, said in an interview. He called the solar industry's role in the chip-focused trade investigation "collateral damage."
