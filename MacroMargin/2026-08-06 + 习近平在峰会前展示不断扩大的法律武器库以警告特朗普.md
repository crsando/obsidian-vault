> 📌 **核心摘要**
>
> 中国近期对美反制措施密集出台，展示不断扩大的法律工具库，以在习近平访美前警告特朗普政府不要进一步升级技术限制。
> • 中国商务部等五部门连发声明，首次依据修订后《对外贸易法》启动国家安全调查，收紧无人机出口管制，制裁七家美国实体，并暂停对美产品输华检查合作。
> • 美国FCC和DHS近期限制中国机器人、逆变器并黑名单43家中国企业，中方反击瞄准合规、审计等基础设施。
> • 专家认为北京准备迅速对等反制，并针锋相对升级常态化；同时双方保持谈判与高层沟通，试图维持脆弱贸易休战。

### 中文译文

## 习近平在峰会前展示不断扩大的法律武器库以警告特朗普

中国最近对美国发动的一系列[反制措施](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/china-hits-back-at-us-with-tighter-drone-export-curbs-sanctions)利用了不断扩大的法律工具，展示了北京日益增长的报复能力，同时在习近平预计下月访问前警告华盛顿不要进一步实施技术限制。

周三公布的一揽子措施包括根据中国最近修订的《对外贸易法》开展的首次国家安全调查，以及对无人机相关出口的更严格审查。它还包括对七家美国实体的制裁，以及暂停与美国在对华目的产品的检查方面的合作。

在美国采取行动[禁止新的外国机器人](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs)一周后，中国以连发的五份声明公布了这一行动，其速度和广度表明，即使在为局势降级保留空间的同时，中国也有意愿和能力加大压力。

一天后，中国网络安全主管部门[启动](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/china-launches-cybersecurity-review-into-palo-alto-networks)了对美国科技公司[Palo Alto Networks Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PANW%20US%20Equity)在华销售产品的正式安全审查，理由是保护关键基础设施的需要。这份一句话声明没有明确说明这是否是针对美国采取的反制措施。

![A woman sits next to a humanoid robot at the World AI Conference in July 2026. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b148141a073f4d042c9186b/3b481cc9-0b14-81f5-8b60-d2df7a04f150-97bb0921ae1ede11032ae3932714ce1a.png)

“最引人注目的不仅在于北京正在继续加强其报复性法律工具箱——这是习近平一直敦促的——还在于它越来越准备在实践中部署这些工具，”新加坡管理大学法学教授高树超（Henry Gao）表示。

他说：“信号很直接：北京准备迅速并以同样方式作出回应，并将针锋相对的升级常态化，作为两国关系的长期特征。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b148141a073f4d042c9186b/3b481cc9-0b14-8198-b6b9-cf8f387cc918-2829cbb7f810d93d6fd4c3780698a3d0.png)

这一揽子措施是在联邦通信委员会7月28日[限制](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-423682A1.pdf)部分外国制造的机器人和电源逆变器之后出台的，这些领域由中国主导。三天后，国土安全部以涉嫌使用强迫劳动为由，将43家中国企业[列入黑名单](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/07/31/dhs-announces-addition-43-companies-uflpa-entity-list)。

北京似乎有意施加足够的代价，以阻止美国进一步升级，同时不危及贸易休战或习近平与唐纳德·特朗普总统预计于9月下旬举行的会晤。

北京与华盛顿之间的为期一年的协议——在特朗普与习近平于韩国举行的峰会上公布，除非延长，否则将于11月到期——已导致一些关税、稀土限制和针对中国造船厂的调查被暂停。

![Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in Beijing on May 15. Photographer: Evan Vucci/Pool/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b148141a073f4d042c9186b/3b481cc9-0b14-8197-b923-d35174929cc9-87f23bf014e971e912d72e1523fbac03.png)

中国商务部发言人将反制措施描述为“总体克制”，并敦促美国回到磋商以解决分歧。同时，该发言人警告称，若华盛顿对华施加新的限制，北京将毫不犹豫地采取额外措施。

虽然中国在两国元首于五月峰会上稳固关系后曾对美进行反击，但相关措施往往很大程度上是象征性的，因为它们针对的是在中国没有业务的军事供应商。例如，6月，北京[限制出口](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/china-places-two-us-rare-earths-producers-on-export-control-list)至10家与美国军方相关的公司，以回应五角大楼决定将部分中国大型科技企业列入中国军事支持者名单。

然而，一些最新制裁针对的是美国限制背后的基础设施，包括涉及合规、可追溯性、尽职调查和审计的公司与组织。

“这轮反制措施给跨国公司带来了一些真正的合规难题，”安可中国顾问公司（Ankura China Advisors）高级董事 Ryan Etzcorn 表示。他说，中国正在利用现有工具直接对风险顾问和尽职调查机构进行报复，将其措施范围扩展到各行各业制造商的“日常供应链合规”中。

包括[应用DNA科学公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BNBX%20US%20Equity)和负责任商业联盟在内的六个实体因被指控参与美国对涉嫌在新疆使用强迫劳动的中国公司的限制而受到制裁。北京还单独对总部位于亚利桑那州的合规测试公司（Compliance Testing）进行制裁，称该公司协助美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）对中国施加限制。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b148141a073f4d042c9186b/3b481cc9-0b14-81ab-a5cf-ecadb7a33631-75c87350c91aada028b012a6deb39a05.png)

作为反制一揽子措施的一部分，中国认证监管机构表示，已决定暂停美国认证机构对依据中国强制性产品认证制度（CCC）认证的产品进行后续工厂检查的授权。

CCC是面向在中国销售的指定产品（无论国产还是进口）所要求的一项强制性安全质量认证。因此，美国制造商可能不得不通过美国境外的授权机构安排所需的后续检查，这可能会推高成本并造成延误。

商务部还宣布对使用外国制造软件的打印机、复印机及其他办公影像设备启动国家安全调查。此次调查是依据中国修订后的《对外贸易法》所设机制的首次使用，该法于3月生效，扩大了政府应对外国制裁和其他限制措施的权力。

这些决定的时机增加了双方在习近平备受期待的访问前控制争端的压力，同时使北京在特朗普政府推进额外限制时拥有进一步可用的工具。

“更激进的行动——限制中国的开放权重AI模型或通过云服务限制中国企业的芯片获取——将标志着政府做法发生重大转变，并使休战面临风险，”欧亚集团分析师（包括Dominic Chiu）在报告中写道。

两国政府正试图维持脆弱的贸易休战，而各种争端仍在酝酿之中。

双方高级官员上周在通话中讨论了两国之间的摩擦点。中国国务院副总理何立峰对华盛顿近期实施的经济和贸易限制表示“严重关切”，而美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特呼吁北京“充分履行”其在提供稀土获取渠道和购买美国农产品方面的承诺。

北京的全面回应得到了中国国内民族主义声音的欢迎，他们视其为政府现在拥有更强大可支配武器库以反击美国压力的证据。

“中国不再是一个只能被动忍受美国打压的国家，其反制措施随时可用，”中国官方支持的民族主义小报《环球时报》前主编胡锡进在微博上写道。“如果美国选择升级双边贸易摩擦和相互的科技出口管制，中国无疑会坚持到底。”

## Xi Flexes Expanding Legal Arsenal to Warn Trump Ahead of Summit

China’s latest barrage of [countermeasures](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/china-hits-back-at-us-with-tighter-drone-export-curbs-sanctions) against the US drew on an expanding legal arsenal, demonstrating Beijing’s growing capacity to retaliate while warning Washington against further tech curbs before Xi Jinping’s expected visit next month.

The package announced Wednesday featured the first national security investigation under China’s recently revised Foreign Trade Law and tighter scrutiny of drone-related exports. It also included sanctions on seven American entities and a suspension of cooperation with the US over the inspection of products destined for China.

The speed and breadth of the action, revealed in a volley of five statements released one week after the US moved to [ban new foreign robots](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs), shows China’s willingness and ability to turn up the pressure even as it preserved room for de-escalation.

A day later, China’s cybersecurity authorities [launched](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/china-launches-cybersecurity-review-into-palo-alto-networks) a formal security review of products sold in the country by US technology firm [Palo Alto Networks Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PANW%20US%20Equity), citing the need to protect critical infrastructure. The one-line statement didn’t specify whether it was a countermeasure taken against the US.

“What is most striking is not only that Beijing is continuing to strengthen its legal toolbox for retaliation — as long urged by Xi — but also that it is increasingly prepared to deploy these instruments in practice,” said Henry Gao, a law professor at Singapore Management University.

“The signal is straightforward: Beijing is prepared to respond quickly and in kind, and to normalize tit-for-tat escalation as a standing feature of the relationship,” he said.

The package materialized after the Federal Communications Commission on July 28 [restricted](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-423682A1.pdf) some foreign-made robots and power inverters, sectors that China dominates. Three days later, the Department of Homeland Security [blacklisted](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/07/31/dhs-announces-addition-43-companies-uflpa-entity-list) 43 Chinese companies over allegations that they used forced labor.

Beijing appeared intent to impose enough costs to deter further US escalation without jeopardizing the trade truce or Xi’s upcoming meeting with President Donald Trump that’s expected in late September.

The one-year deal between Beijing and Washington — unveiled at the summit between Trump and Xi in South Korea and set to expire in November, unless extended — has led to a suspension of some tariffs, rare earth curbs and investigations into China’s shipbuilders.

A Commerce Ministry spokesperson described the countermeasures as “generally restrained” and urged the US to return to consultations to resolve differences. At the same time, the spokesperson warned that Beijing wouldn’t hesitate to deploy additional measures should Washington impose new restrictions against China.

While China had hit back at the US after their presidents steadied ties in a May summit, the measures were often largely symbolic because they targeted military suppliers with no business in the world’s second-largest economy. In June, for example, Beijing [restricted exports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/china-places-two-us-rare-earths-producers-on-export-control-list) to 10 US military-related companies in response to the Pentagon’s decision to add some of China’s biggest tech firms to a roster of Chinese military backers.

Some of the latest sanctions, however, targeted the infrastructure behind American restrictions, including companies and organizations involved in compliance, traceability, due diligence and auditing.

“This round of countermeasures whip up some real compliance headaches for multinationals,” said Ryan Etzcorn, a senior director at Ankura China Advisors. China is using existing tools to retaliate directly against risk consultants and due-diligence providers, extending the scope of its measures “into daily supply chain compliance” for manufacturers across industries, he said.

Six entities, including [Applied DNA Sciences](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BNBX%20US%20Equity) and Responsible Business Alliance, were sanctioned over their alleged involvement in US restrictions on Chinese companies accused of using forced labor in Xinjiang. Beijing separately targeted Compliance Testing, an Arizona-based company it said had assisted the FCC in imposing restrictions against China.

As part of the retaliation package, China’s certification regulator said it’s decided to suspend the authorization of US-based certification bodies to conduct follow-up factory inspections for products certified under the China Compulsory Certification system known as CCC.

CCC is a mandatory safety and quality certification required for designated products sold in China, whether made domestically or imported. As a result, US manufacturers may have to arrange required follow-up inspections through authorized bodies outside the US, potentially raising costs and causing delays.

The Commerce Ministry also announced a national security investigation into printers, copiers, and other office imaging equipment that uses foreign-made software. The probe marks the first use of the mechanism created under China’s revised Foreign Trade Law, which took effect in March and expanded the government’s authority to respond to foreign sanctions and other restrictions.

The timing of the decisions adds to the pressure on both sides to contain the dispute before Xi’s highly anticipated trip, while leaving Beijing with further tools to deploy if the Trump administration presses ahead with additional restrictions.

“More aggressive actions — restricting Chinese open-weight AI models or curbing Chinese firms’ chip access through cloud services — would signal a step change in the administration’s approach and put the truce at risk,” Eurasia Group analysts including Dominic Chiu wrote in a note.

The two governments are trying to preserve a fragile trade truce while a wide range of disputes continue to simmer.

Senior officials from both sides discussed points of friction between their countries in a call last week. Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng raised “serious concern” over recent economic and trade restrictions from Washington, while US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called on Beijing to “fully meet” its commitments in providing access to rare earths and purchasing US agricultural products.

Beijing’s sweeping response has been welcomed by nationalist voices within China, who see it as evidence that their government now has a stronger arsenal at its disposal to push back against US pressure.

“China is no longer a nation that can only passively endure US suppression, and its countermeasures are readily available,” Hu Xijin, former editor-in-chief of China’s state-backed nationalist tabloid Global Times, wrote on Weibo. “If the US chooses to escalate bilateral trade friction and mutual technology export controls, China will, without a doubt, see it through to the end.”
