## 特朗普的选举诉求在亚利桑那州关键初选中接受考验

唐纳德·特朗普总统希望共和党议员支持他审查11月中期选举的计划，[呼应他仍就2020年失利提出的毫无根据的抱怨](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-17/trump-accuses-china-of-election-meddling-putting-ties-at-risk)。亚利桑那州周二举行的初选是检验这一信息能否激励共和党选民的首次考验。

![Alexander Kolodin Photographer: Samantha Chow/AP Photo](https://prod-files-secure.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/6c376e85-26f0-4147-a310-0844cfbf4875/84a43ea0-27ae-4526-8ad3-696c69ba01cc/Alexander-Kolodin-Photographer-Samantha-Chow-AP-Photo.png?X-Amz-Algorithm=AWS4-HMAC-SHA256&X-Amz-Content-Sha256=UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD&X-Amz-Credential=ASIAZI2LB46662YU57QQ%2F20260722%2Fus-west-2%2Fs3%2Faws4_request&X-Amz-Date=20260722T024345Z&X-Amz-Expires=3600&X-Amz-Security-Token=IQo…[redacted 1287 chars])

至少三位质疑2020年选举结果的候选人参加了亚利桑那州的投票，寻求获得有影响力的州级职位。亚利桑那州与佐治亚州一样，是特朗普对其首次失败的连任竞选抱怨的核心。

全国范围内，选民表示他们越来越关注生活成本和伊朗持续的战争。

与此同时，特朗普继续聚焦与美国选举相关的问题，包括在上周一次罕见的对全国讲话中将其作为核心议题。

亚利桑那州对特朗普而言是一个关键州，他在2020年以微弱劣势输掉该州后，于2024年在此获胜。该州长期以来一直是共和党的堡垒，但正在快速变化，包括凤凰城周边蓬勃发展的郊区和通勤城镇。

数据中心和半导体工厂的建设正在改变经济，并给该州的能源和供水带来新的压力。亚利桑那州也感受到了特朗普政府打击移民的影响。根据人口普查局的数据，该州人口中[约三分之一是西班牙裔](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/AZ/PST045225)。

特朗普淡化了解决可负担性问题的努力——有时称这个问题是骗局，其他时候，包括今年早些时候，[宣布取得胜利](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-19/trump-says-he-won-on-affordability-singaling-messaging-shift)。他还拒绝在本月签署一项旨在解决住房成本的两党法案。

相反，总统仍然专注于以选举为核心的《拯救美国法案》，敦促共和党议员将这项投票限制法案作为他的首要任务，尽管国会中的参议院共和党领袖一再表示该法案没有足够的票数通过。

特朗普上周在向全国发表的讲话中表示：“没有哪个第三世界国家的选举像我们这样。”他还说，任何反对这项立法的人一定“想要作弊”。

在亚利桑那州，他有多位盟友为此目标参加竞选，争取担任可能监督或影响未来选举的职位。其中包括竞选州务卿的州众议员亚历山大·科洛丁，以及竞选亚利桑那州总检察长的州参议院议长沃伦·彼得森。

在总检察长竞选中，彼得森今年3月因表示他已将州参议院对马里科帕县2020年选票审计的细节[交给联邦调查局](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-09/arizona-lawmaker-turns-over-2020-election-data-to-trump-s-fbi)而受到关注。马里科帕县是特朗普质疑乔·拜登是否赢得这个战场州的努力的中心。彼得森表示他是在遵守传票，但民主党人称此举是为了支持对选举结果已被推翻的阴谋论。

彼得森的对手是罗德尼·格拉斯曼，他是一名律师和空军预备役军官，2010年曾作为民主党候选人竞选当时由约翰·麦凯恩占据的美国参议院席位。他的竞选信息主要聚焦于他的法律与秩序背景。格拉斯曼和彼得森都批评现任总检察长克里斯·梅耶斯多次起诉特朗普的行政命令。

在竞选亚利桑那州下一任州务卿（职责是确保选举安全，并在需要时担任代理州长）的角逐中，律师科洛丁最为人熟知的事迹可能是因对2020年选举结果提出一系列法律挑战而受到亚利桑那州律师协会的制裁，其中包括由前特朗普律师西德尼·鲍威尔领导的、后来被称为[“克拉肯”诉讼](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-12-10/sidney-powell-s-kraken-voter-fraud-suit-dismissed-in-arizona)的挑战之一。

鲍威尔与科洛丁共同提起的亚利桑那州诉讼于2020年底被驳回，美国地区法官黛安·休梅特瓦表示，该诉讼的特点是“大量以‘可能、或许、也许、或’开头的短语”。

科洛丁将选举安全作为其竞选的核心议题。他的对手是前亚利桑那州共和党主席吉娜·斯沃博达，她曾表示在2020年大选之后，她的政党在法律上“火力不足”，但没有明确表示选举被操纵。

该州第一国会选区的一场竞争激烈的民主党初选正在进行中，民主党认为在现任共和党众议员戴维·施韦克特决定竞选州长后，该选区有望翻盘。四名民主党人正在争夺党内提名，热门人选包括前学校董事会成员兼记者玛琳·加兰-伍兹，以及州众议员阿米什·沙阿，他是一位医生，在2024年以微弱劣势输给了施韦克特。

在争取共和党州长提名的竞选中，施韦克特正在挑战众议员安迪·比格斯。比格斯是得到特朗普支持的前众议院自由党团主席，他拒绝透露是否认为拜登合法赢得了2020年大选。这场竞选的胜者将与现任州长凯蒂·霍布斯对决。

库克政治报告称，霍布斯是全美“最危险”的民主党在任州长，但该组织仍将11月的这场竞选评为“偏向民主党”。

该州的投票站于当地时间上午6点开放，晚上7点关闭。亚利桑那州选民在选举日需出示某种[身份证明](https://azsos.gov/elections/voters/voting-elections)。

## Trump’s Election Demands Get Test in Key Arizona Primary Races

President Donald Trump wants GOP lawmakers to back his plan to scrutinize the November midterm elections, [echoing unfounded complaints](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-17/trump-accuses-china-of-election-meddling-putting-ties-at-risk) he still makes about his 2020 defeat. Arizona’s primaries on Tuesday are the first test of whether that message is energizing Republican voters.

At least three candidates who questioned the results of the 2020 election are on the ballot and seeking powerful state posts in Arizona, which along with Georgia was at the center of Trump’s gripes about his first, failed reelection bid.

Nationally, voters say they are increasingly concerned about the cost of living and the ongoing war in Iran.

At the same time, Trump continues to center issues related to how US elections are conducted, including making it the core focus of a rare address to the nation last week.

Arizona represents a critical state for Trump, who won here in 2024 after narrowly losing the state in 2020. Long a Republican stronghold, the state is changing rapidly including in the booming suburbs and commuter towns around Phoenix.

The build up of both data centers and semiconductor plants are transforming the economy and adding new strains to the state’s energy and water supplies. Arizona has also felt the impact of the Trump administration’s crackdown on immigration. Its population is about [one-third Hispanic](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/AZ/PST045225), according to the Census Bureau.

Trump has downplayed efforts to address affordability — at points saying the issue was a hoax and at other times, including earlier this year, [declaring victory](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-19/trump-says-he-won-on-affordability-singaling-messaging-shift) on the issue. He also refused to sign a bipartisan bill this month meant to tackle housing costs.

Instead, the president has remained fixated on the election-focused SAVE America Act, urging GOP lawmakers to back the voting restrictions bill as his top priority, despite Senate Republican leaders in Congress repeatedly saying the measure doesn’t have the votes to pass.

“There’s no third-world country that has elections like we have,” Trump said in an address to the nation last week, in which he said anyone opposed to the legislation must “want to cheat.”

In Arizona, he has several allies in that cause on the ballot, seeking positions that could oversee or influence future elections. They include state Representative Alexander Kolodin, who is running for secretary of state, and state Senate President Warren Petersen, who is vying to be Arizona’s attorney general.

In the attorney general’s race, Peterson drew attention in March when he said he [handed over to the FBI details](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-09/arizona-lawmaker-turns-over-2020-election-data-to-trump-s-fbi) of the state senate’s audit of 2020 votes in Maricopa County, which was ground zero in Trump’s efforts to question whether Joe Biden won the battleground state. Petersen said he was complying with a subpoena, but Democrats said the move was part of an effort to bolster discredited conspiracies about the results.

Peterson is facing Rodney Glassman, a lawyer and Air Force reservist who in 2010 ran as the Democratic nominee for the US Senate seat held at the time by John McCain. He’s focused his campaign message on his law-and-order background. Both Glassman and Petersen have criticized the incumbent attorney general, Kris Mayes, for her multiple lawsuits against Trump’s executive orders.

In the race to be Arizona’s next secretary of state — whose job it is to ensure secure elections and serve as acting governor when needed — lawyer Kolodin may be best known for being sanctioned by the Arizona state bar for taking on a slew of legal challenges to the 2020 election results, including one of several led by former Trump lawyer Sidney Powell that became known as the [“Kraken” lawsuits](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-12-10/sidney-powell-s-kraken-voter-fraud-suit-dismissed-in-arizona).

The Arizona lawsuit Powell championed with Kolodin was dismissed in late 2020 after US District Judge Diane Humetewa said it was defined by “a lengthy collection of phrases beginning with the words ‘could have, possibly, might,’ and ‘may have.’”

Kolodin has made election security a central part of his campaign. He’s facing former Arizona GOP chair Gina Swoboda, who has said her party was “outgunned” legally in the aftermath of the 2020 election, without specifically saying the vote was rigged.

One of the tighter Democratic primaries is taking place in the state’s First Congressional District, which the party views as ripe for a flip after incumbent Republican Representative David Schweikert decided to run for governor. Four Democrats are vying to be the party’s nominee, with the favorites including former school board member and journalist Marlene Galan-Woods and state Representative Amish Shah, a physician who narrowly lost to Schweikert in 2024.

In his race to be the GOP nominee for governor, Schweikert is challenging Representative Andy Biggs, the Trump-endorsed former head of the Freedom Caucus who has refused to say whether he believes Biden legitimately won the 2020 vote. The winner of that race will face incumbent Governor Katie Hobbs.

The Cook Political Report says Hobbs is the “most endangered” Democratic incumbent governor in the US, but the group still rates the race as one that “leans Democratic” in November.

Polls in the state open at 6 a.m. local time and close at 7 p.m. Voters in Arizona are required to show some [type of identification](https://azsos.gov/elections/voters/voting-elections) on Election Day.
