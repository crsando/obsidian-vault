### 中文译文

## 中国高管或随习近平访美，贸易休战延长“几乎确定”

![Donald Trump and Xi Jinping are expected to meet in Washington on September 24. Photo: TNS](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b1481ff920bd0e3dd4a1b21/3ca81cc9-0b14-81a9-9250-e7409b12a938-354b246a9ecd76b6882873691c31923f.jpg)

据知情人士透露，中美官员正在讨论是否让商界领袖加入主席[习近平](https://www.scmp.com/topics/xi-jinping)下月[访问美国](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/series/3364401/xis-2026-trip-us)的代表团，双方准备延长去年10月在釜山达成的一年期贸易协议。

谈判进行之际，习近平预计将于9月24日在华盛顿会见美国总统[唐纳德·特朗普](https://www.scmp.com/topics/donald-trump)。特朗普于5月率大批美国顶级CEO访华，其中包括特斯拉、苹果、英伟达和波音的掌门人。

周三在北京会见美国驻华大使戴维·珀杜时，外交部长王毅呼吁双方落实两国元首共识，管控分歧，扫清高层交往的障碍，使中美关系“稳定、健康、可持续”。

珀杜表示，特朗普和习近平提供了“重要战略指引”，并称特朗普5月对中国的访问“非常成功”。他说，美方愿加强沟通，推进合作，为“下一阶段重要高层交往”做好准备，并呼吁在“相互尊重和互惠”基础上建立建设性、稳定的关系。

特朗普的特使后来在社交媒体上写道，这次会晤是为习近平访问所做的准备的一部分。

在此背景下，四名知情人士表示，有关中国商界领袖是否随行的谈判正在进行，这一想法以互惠为核心，因为5月[特朗普曾带领美国高管](https://www.scmp.com/economy/article/3353399/trump-and-his-ceos-want-chinas-business-has-asia-moved)访华。对于是哪一方提出这一要求，各方说法不一，最终决定尚未作出。

![American executives including Apple CEO Tim Cook (left) and Tesla chief Elon Musk (centre) travelled to Beijing in May as part of Donald Trump’s delegation. Photo: AP](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b1481ff920bd0e3dd4a1b21/3ca81cc9-0b14-8190-bf8d-c27ff69cfd5b-e5fb0287d5e45234352d849786028882.jpg)

苹果CEO蒂姆·库克（左）和特斯拉首席执行官埃隆·马斯克（中）等美国高管于5月随唐纳德·特朗普代表团访问北京。图片来源：美联社

两位消息人士称，北京方面热切希望带上商界领袖，并指出许多中国企业仍对美国投资感兴趣。

其中一位消息人士称，讨论表明中国有意加快停滞不前的投资委员会（Board of Investment）谈判，这是5月峰会的重要成果之一。

另一位消息人士称，受贸易委员会（Board of Trade）协议覆盖的中国公司可能成为代表团成员，因为该协议直接关系到它们的商业利益。

贸易委员会是北京峰会的另一项重要成果，启动该委员会的努力已进入最后阶段，重点是确定非敏感行业和产品，这些行业和产品有资格享受每边上限为300亿美元的减税贸易。

另一位接近谈判的人士表示，代表团可能有助于从对抗转向受管理的经济竞争，并让中美商界领袖在政治峰会期间有空间建立联系。

然而，另一位消息人士称，该代表团是应华盛顿方面的要求组建的。

商业代表团和贸易谈判正在并行进行，双方都在寻求9月峰会的具体成果，同时管理更广泛的贸易和投资关系。

据消息人士称，人们普遍认为，2025年10月在[韩国釜山](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3330950/xi-and-trumps-high-stakes-meeting-south-korea-everything-you-need-know)达成的贸易休战将得到延长。

双方当时同意降低美国对中国商品的部分关税，暂停对中国关联公司的额外限制，暂停中国某些稀土出口管制一年，并确保中国对美国大豆和芬太尼前体作出承诺。

消息人士称，釜山贸易协议延长很可能或“几乎确定”，因为尽管近期有报复性措施和美国联邦通信委员会的行动，双方都希望延长，但在延长期限上存在分歧。

一位消息人士称，北京希望将延期持续到特朗普任期结束，而华盛顿则寻求再达成一项为期一年的协议。

该消息人士补充说，北京在谈判延长贸易协议时寻求稳定性，并希望“锁定”其所认为的有利协议。

另一位熟悉谈判的人士表示，特朗普政府在延长期限上“故作姿态”，以制造拥有筹码的印象。

据另一位消息人士称，鉴于目前的讨论状态，该协议将延长一年。

一位消息人士表示，美国对稀土供应流的担忧可以通过对话解决，预计在习近平与特朗普的会谈中，釜山协议不会被重点提及。

### “里程碑式访问”：习近平与特朗普着眼于中美关系稳定

涉及出口管制和针对企业的定向行动的低级别针锋相对措施可能会继续；然而，对误判的担忧依然存在。

联邦通信委员会上月禁止进口和销售新的外国制造的机器人设备和电源逆变器，这些领域中国处于全球领先地位。华盛顿同月还将40多家中国企业列入其《维吾尔强迫劳动预防法》实体清单。北京方面通过收紧无人机出口管制并制裁七家美国公司进行报复。

另一名知情人士表示，该协议可能会被悄悄延长，或并入更广泛的经济谅解，使双方得以保留现有贸易框架，而不会使其成为习近平与特朗普会晤的核心议题。

但据另一位熟悉讨论情况的消息人士称，华盛顿方面并未寻求在两位领导人下月会晤时取得任何重大突破。

消息人士还表示，美国即将对包括中国在内的16个经济体征收的、针对产能过剩的关税不太可能对习近平的访问产生不利影响。

华盛顿已向北京表示，根据《1974年贸易法》第301条征收的额外关税只是取代美国最高法院2月推翻的进口关税。

上月，特朗普政府根据其强迫劳动调查，对来自60个经济体的商品征收10%至12.5%的关税，其中中国面临12.5%的税率。北京反对此举，但未进行报复。

双方都在积极尝试让9月的会晤免受涉及多个领域的近乎例行行动的影响，以防止现有和新的争端破坏峰会前稳定双边关系的更广泛努力。

## Chinese executives may join Xi’s US trip, as trade truce extension ‘almost certain’

Chinese and US officials are discussing whether business leaders will join President [Xi Jinping](https://www.scmp.com/topics/xi-jinping)’s delegation to [the United States next month](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/series/3364401/xis-2026-trip-us), as the two sides prepare to extend a one-year trade agreement reached in Busan last October, according to people familiar with the talks.

The negotiations come as Xi is expected to meet US President [Donald Trump](https://www.scmp.com/topics/donald-trump) in Washington on September 24. Trump travelled to Beijing in May with a large group of top American CEOs, including the heads of Tesla, Apple, Nvidia and Boeing.

In a meeting with US ambassador to China David Perdue in Beijing on Wednesday, Foreign Minister Wang Yi called for both sides to implement the leaders’ consensus, manage differences and remove obstacles to high-level exchanges, so ties could become “stable, healthy and sustainable”.

Perdue said Trump and Xi had provided “important strategic guidance” and described Trump’s May visit to China as “very successful”. Washington was ready to strengthen communication, advance cooperation and prepare for “important high-level interactions in the next phase”, he said, calling for a constructive and stable relationship based on “respect and reciprocity”.

Trump’s envoy later wrote on social media that the meeting was part of preparations for Xi’s visit.

Against that backdrop, four people with knowledge of the discussions said the inclusion of Chinese business leaders was being negotiated, with the idea framed around reciprocity after the [US executives joined Trump](https://www.scmp.com/economy/article/3353399/trump-and-his-ceos-want-chinas-business-has-asia-moved) in May. Accounts differed as to which side was driving the request, and a final decision had yet to be reached.

American executives including Apple CEO Tim Cook (left) and Tesla chief Elon Musk (centre) travelled to Beijing in May as part of Donald Trump’s delegation. Photo: AP

Two sources said Beijing was eager to bring business leaders, noting that many Chinese companies remained interested in investing in the US.

One of the sources said the discussion pointed to China’s interest in accelerating talks on the stalled Board of Investment, one of the key announcements from the May summit.

Another source said Chinese companies covered by the Board of Trade agreement could be part of the delegation, as the pact directly related to their business interests.

The Board of Trade was another important outcome from the summit in Beijing, and efforts to get it up and running are in the final stages, focusing on identifying non-sensitive sectors and products eligible for tariff-reduced trade capped at US$30 billion for each side.

Another person close to the talks said a delegation could potentially help with a shift from confrontation towards managed economic competition and allow Chinese and US business leaders the space to build ties alongside the political summit.

A separate source, however, said the delegation was requested by Washington.

The business delegation and trade talks are unfolding in parallel, with both sides looking for concrete outcomes from the September summit as they manage broader trade and investment ties.

There is a broad understanding that the trade truce agreed in [Busan, South Korea](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3330950/xi-and-trumps-high-stakes-meeting-south-korea-everything-you-need-know) in October 2025 will be extended, according to sources.

The two sides at the time agreed to reduce some US tariffs on Chinese goods, suspend additional US restrictions on Chinese-affiliated companies, pause certain Chinese rare earth export controls for a year, and secure Chinese commitments on American soybeans and fentanyl precursors.

Sources said an extension of the Busan trade deal was likely or “almost certain” as both sides wanted it despite recent retaliatory measures and actions by the US Federal Communications Commission, but they disagreed over its duration.

Beijing preferred an extension that ran through the end of Trump’s presidency while Washington was seeking another one-year deal, one source said.

The source added that Beijing, in negotiating a longer extension of the trade pact, sought stability and wanted to “lock in” what it viewed as a favourable agreement.

Another person familiar with the talks said the Trump administration was “playing coy” over the duration of the extension to create an impression of leverage.

Given the current state of the discussion, the agreement is set to be extended for one year, according to another source.

One source said US concerns over rare earth supply flows could be addressed through dialogue and that the Busan deal was not expected to be given prominence during the Xi-Trump talks.

### ‘A milestone visit’: Xi and Trump set sights on stability for China-US relations

Low-level tit-for-tat measures involving export controls and targeted actions against companies are likely to continue; however, concerns about miscalculation remain.

The Federal Communications Commission last month banned the import and sale of new foreign-made robotic devices and power inverters, sectors in which China is a global leader. Washington also added over 40 Chinese companies to its Uygur Forced Labour Prevention Act entity list the same month. Beijing retaliated by tightening export controls on drones and sanctioning seven American firms.

Another person said the agreement could be extended quietly or folded into a broader economic understanding, allowing both sides to preserve the existing trade framework without making it the centrepiece of the Xi-Trump meeting.

But Washington, according to another source familiar with the discussions, was not seeking any major breakthrough when the two leaders meet next month.

Sources also indicated that the impending US tariffs on excess industrial capacity against 16 economies, including China, were unlikely to adversely affect Xi’s visit.

Washington has conveyed to Beijing that the additional tariffs imposed under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act are simply replacing the import levies that the US Supreme Court struck down in February.

Last month, the Trump administration imposed tariffs of 10 to 12.5 per cent on goods from 60 economies under its forced labour probe, with China facing the 12.5 per cent rate. Beijing opposed the move but did not retaliate.

Both sides are actively attempting to insulate the September meeting from the near-regular actions spanning multiple sectors, seeking to prevent existing and new disputes from derailing broader efforts to stabilise bilateral ties ahead of the summit.
