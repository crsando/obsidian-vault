### 德黑兰提出重大让步的新要求，而就在几天前，白宫还曾暗示就恢复商业通行达成协议已近在咫尺

华盛顿——美国官员称，数周以来，特朗普总统一直在为宣布赢得对[伊朗](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran)战争铺路，只要德黑兰完全重新开放霍尔木兹海峡，他甚至私下向高级助手提出，他愿意在没有核协议的情况下离开。

但这一缩减版目标在[伊朗坚持](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/u-a-e-says-iran-attacked-one-of-its-ships-in-hormuz-bfbec3b2?mod=article_inline)周六以迄今最高的代价换取允许该水道自由通行后变得更加困难，其条件包括寻求数十亿美元的美国付款、美国从该地区撤军以及结束美国海军封锁等。

这一连串快速事态表明，特朗普抽身冲突的选择再次收窄。特朗普曾多次威胁——随后又退缩——[大规模升级其轰炸行动](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/it-isnt-just-pickaxe-trumps-other-potential-nuclear-targets-in-iran-975e960e?mod=article_inline)。但最近谈判中的混乱局面表明，德黑兰已准备好一场漫长而艰苦的冲突。

华盛顿智库战略与国际研究中心中东项目主任莫娜·雅库比安表示：“伊朗最近对重新开放海峡的强硬要求，使总统越来越难以制造一个保全面子的冲突出路。”

欧亚集团咨询公司的伊朗问题专家格雷戈里·布鲁表示：“伊朗人完全相信特朗普想退出，并且专注于开放海峡，因此他们采取强硬态度。”

自4月以来，白宫关于伊朗永远不会获得核武器的最后通牒一直笼罩着外交谈判。特朗普曾试图将核谈判纳入与伊朗更广泛谈判的一部分，但这些谈判被证明过于困难，导致白宫近几周更专注于[重新开放霍尔木兹海峡](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/hormuz-opening-uncertain-after-new-strike-hurdles-in-talks-e2dfd2ce?mod=article_inline)。

伊朗通过零星导弹和无人机袭击基本扼杀这条水道的能力，已被证明是该国强大的筹码，导致商业交通停滞，全球石油市场动荡。特朗普近几周多次宣称霍尔木兹海峡已经完全开放，但每一次这些承诺都被证明为时过早。

如今，距离中期选举不到三个月，汽油价格仍远高于2月底战争开始前的水平，特朗普一直在寻找向美国公众传达战争已经获胜的方法。

![President Donald Trump boarding Air Force One.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481479847c43aafd45b77/3b881cc9-0b14-81a7-b590-f096a3cac706-ab115acf9b9ae76935a5d525ae15cf12.jpg)

特朗普总统表示，白宫不会允许任何纳税人的钱流向伊朗。Julia Demaree Nikhinson/AP

近日，特朗普告诉记者，他准备发动自二战以来最大规模的军事打击，但[被美国盟友劝止](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-stokes-the-middle-east-war-with-drone-attacks-on-kuwait-f25530a0?mod=article_inline)。他坚称协议“即将达成”，尽管尚未实现。

周五，总统在[社交媒体](https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/117054491639962487)上暗示，即使没有核协议，胜利也已到手，并分享了一篇宣称“唐纳德·特朗普赢得了伊朗战争”的文章。

副总统JD·万斯告诉福克斯新闻，美国将继续向伊朗施压，促使其与华盛顿的关系发生长期改变。“如果没有，那也没关系，我们会继续施加我们所能施加的压力，尽可能多地从中东获取石油和天然气，以便美国人享受更低的汽油和能源价格。”

“我们正处于比赛进行中，”万斯说。

德黑兰在2月战争爆发时关闭了该水道，一直坚持不允许任何美国军舰通过，并推动向商业船只收取费用。美国官员表示，华盛顿不会接受伊朗的限制或收费。

周末，协议前景似乎变得黯淡，因为阿联酋称伊朗对其一艘船只发动了导弹袭击，而一名伊朗高级官员提出了一系列允许通行的强硬要求。

据伊朗国家通讯社IRNA报道，伊朗最高国家安全委员会秘书穆罕默德·巴盖尔·佐勒加尔表示，美国需要永久结束战争、解除海上封锁、撤出部队、终止所有制裁、释放[伊朗被冻结的资产](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-frozen-assets-7e926b39?mod=article_inline)、支付战争赔款、停止威胁和侮辱，并停止对伊朗在该地区民兵盟友的军事行动，伊朗才会[开放海峡](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-strait-of-hormuz-trump-talks-ec566cd7?mod=article_inline)。

美国官员表示，特朗普很有耐心，预计将安然度过最新的外交僵局，尤其是在汽油价格保持现状的情况下。据美国汽车协会（AAA）数据，周六全国平均汽油价格为每加仑4.02美元，高于一年前的3.16美元，但比一周前略有下降。

一名白宫官员坚称，美国已完成对伊朗的所有军事目标。该官员表示，特朗普现在的重点是确保世界能源通过霍尔木兹海峡的流动，如果伊朗进一步瞄准船只，他将保留军事选项。

官员们补充说，在核问题担忧方面，特朗普近几周私下告诉高级助手，在美国去年摧毁三个主要核设施后，德黑兰可能无法在他的总统任期内恢复核活动。

总统在近几次会议中表示，美国的情报能力很可能发现伊朗重建这些设施或秘密制造炸弹的任何企图，他相信美国更多袭击的威胁将起到持久威慑作用。

周六，德黑兰一幅描绘伊朗已故最高领袖的壁画。图片来源：Atta Kenare/AFP/盖蒂图片社

官员们表示，如果美国能够控制核计划，且霍尔木兹海峡恢复通航，那么特朗普更有可能无限期延长当前的停火。他们补充说，如果德黑兰完全重新开放海峡，特朗普预计还将解除美国对伊朗港口的军事封锁。

特朗普政府面临的一个主要复杂问题是资金。伊朗除了要求达成协议解冻其在全球的资产外，还寻求数十亿美元的战争赔款。特朗普曾表示，白宫不会允许任何纳税人的钱流向伊朗，尽管如果没有美国解冻部分资产（可能附带条件），协议似乎不太可能达成。

关于美国是否应签发豁免以允许伊朗在公开市场出售石油，也存在争议。作为与伊朗达成的[临时和平协议](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/an-annotated-analysis-of-a-u-s-draft-of-the-iran-deal-6a9ec49f?mod=article_inline)的一部分，美国去年6月同意了这些豁免。在伊朗袭击海峡船只后，该停火破裂，美国重新对伊朗石油贸易实施制裁。

特朗普最近的言论表明他正在寻找出路。

美国对伊朗港口的封锁意味着霍尔木兹海峡“眼下算是开放的”，特朗普周四对记者表示，不过总统承认德黑兰仍可能用导弹、无人机和水雷毁伤船只。他还暗示，伊朗埋藏在地下的铀库存不成问题。

“我们已经拥有核材料，因为它深埋地下，”特朗普在北大西洋公约组织（NATO）7月峰会上说。“除了我们，没人能拿到它。”

## Trump Thought Opening the Strait of Hormuz Was Imminent. Iran Had Other Plans.

## **Trump Thought Opening the Strait of Hormuz Was Imminent. Iran Had Other Plans.**

### Tehran’s new demands for major concessions come days after the White House had signaled a deal to clear commercial passage was within reach

By

Alexander Ward

Aug. 9, 2026 7:00 am ET

Commercial vessels anchored last week in the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Bandar Abbas, Iran. Amirhosein Khorgooi/ISNA/AP

- Iran’s new demands to reopen the Strait of Hormuz have complicated President Trump’s efforts to find an off-ramp from the conflict.
- Tehran is demanding billions of dollars, the removal of U.S. troops from the region and an end to the naval blockade in exchange for allowing the free flow of traffic in the waterway.
- Trump has privately signaled to senior aides that he is willing to walk away from the conflict without a nuclear deal with Iran.
- Iran’s new demands to reopen the Strait of Hormuz have complicated President Trump’s efforts to find an off-ramp from the conflict.**View more**
WASHINGTON—For weeks, President Trump had been laying the groundwork to declare victory in the [Iran](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran) war should Tehran fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz, even floating the idea privately to senior aides that he’s willing to walk away without a nuclear deal, U.S. officials said.

But that scaled-back objective became more difficult when [Iran insisted](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/u-a-e-says-iran-attacked-one-of-its-ships-in-hormuz-bfbec3b2?mod=article_inline) Saturday on its highest price yet for permitting the free flow of traffic in the waterway, seeking billions of dollars in U.S. payments, the removal of American troops from the region and an end of the U.S. naval blockade, among other things**.**

This rapid chain of events suggests Trump’s options for walking away from the conflict have again narrowed. Trump has repeatedly threatened—and then backed away from—a [massive escalation of its bombing campaign](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/it-isnt-just-pickaxe-trumps-other-potential-nuclear-targets-in-iran-975e960e?mod=article_inline). But the recent chaos in negotiations signaled Tehran is prepared for a long and grinding conflict.

“Iran’s recent hardline demands for reopening the strait make it increasingly difficult for the president to manufacture a face-saving off-ramp from the conflict,” said Mona Yacoubian, director of the Middle East program at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, a Washington think tank.

Gregory Brew, an Iran expert at the Eurasia Group consulting firm, said, “The Iranians fully believe Trump wants out and is focused on opening the strait, which is why they are playing hardball.”

Hanging over diplomatic talks since April has been the White House’s ultimatum that Iran will never obtain a nuclear weapon. Trump sought to include nuclear talks as part of broader negotiations with Iran, but those have proven too difficult, leading the White House in recent weeks to focus more on [reopening the Strait of Hormuz](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/hormuz-opening-uncertain-after-new-strike-hurdles-in-talks-e2dfd2ce?mod=article_inline).

Iran’s ability to essentially choke off the waterway through sporadic missile and drone attacks has proven to be powerful leverage for the country, stalling commercial traffic and roiling global oil markets. Trump has declared repeatedly in recent weeks that the Strait of Hormuz was already fully open, but each of those promises has proven premature.

Now, with less than three months until the midterm elections and the price of gasoline still much higher than it was before the war started in late February, Trump has been looking for ways to tell the American public that the war is won.

President Trump has said the White House won’t allow any taxpayer money to go to Iran. Julia Demaree Nikhinson/AP

In recent days, Trump told reporters that he was prepared to launch the biggest military attack since World War II but [was talked down by U.S. allies](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-stokes-the-middle-east-war-with-drone-attacks-on-kuwait-f25530a0?mod=article_inline). He insisted that a deal was “imminent” although one hasn’t materialized yet.

On Friday, the president on [social media](https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/117054491639962487) suggested victory was already in hand even without a nuclear agreement, linking to an article declaring “Donald Trump Won the Iran War.”

Vice President JD Vance told Fox News that the U.S. would continue to pressure Iran to make long-term changes in its relationship with Washington. “And if not, that’s fine, too, we’re just going to keep on applying the pressure we can apply, and getting as much oil and gas out of the Middle East so that Americans can enjoy lower gas and energy prices.”

“We’re in the middle of the game,” Vance said.

Tehran, which closed the waterway once the war began in February, has insisted that no American warships be allowed to traverse the waterway and has pushed to collect revenue from commercial vessels. Washington has said it won’t accept Iranian restrictions or tolling, U.S. officials said.

The prospects of a deal appeared to darken over the weekend when the United Arab Emirates said Iran launched a missile attack on one of its ships and a top Iranian official laid out a series of tough demands for allowing traffic through.

Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, said the U.S. would need to permanently end the war, lift its naval blockade, withdraw its forces, end all sanctions, free [Iran’s frozen assets](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-frozen-assets-7e926b39?mod=article_inline), pay war reparations, end threats and insults, and stop military action against Iran’s militia allies around the region before Iran would [open the strait](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-strait-of-hormuz-trump-talks-ec566cd7?mod=article_inline), according to a report on state news agency IRNA.

U.S. officials said Trump is patient and expected to ride out the latest diplomatic snarl, especially so long as gas prices remain where they are. The average price of gasoline was $4.02 a gallon on Saturday, according to AAA. That is up from $3.16 a year ago but down a bit from a week ago.

A White House official insisted that the U.S. completed all of its military objectives against Iran. Trump’s focus now is securing the flow of the world’s energy through the Strait of Hormuz and will keep military options on the table should Iran further target ships, the official said.

On the nuclear concerns, Trump has told senior aides privately in recent weeks that Tehran is likely unable to revive its nuclear work during his presidency after the U.S. destroyed three primary nuclear sites last year, the officials added.

U.S. intelligence capabilities would likely catch any Iranian attempts to rebuild those facilities or covertly build a bomb, the president has said in recent meetings, and he is confident the threat of more American attacks would serve as an enduring deterrent.

A mural depicting Iran's late supreme leader in Tehran on Saturday. Atta Kenare/AFP/Getty Images

If the U.S. can keep the nuclear program in check and traffic resumes in the strait, then Trump is more likely to extend the current ceasefire indefinitely, the officials said. Trump also is expected to lift the U.S. military blockade on Iranian ports if Tehran fully reopens the strait, they added.

One major complication for the Trump administration is money. Iran is seeking billions of dollars in war reparations in addition to an agreement to unfreeze the country’s assets around the world. Trump has said the White House wouldn’t allow any taxpayer money to go to Iran, although a deal appears unlikely without the U.S. unfreezing some assets, possibly with conditions attached.

There also is a dispute about whether the U.S. should issue waivers to let Iran sell oil on the open market. The U.S. had agreed to the waivers last June as part of an [interim peace agreement](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/an-annotated-analysis-of-a-u-s-draft-of-the-iran-deal-6a9ec49f?mod=article_inline) with Iran. That ceasefire fell apart after Iran attacked ships in the strait, and the U.S. reinstated sanctions on the Iranian oil trade.

Trump’s recent comments indicate he is looking for an exit.

The U.S. blockade on Iranian ports meant the Strait of Hormuz was “sort of open right now,” Trump told reporters on Thursday, though the president acknowledged Tehran could still damage ships with missiles, drones and mines. He also has suggested Iran’s buried uranium stockpile was a nonissue.

“We’ve already got the nuclear material, because it’s so far underground,” Trump said during July’s summit of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization. “Nobody’s going to be able to get it except us.”
