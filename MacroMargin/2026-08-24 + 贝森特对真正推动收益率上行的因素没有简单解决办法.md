### 中文译文

## 贝森特对真正推动收益率上行的因素没有简单解决办法

财政部长斯科特·贝森特上任时抨击前任试图重新改造全球最大债券市场。上周，他自己也尝试了一下。

通过回购大量美国长期债务（这将需要出售更多[短期证券](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/treasury-s-potentially-limitless-buybacks-cloud-t-bill-outlook)），贝森特周四表示，他正在实施“我所说的‘国债扭转’”。这是对美联储[著名的20世纪60年代计划](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2013-10-31/yalies-yellen-hamada-putting-tobin-s-twist-theory-to-work-in-qe)的致敬，该计划旨在调整国债收益率。贝森特表示，目前这些收益率与“均衡”水平不符。

国债的确发生了扭转——但只持续了一天。该计划[宣布](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/long-dated-treasuries-rally-as-treasury-boosts-bond-buybacks)后，周三长期债券收益率大幅下降。但随后又直接回升。贝森特[青睐的](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-05/bessent-says-he-trump-focus-on-10-year-yields-not-pushing-fed)10年期基准收益率本周收于4.73%，接近他上任以来的最高水平，周一则下跌了3个基点。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481b9b382febc34f12145/3c681cc9-0b14-81af-9383-db15bd82af15-1dd0111aeb230b920e39f4c01856405a.png)

所有这一切都表明，这位财政部长降低借贷成本的努力——尤其是在11月中期选举临近之际——正遇到他无法控制的力量，而这些力量正在推高借贷成本。

这其中包括创纪录的债务水平，不仅是在美国（本周一项指标[超过40万亿美元](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-public-debt-hits-40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk)），而且在发达国家也是如此。在人工智能热潮的带动下，[企业债券发行](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/record-pace-of-bond-sales-jolts-europe-out-of-its-summer-lull)也激增。自唐纳德·特朗普总统因与伊朗开战而扰乱能源市场以来，通胀飙升，而对美联储主席凯文·沃什策略的困惑加剧了投资者的担忧。

“任何让长期债券收益率持续缓解的路径，都要经过政府不想要的东西，”Satori Insights创始人马特·金表示。他表示，美国预算赤字缩小、股市下跌或人工智能投资下降，都可能压低长期收益率。

### **‘回归常态’**

一些市场参与者认为，一开始就没有什么不正常。

“我认为我们已经回归正常利率水平，4%到5%是正常的，”爱德华·亚德尼——他创造了“[债券义警](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-11-17/why-bond-vigilantes-are-stirring-in-age-of-trump-quicktake-q-a)”这一说法——在贝森特意外举动前约一小时对彭博电视台如此表示。尽管美国财政部表示其干预是为了支持流动性，但摩根大通公司的利率策略团队周四报告称，“市场运行今年已明显改善。”

贝森特对收益率曲线控制（即影响不同期限利率）的设想，不仅限于美国国债。它还包括所谓的“超大规模企业”，即那些大举投资人工智能并为此举债的公司。本月早些时候，Alphabet Inc. 出售了[期限长达40年](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/alphabet-returns-to-bond-market-amid-ai-spending-worries)的债券。

财长本周表示，这些投资最终将以更快且无通胀的经济增长形式获得回报——但与此同时，“这正在引发短期的[资本竞争](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything)”。他说：“如果我坐在首席财务官的位子上，我会考虑更多发行所谓的‘腹部债务’，也就是五年期债券。”

![Scott Bessent Photographer: Yuri Gripas/Abaca/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481b9b382febc34f12145/3c681cc9-0b14-81bc-b49f-dfec07ddc8e7-cffb41b02715ba927a19e87c7c9b0a1d.png)

这些明显的塑造收益率的尝试，甚至引发了关于现在是否存在“贝森特看跌期权”的辩论，这让人想起前美联储主席艾伦·格林斯潘总是会救助股市的旧信念。

荷兰国际集团全球市场主管克里斯·特纳是使用这一措辞的人之一，不过许多人怀疑贝森特是否有足够火力对债券收益率实现类似的效果。

美国财政部未回应就贝森特债券市场干预置评的请求。

### **“坏信息”**

在谈到美国国债收益率攀升时，贝森特表示投资者正依据“坏信息”行事，而他掌握着“不对称”的真实情况。

“关于赤字的实际情况，存在很多错误信息，”他说，并承诺将重新聚焦在他所称的特朗普财政整合计划上。

“贝森特无法控制通胀预期，也无法强制压低名义长期利率，因此利用回购来清除部分流动性较低的久期证券。不过，最新计划必须让投资者相信，计划中的回购是通向更好债务轨迹的桥梁，而非在不解决赤字的情况下压制收益率的努力。” 艾丽斯·安德烈斯，MLIV宏观策略师。

未来几天，贝森特表示，他和白宫预算负责人拉斯·沃特“将研究我们在收入和成本方面能做什么。”他建议打击欺诈，并减少对州的转移支付。

埃隆·马斯克领导的政府效率部去年曾尝试类似做法，但监督机构发现，马斯克的DOGE服务夸大了其节省总额，并对其支出削减估计也有夸大。

“我们怀疑政府目前能在赤字问题上采取任何实质性措施，” Evercore ISI 首席策略师莎拉·比安奇在报告中写道。

除了财政部的利息账单——目前每年超过[1万亿美元](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/us-budget-deficit-surged-in-july-on-acceleration-in-spending)—社会保障、联邦医疗保险和医疗补助支出是财政赤字的主要驱动因素，预计今年财政赤字约占GDP的6%。比安奇写道，改革这些福利计划“在短期内是行不通的”，如果11月之后民主党赢得至少一个议院，那就更是如此。

### **贝森特 vs 沃什？**

贝森特职权范围内的是改革债务销售和回购。

此举是在美国财政部对发行指引进行更广泛调整两周后做出的。分析师表示，这为削减最长期限国债（即收益率最高的国债）的销售打开了大门。

这些举措看起来很像珍妮特·耶伦的债券发行策略，而贝森特曾 [批评](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-07/trump-ally-bessent-says-yellen-using-post-to-aid-biden-2024-bid) 过这种做法。它们也表明与沃什存在隐性分歧。

沃什非但没有指出收益率失衡，反而几乎认可了它们的上涨。尽管美联储没有在通胀上升的情况下收紧政策，但“市场已经，联邦储备系统理事会主席凯文·沃什举行新闻发布会，日程最终，”他在7月29日表示。“市场价格将继续按他们认为合适的方向和幅度做出反应。”

沃什自己也将迎来关键沟通时刻，他将于周五在堪萨斯城联储年度杰克逊霍尔研讨会上发表讲话。

投资者将关注他是否会试图在上月一场反应不佳的新闻发布会后修复信誉。沃什未能阐明维持利率不变的理由，回避了未来几个月可能加息的任何暗示，并暗示美联储可能在1月份调整通胀目标。

“我们觉得贝森特的行动让沃什陷入了有些困难的境地，”加拿大皇家银行BlueBay资产管理公司固定收益首席投资官马克·道丁写道。

### **‘翻转剧本’**

“如果沃什真的解释了他们将如何提供指标、如何使用这些信息，并且未来三到六个月有一个行动计划，那他就真的翻转了剧本，”三菱日联金融集团美国宏观策略主管乔治·贡萨尔维斯说。“至少要让市场知道该关注什么。”

沃什（Warsh）希望改革中央银行的资产负债表，目前该表上约有4.54万亿美元的美国国债。他还谈到了新的“美联储-财政部协议”，但没有详细说明[那将涉及什么](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-08/warsh-call-for-fed-treasury-accord-stirs-debate-bond-market)。

1951年的原始协议大幅限制了美联储在债券市场的影响，并结束了收益率曲线控制策略。如果美国政策制定者现在真的想压低借贷成本，可能需要反其道而行之。

摩根大通和桥水联合基金资深人士、现任外交关系委员会高级研究员的丽贝卡·帕特森（Rebecca Patterson）表示，“回购[更多是信号而非实质](https://www.cfr.org/articles/what-the-treasurys-buyback-surprise-says-about-the-bond-market)”，即使规模更大也不会改变市场动态。“更有效且可持续的政策途径是通过美联储的量化宽松。”

沃什在2010年代初担任美联储理事时反对量化宽松，此后一直是量化宽松最激烈的批评者之一。

如果二人不做出这样的转变，那么设定收益率曲线的将是投资者。

摩根大通资产管理公司投资组合经理普里亚·米斯拉（Priya Misra）表示：“经济一直保持韧性，全球资本竞争激烈，利率走高是有道理的。”

### English Original

## Bessent Has No Easy Fix for What’s Really Driving Yields Up

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent came to office blasting his predecessor for trying to re-engineer the world’s largest bond market. Last week he took a stab at it himself.

By buying back a swath of long-term US debt, which will require selling more [short-dated securities](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/treasury-s-potentially-limitless-buybacks-cloud-t-bill-outlook), Bessent said Thursday he’ll be doing “what I would call a Treasury twist.” It was a nod to the Federal Reserve’s [famous 1960s plan](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2013-10-31/yalies-yellen-hamada-putting-tobin-s-twist-theory-to-work-in-qe) to rejigger Treasury yields. Right now, Bessent said, those yields are out of whack with “equilibrium” levels.

And twist Treasuries did — for a day. Yields on the long bonds dropped sharply on Wednesday after the plan was [announced](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/long-dated-treasuries-rally-as-treasury-boosts-bond-buybacks). But then they climbed straight back up. Bessent’s [favored](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-05/bessent-says-he-trump-focus-on-10-year-yields-not-pushing-fed) 10-year benchmark closed the week at 4.73%, near the highest since he took office, before falling three basis points on Monday.

All of this suggests that the Treasury chief’s drive to get borrowing costs down, especially with November’s midterm election looming, is running into forces beyond his control that are pushing them up.

That includes record debt levels not just in the US, where one gauge [surpassed $40 trillion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/us-public-debt-hits-40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk) this week, but across developed nations. There’s a surge in [corporate issuance](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/record-pace-of-bond-sales-jolts-europe-out-of-its-summer-lull) too, led by the artificial intelligence boom. Inflation has jumped since President Donald Trump upended energy markets by starting a war with Iran, and confusion over Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh’s strategy is adding to investor concerns.

“Every route to lasting relief for the long end runs through something the administration doesn’t want,” said Matt King, founder of Satori Insights. He said a smaller US budget deficit, a slide in the stock market or a decline in AI investment could bring longer-term yields down.

### **‘Back to Normal’**

Some market participants don’t reckon there was anything out of whack to begin with.

“I think we are back to normal interest rates, 4% to 5% is normal,” Edward Yardeni — who coined the term “[bond vigilantes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-11-17/why-bond-vigilantes-are-stirring-in-age-of-trump-quicktake-q-a)” — told Bloomberg TV about an hour before Bessent’s shock move. And while the Treasury said its intervention was to support liquidity, JPMorgan Chase & Co.’s rates strategy desk reported Thursday that “market functioning has improved notably this year.”

Bessent’s vision of yield-curve control, or influencing rates across different maturities, extends beyond Treasuries. It includes the so-called hyperscalers, companies pouring money into AI and borrowing to do it. Earlier this month, Alphabet Inc. sold bonds [ranging up to 40 years](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/alphabet-returns-to-bond-market-amid-ai-spending-worries).

The investment will pay off eventually in the form of faster and non-inflationary economic growth — but meantime “it is causing a short-term [competition for capital](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything),” the Treasury chief said this week. “If I were sitting in the chief financial officer’s seat, I would think about issuing more what’s called the belly debt,” or five-year maturities.

The apparent attempts to shape yields even prompted debate over whether there’s now a “Bessent put,” an echo of the old belief that former Fed Chair Alan Greenspan would always bail out the stock market.

Chris Turner, global head of markets at ING Groep NV, was among those using the term, though many doubt that Bessent has the firepower to pull off anything similar for bond yields.

The Treasury didn’t respond to a request for comment about Bessent’s bond-market interventions.

### **‘Bad Information’**

Addressing the climb in Treasury yields, Bessent said investors are acting on “bad information” while he has “asymmetric” access to the real picture.

“There’s been a lot of misinformation in terms of what’s going on with the deficit,” he said, vowing to refocus attention on what he described as Trump’s fiscal-consolidation program.

“Bessent cannot control inflation expectations nor force nominal long rates down, thus the use of buybacks to remove some less-liquid duration securities from circulation. The latest plan though must convince investors that planned buybacks are a bridge to a better debt trajectory rather than an effort to suppress yields without addressing deficits.” Alyce Andres, MLIV macro strategist.

In coming days, Bessent said he and White House budget chief Russ Vought “will be examining both on the revenue side and the cost side what we can do.” He suggested a crackdown on fraud, and reductions in transfers to states.

The Elon Musk-led Department of Government Efficiency attempted something on similar lines last year, but Musk’s DOGE Service Inflated Its Savings Totals, Watchdog Finds of its own estimates for spending cuts.

“We are skeptical the administration can realistically do anything at this point on the deficit that would be material,” Sarah Bianchi, chief strategist at Evercore ISI, wrote in a note.

Besides the Treasury’s interest bill — now running well in excess of [$1 trillion a year](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/us-budget-deficit-surged-in-july-on-acceleration-in-spending) — Social Security, Medicare and Medicaid spending are the main drivers of a fiscal deficit forecast at around 6% of GDP this year. Overhauling those entitlement programs is “a non-starter in the near term,” and even more so after November if Democrats win at least one chamber of Congress, Bianchi wrote.

### **Bessent vs Warsh?**

What does lie within Bessent’s authority is revamping debt sales and buybacks.

The move came two weeks after a tweak in the Treasury’s broader forward guidance on issuance. That, analysts said, opened the door to potential cuts in sales of the longest-dated securities — the ones with the highest yields.

Such steps look a lot like the debt-issuance tactics of Janet Yellen, which Bessent used to [criticize](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-07/trump-ally-bessent-says-yellen-using-post-to-aid-biden-2024-bid). They also point to an implicit split with Warsh.

Far from calling out yields as out-of-equilibrium, Warsh has come close to endorsing their rise. While the Fed hadn’t tightened policy in the face of higher inflation, “markets have Federal Reserve System Board Of Governors Chairman Kevin Warsh holds a news conference, sked FINAL,” he said on July 29. “Market prices will continue to respond in the direction and magnitude they see fit.”

Warsh has his own key communications moment looming, in his speech Friday at the Kansas City Fed’s annual Jackson Hole symposium.

Investors will be watching to see whether he’ll seek to repair credibility after a poorly received press conference last month. Warsh failed to articulate a rationale for keeping rates unchanged, avoided any suggestion they would be raised in the coming months and suggested the Fed’s inflation target could be altered in January.

“It strikes us that Bessent’s actions put Warsh in something of a difficult position,” wrote Mark Dowding, chief investment officer, fixed-income at RBC BlueBay Asset Management.

### **‘Flip the Script’**

“Warsh would really flip the script if he actually explained how they’re going to provide metrics, how he’s using the information, and there’s a game plan for the next three to six months,” said George Goncalves, head of US macro strategy at MUFG. “At least provide markets with what to look out for.”

Warsh wants to revamp the central bank’s balance sheet, which currently features some $4.54 trillion of Treasuries. He’s also spoken of a new “Fed-Treasury accord,” without spelling out [what that would involve](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-08/warsh-call-for-fed-treasury-accord-stirs-debate-bond-market).

The original pact, in 1951, dramatically limited the Fed’s footprint in the bond market and ended the strategy of yield-curve control. If US policymakers really want to drive borrowing costs down now, it might require a move in the opposite direction.

“Buybacks are [more signal than substance](https://www.cfr.org/articles/what-the-treasurys-buyback-surprise-says-about-the-bond-market)” and even larger ones wouldn’t shift market dynamics, according to Rebecca Patterson, a JPMorgan and Bridgewater Associates veteran who’s now a senior fellow at the Council on Foreign Relations. “The more effective — and sustainable — policy approach is through Fed quantitative easing.”

Warsh opposed QE in the early 2010s when he was on the Fed board, and has been one of its most vociferous critics since.

Absent such an about-face by the duo, it’s investors who are setting the yield curve.

“The economy has been resilient and there is a global competition for capital,” said Priya Misra, a portfolio manager at JPMorgan Asset Management. “It makes sense that rates have been moving higher.”
