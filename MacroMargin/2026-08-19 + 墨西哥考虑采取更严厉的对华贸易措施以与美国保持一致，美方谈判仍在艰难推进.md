### 中文译文

## 墨西哥考虑采取更严厉的对华贸易措施以与美国保持一致，美方谈判仍在艰难推进

### **要点**

- 墨西哥正在考虑对来自中国和其他国家的部分产品施加进一步的贸易限制，同时对其他产品提高现有进口关税。
- 经济部和财政部正在评估哪些产品可能面临新的关税，钢铁产品和车辆是主要候选对象。
- 正在考虑的计划旨在加强墨西哥与华盛顿的一致立场，支持国内制造业的增长，同时减少对中国进口的依赖。
据四位知情人士透露，墨西哥正在考虑对来自中国和其他国家的部分产品实施进一步贸易限制，同时提高对其他产品的现有进口关税。

所谓的反倾销措施将加深墨西哥与华盛顿的一致性，因为总统克劳迪娅·欣鲍姆寻求将北美贸易协定（USMCA）延长多年，同时在国内投资低迷之际支持她推动国内制造业增长。

墨西哥经济部和财政部正在评估哪些目前未被双边贸易协定覆盖的产品可能面临新的关税，哪些产品可能面临更高的税率。据其中一位知情人士称，钢铁产品和车辆是主要候选对象。

经济部在一份声明中表示，目前没有实施新关税调整的具体计划或提案。但负责对外贸易的经济部表示，正在与商业界进行持续磋商，这些磋商类似于导致设计针对亚洲进口商品的关税方案并今年早些时候实施的磋商。

墨西哥表示将继续对涉嫌以低于生产成本的价格倾销的进口商品进行逐案调查，这可能导致征收新的关税。

墨西哥财政部拒绝置评。

1月，墨西哥对约1500类来自未与其签署自由贸易协定国家的产品提高了关税，[最高达50%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-11/mexico-aligns-with-us-on-tougher-tariffs-on-chinese-asian-goods)，这是辛鲍姆为保护本土生产商并缓解与美国总统唐纳德·特朗普紧张关系所做努力的一部分。受影响产品包括汽车、汽车零部件和钢铁，其中中国进口商品受这些进口关税的影响更大。

在特朗普[决定不再续签](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-01/us-decides-against-renewing-usmca-shifting-to-rolling-talks)这项支撑墨西哥享受美国市场优惠准入的协定后，美墨加协定前景未卜，墨西哥正在考虑的新贸易措施由此而来。取而代之的是，该协定进入滚动年度审议程序。

特朗普政府已要求墨西哥政府对中国钢铁和铝征收关税，以与美国依据《美国法典》第232条加征的关税保持一致。这一要求标志着美国试图在保留美国、墨西哥和加拿大产品更优惠待遇的同时，建立针对中国的共同政策。

据彭博社上月报道，辛鲍姆愿意将这一要求作为美墨加协定审议的一部分来推进。

正在考虑的计划还旨在强化“墨西哥计划”，这是辛鲍姆提出的以国家为主导的经济发展蓝图，包括税收减免和其他激励措施，旨在吸引私人投资并扩大本地供应链。

官员们押注，额外的关税保护和“墨西哥计划”的激励措施能促使企业在本地增加支出，同时减少对中国进口的依赖。

钢铁制造商、纺织品和其他服装生产商以及重型车辆制造商一直呼吁加强对低成本亚洲进口商品的保护，声称不公平的贸易做法削弱了生产并导致失业。

墨西哥对关税的采纳导致来自某些国家的特定进口产品大幅下降，尤其是中国——中国与墨西哥没有自由贸易协定。根据经济部提供的数据，今年前五个月，受关税影响的中国商品价值同比下降了近三分之一。

这一下滑在中国轻型汽车、汽车零部件和鞋类进口中尤为明显，这些产品的进口量均下降超过40%，凸显出自墨西哥关税生效以来贸易流向的变化。

## Mexico Weighs Tougher Trade Rules for China as US Talks Grind On

### **Takeaways**

- Mexico is weighing plans to impose further trade restrictions on selected products from China and other countries while raising existing import taxes on others.
- The economy and finance ministries are evaluating which products might face new duties, with steel products and vehicles among the leading candidates.
- The plans under consideration aim to strengthen Mexico's alignment with Washington and support the growth of domestic manufacturing, while also reducing dependence on Chinese imports.
Mexico is weighing plans to impose further trade restrictions on selected products from China and other countries while raising existing import taxes on others, according to four people familiar with the matter.

The so-called anti-dumping measures would deepen Mexico’s alignment with Washington as President Claudia Sheinbaum pursues a multi-year extension of the North American trade pact known as the USMCA, while also supporting her push to grow domestic manufacturing at a time of lackluster investment at home.

Mexico’s economy and finance ministries are evaluating which products that aren’t currently covered by a bilateral trade deal might face new duties, and which could be subject to higher rates. Steel products and vehicles are among the leading candidates, according to one of the people.

There is currently no concrete plan or proposal to implement new tariff adjustments, the economy ministry said in a statement. But the ministry, which oversees foreign trade, said it’s engaged in ongoing consultations with businesses that are similar to those that led to the design of a tariff package targeting Asian imports and implemented earlier this year.

It said it would continue case-by-case investigations into alleged dumping of imports below their production costs, which could lead to the imposition of new duties.

The finance ministry declined to comment.

In January, Mexico raised tariffs [as high as 50%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-11/mexico-aligns-with-us-on-tougher-tariffs-on-chinese-asian-goods) on roughly 1,500 categories of products from nations that haven’t inked a free trade deal with it, part of Sheinbaum’s efforts to protect local producers as well as ease tensions with US President Donald Trump. The affected products included vehicles, auto parts and steel, with Chinese imports facing more impacts from the import taxes.

The new trade measures under consideration come as the USMCA hangs in the balance, after Trump [decided not to renew](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-01/us-decides-against-renewing-usmca-shifting-to-rolling-talks) the deal that underpins Mexico’s preferential access to the US market. That set in motion rolling annual reviews instead.

The Trump administration has asked the Mexican government to adopt tariffs on Chinese steel and aluminum that mirror duties Washington has imposed under Section 232 of US law. The request marked an attempt to preserve more favorable treatment for products from the US, Mexico and Canada while building up a common policy against Beijing.

Sheinbaum is open to pursuing that request as part of the USMCA review, Bloomberg reported last month.

The plans under consideration also aim to strengthen Plan Mexico, Sheinbaum’s state-led economic development blueprint that includes tax breaks and other incentives meant to lure private investment and expand local supply chains.

Officials are betting that added tariff protection and Plan Mexico incentives can spur businesses to spend more locally while reducing dependence on Chinese imports.

Steelmakers, textile and other apparel producers and heavy vehicle manufacturers have pushed for stronger protections from low-cost Asian imports, alleging that unfair trade practices have undercut production and cost jobs.

Mexico’s embrace of tariffs has led to a sharp decline in selected imports from some countries, particularly China, which doesn’t have a free trade deal with Mexico. During the first five months of this year, the value of Chinese goods covered by the tariffs fell by nearly a third from the same period last year, according to data provided by the economy ministry.

The slide was especially pronounced for imports of Chinese light vehicles, auto parts and footwear, all of which dropped over 40%, underscoring the shift in trade flows since the Mexican tariffs took effect.
