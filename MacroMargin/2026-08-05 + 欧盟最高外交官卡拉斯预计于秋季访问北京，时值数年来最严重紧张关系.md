### 中文译文

## 欧盟最高外交官卡拉斯预计于秋季访问北京，时值数年来最严重紧张关系

## **欧盟最高外交官卡拉斯预计于秋季访问北京，时值数年来最严重紧张关系**

这将是卡娅·卡拉斯自2024年上任以来首次单独访问中国。

据多位知情人士透露，欧盟外交与安全政策高级代表卡娅·卡拉斯预计将于秋季访问中国，这是她自去年夏天一场激烈的[布鲁塞尔交锋](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3316707/wang-yi-tells-top-eu-officials-brussels-unite-against-unilateralism-and-bullying)以来，首次与外交部长王毅面对面会晤。

这也将是卡拉斯自2024年上任以来首次单独访问北京。

此次访问的日期尚未确定，将围绕双边“战略对话”展开，该对话大多是每年一次，双方在对话中讨论外交和安全政策问题。

欧盟议程上的首要议题将包括中俄关系、持续的中东危机以及台湾海峡的紧张局势等热点问题。

在上一次此类会议上，王毅令他的欧洲对话者震惊，他告诉他们，[北京不希望俄罗斯输掉战争](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3316875/china-tells-eu-it-cannot-afford-russian-loss-ukraine-war-sources-say)，因为那将意味着美国将把全部注意力转向亚洲。

内部人士称，这场会晤从一开始就气氛紧张，出现了一些小的礼仪失误，卡拉斯在镜头前以咄咄逼人的姿态开场，就中国对俄罗斯的支持向王毅施压。

当时消息人士称，在欧方未主动举杯后，王毅觉得有必要提议祝酒，气氛进一步恶化。

此后，这位爱沙尼亚前总理对北京保持强硬立场。6月，她表示欧盟已核实有关中国军队“训练俄罗斯军事人员到乌克兰作战”的报告——北京否认这些指控。

通常情况下，该对话为中欧峰会设定政治议程，尽管预计今年不会举行此类峰会。虽然北京已提议由国务院总理李强赴布鲁塞尔参加峰会，但欧盟认为会谈应在习近平主席层面进行。

欧盟被告知，习近平无意访问比利时首都，而欧盟领导人不愿连续第三次赴北京举行峰会，担心在竞争加剧之际做出此举示弱的影响。

“欧中战略对话每年举行一次。高级代表的秋季出行计划尚未确定，但双方正在就时间表进行沟通，”卡拉斯办公室在一份声明中表示，其中提及她的正式头衔“欧盟外交与安全政策高级代表”。

尽管王毅和卡拉斯本月都出席了在马尼拉举行的年度东南亚国家联盟（东盟）外长会议，但双方没有进行双边会晤。

在会见包括美国国务卿马尔科·鲁比奥和新任英国外交大臣埃德·米利班德在内的多位同行后，王毅启程前往吉尔吉斯斯坦参加上海合作组织会议。

然而，在全体会议期间，中国外交部副部长[华春莹](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3264229/outspoken-head-chinas-foreign-ministry-press-corps-hua-chunying-promoted-deputy-foreign-minister)告诉卡拉斯，欢迎她在秋季来访。欧方被告知，虽然双方曾讨论过在马尼拉举行双边会晤，但日程问题使之未能实现。

卡拉斯此次北京之行正值欧中对话全面加强之际，但同时也处于极度紧张的时刻。双方正处于贸易战边缘，欧盟委员会已设定10月为重新平衡双边关系取得进展的最后期限，否则将承诺对北京采取更强硬的贸易政策。

与此同时，卡拉斯领导下的欧洲对外行动署（EEAS）本月发布了一份文件，指责中国与俄罗斯一道试图“按照自身利益重塑全球秩序”，并“助长势力范围逻辑的回归”。

该文件被视为欧盟迄今对北京最严厉的官方批评。文件指责北京既是俄罗斯对乌克兰战争的“关键推动者”，也是“至关重要的推动者”，这一措辞此前曾被北约使用。

中国否认了欧洲普遍认为其站在俄罗斯一边的看法，声称在乌克兰战争中保持中立。针对该文件，中国驻欧盟使团向欧盟事实上的外交部——欧洲对外行动署提出了外交照会。

在近期与欧洲官员的接触中，北京抨击了他们关于中国提供支持俄罗斯军事的两用商品的指控，坚称如果没有中国产品，乌克兰的战争努力也将无法继续。

这说明了欧洲在担忧北京可能切断对基辅的无人机零部件等产品供应的情况下，不得不走钢丝。这一困境在2024年底得到了体现。当时，来自多个国家的欧洲官员表示，新疆维吾尔自治区的一家无人机工厂正在为俄罗斯军方制造飞行器。

据多位知情人士透露，不久后，新上任的卡拉斯和欧洲理事会主席安东尼奥·科斯塔前往乌克兰。在那里，乌克兰总统泽连斯基告诉他们不要再谈论该工厂，以免危及基辅自身的供应链。

自去年7月在布鲁塞尔会面以来，王毅和卡拉斯已两次通话。第一次是在马来西亚举行的2025年东盟外长会议期间的一次短暂交流，就在他们在比利时的激烈会谈一周之后。

第二次是4月2日就伊朗危机进行的更实质性的通话，此前数月，北京一直对欧盟建立对话的努力保持沉默。

那次通话中，两人再次发生冲突——这次是在贸易问题上。据熟悉交流的消息人士透露，当卡拉斯解释欧盟最近的提案（包括《工业加速器法案》和《网络安全法案》）时，王毅猛烈回击。王毅表示，北京将进行强烈报复，欧洲最终会比中国受到更大伤害。

## Top EU diplomat Kallas expected to visit Beijing in autumn amid deepest strains in years

## **Top EU diplomat Kallas expected to visit Beijing in autumn amid deepest strains in years**

It will be Kaja Kallas’ first solo trip to China since taking on her role in 2024.

The European Union’s foreign affairs chief, Kaja Kallas, is expected to travel to China in the autumn for her first face-to-face meeting with Foreign Minister Wang Yi since a bruising [encounter in Brussels](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3316707/wang-yi-tells-top-eu-officials-brussels-unite-against-unilateralism-and-bullying) last summer, according to multiple people familiar with the planning.

It would also mark Kallas’ first solo trip to Beijing since taking on her role in 2024.

The trip – with a date yet to be confirmed – would revolve around the bilateral “strategic dialogue”, a mostly annual format in which the sides discuss foreign and security policy issues.

High on the EU agenda will be hot topics such as China’s relations with Russia, the ongoing crises in the Middle East and the tense situation in the Taiwan Strait.

At the last such meeting, Wang shocked his European interlocutors by telling them that [Beijing did not want Russia to lose the war](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3316875/china-tells-eu-it-cannot-afford-russian-loss-ukraine-war-sources-say) in Ukraine because that would mean the United States would turn its full attention to Asia.

Insiders described the encounter as tense from the outset, with minor protocol mix-ups and a combative on-camera opening from Kallas in which she pressed Wang over China’s support for Russia.

The mood further soured when Wang felt obliged to raise a toast after the Europeans had not done so, sources said at the time.

Since then, the former Estonian prime minister has maintained her tough line towards Beijing. In June, she said the bloc had verified reports that China’s military had been “training Russian military personnel to fight in Ukraine” – charges denied by Beijing.

Ordinarily, the dialogue sets the political agenda for an EU-China summit, although such an event is not expected to be held this year. While Beijing has offered to send Premier Li Qiang to a summit in Brussels, the EU believes the talks should take place at President Xi Jinping’s level.

Xi has no intention of travelling to the Belgian capital, the bloc has been told, while EU leaders are not keen to travel to Beijing for a third summit in a row, wary of the optics of such a concession at a time of heightened rivalry.

“The EU-China Strategic Dialogue is held annually. The HRVP’s travel plans for autumn are not set yet, but both sides are in touch regarding schedules,” Kallas’ office said in a statement, referring to her full title of High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy.

While both Wang and Kallas attended the annual Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) Foreign Ministers’ Meeting in Manila this month, there was no bilateral meeting.

After meeting several counterparts, including US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and new British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband, Wang left for a Shanghai Cooperation Organisation meeting in Kyrgyzstan.

During the plenary session, however, [Hua Chunying](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3264229/outspoken-head-chinas-foreign-ministry-press-corps-hua-chunying-promoted-deputy-foreign-minister), Chinese vice-minister of foreign affairs, told Kallas that she would be welcome in the autumn months. While the sides had discussed a bilateral meeting in Manila, scheduling issues prevented it, the EU side was told.

Kallas’ Beijing trip will come amid a ramping up of EU-China dialogue across the board, but also at a moment of extreme tension. The sides are on the brink of a trade war, with the European Commission setting an October deadline for progress in rebalancing ties. Otherwise, it has pledged to adopt a more forceful trade policy towards Beijing.

Kallas’ European External Action Service (EEAS), meanwhile, published a paper this month that accused China – along with Russia – of trying to “reshape the global order in line with their interests” and “fostering a return to a sphere-of-influence logic”.

The paper is seen as the bloc’s strongest-ever official criticism of Beijing. It accuses Beijing of being both a “key enabler” and “crucial enabler” of Russia’s war on Ukraine, language previously used by Nato.

China has denied the widely held perception in Europe that it has taken Russia’s side, claiming to be neutral in the Ukraine war. In response to the paper, its EU embassy issued a diplomatic demarche against the EEAS, the bloc’s de facto foreign office.

In recent encounters with European officials, Beijing has blasted their accusations that it provides dual-use goods that power Russia’s military, insisting that Ukraine’s war effort would also cease to function without products from China.

This illustrates the fine line Europe has to walk amid fears Beijing could cut off supplies of products such as drone components to Kyiv. The dilemma was illustrated late in 2024. At the time, European officials from several countries said that a drone factory in Xinjiang Uygur autonomous region was making craft for Russia’s military.

Soon afterwards, the freshly appointed Kallas and European Council President Antonio Costa went to Ukraine. There, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told them to stop talking about the factory lest it jeopardise Kyiv’s own supply lines, according to multiple people familiar with the exchange.

Since meeting in Brussels last July, Wang and Kallas have spoken twice. The first was a brief exchange on the margins of the 2025 Asean Foreign Ministers’ Meeting in Malaysia, a week after their explosive talks in Belgium.

The second was a more substantive call on April 2 to discuss the Iran crisis, scheduled after months of silence from Beijing in response to EU efforts to set up a conversation.

The pair clashed again in that call – this time over trade. As Kallas explained recent EU proposals, including the Industrial Accelerator Act and the Cybersecurity Act, Wang fired back with ferocity, according to sources familiar with the exchange. Wang said Beijing would retaliate fiercely and Europe would end up being hurt more than China.
