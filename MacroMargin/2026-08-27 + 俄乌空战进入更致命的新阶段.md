## 俄乌空战进入更致命的新阶段

两国正以远程无人机和导弹互相造成越来越大的破坏

![People watch dark smoke rising over buildings amid a Russian drone strike in Kyiv, Ukraine.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481f597f8f3cbac77a7d4/3c981cc9-0b14-8102-a308-f9c2c4dc7aca-b553a29afa53cd5d0cffb0b840367778.jpg)

本月乌克兰遭受了空中袭击的沉重打击。Oleksandr Ratushniak/路透社

上周，俄罗斯的[导弹和无人机齐射](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/dwindling-patriot-supplies-leave-kyiv-in-the-line-of-fire-21df615b?mod=article_inline)袭击了基辅周边的儿童医院、学校、公寓和仓库，造成至少16人死亡。[乌克兰](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/ukraine)由于缺乏爱国者拦截器，未能击落任何俄罗斯弹道导弹。

乌克兰本周的回应包括周三发动的一次无人机袭击，导致俄罗斯最大的炼油厂之一（位于下诺夫哥罗德附近）发生大火。与此同时，在俄罗斯中部坦波夫地区，电子商务巨头Wildberries的一个仓库起火。

[俄罗斯](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/russia)与乌克兰之间的空战已进入新阶段。随着空中武库的扩充和防空火力的不足，双方正在一场赤手空拳的较量中互相痛击，造成越来越广泛的破坏和不断上升的平民伤亡。

![Firefighters stand next to debris after recent strikes in Kyiv.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481f597f8f3cbac77a7d4/3c981cc9-0b14-810e-8d24-f7bea67ce794-cfc104f9d15e8695f2de7d51728042a7.jpg)

在基辅最近一次袭击后，消防员站在废墟旁。Tetiana Dzhafarova/法新社/盖蒂图片社

![The site of a strike in Kyiv last week.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481f597f8f3cbac77a7d4/3c981cc9-0b14-8145-82b9-eec574a533be-6b406d4646783989e72911fc331aa605.jpg)

上周基辅一处遭袭地点。Gleb Garanich/路透社

莫斯科正押注于能够摧毁[乌克兰平民百姓](https://www.wsj.com/world/russian-strikes-scar-a-kyiv-neighborhoodand-bring-it-together-6d787265?mod=article_inline)的意志，从而取得其[军队在乌克兰东部一直未能实现的突破](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/putin-overhauls-war-effort-in-ukraine-as-russia-seeks-momentum-61cfb9b1?mod=article_inline)。基辅则试图通过削弱[支撑其经济](https://www.wsj.com/world/why-ukraine-is-ramping-up-its-aerial-war-against-russia-b46dba14?mod=article_inline)的工业，使俄罗斯的入侵难以为继。

然而，双方似乎都没有接近崩溃。战争变得更加残酷和具有破坏性——但距离结束却更远了。

俄罗斯总统弗拉基米尔·普京威胁要扩大在乌克兰的打击目标范围，以回应基辅对其石油工业的袭击。上周他表示：“基辅政权试图破坏我们的经济。它做了什么？它自己打开了这个潘多拉魔盒。”

#### **俄罗斯的痛苦策略**

俄罗斯和乌克兰的远程空袭行动都使用无人机和导弹，但它们的战略不同。

俄罗斯于2022年秋季开始对乌克兰能源基础设施进行系统性打击，当时其入侵行动停滞，军队遭遇了一系列挫折。随着冬季临近，莫斯科转而将乌克兰城市陷入寒冷和黑暗之中。

连续四个冬天，这都没有奏效。乌克兰人设法保卫、修复或更换了足够的供暖和电力供应，以维持城市运转。但每年，对乌克兰电网的累积损害都在增加。

爱国者拦截导弹的短缺——自[美伊战争](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/russia-ukraine-iran-war-9722d527?mod=article_inline)以来在全球供应紧张——让基辅的气氛日益沉重。俄罗斯的远程打击今夏已导致数百名乌克兰平民死亡，其中“伊斯坎德尔-M”弹道导弹造成了大量伤亡。

许多基辅居民担心俄罗斯将试图摧毁该市的供水和污水处理系统，因为莫斯科在寻找制造人道主义危机的新方法。一些基辅人正在讨论，如果今年冬天首都的形势变得过于糟糕，是否暂时搬到乡下。很少有人相信俄罗斯会以这种方式取得胜利。

“理论上是，痛苦加上疲惫会产生政治屈服，”常驻维也纳的军事分析师、Gady Consulting负责人弗朗茨-斯特凡·加迪表示。但自2022年以来的经验，“以及此前一个世纪的战略轰炸经验表明，持续的空中打击会让社会变得更强硬，而不是使其崩溃，乌克兰的数据中没有任何相反迹象，”他说。

调查显示，乌克兰公众近几个月来越发反对向莫斯科做出领土让步。

#### **猎杀导弹发射车**

俄罗斯的打击未能阻止乌克兰国防技术行业的快速增长，该行业现在正在分散且隐蔽的设施中每年生产数百万架无人机。

由于西方盟友仅提供少量“爱国者”拦截弹，乌克兰正在研究其他方式破坏俄罗斯的导弹齐射。

![Wreckage of a Russian drone that crashed in Kharkiv, Ukraine.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481f597f8f3cbac77a7d4/3c981cc9-0b14-8127-a782-ea635bfd443a-d5e7406c5c2b9e43d077049846537bf3.jpg)

乌克兰哈尔科夫一架俄罗斯无人机的残骸。Maria Senovilla/EPA/Shutterstock

乌克兰无人机一直在摧毁俄罗斯南部邻近地区的防空系统，这可能为更大规模地猎杀俄罗斯导弹发射车（包括相对短程的伊斯坎德尔-M导弹的发射车）铺平道路。

“如果空域中布满无人机，可能会迫使导弹发射车隐藏或后撤，”基辅军事分析师、费城外交政策研究所高级研究员罗布·李表示。“这不是百分之百的解决办法，但可能让俄罗斯的日子更难过。”

李指出，弹道导弹并不是唯一的威胁。俄罗斯正在扩充其[喷气动力无人机](https://www.wsj.com/world/how-the-iranian-shahed-drone-transformed-warwith-chinas-help-2671bb43?mod=article_inline)武器库，例如以伊朗“沙赫德”无人机为基础的“天竺葵-5”（Geran-5），以及成本相对较低的“班德罗尔”（Banderol）巡航导弹。他说，这两种武器都比常规导弹便宜，能够大规模生产，并可能进一步消耗乌克兰的防空力量。

#### **无动于衷的沙皇**

过去一年，乌克兰的远程打击能力大幅提升。其纵深打击行动的范围已从炼油厂、能源出口基础设施和导弹零部件工厂，扩大至黑海航运以及俄罗斯最大电商连锁企业Wildberries和Ozon的仓库。这些打击已造成平民死亡，尽管规模不及俄罗斯的打击。

![Satellite image showing smoke rising from an Ozon warehouse in Russia.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481f597f8f3cbac77a7d4/3c981cc9-0b14-8182-88ff-f09f70f64d2d-56692c758669556954bc632b3e78d78d.jpg)

乌克兰的打击瞄准了俄罗斯电商企业。Vantor/路透社

![An employee works with a long-range drone at a production facility in Ukraine.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481f597f8f3cbac77a7d4/3c981cc9-0b14-8125-b111-c0bac48daf7e-31e0b1867fb180a0ab859f8c41197c42.jpg)

乌克兰的一处无人机生产设施。Valentyn Ogirenko/路透社

基辅宣称的目标是打击军事供应链，切断为莫斯科战争机器提供资金的出口收入，并造成日益扩大的经济混乱，让俄罗斯民众切身感受到战争。

然而，大多数俄罗斯问题观察人士怀疑，俄罗斯民众或商业精英的不满能否促使普京接受停火。他押注自己能够[压制异见](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/russia-yabloko-party-putin-ukraine-war-bf5e2c5b?mod=article_inline)，并且俄罗斯经济比乌克兰更能承受痛苦。

加利福尼亚州蒙特雷詹姆斯·马丁防扩散研究中心俄罗斯问题专家汉娜·诺特表示：“远程打击行动对俄罗斯社会和俄罗斯经济的影响必须大大恶化，才可能接近改变普京的盘算。”

尽管一些俄罗斯分析人士最近表示，莫斯科[无法实现其在乌克兰的战争目标](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/russias-elite-is-souring-on-the-war-putin-doesnt-seem-to-care-2430f3ff?mod=article_inline)，但诺特表示，“他们更多是出于对前线缺乏进展的担忧，而非远程打击行动的影响。”

加迪表示，战争的决定性变量仍然是军事人力，包括普京是否会在未来几个月内发起[大规模动员](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/russia-tests-readiness-for-new-round-of-military-mobilization-87d61b96?mod=article_inline)，以加强其地面攻势。

“远程打击是双方都在采取的行动，因为这是地面战争陷入僵局时他们仍能采取行动的领域，”他说，“它将稳步变得更加具有破坏性，但不会具有决定性。”

## The Russia-Ukraine Air War Has Entered a New, Deadlier Phase

Both countries are inflicting mounting destruction with long-range drones and missiles

Ukraine has been hammered by aerial attacks this month. Oleksandr Ratushniak/Reuters

A Russian [barrage of missiles and drones](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/dwindling-patriot-supplies-leave-kyiv-in-the-line-of-fire-21df615b?mod=article_inline) hit a children’s hospital, a school, apartments and warehouses around Kyiv last week, killing at least 16 people. [Ukraine](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/ukraine), lacking Patriot interceptors, didn’t shoot down any of Russia’s ballistic missiles.

Ukraine’s response this week included a drone strike that caused a massive fire at one of Russia’s largest oil refineries, near Nizhny Novgorod, on Wednesday. Meanwhile, in the central Russian region of Tambov, a warehouse belonging to e-commerce giant Wildberries burned.

The air war between [Russia](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/russia) and Ukraine has entered a new phase. With expanded aerial arsenals and inadequate air defenses, both sides are bludgeoning each other in a bare-knuckle fight that is causing a widening swath of destruction and a rising civilian toll.

Firefighters stand next to debris after recent strikes in Kyiv. Tetiana Dzhafarova/AFP/Getty Images

The site of a strike in Kyiv last week. Gleb Garanich/Reuters

Moscow is betting that it can crush the will of [Ukraine’s civilian population](https://www.wsj.com/world/russian-strikes-scar-a-kyiv-neighborhoodand-bring-it-together-6d787265?mod=article_inline), giving it the breakthrough that has [eluded its army in eastern Ukraine](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/putin-overhauls-war-effort-in-ukraine-as-russia-seeks-momentum-61cfb9b1?mod=article_inline). Kyiv is trying to make Russia’s invasion unsustainable by crippling the industries that [underpin its economy](https://www.wsj.com/world/why-ukraine-is-ramping-up-its-aerial-war-against-russia-b46dba14?mod=article_inline).

Yet neither side appears to be close to breaking. The war is getting more ruthless and destructive—but no closer to ending.

Russian President Vladimir Putin threatened to expand the range of targets in Ukraine in response to Kyiv’s strikes on its oil industry. “The Kyiv regime set out to damage our economy. What did it do? It opened this Pandora’s box itself,” he said last week.

#### **Russia’s strategy of pain**

Russia’s and Ukraine’s long-range air campaigns both use drones and missiles, but their strategies are different.

Russia began a systematic campaign against Ukraine’s energy infrastructure in the fall of 2022, when its invasion had stalled and its army had suffered a series of setbacks. Moscow pivoted to plunging Ukrainian cities into cold and darkness as winter approached.

For four winters in succession, it hasn’t worked. Ukrainians have managed to defend, repair or replace enough of their heating and electricity supply to keep their cities going. But each year, the cumulative damage to Ukraine’s energy grid has grown.

The lack of Patriot interceptors—in short supply globally since the [U.S.-Iran war](https://www.wsj.com/world/europe/russia-ukraine-iran-war-9722d527?mod=article_inline)—is darkening the mood in Kyiv. Russia’s long-range strikes have killed hundreds of Ukrainian civilians this summer, with Iskander-M ballistic missiles doing much of the killing.

Many residents of Kyiv fear Russia will try to destroy the city’s water and sewage systems, as Moscow looks for new ways to create a humanitarian crisis. Some in Kyiv are discussing whether to move temporarily to the countryside if conditions in the capital get too bad this winter. Few believe Russia will achieve victory this way.

“The theory is that pain plus exhaustion produces political capitulation,” said Franz-Stefan Gady, a Vienna-based military analyst and head of Gady Consulting. But experience since 2022, “and a century of strategic bombing before that, say sustained air attack hardens societies rather than breaking them, and there is nothing in the Ukrainian data suggesting otherwise,” he said.

Surveys suggest the Ukrainian public has become more opposed in recent months to making territorial concessions to Moscow.

#### **Hunting missile launchers**

Russia’s strikes have failed to stop the rapid growth of Ukraine’s defense-technology sector, which is now churning out millions of drones a year in decentralized and well-hidden facilities.

With only a trickle of Patriot interceptors arriving from Western allies, Ukraine is working on other ways to disrupt Russia’s missile salvos.

The wreckage of a Russian drone in Kharkiv, Ukraine. Maria Senovilla/EPA/Shutterstock

Ukrainian drones have been destroying air defenses in neighboring regions of southern Russia, potentially paving the way for a larger-scale effort to hunt down Russian missile launchers—including for the relatively short-range Iskander-Ms.

“If the airspace is cluttered with drones, it could force missile launchers to hide, or push them further back,” said Rob Lee, a Kyiv-based military analyst and a senior fellow at the Foreign Policy Research Institute in Philadelphia. “It’s not a 100% solution, but it could make things more difficult for Russia.”

Ballistic missiles aren’t the only threat, Lee noted. Russia is building up its arsenal of [jet-powered drones](https://www.wsj.com/world/how-the-iranian-shahed-drone-transformed-warwith-chinas-help-2671bb43?mod=article_inline) such as the Geran-5, based on the Iranian Shahed, and the relatively low-cost Banderol cruise missile. Both are cheaper than regular missiles, allowing for large-scale production, and could further stretch Ukraine’s air defenses, he said.

#### **The czar unmoved**

Ukraine’s long-range capabilities have risen dramatically over the past year. Its deep-strike campaign has widened from oil refineries, energy-export infrastructure and missile-component factories to Black Sea shipping and the warehouses of Russia’s biggest e-commerce chains, Wildberries and Ozon. The strikes are killing civilians, although not on the same scale as Russia’s.

Ukrainian strikes have targeted Russian e-commerce companies. Vantor/Reuters

A drone-production facility in Ukraine. Valentyn Ogirenko/Reuters

Kyiv’s declared aims are to hit military supply chains, cut off the export revenues that fund Moscow’s war machine, and cause widening economic disruption that makes the war visible to Russia’s population.

Yet most observers of Russia doubt that discontent among the country’s population or its business elite will move Putin to accept a ceasefire. He is betting he can [suppress dissent](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/russia-yabloko-party-putin-ukraine-war-bf5e2c5b?mod=article_inline), and that the economy can absorb the pain for longer than Ukraine can.

“The effects of the long-range strike campaign on Russian society and the Russian economy would have to get a lot worse for it to be anywhere close to changing Putin’s calculus,” said Hanna Notte, a Russia expert at the James Martin Center for Nonproliferation Studies in Monterey, Calif.

Although a few Russian analysts have said recently that Moscow [can’t achieve its war aims](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/russias-elite-is-souring-on-the-war-putin-doesnt-seem-to-care-2430f3ff?mod=article_inline) in Ukraine, “they were less driven by the effects of the long-range strike campaign, and more worried about the lack of movement at the front,” said Notte.

The decisive variable in the war remains military manpower, said Gady, including whether Putin will launch a [large-scale mobilization](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia/russia-tests-readiness-for-new-round-of-military-mobilization-87d61b96?mod=article_inline) in the coming months to redouble his ground campaign.

“Long-range strike is what both sides do because it is the one domain where they can still act when the ground war is stalemated,” he said. “It will get steadily more destructive without being decisive.”
