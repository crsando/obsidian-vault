> 📌 **核心摘要**
>
> 特朗普的移民政策虽恢复边境秩序，但大规模驱逐和限制合法移民将削弱美国经济与长期竞争力。
> • 非法越境减少，边境恢复平静，但驱逐行动造成劳动力减少100万，短期经济受损。
> • 大规模驱逐行动不得人心，政府以各种手段扩大驱逐对象，并剥夺临时庇护身份。
> • 合法移民渠道被严重收紧，学生签证减少29%，H1-B签证申请费超10万美元，损害人才流入。
> • 移民是美国创新动力，近半财富500强企业由移民创立，限制移民将削弱美国文化活力。
> • 未来需构建合理移民政策：庇护申请需从首个安全国提出，开放人才引进，避免拆散家庭。

### 中文译文

## 不欢迎的美国

*通过试图改变其人口结构轨迹，唐纳德·特朗普将使他的国家变得更弱*

![Illustration: The Economist/Ricardo Rey](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481f58a07f0381401665f/3c981cc9-0b14-81ea-be40-d4b6d10b0155-6dbc8206c763d1ec0041e1164b48ddfc.jpg)

首先，给唐纳德·特朗普一些肯定。他承诺恢复边境秩序，他做到了。诚然，非法越境的放缓始于乔·拜登时期，他姗姗来迟地堵住了那个几乎任何人都可以入境、申请庇护并加入劳动力市场的漏洞。特朗普先生[更严厉地打击](https://www.economist.com/interactive/united-states/2026/08/27/donald-trump-is-at-last-building-his-border-wall)。南部边境在拜登执政初期显然混乱，如今已平静下来。

这一成功本可以成为构建合理移民政策的基础。选民喜欢对入境者有掌控感，这就是为什么特朗普先生的边境墙让许多人安心。一旦他们认为政府已掌控局面，就有政治空间来讨论出入境规则。

有两个问题必须解决。政府应该如何处理那些已经非法滞留在美国土地上的人？美国未来应该欢迎什么样的人？特朗普先生对第一个问题给出了残忍的答案，对第二个问题给出了自我伤害的答案。

他承诺进行“我国历史上最大规模的驱逐行动”。他的一名执法者表示要驱逐1亿人，大约是美国非法移民数量的七倍。白宫夸耀说正在围捕“最恶劣的人”，尽管大多数被拘留者从未被定罪。大规模部署训练粗糙、态度嚣张的移民执法人员到明尼阿波利斯，引发了政治强烈反弹，因为有人拍到他们杀害了两名美国抗议者。

自那以后，抢眼球的冲突减少了，因为一位爱作秀的国土安全部主管已被更有能力的人取代。但驱逐机器并未减速，只是[变得更安静了](https://www.economist.com/briefing/2026/08/27/donald-trump-is-delivering-the-deportations-he-promised)。执法人员不再从大楼上速降，也不在沃尔玛停车场抓人，而是与友好的警察合作，在机场拘留签证过期的人。特朗普先生还剥夺了数十万海地人、阿富汗人和其他人的临时庇护身份，从而创造了一类新的可驱逐者。

总体而言，驱逐行动不得人心。大多数美国人赞成驱逐犯罪分子，但不赞成拆散那些已在美国生活数十年的勤劳民众的家庭。然而，MAGA世界中的一些人渴望一个多样性更低的美国。特朗普的高级助手如斯蒂芬·米勒距离公开的种族主义仅一步之遥，他们谈论“传统美国人”，说这些人不知何故比新来者更像美国人，以及“再移民”，这是本土主义右翼中关于将异质文化人群送回家的流行词。

这种态度似乎也影响了关于允许谁入境的政策。特朗普先生领导下唯一获准入境的大批难民是南非白人，他们关于受迫害的说法牵强附会。政府封锁了75个非富裕国家公民的所有新移民签证。因此，如果美国人娶了尼日利亚人，他们就不能一起生活在美国，这是对美国自由的限制，让人想起一段黑暗的过去。上周法院裁定该政策非法；政府目前正在拖延，或许在寻找变通办法。

特朗普先生正在改变美国的人口结构轨迹。2025年净移民为零。短期经济影响[很糟糕](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2026/08/27/the-economic-costs-of-donald-trumps-immigration-crackdown)。自1月以来，劳动力减少了100万。且不说处理、束缚和驱逐每人1.7万美元的费用；美国的建筑商和酒吧因缺乏劳动力而举步维艰。

长期影响可能更具破坏性。尽管特朗普先生常说支持合法移民，尤其是技术移民，但他的行动表明并非如此。他正在稳步阻塞外国聪明人前来学习和工作的管道。据估算，2025年签发的学生签证比上一年减少了29%，新规定使得在美国学习更缺乏吸引力。外国人必须在比普通学生更短的时间内完成课程，然后在30天内找到工作，否则就得回国。与此同时，想要为高技术外国员工申请H1-B签证的美国公司将不得不为每位工人支付超过10万美元的申请费，而且仍可能被拒绝。这非但不能提高本土出生的IT专业人士的工资，反而会使他们的雇主更难扩张。

MAGA内部的一些人认为，将外国人拒之门外能保护美国文化。恰恰相反，美国文化的精髓是活力与创造力，而这也一直由源源不断涌入的勤奋新人和新鲜视角所滋养。*财富*500强企业近一半由移民或其子女创立。许多美国最杰出的企业家，包括埃隆·马斯克，都是通过大学到硅谷的管道进入的，而这条管道如今正在收窄。堡垒美国将不再美国。

敦促特朗普先生调整他的移民政策意义不大。最好的希望或许是法院能限制他非法推行政策的种种途径。（例如，他仍声称宪法关于出生公民权的条文并非其字面意思。）然而，民主党人在争夺联邦权力之际，不应假定总统在一切问题上都是错误的。

### **一扇又大又美的门**

他说得对，庇护制度确实被滥用了。未来的民主党总统明智的做法是坚持让寻求庇护者从他们到达的第一个安全国家申请，而不是优先考虑那些有能力到达墨西哥然后踏入得克萨斯的人。这（远比修墙）才是避免未来边境混乱的办法。并且，只有说服选民相信他们的边境依然安全——而不是口头嚷嚷废除ICE——未来的政府才有希望通过美国需要的那种开明的移民改革。这样的改革应当让国家向世界上最优秀的人才更加开放，减少对拆散家庭的轻率态度。那或许才能真正让美国再次伟大。■

## The Unwelcoming States of America

## The Unwelcoming States of America

*By trying to change its demographic trajectory, Donald Trump will make his country weaker*

First, give Donald Trump some credit. He promised to restore order to the border, and he did. Granted, the slowdown in irregular border-crossings began under Joe Biden, who belatedly closed the loophole whereby nearly anyone could walk across, claim asylum and join the labour market. Mr Trump [clamped down even harder](https://www.economist.com/interactive/united-states/2026/08/27/donald-trump-is-at-last-building-his-border-wall). The southern frontier, which was visibly chaotic in the early Biden years, is now calm.

That success could have been the foundation on which to build a rational immigration policy. Voters like to feel in control of who comes in, which is why Mr Trump’s border wall has reassured many. Once they think the government has a grip, there is political space to discuss the rules of entry and exit.

Two questions must be addressed. What should the government do about the people who are already on American soil illegally? And whom should America welcome in the future? Mr Trump has offered a brutal answer to the first question, and a self-harming one to the second.

He has promised the “largest deportation operation in the history of our country”. One of his enforcers said he wanted to deport 100m people, roughly seven times the number of illicit migrants in America. The White House boasted it was rounding up “the worst of the worst”, though most of those detained have never been convicted of a crime. The mass deployment of shoddily trained, theatrically aggressive immigration agents to Minneapolis caused a political backlash, after they were filmed killing two American protesters.

Since then, there have been fewer headline-grabbing clashes, as a showboating homeland-security chief has been replaced by a more competent one. But the deportation machine has not slowed; it has merely [grown quieter](https://www.economist.com/briefing/2026/08/27/donald-trump-is-delivering-the-deportations-he-promised). Rather than rappelling down buildings and seizing people in Walmart car parks, agents are co-operating with friendly cops and detaining people with expired visas at airports. And Mr Trump has created a new class of deportables by stripping temporary asylum-like status from hundreds of thousands of Haitians, Afghans and others.

Overall, the deportation campaign is unpopular. Most Americans favour kicking out criminals, but not tearing apart the families of hard-working people who have been in the country for decades. Yet some in MAGA-world hanker for a less diverse America. Senior Trump aides such as Stephen Miller stop only a few inches short of overt racism, talking of “heritage Americans”, who are somehow more American than newer arrivals, and “remigration”, a buzzword on the nativist right for sending people from alien cultures home.

Such attitudes seem to inform policies on whom to let in, too. The only sizeable group of refugees admitted under Mr Trump has been white South Africans, whose claims of persecution are far-fetched. The administration blocked all new immigrant visas for citizens of 75 non-rich countries. Thus, if an American marries a Nigerian, they cannot live together in America, a curb on American liberty with echoes of a darker past. A court last week ruled the policy illegal; the administration is now stalling and perhaps looking for a workaround.

Mr Trump is altering America’s demographic trajectory. Net migration in 2025 was zero. The short-term economic effects [are ugly](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2026/08/27/the-economic-costs-of-donald-trumps-immigration-crackdown). The labour force has shrunk by 1m since January. Leave aside the $17,000-a-head cost of processing, shackling and deporting people; American builders and bars are floundering for lack of labour.

The long-term effects could be even more damaging. Though Mr Trump has often said he favours legal migration, especially the skilled variety, his actions suggest otherwise. He is steadily obstructing the pipeline through which brainy foreigners come to study and work. By one estimate, 29% fewer student visas were issued in 2025 than in the previous year, and new rules are making studying in America even less attractive. Foreigners must complete their courses in less time than the average student takes, and then find a job within 30 days or go home. Meanwhile, American firms that want H1-B visas for highly skilled foreign staff will have to stump up more than $100,000 per worker to apply, and may still be rejected. Rather than boosting wages for native-born IT professionals, this will make it harder for their employers to expand.

Some within MAGA feel that shutting out foreigners will protect American culture. On the contrary, the essence of that culture is dynamism and creativity, which has always been fed by a steady influx of industrious newcomers with fresh perspectives. Nearly half of *Fortune *500* *firms were founded by immigrants or their children. Many of America’s most brilliant entrepreneurs, including Elon Musk, entered via the college-to-Silicon Valley pipeline that is now narrowing. A fortress America would be unAmerican.

There is little point in urging Mr Trump to recalibrate his immigration policies. The best hope may be that the courts can limit the number of ways in which he pursues them illegally. (He still claims the constitution doesn’t mean what it says about birthright citizenship, for example.) However, Democrats should not assume, as they scramble to regain a slice of federal power, that the president was wrong about everything.

### **A big, beautiful door**

He is right that the asylum system was being abused. A future Democratic president would be wise to insist that asylum-seekers apply from the first safe country they reach, rather than giving preference to those with the wherewithal to make it to Mexico and then step into Texas. This (far more than wall-building) is the way to avoid future mayhem on the border. And only by persuading voters that their frontiers remain secure—rather than grandstanding about abolishing ICE—can a future administration hope to pass the kind of enlightened immigration reform that America needs. Such a reform should make the country more open to the world’s most talented people, and less cavalier about breaking up families. That might truly make America great again. ■
