## 微软在中国市场撤退，但人工智能热潮助其保留一扇窗

北京/上海，8月13日（路透社）——微软曾认为退出中国的想法是不可思议的。

那是2010年，谷歌因担心审查和网络攻击而即将退出。这一决定受到民主活动人士的赞扬，但比尔·盖茨和微软时任首席执行官史蒂夫·鲍尔默并不认同，他们认为谷歌反应过度。

然而，公司文件显示，过去五年中，微软在中国至少有15家分支机构和合资企业被关闭，且微软正在推行五名公司消息人士所称的撤退战略。

其中一位消息人士称，该公司曾在2023年考虑退出中国市场，因为一些高管认为其承担的地缘政治风险过大而经济回报过低，但他同时强调微软目前没有退出计划。微软在2024年表示，中国仅占其全球收入的1.5%。

这五位知情人士表示，华盛顿与北京之间信任的削弱使微软受到重大打击。自2017年以来，中国一直推动使用国产软件，北京认为这些软件更安全，其质量也与Windows和Office的竞争力越来越强。[美国限制措施](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/microsoft-stop-using-engineers-china-tech-support-us-military-hegseth-orders-2025-07-18/)（包括对先进技术的出口管制）同时阻碍了微软在中国扩大其利润丰厚的人工智能和云业务的努力。

此前从未有报道披露微软关于其在中国未来的内部讨论细节。

其他在中国拥有大量业务的美国科技巨头也在地缘政治紧张局势下重新考虑其风险敞口。苹果[计划](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/microsoft-stop-using-engineers-china-tech-support-us-military-hegseth-orders-2025-07-18/)在印度生产大部分面向美国消费者销售的iPhone，而埃隆·马斯克上月否认了有关特斯拉[正在讨论分拆](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/tesla-weighs-sale-china-business-pave-way-potential-spacex-merger-wsj-reports-2026-07-31/)其中国业务的报道。

据三位知情人士透露，微软最终决定留下，是因为它已经开辟了一项盈利业务，为像TikTok母公司字节跳动这样的中国公司提供服务，这些公司需要西方技术来管理海外业务。其中两位消息人士称，该公司还认为，它需要保持存在，以便持续接触中国世界级的工程人才。

微软还与中国政府培养了深厚的关系，其深度在科技公司中名列前茅，微软前中国区负责人柯睿杰（Alain Crozier）对路透社表示。“由于地缘政治……有些日子会艰难一些，但我们从未遇到过危机，”他说。

微软发言人未回应有关公司对中国业务考量的问题，但表示公司在一个“适用于所有国际供应商”的监管环境中运营，并继续致力于中国市场。

微软表示，其中国业务的现状反映了市场竞争、监管要求和技术趋势。

字节跳动未回应有关其与微软关系的问题。

### 中国困境

微软与中国政府最高层的接触可追溯到20世纪90年代初。盖茨于1994年进行了多次访问中的首次，受到了国家主席江泽民的接见，后者建议这位微软联合创始人学习中国历史。

此后，该公司一直努力与执政的共产党建立关系。微软与政府共同投资了创业孵化器，并[遵守了审查要求](https://www.reuters.com/technology/linkedin-replace-networking-platform-with-jobs-only-version-china-2021-10-14/)，而谷歌——现在是Alphabet的一部分——却无法接受。

然而，到2010年代中期，在披露[美国公司曾帮助华盛顿监视](https://www.reuters.com/article/technology/german-parliament-cuts-ties-with-verizon-in-wake-of-spying-row-idUSKBN0F21E2/)外国政府之后，中国对西方技术越来越怀疑。这对微软来说是个问题，因为中国最大的公司要么是国有企业，要么与政府保持密切关系。

微软的回应是推出Windows 10中国政府版，据知情人士透露，该版本的发布是由首席执行官萨提亚·纳德拉与财政部官员亲自协商的。

克罗泽表示，该产品被几家政府机构采用，但并未像微软希望的那样腾飞。克罗泽负责中国业务直到2021年。

大约在2017年Windows发布之时，中国政府出台了新的采购指南，并称其为购买“安全可靠”服务的框架。微软表示，包括Windows在内，没有任何外国操作系统被政府视为符合这些政策。

项目1/2：2023年5月25日，中国北京，微软标志出现在其办公楼外。路透社/Tingshu Wang/资料图片

华盛顿中国科技政策专家保罗·特里奥罗表示，不合规并不意味着产品被禁，但这使得使用此类服务的技术管理员受到审查，包括必须进行更多安全检查并寻求额外批准。特里奥罗任职于DGA-Albright Stonebridge Group。

路透社查阅了2023年12月至2026年5月间发布的六份中国政府计算机系统采购指南。其中五份未推荐微软。第六份包含了Windows 10中国政府版，但表示其使用须遵守“额外管理要求”，但没有详细说明。

中国科技部和财政部没有回应有关这些法规对微软业务影响的提问。

长期以来，在华经营的美国企业一直抱怨竞争环境不公平，而中美关系恶化进一步打击了它们的信心。在中国美国商会最新的商业环境调查中，仅52%的受访者表示中国是首要全球投资优先地，低于2019年的62%。

尽管微软成为中国国有科技供应商的努力未获成功，但它却在私营领域找到了新的生机。

两位公司消息人士称，字节跳动和超快时尚零售商希音（Shein）等公司拥有面向西方客户的关键业务，并依赖微软的Azure云来管理数据以符合外国法规。微软还通过Azure向中国企业客户独家提供西方AI模型，如来自OpenAI的模型，这些模型并不直接服务中国市场。

三位知情人士表示，到2020年代中期，帮助中国企业出海已成为微软最大的在华相关业务。其中两人强调，按照微软的全球标准，这部分销售额仍然较小。

分析师还质疑该AI业务的可持续性，因为它依赖OpenAI等第三方供应商。如果中国企业使用像Kimi这样的国产AI模型，也未必需要Azure，这些模型[与西方替代品的竞争力日益增强](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-moonshot-unveils-worlds-largest-open-ai-model-closing-us-rivals-2026-07-17/)，且价格便宜得多。

OpenAI和希音未回应提问。

### 人力资本

自1990年代以来，微软在中国科技人才基础建设中发挥了核心作用。

在招聘侧重商业的工程师的同时，微软还成立了专注于先进技术的微软中国研究院。该实验室的校友包括人工智能先驱商汤科技和深度求索（DeepSeek）的高级管理人员。

但近期的政治压力影响了微软留住人才的能力。

美国对芯片和人工智能模型的出口管制限制了微软在华工程师接触尖端技术的渠道。微软总裁布拉德·史密斯（Brad Smith）2023年告诉美国议员，该公司不在中国开展量子计算和其他敏感技术的研究。

据两位知情人士透露，微软曾考虑关闭该实验室，但最终决定迁移部分顶尖人才。自美国开始限制人工智能出口以来，微软中国研究院（现更名为微软亚洲研究院）已在温哥华、新加坡和东京设立实验室。

然而，该公司一直难以说服开发者离开中国。消息人士称，2024年，微软为1000名顶尖工程师提供了迁往美国和其他三个西方国家的机会，但只有约三分之一的人接受。

微软确认当年提供了调任机会，但拒绝透露更多细节。

两位消息人士均表示，许多资深工程师反而去了中国高校和科技公司，在那里既能进行顶级研究，又能离家人更近。

微软此前曾抵挡住国内竞争对手的挖角。该公司在2015年前后的人员流失率约为17%，但克罗泽表示，微软通过发展新业务（如为字节跳动提供服务）和为员工提供全球机会，将流失率降至10%以下。

他说：“在人员数量以及在那里开发的一些项目方面，有起有落。但我们丝毫不会改变这一事实：我们将把技术带到中国……为中国，为中国企业。”

## Microsoft retreats in China, but AI boom helps it keep a window open

BEIJING/SHANGHAI, Aug 13 (Reuters) - Microsoft once regarded the idea of quitting China as unthinkable.

The year was 2010 and Google was about to exit due to concerns over censorship and cyberattacks. That decision was lauded by democracy activists, but not Bill Gates and Microsoft's then-CEO Steve Ballmer, who suggested Google was overreacting.

In the past five years, however, at least 15 Microsoft branch offices and joint ventures in China have been shut, corporate filings show, and Microsoft is pursuing what five company sources described as a strategy of retreat.

The firm considered quitting the market in 2023 because some executives felt it took on too much geopolitical risk for too little economic return, one of them said, while stressing Microsoft has no current plans to exit. China accounted for just 1.5% of global revenue, Microsoft said in 2024.

Microsoft took a major hit from the erosion of trust between Washington and Beijing, the five people said. China has since 2017 pushed the use of domestic software, which Beijing sees as more secure and whose quality is increasingly competitive with Windows and Office. [U.S. restrictions](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/microsoft-stop-using-engineers-china-tech-support-us-military-hegseth-orders-2025-07-18/), including export controls on advanced technology, have meanwhile hindered efforts to scale Microsoft's lucrative AI and cloud businesses in China.

Details of Microsoft's internal deliberations about its future in China have not previously been reported.

Other U.S. tech giants with large China businesses are also reconsidering their exposure amid geopolitical tensions. Apple [plans to manufacture](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/microsoft-stop-using-engineers-china-tech-support-us-military-hegseth-orders-2025-07-18/) in India most iPhones sold to Americans by the end of 2026, while Elon Musk last month denied reports that [Tesla is debating separating](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/tesla-weighs-sale-china-business-pave-way-potential-spacex-merger-wsj-reports-2026-07-31/) its China business.

Microsoft ultimately decided to remain because it had carved out a profitable business servicing Chinese companies like TikTok owner ByteDance, which need Western technology to manage overseas operations, according to three people familiar with the matter. The company also believed that it needed a presence to maintain access to China's world-class engineering talent, two of them said.

Microsoft had also cultivated a relationship with the government that is among the deepest of any tech company, its former China head Alain Crozier told Reuters. "Because of the geopolitics … some days it's a little bit harder, but we never had a crisis," he said.

A Microsoft spokesperson did not address questions about the firm's deliberations on its China business but said it operates in a regulatory "environment that applies to every international supplier" and that it remains committed to the Chinese market.

The state of Microsoft's China business reflects market competition, regulatory demands and technological trends, the company said.

ByteDance did not respond to questions about its relationship with Microsoft.

### CHINA BLUES

Microsoft's engagement with the highest levels of China's government dates back to the early 1990s. Gates made the first of his many visits in 1994 and was received by President Jiang Zemin, who advised the Microsoft co-founder to study Chinese history.

The company has since made various efforts to build a relationship with the ruling Communist Party. Microsoft co-invested in startup incubators with the government and [complied with censorship requirements](https://www.reuters.com/technology/linkedin-replace-networking-platform-with-jobs-only-version-china-2021-10-14/) that Google — now part of Alphabet — could not countenance.

By the mid-2010s, however, China had become increasingly suspicious of Western technology after revelations that [U.S. firms had helped Washington spy](https://www.reuters.com/article/technology/german-parliament-cuts-ties-with-verizon-in-wake-of-spying-row-idUSKBN0F21E2/) on foreign governments. That was problematic for Microsoft as China's largest companies are either state-owned or maintain close government ties.

Microsoft's response was Windows 10 China Government Edition, whose release was personally negotiated between chief executive Satya Nadella and finance ministry officials, according to a person familiar with the matter.

The product was adopted by several government agencies, but did not take off as Microsoft hoped, said Crozier, who ran China operations through 2021.

At around the time of the Windows announcement in 2017, the Chinese government introduced new procurement guidelines that it billed as a framework for purchasing "safe and reliable" services. No foreign operating system, including Windows, has been regarded by the government as compliant with those policies, Microsoft said.

Item 1 of 2 A sign of Microsoft is pictured on its office building in Beijing, China May 25, 2023. REUTERS/Tingshu Wang/File Photo

Non-compliance did not mean products were banned but it subjected tech administrators who used such services to scrutiny, including having to run more security checks and seek additional approval, said Paul Triolo, a Washington-based China tech policy expert at DGA-Albright Stonebridge Group.

Reuters reviewed six Chinese government computer-system procurement guides published between December 2023 and May 2026. Five did not recommend Microsoft. The sixth included Windows 10 China Government Edition but said its usage was subject to "additional management requirements," without elaborating.

The Chinese tech and finance ministries did not respond to questions about the effect of regulations on Microsoft's business.

U.S. businesses operating in China, which have long complained about an uneven playing field, have had their confidence further dented by deteriorating Sino-American ties. Just 52% of respondents to the American Chamber of Commerce in China's latest business climate survey said China was a top global investment priority, down from 62% in 2019.

While its efforts to become the Chinese state's tech vendor of choice did not pay off, Microsoft found a second wind with the private sector.

Firms like ByteDance and ultra-fast-fashion retailer Shein have key businesses serving Western customers and rely on Microsoft's Azure cloud to manage data in compliance with foreign regulations, two company sources said. Microsoft also offers Chinese enterprise clients exclusive access via Azure to Western AI models from providers like OpenAI, which do not serve China.

By the mid-2020s, helping Chinese firms go global had become Microsoft's largest China-linked business, three people said. Two of them stressed that sales remained small by the firm's global standards.

Analysts have additionally questioned the sustainability of that AI business, which relies on third-party suppliers like OpenAI. Chinese businesses also do not need Azure if they use domestic AI models like Kimi, which are [increasingly competitive with Western alternatives](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-moonshot-unveils-worlds-largest-open-ai-model-closing-us-rivals-2026-07-17/) while being far cheaper.

OpenAI and Shein did not respond to questions.

### HUMAN CAPITAL

Microsoft has since the 1990s played a central role in building China's tech talent base.

Alongside hiring commercially focused engineers, it also established Microsoft Research China, which concentrates on advanced technologies. The lab's alumni include senior leaders at AI pioneers SenseTime and DeepSeek.

But the recent political pressures have affected Microsoft's ability to retain talent.

U.S. export controls on chips and AI models have restricted the access of Microsoft's China-based engineers to cutting-edge technology. The firm doesn't conduct research on quantum computing and other sensitive technologies in China, Microsoft president Brad Smith told U.S. lawmakers in 2023.

Microsoft considered shutting the lab down but ultimately decided to relocate some top talent, according to two people familiar with the matter. Since the U.S. began restricting AI exports, Microsoft Research China — now known as Microsoft Research Asia — has opened labs in Vancouver, Singapore and Tokyo.

The firm has, however, struggled to convince developers to leave China. It offered 1,000 top engineers relocation to the U.S. and three other Western countries in 2024, but only about a third accepted, the sources said.

Microsoft confirmed it offered transfer opportunities that year but declined to provide more details.

Many senior engineers instead left for Chinese universities and tech firms, where they can conduct top-level research while remaining close to family, both sources said.

Microsoft had previously warded off poaching efforts by domestic rivals. The firm had an attrition rate of roughly 17% in the mid-2010s, though Crozier said Microsoft reduced it to under 10% by growing new businesses, like servicing ByteDance, and offering staff global opportunities.

There is "up and down in terms of the number of people and maybe some of the things that were developed over there," he said. "But we never change one inch of the fact that we will bring technology into China… for China, for Chinese companies."
