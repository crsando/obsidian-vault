### 中文译文

## 香港拟将交易公司纳入‘大爆炸’税制改革

![Hong Kong’s financial industry generates a significant proportion of the city’s tax revenue © Peter Parks/AFP/Getty Images](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A1c1a96e4-ae2e-4eba-b496-4347bca5abf0?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

香港金融业为这座城市贡献了相当大比例的税收收入 © Peter Parks/法新社/盖蒂图片社

Jane Street和Citadel Securities等交易公司可能会被纳入香港正在考虑的改革中，这些改革将为资产管理人带来‘大爆炸’式减税。

这些变革发生在新加坡、纽约、迈阿密和香港等城市争夺顶尖金融人才的背景下。它们也标志着这个中国特别行政区在多年停滞之后，试图提升其作为金融中心的全球吸引力。

两位知情人士表示，香港当局正在考虑修改拟议中的立法，以便自营交易公司的员工无需为绩效相关薪酬缴税。

他们表示，官员们也可以只是发布‘指引’，明确交易者有资格享受税收优惠，而不是在法案目前提交立法会之际进行迟来的修订。他们补充说，可能并非所有自营交易公司都能受益。

该法案于6月提出，旨在‘吸引更多基金和家族办公室在[香港](https://www.ft.com/hong-kong)设立据点’。

这促使[新加坡考虑自己的减税措施](https://www.ft.com/content/d3221c90-c2a4-4bdd-8e0d-6e069bf0ca25?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，因为它寻求保持与香港的竞争力，同时担心一些投资组合经理可能搬迁。

Jane Street和Citadel Securities等自营交易集团——它们运用自有资本或员工资本，而不是代表养老金计划、政府或富裕个人管理资金——正成为华尔街的新兴巨头。

香港正提议允许更广泛投资的利润按附带权益征税，而不仅仅是私募股权交易。

Charltons律师事务所在一份说明中澄清，“附带权益并非酌情发放的花红，而是与基金投资表现挂钩的合同约定的基金利润分成。”

更广泛的税制改革意味着对冲基金、私募股权、风险投资、私人信贷甚至家族办公室的管理者可以通过架构安排进一步降低本已很低的香港税负。

这些改革有可能成为“[税制改革的大爆炸](https://www.ft.com/content/b018d802-a7d1-4853-9acd-d3007a61cf72?syn-25a6b1a6=1)”，一位熟悉相关提议的人士此前对英国《金融时报》表示。

该提议发布之际，香港正从由民主抗议和新冠疫情引发的长期交易低迷中复苏。香港的[IPO市场](https://www.ft.com/content/886d5a1b-f195-4966-9cc3-364ee09a1343?syn-25a6b1a6=1)因中国企业（包括宁德时代和中际旭创）上市潮以及许多外籍人士的回流而重新活跃起来。

涌入香港的资本大部分来自中国内地，通过沪港通和深港通将香港与内地投资者连接起来。

香港的金融业至关重要，因为它为这座城市贡献了很大比例的税收收入，而香港的所得税税率远低于西方。

去年，Jane Street以每月400万美元租下一处新海旁开发项目的六层楼，高调承诺在香港发展。据报道，肯·格里芬（Ken Griffin）旗下做市商Citadel Securities也在发展新业务的同时扩大在香港的业务。

“拟议的基金及附带权益强化税务优惠的范围并不局限于特定类型的基金或资产管理人，而是取决于是否符合相关条件和要求。”香港财经事务及库务局在回应英国《金融时报》关于此次税务改革的提问时如此表示。

“如有必要，可在适当时候发布行政指引，以澄清和阐述有关优惠实施、关键概念诠释及操作要求等事宜。”

## Hong Kong set to include trading firms in ‘big bang’ tax reforms

Hong Kong’s financial industry generates a significant proportion of the city’s tax revenue © Peter Parks/AFP/Getty Images

Trading firms such as Jane Street and Citadel Securities may be included in reforms being considered by Hong Kong that are set to trigger “big bang” tax cuts for asset managers.

The changes come amid a battle for top-tier financial talent between cities such as Singapore, New York, Miami and Hong Kong. They also mark the Chinese territory’s attempt to boost its global attractiveness as a financial hub after years of stagnation.

Hong Kong authorities are considering amending proposed legislation so that staff of proprietary trading firms do not incur tax on performance-related pay, two people familiar with the process said.

They said officials could also simply issue “guidance” to clarify that traders qualify for the tax break, rather than make a late amendment to the bill now before the legislative council. It was possible that not all proprietary trading firms would benefit, they added.

The bill was introduced in June with the aim of “attracting more funds and family offices to establish a presence in [Hong Kong](https://www.ft.com/hong-kong)”.

This prompted [Singapore to consider its own tax cuts](https://www.ft.com/content/d3221c90-c2a4-4bdd-8e0d-6e069bf0ca25?syn-25a6b1a6=1) as it sought to remain competitive with Hong Kong amid fears that some portfolio managers could relocate.

Proprietary trading groups such as Jane Street and Citadel Securities — which deploy their own capital or that of employees rather than managing money on behalf of pension plans, governments or wealthy individuals — are the emerging giants of Wall Street.

Hong Kong is proposing allowing profits from a wider range of investments to be taxed as carried interest, rather than just private equity transactions.

Law firm Charltons clarified in a note that “carried interest is not a discretionary bonus but a contractual share of a fund’s profits that is linked to the fund’s investment performance”.

The broader tax overhaul would mean managers of hedge funds, private equity, venture capital, private credit and even family offices could structure themselves to reduce further their already low Hong Kong tax bills.

The changes have the potential to be a “[big bang of tax reforms](https://www.ft.com/content/b018d802-a7d1-4853-9acd-d3007a61cf72?syn-25a6b1a6=1)”, one person familiar with the proposals previously told the FT.

The proposal comes at a time when Hong Kong is recovering from a prolonged slump in dealmaking triggered by democracy protests and the Covid-19 pandemic. The territory’s [IPO market](https://www.ft.com/content/886d5a1b-f195-4966-9cc3-364ee09a1343?syn-25a6b1a6=1) has been reignited by a surge of listings by Chinese companies including CATL and [Zhongji Innolight](https://www.ft.com/content/cc7d24ec-abfe-440b-9e85-4d725567f278?syn-25a6b1a6=1) and the return of many expats.

Much of the capital pouring into the territory is Chinese, via the stock connect that links Hong Kong with mainland investors through Shanghai and Shenzhen.

Hong Kong’s financial industry is crucial as it generates a significant proportion of the tax revenue in a city where income tax rates are far lower than in the West.

Last year Jane Street made a [high-profile commitment](https://www.ft.com/content/7aaf2208-0023-42b8-aa8e-679124be7e8e?syn-25a6b1a6=1) to Hong Kong by paying $4mn a month to lease six floors of a new harbourside development. Citadel Securities, Ken Griffin’s market maker, is also reportedly expanding in Hong Kong as it develops new business lines.

“The scope of the proposed enhanced tax concessions for funds and carried interest is not confined to particular types of funds or asset managers. Rather, eligibility is dependent on whether the relevant conditions and requirements are met,” Hong Kong’s Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau told the FT when asked about the tax overhaul.

“Where necessary, administrative guidance may be issued at an appropriate time to provide clarification and elaboration on matters relating to the implementation of the concessions, interpretation of key concepts and operational requirements.”
