### 中文译文

## 药明康德在“中国军方”标签上获得临时缓刑

一名美国法官阻止国防部执行将药明康德指定为中国军方公司的决定，给予这家大型医药承包商临时救济。

根据法院文件，美国哥伦比亚特区联邦地区法院首席法官詹姆斯·E·博斯伯格周五批准了药明康德关于初步禁令的请求，称该公司“证明了该指定存在任意和反复无常的可能性”。

这一裁决对国防部限制其认为与中国军方和国家机器有关的公司的努力是一个挫折。《国防授权法》第1260H条允许政府指定与中国军方和国家组织有特定所有权或附属关系的公司。

法官在文件中表示，该[指定](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/pentagon-names-biotech-firm-wuxi-apptec-to-chinese-military-list)“正在对药明康德造成日后救济无法弥补的伤害”。“由于其余禁令因素也有利于原告，法院将批准动议，并初步禁止被告执行或落实该指定。”

药明康德表示欢迎法院批准禁令的决定。这家中国公司去年约四分之三的销售额来自其美国业务。

公司发言人在电邮声明中表示：“这项裁决使我们免于在诉讼过程中因错误的1260H指定而立即遭受不利后果。”“我们相信，我们在法庭文件中陈述的事实将在客观公正的司法审查后得到证实。与此同时，我们的运营完全正常，我们将继续专注于为客户以及依赖他们生产的救命和改善生活药物的患者提供服务的承诺。”

五角大楼发言人表示，国防部不对未决诉讼发表评论。

法院认定，国防部关于与中国航空工业集团有限公司（AVIC）有关联的一只投资基金拥有药明康德5.32%股份的结论是对证据的“误读”。法院认为，5.32%这一数字指的是该中航工业基金自身投资组合中投资于药明康德的比例，而非该基金所持药明康德的股份比例。

博斯伯格要求国防部就其将药明康德列入名单所引用的三个理由进行详细说明——据称由中国国务院国有资产监督管理委员会间接持股；据称与国家国防科技工业局（SASTIND）有关联；以及据称与中国人民解放军有关联。

“当一个机构提供不准确或缺乏合理依据的理由时，其行为即为武断和反复无常，”博斯伯格表示，他认定药明康德很可能在挑战中获胜。“国防部的每项理由都基于误读：将基金中的股份报告为在该公司的股份，以及将第三方实验室工作报告为药明康德（WXAT）与SASTIND和解放军关联机构合作进行的研究，”他说道，其中WXAT是药明康德的缩写。

法院还认定，政府的认定已对药明康德造成“任何损害赔偿金都无法弥补”的损失。法院称，这家中国公司的一些客户已暂停或终止项目，将工作转移到竞争对手处，并重新考虑新业务，而至少一家供应商已暂停发货。

根据法院文件，“仅这一小部分客户行动就涉及每年数百万美元的收入、现有承诺以及长期和短期项目。”

### English Original

## WuXi AppTec Gets Temporary Reprieve on China Military Label

A US judge blocked the Defense Department from enforcing its designation of WuXi AppTec Co. as a Chinese military company, granting temporary relief to the major pharmaceutical contractor.

Chief Judge James E. Boasberg of the US District Court for the District of Columbia granted WuXi AppTec’s request for a preliminary injunction Friday, saying the company “established a likelihood that the designation was arbitrary and capricious,” according to a court filing.

The ruling is a setback for the Defense Department’s effort to restrict companies it considers linked to China’s military and state apparatus. Section 1260H of the National Defense Authorization Act allows the government to designate companies with specified ownership or affiliation ties to Chinese military and state organizations.

The [designation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/pentagon-names-biotech-firm-wuxi-apptec-to-chinese-military-list) “is inflicting harm on WuXi that later relief cannot repair,” the judge said in the filing. “Because the remaining injunction factors also favor Plaintiff, the Court will grant the Motion and preliminarily enjoin Defendants from enforcing or giving effect to the designation.”

WuXi AppTec said it welcomed the court’s decision to grant the injunction. About three-quarters of the Chinese firm’s sales last year came from its US business.

“This ruling relieves us from the immediate adverse consequences of the erroneous 1260H designation during the litigation process,” a company spokesperson said in an emailed statement. “We believe that the facts, as demonstrated in our court filings, will prevail after an objective and fair judicial review. In the meantime, our operations remain fully functional, and we remain focused on our commitment to serving our customers and the patients relying on the lifesaving and life-improving medicines they make.”

A Pentagon spokesperson said the department doesn’t comment on ongoing litigation.

The court found the Defense Department’s conclusion that an investment fund associated with Aviation Industry Corp. of China, or AVIC, owned 5.32% of WuXi AppTec was a “misreading” of the evidence. The 5.32% figure referred to the proportion of the AVIC fund’s own portfolio invested in WuXi AppTec, rather than the percentage of WuXi AppTec owned by the fund, the court found.

Boasberg sought elaborations with all three grounds the Defense Department cited for putting WuXi AppTec on the list — alleged indirect ownership by China’s State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission; alleged affiliation with the State Administration of Science, Technology and Industry for National Defense, or SASTIND; and alleged affiliation with the People’s Liberation Army.

“An agency acts arbitrarily and capriciously when it offers inaccurate or unreasoned justifications,” Boasberg said, concluding that WuXi was likely to succeed on its challenge. “The Department’s justifications each rested on a misreading: a stake in a fund reported as a stake in the company, and third-party laboratory work reported as studies WXAT conducted in partnership with SASTIND- and PLA-affiliated institutions,” he said, using an abbreviation for WuXi AppTec.

The court also found that the government’s designation has caused losses to WuXi AppTec “that no damages award can return.” It said some of the Chinese company’s customers had paused or terminated projects, moved work to competitors and reconsidered new business, while at least one supplier suspended shipments.

“That sliver of customer actions alone implicates millions of dollars in annual revenue, existing commitments, and both long- and near-term programs,” according to the filing.
