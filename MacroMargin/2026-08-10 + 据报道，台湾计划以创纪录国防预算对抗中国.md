### 中文译文

## 据报道，台湾计划以创纪录国防预算对抗中国

据一家半官方媒体报道，台湾计划在2027年将军费支出提高到创纪录的1.1万亿新台币（341亿美元），此举正值美国敦促台湾加强威慑中国之际。

总部位于台北的中央通讯社援引知情人士报道，2027年的国防预算将超过GDP的3%。相比之下，内阁[今年早些时候提出](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-21/taiwan-aims-to-placate-trump-with-2026-military-spending-boost)的数字为新台币9495亿元。

内阁定于8月20日讨论该支出计划。最终金额可能变化，因为需要由反对派控制的立法机构批准。

台湾总统赖清德自两年多前上任以来，一直将增加国防开支和军事准备作为重点。这份预算计划报告发布之际，军方正在举行年度旗舰“汉光”演习。

去年2月，赖清德设定了将GDP的3%用于武装部队的目标。此前，特朗普质疑台湾这个民主政体自卫的承诺。中国[将台湾视为](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-02/taiwan-warns-of-rise-in-chinese-military-activity-around-region)其领土的一部分，并认为如有必要必须用武力加以控制。

台北拒绝北京的观点，并谴责中国近年来在台湾附近加强军事和海警活动。

今年早些时候，台湾立法者批准了一项248亿美元的[特别国防预算](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-08/taiwan-passes-special-defense-budget-to-better-deter-china)——这一数字低于赖清德寻求的金额。部分资金将用于旨在保护台湾免受空中威胁的[T-Dome防空系统](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-05/taiwan-s-t-dome-what-is-the-missile-system-and-how-will-it-work)。

台湾今年的初始军事预算[激增了23%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-21/taiwan-aims-to-placate-trump-with-2026-military-spending-boost)，不过官员首次将海警和退伍军人养老金纳入该数字。由于政党之间的僵局，包括军事资金在内的2026年中央政府预算尚未得到立法机构批准。

### English Original

## Taiwan Plans Record Defense Budget to Counter China, Report Says

Taiwan plans to spend a record NT$1.1 trillion ($34.1 billion) on its military in 2027, a semi-official media outlet reported, a move that comes as the US pushes it to do more to deter China.

The defense budget for the year would exceed 3% of GDP, the Taipei-based Central News Agency reported, citing a person familiar with the matter. The figure compares with the NT$949.5 billion the cabinet [earlier proposed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-21/taiwan-aims-to-placate-trump-with-2026-military-spending-boost) for this year.

The cabinet is set to discuss the spending plan on Aug. 20. The final amount could change because it needs the approval of the legislature, which the opposition controls.

Taiwan President Lai Ching-te has made stepping up defense spending and military preparation a focus since taking office more than two years ago. The report about the budget plan comes as the military holds its flagship annual Han Kuang exercises.

In February last year, Lai set a target of spending 3% of gross domestic product on the armed forces. That move followed Trump questioning the democracy’s commitment to protecting itself from China, which [sees Taiwan](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-02/taiwan-warns-of-rise-in-chinese-military-activity-around-region) as part of its territory that must be brought under its control by force if needed.

Taipei rejects Beijing’s view, and condemns China for stepping up military and coast guard activity near Taiwan in recent years.

Earlier this year, Taiwanese lawmakers approved a $24.8 billion [special defense budget](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-08/taiwan-passes-special-defense-budget-to-better-deter-china) — a figure that was less than Lai sought. Some of the funds will go toward the [T-Dome air-defense system](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-05/taiwan-s-t-dome-what-is-the-missile-system-and-how-will-it-work) that is intended to protect the island from aerial threats.

Taiwan’s initial military budget for this year [surged by 23%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-21/taiwan-aims-to-placate-trump-with-2026-military-spending-boost), though officials included money for the coast guard and veteran pensions in the figure for the first time. The central government budget for 2026, including military funds, hasn’t been approved by the legislature yet due to a standoff between political parties.
