### 中文译文

## 台湾抨击中国与印度尼西亚海军演习

![A Chinese People’s Liberation Army Navy ship in Surabaya, Indonesia, on August 2 © Juni Kriswanto/AFP/Getty Images](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3Aea84909f-7e3b-431a-a1db-e23f7363ae60?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

一艘中国人民解放军海军舰艇于8月2日在印度尼西亚泗水 © Juni Kriswanto/AFP/Getty Images

台湾政府抨击中国计划与印度尼西亚军舰在台湾东海岸附近进行海军演习，称这一不寻常举动是“破坏现状的危险行为”。

中国国防部周二表示，将于8月中旬在[台湾](https://www.ft.com/taiwan)以东海域举行联合“航行演习”，但没有说明具体地点。

计划中的演习——很少涉及外国——是在中国海警近期巡逻之后进行的，这些巡逻引发了国际社会对北京方面对相关海域提出[更强硬主张](https://www.ft.com/content/c52f0a98-2e6a-4acc-a668-d4ce442c4aba?syn-25a6b1a6=1)的担忧。

与此同时，台湾本月正在举行一年一度的[汉光演习](https://www.ft.com/content/4dcc9154-a50d-4877-88f9-3921e3994ebc?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，旨在为台湾应对中国可能的军事行动做准备。北京声称对台湾拥有主权，并威胁如果台北无限期抗拒其控制，将采取军事行动。

台湾大陆委员会在一份声明中称这些计划中的演习是“政治操弄”，并指责北京“试图在国际社会制造中国共产党对台湾东部海域拥有管辖权的假象”。

该声明呼吁停止这些计划，但没有直接提及印度尼西亚，而是指出北京计划与“相关国家”进行联合军事演习。

[中国](https://www.ft.com/china)国防部在公告中表示，演习将侧重于通信和海上补给，旨在“提升两国海军的联合作战能力”。

新加坡拉惹勒南国际研究院高级研究员许瑞麟（Collin Koh）表示，北京“显然想利用这个机会来表明，他们得到了东南亚国家和一个大型群岛国家的支持”。

许瑞麟补充说，印尼此举的动机尚不清楚，在该地区看来，印尼将是“中国的一个共同交战国”。但他表示，雅加达的参与可能会向北京展示，它是一个乐于合作的伙伴。

印尼海军在回答提问时表示，其军舰 KRI I Gusti Ngurah Rai-332 将在从俄罗斯返航途中与途经的国家举行“过航演习”（passing exercise）。过航演习通常指不同国家的海军舰艇在海上相遇或港口访问期间进行的演习。

声明并未提及中国或台湾。

印尼海军表示：“过航演习并非作战性质，而是各国海军在穿越他国领海时遵循的普遍传统。”

雅加达此前曾在印尼境内与中国举行联合军事演习。此次参与台湾军演，可能会引发国内及东南亚邻国的批评，其中许多国家与中国在南海存在领土争议。

今年5月，日本与菲律宾达成的旨在划定台湾以东海域海上边界的一项双边协议，引发北京的强烈反应，北京称该协议“违反国际法”。

自上任以来，印尼总统普拉博沃·苏比安托（Prabowo Subianto）——前将军——加强了外交活动，进行了多次外访并举行军事演习，包括该国于2024年11月首次与俄罗斯进行的联合海军演习。

同月，普拉博沃与中国国家主席习近平发表联合声明，称两国将在“重叠主张”地区开展“联合海上开发”，此举引发争议。

该声明被视为对中国“九段线”的认可，北京用“九段线”来划分其在南海的主张。此举也标志着与印尼长期外交政策的明显背离，因为雅加达此前从未承认中国的主张。

九段线与印尼在北纳图纳海的专属经济区重叠，近年来中国船只不断侵入印尼的专属经济区。

印尼外交部随后发表声明称，该国不承认九段线。

今年4月，习近平会见了来访的台湾在野党国民党主席成力文（Cheng Li-wun）。他警告台北不要有任何独立举动，称独立是“破坏台海和平的罪魁祸首”。

## Taiwan hits out at China over naval drill with Indonesia

A Chinese People’s Liberation Army Navy ship in Surabaya, Indonesia, on August 2 © Juni Kriswanto/AFP/Getty Images

The Taiwanese government has hit out at China over planned navy drills with an Indonesian warship off its east coast, calling the unusual move “dangerous behaviour that undermines the status quo”.

China’s defence ministry on Tuesday said a joint “navigation exercise” in the waters east of [Taiwan](https://www.ft.com/taiwan) would take place in mid-August, without specifying an exact location.

The planned exercises — which are rare in involving a foreign nation — follow recent Chinese coastguard patrols that have sparked international concerns over signs of Beijing making [more assertive claims](https://www.ft.com/content/c52f0a98-2e6a-4acc-a668-d4ce442c4aba?syn-25a6b1a6=1) to the waters.

It also comes as Taiwan this month is holding its annual [Han Kuang training exercises](https://www.ft.com/content/4dcc9154-a50d-4877-88f9-3921e3994ebc?syn-25a6b1a6=1), aimed at preparing the nation against possible military action by China. Beijing claims sovereignty over Taiwan and has threatened military action if Taipei resists its control indefinitely.

In a statement, Taiwan’s Mainland Affairs Council called the planned drills “political manipulation” and accused Beijing of “attempting to create the illusion in the international community that the Chinese Communist Party has jurisdiction over the eastern waters of Taiwan”.

The statement, which called for the plans to be stopped, did not mention Indonesia directly but noted that Beijing was planning to conduct joint military exercises with “relevant countries”.

In its announcement, [China](https://www.ft.com/china)’s defence ministry said the drills would focus on communication and at-sea resupply and aimed to “enhance the joint operational capabilities of the two navies”.

Beijing “clearly wants to weaponise this opportunity to show that they have south-east Asian countries and a big archipelago on its side”, said Collin Koh, a senior fellow at Singapore’s S Rajaratnam School of International Studies.

The motive for Indonesia, which would be seen in the region as “a co-belligerent alongside China”, was unclear, Koh added. But Jakarta’s participation could potentially demonstrate to Beijing that it was an amenable partner, he said.

In response to questions, the Indonesian navy said its warship, KRI I Gusti Ngurah Rai-332, would carry out “passing exercises” with countries it passed on its way back from Russia. A passing exercise typically refers to drills conducted when naval vessels from different countries meet at sea or during a port visit.

The statement did not mention China or Taiwan.

“The passing exercise is not in the nature of warfighting, but is a universal navy tradition observed when crossing a country’s territorial waters,” the navy said.

Jakarta has previously held joint military exercises with China within Indonesia. Its participation in the Taiwan drill is likely to trigger criticism at home and among its south-east Asian neighbours, many of whom hold contesting claims with Beijing in the South China Sea.

A bilateral agreement in May between Japan and the Philippines to [delimit maritime boundaries](https://www.ft.com/content/6ac24ea6-50c2-4e25-92b7-79d52edc0b8b?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in the area east of Taiwan sparked a strong reaction from Beijing, which said that the agreement “violates international law”.

Since taking office, Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto — a former general — has stepped up diplomatic activity, making numerous foreign visits and holding military exercises, including the country’s first-ever [joint naval drills with Russia](https://www.ft.com/content/113b8345-0ee9-47be-a98f-b15ec25c500b?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in November 2024.

That same month, Prabowo sparked controversy when he issued a joint statement with Chinese President Xi Jinping saying the two countries would pursue “joint maritime development” in areas of “overlapping claims”.

The statement was seen as an endorsement of China’s “nine-dash line”, which Beijing uses to demarcate its claims to the South China Sea. It also marked a stark departure from Indonesia’s longstanding foreign policy, as Jakarta had not previously acknowledged China’s claims.

The nine-dash line overlaps with Indonesia’s exclusive economic zone in the North Natuna Sea, and in recent years Chinese vessels have encroached into Indonesia’s EEZ.

Indonesia’s foreign ministry later issued a statement saying the country does not recognise the nine-dash line.

In April, Xi met Cheng Li-wun, the head of Taiwan’s opposition Kuomintang, who was visiting China. He warned Taipei against any move towards independence, calling it the “chief culprit destroying peace in the Taiwan Strait”.

*Additional reporting by Diana Mariska in Jakarta*
