### 中文译文

## 中国科技股热潮中，上海科创板跑赢香港

上海科创板正受益于一波投资者对中国科技股的狂热，自成立以来跑赢包括香港在内的竞争对手，优势幅度为史上最大。

科创50指数包含上海科创板的主要股票，今年已上涨23%。该指数遥遥领先于深圳的科技指数（上涨10%），远好于香港恒生科技指数14%的跌幅。

[长鑫存储（CXMT）](https://www.ft.com/content/8e82e939-908b-42bf-a314-0bb02a3f1b07?syn-25a6b1a6=1)和[宇树科技（Unitree）](https://www.ft.com/content/14cd8246-7fb1-4f8f-81b4-8de11ced79e5?syn-25a6b1a6=1)分别于上月和本周在上海重磅亮相，但尚未被纳入科创指数——这意味着如果它们被纳入，涨幅将会更大。

近期，选择在上海而非香港上市的顶尖公司数量有所增加，与此同时，北京方面越来越担心资本外逃，并希望确保其顶尖科技公司仍处于国内控制之下。

![Line chart of year-to-date performance (%) showing Shanghai’s tech index has outperformed Hong Kong and Shenzhen](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c381cc90b14810da359d213c929a70c/3c381cc9-0b14-81d2-8bb1-fadf22ef9339-00d25976be8b04f26e9c6b896067ccdf.png)

[中国](https://www.ft.com/china)金融监管机构“将科创板作为首要优先事项”，法国巴黎银行亚太股票与衍生品策略主管Jason Lui表示，他还补充说，该板块受益于“强有力的政策支持”。

[科创板](https://www.ft.com/content/537a97c8-ad20-11e9-8030-530adfa879c2?syn-25a6b1a6=1)于2019年设立，是北京方面为国内创新提供融资的努力的一部分，当时与美国在关键技术上的紧张局势不断加剧。

科创板对盈利能力的要求较为宽松，允许涉及“硬科技”的公司相对快速地上市，这类科技包括半导体、机器人和新材料等行业。

北京推行这一政策，部分原因在于内地风险投资资金市场相对较浅。

该政策帮助上海成为中国快速增长的半导体行业中心。该交易所的一些顶级成分股包括海光信息技术和寒武纪科技——这两家芯片制造商对北京推动半导体自给自足至关重要。

科创板的成功正在促使深圳和香港做出改变，它们希望成为中国科技企业上市更具吸引力的场所。

今年4月，深圳证券交易所发布了一套新规则，支持尚未盈利但具有创新性的企业，这些规则类似于科创板的规则。

上个月，香港交易所公布了更宽松的上市标准，其中包括允许较小的公司采用加权投票权结构，这种结构在希望保留对其公司更多控制权的科技创始人中很受欢迎。

长鑫存储（CXMT）上个月在上海重磅上市。© Han Guan/AP

恒生指数公司和香港交易所正在改革其科技指数，将Z.AI、MiniMax、兆易创新（GigaDevice）和澜起科技（Montage）等今年在香港上市的公司纳入其中。

香港面临的风险是，随着更多创新公司进入内地市场，在港上市的中国科技公司可能会变得不那么重要。“恒生科技指数全是老科技股，”纽约梅隆银行高级策略师Wee Khoon Chong表示。

本月早些时候，恒生指数公司提议对其编制方法进行改革，将香港科技指数成分股从30只扩大到50只，并将销售增长和市值一并作为筛选标准。与此同时，香港交易所本周表示，正在改革其科技100指数。

这些措施包括将指数中单一公司的最大权重降至8%。这实际上将降低[阿里巴巴](https://www.ft.com/stream/085b1a45-6c58-4f62-b923-047085e89fa0)和[腾讯](https://www.ft.com/stream/26272ebc-a21c-450e-a743-be89c41d1eb8)的权重——这两只股票是今年中国股市中表现落后的股票。

尽管香港进行了这些调整，但当局更倾向于让具有战略重要技术（如半导体和其他实体“硬科技”）的中国公司首先在中国内地上市，Ninety One 的投资组合经理 Joanna Yang 表示。

这一偏好部分是为了减少资金外流，而此时北京正日益担忧中国内地的资本外逃。

“如果所有这些优质资产都去香港上市，所有这些中国投资者都会试图把钱带出中国，”伯恩斯坦的中国半导体分析师 Qingyuan Lin 表示，“他们试图把优质资产留在中国，这样本地资金就会留在本地交易所。”

然而，对于寻求为海外扩张融资的中国科技公司来说，香港仍然扮演着重要角色。

今年香港已有41宗科技企业上市，而科创板为16宗。

伯恩斯坦的林表示，香港对于希望吸引全球人才的中国内地企业也很重要，因为在香港发行股票，可以让企业为外籍员工发放股票激励，而无需受资本管制约束。

## Shanghai’s answer to Nasdaq outstrips Hong Kong amid Chinese tech frenzy

Shanghai’s answer to Nasdaq is benefiting from a wave of investor frenzy for Chinese technology stocks, outperforming rivals including Hong Kong by the widest margin since its inception.

The Star 50 index, which includes leading stocks on the city’s Star Market, has risen by 23 per cent this year. It is well ahead of the tech index in Shenzhen, which is up 10 per cent, and far better than the 14 per cent decline for Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Tech Index.

[CXMT](https://www.ft.com/content/8e82e939-908b-42bf-a314-0bb02a3f1b07?syn-25a6b1a6=1) and [Unitree](https://www.ft.com/content/14cd8246-7fb1-4f8f-81b4-8de11ced79e5?syn-25a6b1a6=1), which made blockbuster debuts in Shanghai last month and this week respectively, are yet to be included in the Star Index — meaning its gains would be even greater had they been.

The recent uptick in top companies choosing to list in Shanghai rather than Hong Kong comes as Beijing becomes increasingly concerned about capital flight and ensuring its top technology companies remain under domestic control.

[China](https://www.ft.com/china)’s financial regulators have “focused on the Star board as the primary priority”, said Jason Lui, head of Asia-Pacific equities and derivatives strategy at BNP Paribas, who added that it had benefited from “strong policy backing”.

The [Star Market](https://www.ft.com/content/537a97c8-ad20-11e9-8030-530adfa879c2?syn-25a6b1a6=1) was established in 2019 as part of Beijing’s efforts to finance domestic innovation as tensions with the US over key technologies mounted.

Star had looser profitability requirements that allowed companies involved in “hard technology”, which includes industries such as semiconductors, robots and new materials, to list relatively quickly.

Beijing pursued this policy partly because of the mainland’s relatively shallow market for venture capital cash.

The policy has helped Shanghai become a hub for China’s rapidly growing semiconductor sector. Some of the exchange’s top constituents include Hygon Information Technology and Cambricon Technologies — two chipmakers that are central to Beijing’s push for semiconductor self-sufficiency.

The Star Market’s success is prompting changes in Shenzhen and Hong Kong that want to become more attractive venues for Chinese tech listings.

In April the Shenzhen Stock Exchange announced a new set of rules supporting unprofitable but innovative firms that resemble the regulations for the Star Board.

Last month HKEX unveiled less stringent listing standards that included allowing smaller companies to use a weighted voting rights structure popular among tech founders wanting to retain more control over their firms.

CXMT made a blockbuster debut in Shanghai last month © Han Guan/AP

Hang Seng and the Hong Kong Stock Exchange are reforming their tech indices to include companies such as Z.AI, MiniMax, GigaDevice and Montage that have added listings in Hong Kong this year.

The risk for the city is that the Chinese technology companies trading in Hong Kong could become less relevant as more innovative companies arrive on mainland markets. “Hang Seng Tech is all the old tech,” said Wee Khoon Chong, a senior strategist at BNY.

Earlier this month Hang Seng Indexes proposed overhauling its methodology to expand the number of constituents in the Hong Kong tech index from 30 to 50 and include sales growth as well as market capitalisation as selection criteria. Meanwhile, HKEX said this week it was overhauling its Tech 100 index.

The measures include reducing the maximum weight in the index for a single company to 8 per cent. That will in effect reduce the weightings of [Alibaba](https://www.ft.com/stream/085b1a45-6c58-4f62-b923-047085e89fa0) and [Tencent](https://www.ft.com/stream/26272ebc-a21c-450e-a743-be89c41d1eb8) — two of China’s stock market laggards this year.

Despite the changes by Hong Kong, authorities prefer Chinese companies behind strategically important technologies such as semiconductors and other physical “hard technology” to first list on the mainland, said Joanna Yang, a portfolio manager at Ninety One.

This preference is partly to reduce outflows at a time when Beijing is increasingly concerned about capital flight from mainland China.

“If all these good assets go to Hong Kong for listing, all these Chinese investors would try and get their money out of China,” said Qingyuan Lin, China semiconductors analyst at Bernstein. “They’re trying to retain good assets in China so local money stays on local exchanges.”

However, Hong Kong continues to play an important role for Chinese tech firms looking to raise funds for overseas expansion.

There have been 41 tech listings in Hong Kong this year, compared with 16 for the Star Market.

Bernstein’s Lin said Hong Kong was also important for mainland Chinese firms looking to attract global talent because issuing shares in the territory permits companies to issue stock incentives for foreign hires that are not encumbered by capital controls.
