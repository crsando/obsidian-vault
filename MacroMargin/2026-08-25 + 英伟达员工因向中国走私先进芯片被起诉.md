### 中文译文

## 英伟达员工因向中国走私先进芯片被起诉

英伟达一名员工在台湾被指控向中国走私先进AI芯片，这是美国芯片制造商员工因涉嫌非法出口而首次被起诉。

台湾基隆地方检察署周一指控九人（包括服务器制造商超微电脑的员工）非法向中国客户出售高端AI服务器。

检察官指控被告串谋追求巨额利润，并“严重损害台湾的国际形象”。[英伟达](https://www.ft.com/nvidia)的许多芯片和服务器都在台湾制造。

他们称，英伟达一名驻台湾的高级经理（姓张）是涉及该芯片制造商130台旗舰B300服务器的计划中的“核心人物”。

B300是训练AI模型的热门服务器，搭载英伟达的先进芯片。由于华盛顿试图限制中国开发尖端AI的能力，这些服务器受到美国出口管制。

美国的限制催生了一个利润丰厚的黑市，在那里[价格可能翻倍或涨两倍](https://www.ft.com/content/57fcd3ce-464f-4dc2-8ea2-5712d4972c69)。

检察官表示，张、两名超微高级销售人员、一家台湾经销商及其他中间人串通，绕过英伟达和超微的客户核查。超微是多家将英伟达AI芯片组装进服务器的公司之一。

他们指控被告谎称这些服务器将用于台湾的一个数据中心，尽管该数据中心的供电和网络带宽不足以运行这些服务器。

根据检方说法，在130台服务器中，74台最终运往中国客户，要么直接从台湾发货，要么经印度尼西亚或日本转运。其余56台被台湾海关官员截获。

据称，张（Zhang）曾帮助确保服务器分配，并参与了对据称将使用这些服务器的数据中心的检查。

检方称，他故意隐瞒数据中心买家缺乏足够财务资源的事实，且用于购买服务器的资金实际上来自一家中国公司。

检方已请求对张和其他三名被告判处法定最高刑期五年监禁，而另外五名被告则被建议判处较短刑期。

英伟达（Nvidia）一位发言人表示：“我们的员工有充分的动力与客户勤勉合作，以确保遵守所有适用法律。我们将与台湾当局合作，帮助他们尽快解决这些指控。”

超微（Super Micro）表示，其“与台湾当局的合作促成了被起诉个人的被捕”，并且该公司“不是他们调查的目标，也未受到任何不当行为的指控”。

*迈克尔·阿克顿（Michael Acton）在旧金山补充报道*

### English Original

## Nvidia employee charged with smuggling advanced chips into China

An Nvidia employee has been charged in Taiwan with smuggling advanced AI chips into China in the first known indictment of a worker at the US chipmaker over alleged illegal exports.

Taiwan’s Keelung District Prosecutors’ Office charged nine people, including employees of server maker Super Micro Computer, on Monday with illegally selling high-end AI servers to Chinese customers.

Prosecutors accused the defendants of colluding in pursuit of huge profits and “seriously damaging Taiwan’s international image”. Many of [Nvidia](https://www.ft.com/nvidia)’s chips and servers are made in Taiwan.

They described a Taiwan-based senior manager at Nvidia surnamed Zhang as the “central figure” in a scheme involving 130 of the chipmaker’s flagship B300 servers.

The B300 is a popular server for training AI models and contains Nvidia’s advanced chips. The servers are subject to US export controls as Washington tries to constrain China’s ability to develop cutting-edge AI.

Washington’s restrictions have created a lucrative black market under which [prices can double or triple](https://www.ft.com/content/57fcd3ce-464f-4dc2-8ea2-5712d4972c69).

Prosecutors said Zhang, two senior Super Micro salespeople, a Taiwanese distributor and other intermediaries colluded to circumvent customer checks by both Nvidia and Super Micro, which is one of several companies that package Nvidia’s AI chips into servers.

They alleged that defendants falsely claimed the servers would be used in a data centre in Taiwan, even though its electricity supply and network bandwidth were insufficient to operate them.

Of the 130 servers, 74 were ultimately shipped to Chinese customers, according to prosecutors, either directly from Taiwan or routed through Indonesia or Japan. The remaining 56 were stopped by Taiwan customs officials.

Zhang allegedly helped secure an allocation of servers and participated in an inspection of the data centre where they would purportedly be used.

Prosecutors said he deliberately concealed that the data-centre buyer lacked sufficient financial resources and that the funds used to purchase the servers actually came from a Chinese company.

They have requested the statutory maximum sentence of five years in prison for Zhang and three other defendants, while the other five were recommended for shorter terms.

An Nvidia spokesperson said: “Our employees have every incentive to work diligently with customers to ensure compliance with all applicable laws. We will work with the Taiwan authorities to help them resolve the allegations as quickly as possible.”

Super Micro said its “co-operation with Taiwanese authorities led to the arrests of the indicted individuals” and that it was “not a target of their investigation and has not been accused of any wrongdoing”.

*Additional reporting by Michael Acton in San Francisco*
