### 对计算能力的不懈需求让投资者寻求拥有AI背后的物理基础设施的机会

安妮莎·加迪齐

2026年7月13日 美国东部时间晚上8:30

![Aerial view of a data center construction site in Atlanta.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39d81cc90b14817c95f7e766b1ca0c18/39d81cc9-0b14-8165-b2d4-f86c80efb736-353c1b9898e1423ba1489c67079e422d.jpg)

美国的数据中心开发商已准备好在人工智能热潮中套现。

据知情人士透露，全美各地的数据中心开发商和运营商正与银行家合作，计划今年夏天出售其公司价值数百亿美元的多数股权。

银行家们正致力于出售Netrality [数据中心](https://www.wsj.com/topics/industry/data-centers)、DataBank、Edged、EdgeCore Digital Infrastructure等公司的股权，这些公司的资产分布在从凤凰城到亚特兰大的各地。他们正在向私募股权公司推销这一热门资产类别，该类别受益于对计算能力的不懈需求。

数据中心运营商的出售活动正在增加，因为这些公司的所有者寻求退出，而投资者对拥有先进人工智能背后的物理基础设施的兴趣日益增长。服务器容量的巨大需求积压促使公司采取新颖策略来获取更多容量，从向直接竞争对手租用芯片，到[将数据中心送入轨道](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/data-centers-in-space-a-pipe-dream-or-ais-next-big-thing-c13bb184?mod=article_inline)。

从电工、水管工到燃气轮机和存储芯片，各类资源的短缺推高了新建数据中心的成本。[英伟达](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/NVDA)首席执行官黄仁勋最近估计，使用其公司架构建造一千兆瓦新计算能力的成本可能很快达到800亿至1000亿美元。据几位参与交易的人士称，一些运营商被迫寻找资金更雄厚的支持者来资助下一阶段的扩张。

据知情人士透露，正在筹备的最大潜在交易之一是出售总部位于达拉斯的DataBank的多数股权，交易价值可能高达250亿美元。DataBank由DigitalBridge牵头的财团所有，该财团还包括[瑞士人寿](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/CH/XSWX/SLHN)、EDF Invest和澳大利亚养老基金AustralianSuper。

据知情人士透露，总部位于丹佛的数据中心开发商和运营商EdgeCore Digital Infrastructure上周要求潜在买家提交报价。EdgeCore由瑞士私人投资公司Partners Group所有，后者已向该公司投入数十亿美元，支持其在弗吉尼亚州、亚利桑那州等市场的扩张。

上述人士称，这些潜在的股权出售谈判处于不同阶段，部分可能不会达成交易。据另一位知情人士表示，KKR新成立的Helix Digital Infrastructure以及其他公司正在评估这些资产。

当前的推动力度创下纪录。根据S&P Global Market Intelligence的数据，2025年数据中心并购交易额约为500亿美元，是前一年的两倍多。

今年夏天是否有足够多的买家有能力同时消化这么多大型交易仍是一个悬而未决的问题。Paul Weiss的基础设施联席主管Ravi Purohit表示，虽然许多投资者希望涉足数据中心领域，但只有少数公司能够负担得起数十亿美元的交易。

“能开出这些支票的人并不多，”Purohit说。

参与今年夏季数据中心销售的银行家和投资者表示，电力供应的保障是交易估值的一个主要因素。他们表示，对成本上升和延误的担忧也可能促使一些买家将部分付款与里程碑的完成挂钩。

私募股权公司越来越多地将数据中心运营商纳入其投资组合。

今年4月，黑石集团收购了由Quinbrook Infrastructure Partners支持的超大规模数据中心开发商Rowan Digital Infrastructure约49%的股权。这家私募股权巨头还在2021年以约100亿美元收购了数据中心开发商QTS。

去年，由贝莱德（BlackRock）旗下Global Infrastructure Partners和MGX牵头的财团同意以约400亿美元收购Aligned Data Centers，这是有史以来规模最大的数据中心交易。这笔收购预计将在未来几周内完成。此前的纪录是黑石集团（Blackstone）在2024年以约160亿美元收购AirTrunk。

但随着这些巨额交易规模扩大，开发商越来越频繁地遭遇当地居民的强烈反对，原因包括居民对水电费上涨、噪音以及普遍的高级AI服务感到焦虑。在某些情况下，这已迫使开发商暂停或放弃项目。投资者表示，所有这些都使得投资数据中心的风险比过去更高。

“我认为人们目前低估了美国‘邻避主义’的重要性，”Purohit说，并补充说，那些寻求今夏出售的公司将因其计划如何应对这一问题而受到审视。

“他们越能向买家展示自己与这些社区建立了建设性关系……这实际上大有裨益，”他说。

## **Data-Center Builders Are Racing to Offload Stakes Worth Billions**

### Unrelenting demand for computing power has investors looking for chances to own the physical infrastructure behind AI

By

Anissa Gardizy

July 13, 2026 8:30 pm ET

America’s data-center developers are ready to cash in on the AI boom.

Data-center builders and operators across the U.S. are working with bankers to sell majority equity stakes worth tens of billions of dollars in their companies this summer, according to people familiar with the efforts.

Bankers are working to sell stakes in firms including Netrality [Data Centers](https://www.wsj.com/topics/industry/data-centers), DataBank, Edged, EdgeCore Digital Infrastructure and others with properties located from Phoenix to Atlanta. They are pitching private-equity firms on a hot asset class benefiting from unrelenting demand for computing power.

Sales of data-center operators are on the rise as owners of these firms seek exits and investor interest in owning the physical infrastructure behind advanced artificial intelligence grows. A massive backlog of demand for server capacity pushed companies to pursue novel strategies to secure more of it, from renting chips from direct competitors to [launching data centers into orbit](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/data-centers-in-space-a-pipe-dream-or-ais-next-big-thing-c13bb184?mod=article_inline).

Shortages of everything from electricians and plumbers to gas turbines and memory chips have driven the cost of building new data centers ever higher. [Nvidia](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/NVDA) Chief Executive Jensen Huang recently estimated the cost of building a gigawatt of new computing power using his company’s architecture could soon reach $80 billion to $100 billion. Some operators have been forced to seek deeper-pocketed backers to fund their next phase of expansion, according to several people working on the deals.

Among the largest potential deals in the works is the sale of a majority stake in Dallas-based DataBank, which could reach as much as $25 billion, according to people with knowledge of the process. DataBank is owned by a consortium led by DigitalBridge that also includes [Swiss Life](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/CH/XSWX/SLHN), EDF Invest and Australian pension fund AustralianSuper.

EdgeCore Digital Infrastructure, a Denver-based data-center developer and operator of data centers for large cloud providers, asked potential buyers to submit offers last week, according to a person with direct knowledge. EdgeCore is owned by Partners Group, a Swiss private investment firm that has poured billions into the company to support expansions in Virginia, Arizona and other markets.

The potential stake-sale talks are at varying stages, and some might not result in sales, the people said. [KKR’s](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/KKR) newly formed Helix Digital Infrastructure is among those evaluating the assets, according to a person with direct knowledge, in addition to other firms.

The current push builds on a record. In 2025, data-center M&A hit about $50 billion, more than double the prior year, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence.

Whether there are enough buyers with the means to absorb so many large deals at once this summer remains an open question. Ravi Purohit, the co-head of infrastructure at Paul Weiss, said that while plenty of investors want exposure to data centers, only a handful of firms can afford multibillion-dollar deals.

“There are not that many people who can write those checks,” Purohit said.

Bankers and investors involved in data-center sales this summer say guaranteed access to electrical power is a major factor in deal valuations. Concerns around rising costs and delays might also drive some buyers to tie some of their payments to completion of milestones, they said.

Private-equity firms have increasingly added data-center operators to their portfolios.

Blackstone took a roughly 49% stake in Rowan Digital Infrastructure, a hyperscale developer backed by Quinbrook Infrastructure Partners, in April. The private-equity giant also bought data center developer QTS for around $10 billion in 2021.

Last year, a consortium led by [BlackRock’s](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/BLK) Global Infrastructure Partners and MGX agreed to buy Aligned Data Centers for about $40 billion, the largest data-center deal ever. The acquisition is expected to close in the coming weeks. The prior record was Blackstone’s roughly $16 billion acquisition of AirTrunk in 2024.

But as these megadeals scale up, developers are increasingly running into [fierce local opposition](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/the-american-rebellion-against-ai-is-gaining-steam-94b72529?mod=article_inline), driven by residents’ anxieties over rising utility bills, noise and advanced AI services in general. In some cases, it has pushed developers to pause or walk away from projects. This all makes investing in data centers riskier than it was in the past, investors said.

“I think people are downplaying the significance of Nimbyism in this country right now,” Purohit said, adding that companies looking to sell this summer will be scrutinized for how they plan to handle it.

“The more they can demonstrate to buyers that they have a constructive relationship with these communities…that actually goes a really long way,” he said.
