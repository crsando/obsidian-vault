### 中文译文

## 特朗普向国会提交与沙特阿拉伯的核协议

![President Donald Trump](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b1481a19f88fca43eb62937/3c881cc9-0b14-81f3-a5e0-eae4197e916d-49f3e2819df26e7c1cfaec61c437c11e.jpg)

*特朗普总统要求沙特阿拉伯加入《亚伯拉罕协议》是在该协议谈判并签署后宣布的。ALEX BRANDON/AP*

特朗普总统已将一项与[沙特阿拉伯](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/saudi-arabia)具有里程碑意义的核协议提交国会审议，但并未放弃其最后一刻提出的要求，即沙特王国与以色列关系正常化，一位政府官员表示。

此举预计将引发立法者之间长达数月的激烈辩论，讨论如何在不扩散中东大规模杀伤性武器的同时提振美国核工业。

特朗普官员上个月宣布这项为期30年的协议时表示，该协议将使美国公司在开发沙特阿拉伯核基础设施方面发挥核心作用，同时排除外国竞争。

但该协议已被证明具有争议性，因为它可能为在沙特领土上进行铀浓缩打开大门。

根据美国法律，该协议必须提交国会审议。该协议已于周一送交立法者，他们将难以阻止。若想阻止，需要一项联合决议，并以三分之二多数票推翻总统可能的否决。

沙特阿拉伯驻美国大使馆未立即回应置评请求。

沙特官员辩称，沙特王国需要发展民用核工业，这将利用沙特称其希望开采的铀矿矿床来满足国内能源需求。他们说，这将释放更多石油用于出口，并增加收入。

[作为协议的一部分，](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-approves-landmark-nuclear-deal-with-saudi-arabia-in-big-win-for-kingdom-2ed77584?mod=article_inline)美国和沙特官员将进行一项为期两年的研究，以确定美国公司在沙特王国建造铀浓缩设施的商业可行性。

如果该研究确定本土铀浓缩是合理的，美国公司将根据一项不会与沙特分享敏感技术的安排建造该设施。

然而，尽管特朗普政府官员表示，美国的角色将确保核计划保持和平，但批评者认为，这将鼓励核技术在动荡的中东扩散。

“国会应反对一项为沙特境内的铀浓缩打开大门的协议——即使是在美国运营的工厂——因为这将终结美国几十年来的不扩散政策，该政策旨在防止敏感的核燃料制造技术扩散，”保卫民主基金会智库的安德烈娅·斯特赖克表示。

特朗普提出的政治条件，即沙特必须加入《亚伯拉罕协议》以实现与以色列关系正常化，为实施该核协议带来了新的障碍。特朗普在上月协议谈判并签署后宣布了这一条件，并坚持这一条件。

“总统的立场没有改变，即只有沙特加入《亚伯拉罕协议》，该协议才会推进，”一位政府官员表示。

2020年的[协议](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/iran-strikes-latest-updates/card/what-are-the-abraham-accords--WdseYCs8ZwwyuHXDQ9zw?mod=article_inline)由美国促成，并由美国、以色列、阿拉伯联合酋长国和巴林签署。

在拜登政府期间，沙特阿拉伯似乎接近与以色列实现关系正常化，作为一项大型协议的一部分，该协议本将包括美国防务条约和民用核计划合作。

但继哈马斯2023年10月袭击以色列以及以色列军方随后在加沙的军事行动之后，沙特阿拉伯退却了。

## Trump Sends Nuclear Agreement With Saudi Arabia to Congress

*President Trump’s requirement that Saudi Arabia join the Abraham Accords was announced after the deal was negotiated and signed. ALEX BRANDON/AP*

President Trump has submitted a landmark nuclear accord with [Saudi Arabia](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/saudi-arabia) to Congress for review, but hasn’t dropped his last minute condition that the kingdom normalize relations with Israel, an administration official said.

The move is expected to trigger months of intensive debate among lawmakers about how to boost the American nuclear industry while curbing the spread of weapons of mass destruction in the Middle East.

When Trump officials announced the 30-year accord last month they said then that it would give U.S. companies a central role in developing Saudi Arabia’s nuclear infrastructure while excluding foreign competition.

But the deal has proved to be controversial because it would potentially open the door for uranium enrichment to take place on Saudi territory.

Under U.S. law, the agreement must be submitted to Congress for review. The deal was transmitted to lawmakers on Monday and will be difficult for them to block. Doing so would require a joint resolution and a two-thirds majority vote to override a potential presidential veto.

The Saudi embassy in the U.S. didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment.

Saudi officials have argued that the kingdom needs to develop a civilian nuclear industry, which would draw on uranium ore deposits the Saudis say they want to exploit to meet domestic energy needs. That, they say, would free up more of its oil production for export and boost revenues.

[As part of the deal,](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-approves-landmark-nuclear-deal-with-saudi-arabia-in-big-win-for-kingdom-2ed77584?mod=article_inline)U.S. and Saudi officials would conduct a two-year study to determine the commercial viability of American companies building a uranium enrichment facility in the kingdom.

If that study determines that indigenous enrichment is warranted, American companies would build the facility under an arrangement that would preclude sharing sensitive technology with the Saudis.

But while Trump administration officials say the American role would ensure that the nuclear program remains peaceful, critics argue that it will encourage the spread of nuclear technology in the volatile Middle East.

“Congress should oppose an agreement that opens the door to uranium enrichment on Saudi soil—even in a U.S.-operated plant—because it would end several decades of U.S. nonproliferation policy aimed at preventing the spread of sensitive nuclear fuel-making technology,” Andrea Stricker of the Foundation for the Defense of Democracies think tank said.

Trump’s political requirement that Saudi Arabia normalize relations with Israel by joining the Abraham Accords has introduced a fresh obstacle for implementing the nuclear agreement. Trump announced that condition last month after the deal was negotiated and signed and is sticking by it.

“The President’s position has not changed that the agreement will only move forward if Saudi Arabia joins the Abraham Accords,” an administration official said.

The 2020 [accords](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/iran-strikes-latest-updates/card/what-are-the-abraham-accords--WdseYCs8ZwwyuHXDQ9zw?mod=article_inline) were brokered by the U.S. and signed by the U.S., Israel, the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain.

Saudi Arabia appeared to be moving close to normalizing relations with Israel during the Biden administration as part of a megadeal that would have included a U.S. defense pact and cooperation on a civilian nuclear program.

But following Hamas’s October 2023 attack on Israel and the Israeli military’s subsequent campaign in Gaza, Saudi Arabia pulled back.
