## 习近平前所未有的追缴税款行动重创中国上市公司

习近平 *摄影师：吴汉关/美联社照片*

彭博新闻社 2026年8月26日 上午7:00 GMT+8

### **要点**（彭博AI）

- 黑龙江农业股份有限公司预计将报告上半年亏损，因为一笔税款账单总额相当于其2025年净利润的120%。
- 2026年上半年，中国超过100家上市公司被追缴税款和滞纳金，总额约77亿元人民币。
- 中国政府正加大对公司税的征管力度，因为地方省份寻求新的收入来源，税收收入低于2023年峰值，卖地收入以两位数速度萎缩。
在作为上市公司的二十多年里，黑龙江农业股份有限公司从未报告过上半年亏损。这一记录今年将终结。

导火索不是收成失败、欺诈或股市崩盘，而是习近平主席的税务人员向这家中国第三大农业集团旗下的上市子公司送达了一份《600598 黑龙江北大荒农业股份有限公司关于补缴税款相关事项的公告》，总额相当于其2025年净利润的120%，要求补缴此前享受但无资格享受的税收优惠款项。仅6月份的三天内，该公司的股价蒸发了数亿美元——相当于其市值的五分之一——投资者消化了这一坏消息。

这并非异常案例，类似情况正在中国各地上演。根据彭博新闻社对申报文件的分析，在2026年前六个月，超过100家上市公司被追缴税款并收取滞纳金，总额约为77亿元人民币（合11亿美元）。这一数额超过了自2012年习近平上台以来14年间追缴总额的总和。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b1481c7bb0be01c14597c28/3c881cc9-0b14-8102-a7f9-caecc0350454-0d294585aafc1f0ffeb1577f933cf2e2.png)

此次追缴行动的所得，仅相当于龙洲经讯（Gavekal Dragonomics）估计的去年约4000亿元人民币产业政策支持资金的一小部分，这些资金主要用于降低企业财务负担。但根据彭博社的分析，伤害不可否认，追缴行动抹去了许多公司上半年收益的很大一部分。而且这可能是未来情况的预演。

习近平正启动他最大规模的企业税款追缴行动，与此同时，各省份正在寻找新的收入来源。在国内需求萎缩的背景下，税收收入较2023年峰值有所下降，而曾经作为可靠“摇钱树”的土地出让收入正以两位数速度萎缩。此次行动标志着与一种持续数十年的增长模式决裂，这种模式建立在各省通过慷慨补贴来实现经济目标的基础上。

“GDP锦标赛模式正在降温，”法国外贸银行（Natixis）亚太区首席经济学家艾丽西亚·加西亚-埃雷罗（Alicia Garcia-Herrero）表示。“预计地方上将出现更加谨慎、更少创业精神的行为。”

中国西部某城市一位不愿具名的税务官员表示，当局刻意针对上市公司或资金充裕的大公司。另一位管理人员称，北京税务高层发税单的压力正遭到地方官员的抵制，他因讨论敏感问题而要求匿名。

长期以来，各省负责人竞相通过退税、补贴和定制税收协议来吸引投资——这一过程推动了自20世纪70年代末以来数十年的飞速增长，并培育了电动汽车和太阳能电池板等新兴产业。即使一些资金紧张的城市难以支付公务员工资和维护公交线路，它们仍然依靠提供优惠来吸引企业并实现增长目标。

当前发出的追缴要求包括错误适用税收减免、多申报增值税抵扣，以及撤销使符合条件的企业能够享受优惠税率的资格认定。

大多数上市公司在过去十年受益于一些税收减免。今年早些时候，中信证券（Citic Securities Co.）的一份分析发现，约64%的A股上市公司——中国投资者面临新的税收风险：持股——享有优惠的企业所得税税率。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b1481c7bb0be01c14597c28/3c881cc9-0b14-810f-9b83-e54dc6c1242d-bbe82322f545977b7d8886692213290a.png)

今年补缴税款账单最大的公司包括国有企业矿产、能源和科技巨头，如云南铜业股份有限公司和无锡太极实业股份有限公司，以及私营的爱尔眼科医院集团股份有限公司和电子产品批发商深圳市爱施德股份有限公司。这些公司大多数多年来一直持续盈利。

现在许多公司正遭受远超上半年利润的追缴税款账单冲击。爱施德承担的成本相当于其上半年利润的六倍多。总体而言，截至6月，受影响最严重的10家公司贡献了今年上市公司报告的全部补缴税款的70%以上。

以黑龙江农业公司为例，该公司不得不结清一笔与租赁土地企业所得税减免相关的款项，该减免可追溯至2021年。这导致其上半年预计亏损近5.37亿元人民币。6月披露的账单使其股价连续两个交易日下跌10%的每日限制——这是十多年前市场崩盘以来从未发生过的情况。

多家公司在交易所公告中提供了和解细节，称重点是企业所得税或增值税。大多数表示问题是在税务机关要求的“自查”中发现的，许多公司强调未受到行政处罚。

在一些最大的补缴税款企业中，对利润的影响十分明显，这些企业提供了2026年上半年净利润指引及税务支出的影响。黑龙江农业公司预计亏损，而湖南华菱钢铁股份有限公司和深圳市爱施德股份有限公司报告净利润同比下降至少79%。

中国西部的一位官员表示，迄今为止，规模较小的公司大多得以幸免，因为当局不愿过度开展该行动。该官员将此做法描述为务实，并表示企业正受到宽容对待——只要它们支付应缴税款的合理部分，即使低于法律规定的应缴金额。

随着地方官员不再使用税收补贴刺激增长，他们正在响应北京推动资本流向关键领域，并大力投资科技初创企业。内存芯片制造商长鑫存储技术股份有限公司（CXMT Corp.）等重磅上市，增加了人们对这些长期押注能够产生回报并帮助减轻债务负担的希望。

中国中部的安徽省已被证明是一个成功案例，得益于其通过实体网络持有CXMT约40%的股份。截至周二，这项投资价值约1.5万亿元人民币，是该省今年预计一般公共预算收入的三倍以上。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b1481c7bb0be01c14597c28/3c881cc9-0b14-8126-a7b2-d2a31bbd7144-8b9533628c1a8cf93e7594812f073332.png)

这一转变发生之际，中国正在告别已故总理李克强倡导的通过减税支持企业的十年。今年早些时候，政府公布五年发展蓝图时，中国首次承诺将税收维持在相对于国内生产总值的“合理”水平，这使该转变正式化。

财政部副部长廖岷在周五的[吹风会](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/china-plans-new-fiscal-support-policies-this-year-amid-slowdown)上表示，政府将“系统性审查”任何过时的税收优惠，逐步取消被认为不必要的税收优惠，并完善其他优惠，并补充说目标是减少他所称的碎片化政策。

执行这一转变的核心是金税四期——中国于2021年开始推行的行政[系统](https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/chinatax/n810219/n810744/c101763/c101790/c5177480/content.html)，它将税务数据与银行、海关、市场监管、公安和支付平台维护的数据库相融合。随着[全国电子发票](https://m.huanqiu.com/article/4IofDUesGM2)在2025年基本完成，北京现在对大多数商业交易拥有近乎实时的可见性，使得少缴税款更难隐藏。

中国西部的一位官员表示，该系统会标记问题，促使当局要求企业先进行自查，然后再进行检查。该官员补充说，自去年年底以来，执法力度明显加强。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b1481c7bb0be01c14597c28/3c881cc9-0b14-817c-ade9-e14c8985b002-6a2acbe50e3720c86529f90e0276e5ce.png)

为了解决地方不计后果地争夺投资的问题，当局还推动各省废除或修订约830份涉及非法税收政策的文件和合同条款，据国家税务总局消息。

“政府收入不足，但新的税源又难以寻找，”华中地区一家家电制造商的老板 Kevin Gao 说。“因此，当局别无选择，只能从现有税源中挤出更多。”

Gao 表示，他所在城市的官员从去年年中起加强了税收执法，几乎对每家企业进行全面审查。他说，审计将给总税基带来持续增长，而不仅仅是一次性的收入提振，因为企业现在“在修订后的合规标准下面临更高的税单。”

更广泛地说，中国正在缩减官员们过去提供的多个行业的税收优惠，实际上增加了部分企业和个人的成本，同时增加了政府收入。面对海外对中国商品涌入的反弹，北京自4月起取消或削减了数百种商品的出口退税，包括太阳能电池和电池相关产品，并且今年将新能源汽车购置的税收优惠削减了一半。

各省不得不更加依赖中央政府来满足其日常支出需求。根据彭博社基于官方数据的计算，来自中央的转移支付目前占其预算支出的42%，该预算涵盖教育、医疗和社会支出等事务，而十年前这一比例为37%。

新加坡国立大学中国研究教授戴慕珍（Jean Oi）表示，地方政府在寻找新的收入来源时“陷入困境”。她还说，尽管北京明白需要进行财政改革以增强其创收潜力，但进展甚微。

戴慕珍说：“中央和地方政府都有兴趣征收更多税收。”她目前正从斯坦福大学学术休假，她在那里负责中国项目。“他们试图让每个人都纳税并遵守规则，但这足够吗？我非常担心。”

## Xi’s Unprecedented Tax Clawback Hammers Chinese Listed Companies

Xi Jinping*Photographer: Ng Han Guan/AP Photo*

By Bloomberg NewsAugust 26, 2026 at 7:00 AM GMT+8

- Heilongjiang Agriculture Co. is expected to report a first-half loss due to a tax bill totaling 120% of its net income in 2025.
- More than 100 listed companies in China were sprung with tax clawbacks and charges for delayed tax payments totaling around 7.7 billion yuan in the first six months of 2026.
- The Chinese government is cracking down on corporate taxes as local provinces hunt for new revenue sources, with tax receipts down from a 2023 peak and income from selling land shrinking at a double-digit pace.
During more than two decades as a public company, [Heilongjiang Agriculture Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/600598%20CH%20Equity) never once reported a first-half loss. That run is ending this year.

The trigger wasn’t a harvest failure, fraud or a stock market crash. It was President Xi Jinping’s tax collectors serving the listed arm of China’s third-largest farming conglomerate with a 600598 黑龙江北大荒农业股份有限公司关于补缴税款相关事项的公告 totaling 120% of its net income in 2025, as they demanded back payment for previously enjoyed perks that they said it wasn’t eligible to claim. Over just three days in June, hundreds of millions of dollars were erased from the company share price — a fifth of its market cap — as investors digested the bad news.

Far from an aberration, cases like this are playing out across China. More than 100 listed companies were sprung with tax clawbacks and charges for delayed tax payments totaling around 7.7 billion yuan ($1.1 billion) in the first six months of 2026, according to a Bloomberg News analysis of filings. That tally exceeded the entire amount reclaimed in the 14 years since Xi came to power in 2012.

Proceeds of the campaign amount to a fraction of roughly 400 billion yuan in industrial policy support that Gavekal Dragonomics estimates largely went to lowering the financial burden for companies last year. But the damage is undeniable, with clawbacks wiping out a large portion of first‑half earnings for many firms, according to Bloomberg’s analysis. And they could be a preview of what’s to come.

Xi is embarking on his biggest push to recover corporate taxes as local provinces hunt for new revenue sources. Tax receipts are down from a 2023 peak amid dwindling domestic demand, while income from selling land — once a reliable cash cow — is shrinking at a double-digit pace. The crackdown signals a break from a decades-old growth model built on provinces using generous subsidies to hit economic targets.

“The GDP tournament model is being dialed down,” said Alicia Garcia-Herrero, chief economist for Asia Pacific at Natixis. “Expect more cautious, less entrepreneurial behavior on the ground.”

One tax official in a western Chinese city, who asked not to be identified, said authorities were deliberately targeting public companies or big firms flush with money. Pressure from tax bosses in Beijing to issue such bills was meeting resistance from local officials, according to another administrator, who asked for anonymity to discuss sensitive matters.

Provincial chiefs have long jostled to lure investment with rebates, subsidies and bespoke tax deals — a process that helped drive decades of breakneck growth from the late 1970s and foster emerging industries such as electric cars and solar panels. Even as some cash-strapped cities struggled to pay officials’ salaries and maintain bus routes, they still relied on offering perks to entice companies and meet growth goals.

Payment demands being made now range from the misapplication of tax relief and over-claimed VAT deductions to the revocation of designations that enabled qualified companies to pay a favorable rate.

Most public firms have benefited from some tax breaks over the past decade. Companies with the biggest back-tax bills this year include state-owned mineral, energy and tech giants such as [Yunnan Copper Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/000878%20CH%20Equity) and [Wuxi Taiji Industry Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/600667%20CH%20Equity), as well as privately-owned [Aier Eye Hospital Group Co](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300015%20CH%20Equity). and electronics wholesaler [Shenzhen Aisidi Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/002416%20CH%20Equity) Most had been racking up profits for multiple years running.

Now many companies are being hit with clawback bills that far exceed their first-half earnings. Shenzhen Aisidi absorbed a cost equivalent to more than six times its half-year profit. Taken together, the 10 worst-hit companies contributed more than 70% of all the back taxes reported by listed firms this year as of June.

In Heilongjiang Agriculture’s case, it had to settle a charge linked to corporate income tax breaks for leased land stretching back to 2021. That resulted in an expected loss of nearly 537 million yuan in the first half. Disclosure of the bill in June sent its stock price plunging by the 10% daily limit for two straight days — something that hadn’t happened since a market crash over a decade ago.

Several companies provided details about their settlements in stock exchange filings, saying the focus was on corporate income taxes or the value-added levy. Most said problems were uncovered during “self-reviews” requested by tax authorities, with many emphasizing no administrative penalties were imposed.

Implications for profits were stark among some of the largest payers that provided guidance for net income in the first half of 2026 and the impact from their tax expenses. Heilongjiang Agriculture expected a loss while [Hunan Valin Steel Co](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/000932%20CH%20Equity). and Shenzhen Aisidi reported a year-on-year profit drop of at least 79%.

The official in western China said smaller firms have been mostly spared so far because authorities prefer not to overdo the campaign. Framing the approach as pragmatic, the official said companies are being shown leniency — provided they pay a reasonable portion of what they owe even if it’s less than the amount due under the law.

As local officials move away from using tax subsidies to spur growth, they’re embracing Beijing’s push to steer capital toward critical sectors and investing heavily in tech startups. Blockbuster listings such as that of memory-chip maker CXMT Corp. have raised hopes these long-term bets could generate returns and help relieve debt burdens.

Anhui in central China has proved to be a success story thanks to its stake of about 40% in CXMT through a network of entities. That’s an investment worth around 1.5 trillion yuan as of Tuesday and more than three times as large as the province’s expected general public revenue this year.

The shift comes as China’s is turning the page on a decade of supporting businesses through tax cuts championed by late Premier Li Keqiang. The change was formalized earlier this year when China vowed for the first time to maintain taxes at a “reasonable” level relative to gross domestic product when the government unveiled its five-year blueprint for development.

The government will “systematically review” any outdated tax breaks, phasing out those deemed unnecessary and refining others, Vice Finance Minister Liao Min said at a [briefing](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/china-plans-new-fiscal-support-policies-this-year-amid-slowdown) on Friday, adding the goal is to reduce what he described as fragmented policies.

Central to enforcing the shift is Golden Tax IV — an administrative [system](https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/chinatax/n810219/n810744/c101763/c101790/c5177480/content.html) China began rolling out in 2021 that fuses tax data with databases maintained by banks, customs authorities, market watchdogs, public security and payment platforms. With [nationwide e‑invoicing](https://m.huanqiu.com/article/4IofDUesGM2) largely completed in 2025, Beijing now has near real‑time visibility into most business transactions, leaving under-payments harder to hide.

The official in western China said the system flags problems, prompting authorities to ask companies to carry out self-reviews before conducting inspections. Enforcement has noticeably tightened since the end of last year, the official added.

Addressing the problem of reckless local competition for investment, authorities also pushed provinces to abolish or revise some 830 documents and contractual items involving unlawful tax policies, [according](http://www.china.com.cn/zhibo/content_118617616.htm#fullText) to the State Taxation Administration.

“Government revenue is falling short, yet new tax sources are hard to come by,” said Kevin Gao, who owns a home appliance maker in central China. “So the authorities have no choice but to squeeze more out of existing ones.”

Gao said officials in his city strengthened tax enforcement from the middle of last year, subjecting almost every business to full reviews. Audits will deliver lasting growth to the total tax base, he said, rather than just a one-time revenue boost, because companies now “face higher bills under revised compliance standards.”

More broadly, China is scaling back tax breaks on a range of industries officials used to offer, effectively adding costs to some firms and [individuals](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/china-targets-offshore-trusts-in-sweeping-tax-clampdown) while reaping more government income. Facing a backlash abroad against the flood of Chinese goods, Beijing removed or [cut export rebates](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-09/china-to-cut-export-tax-rebates-to-ease-global-trade-tensions) on hundreds of products from April, including solar cells and battery-related goods, and this year [slashed](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202306/content_6887734.htm?f_link_type=f_linkinlinenote&flow_extra=eyJpbmxpbmVfZGlzcGxheV9wb3NpdGlvbiI6MCwiZG9jX3Bvc2l0aW9uIjowLCJkb2NfaWQiOiI2NDRmM2FjZjA0MGY5NzNjLTliOGZhNzMwYzQwMTViMGYifQ%3D%3D) tax breaks for new energy car purchases by half.

Provinces are having to rely more on the national government to meet their day-to-day spending needs. Payments transferred from the center now account for 42% of their expenditure under the budget covering matters such as education, healthcare and social spending, up from 37% a decade ago, according to Bloomberg calculations based on official data.

Local governments are “in a difficult bind” as they search for what’s going to be their new source of revenue, said Jean Oi, a professor of China studies at the National University of Singapore. While Beijing understands fiscal reforms are needed to beef up their revenue-earning potential, little progress has been made, she added.

“Both the central and local governments have an interest in getting more taxes,” said Oi, who’s on academic leave from Stanford University, where she directs the China Program. “They’re trying to get everybody to pay up and follow the rules, but is it enough? I really worry.”
