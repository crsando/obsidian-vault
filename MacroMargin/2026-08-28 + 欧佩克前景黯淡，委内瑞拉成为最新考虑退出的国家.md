### 中文译文

## 欧佩克前景黯淡，委内瑞拉成为最新考虑退出的国家

欧佩克对全球石油市场长达数十年的掌控再次松动，创始成员国委内瑞拉成为数月内第二个考虑退出的国家。

原油交易商表示，委内瑞拉正考虑是否离开这个六十多年前帮助创建的组织，随着该国与美国关系日益紧密，这一决定短期内对全球石油供应的影响可能微乎其微。

但紧随[阿联酋](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/uae-to-leave-opec-and-opec-next-month-to-pursue-new-strategy)退出之后，加之成员国伊拉克也显露不满迹象，这一潜在举动将不可避免地引发质疑：这个由沙特阿拉伯领导的集团能否继续团结一致并对油价施加影响。

如果最终瓦解真的发生，将增加联盟成员相互争夺客户和市场份额的可能性——这可能会重演2020年疫情初期爆发的短暂但破坏性的价格战。

但即使能够避免价格战，石油输出国组织进一步分裂的影响也将被广泛感受到。石油市场将失去一个在供应过剩时介入的全球供应管理者，导致价格更容易下跌，全球石油业也更容易受到冲击。

“欧佩克的凝聚力和信誉可能危在旦夕，”阿曼能源部前石油和天然气营销总干事阿里·里亚米表示。阿曼是更广泛的欧佩克+联盟的成员。“关键问题是，这是否标志着更大规模退出浪潮的开始。”

近年来，欧佩克及其合作伙伴的主导地位受到美国页岩油生产商、巴西和圭亚那等竞争对手崛起的侵蚀。

最近，伊朗战争使沙特阿拉伯和其他海湾国家沦为旁观者，迫使它们削减产量，并将更大的控制权转移给作为平衡石油市场参与者的中国，以及加大出口的美国。俄罗斯的影响力因对乌克兰战争导致出口受挫而受损。

“对OPEC来说，这是该组织对石油市场影响力减弱的又一迹象，可能导致油价更加波动，”凯投宏观的气候与大宗商品经济学家哈马德·侯赛因在谈到委内瑞拉的 deliberations 时表示。

短期内，委内瑞拉的退出不会产生重大影响，因为其产量近年来因制裁和经济危机而下滑，使其豁免于OPEC的减产协议和配额体系。尽管有与美国达成潜在协议的计划，但短期内大幅增产的前景渺茫。

但对OPEC威望的打击，以及此类退出的象征意义，将是重大的。

如果加拉加斯效仿阿联酋退出，这两次退出将使OPEC的日产量能力减少超过500万桶，约占年初核心成员国产量的17%。委内瑞拉还拥有世界最大的石油储量之一，在美国超级巨头的帮助下，未来几十年可能会释放这些储量。

委内瑞拉也被广泛视为1960年创建OPEC的五个国家中发挥最大作用的国家，这得益于时任石油部长胡安·巴勃罗·佩雷斯·阿方佐的不懈外交努力。

“这对OPEC来说将是一个重大打击，”Rystad Energy地缘政治分析主管豪尔赫·莱昂表示，他曾在OPEC秘书处工作。“委内瑞拉是创始成员国。”

OPEC设在维也纳的秘书处和沙特能源部没有回应置评请求。

阿联酋的退出并非近年来首次。

阿布扎比于4月底宣布退出，此前多年该国对产量限制感到不满，这些限制阻碍了其部署新扩大的生产能力。在阿联酋之前，过去十年中有四个国家离开了该组织，包括安哥拉在2024年的不愉快退出。

伊拉克长期以来也抵制OPEC的约束，在阿联酋此举后不久就开始流露出不满。巴格达在6月警告称，如果在正在进行的成员国产能审计中未能获得更高配额，它可能考虑退出该组织。该评估预计将于下月底完成。

由于伊朗冲突迫使沙特、伊拉克和科威特闲置大量产能，OPEC目前几乎无事可做。

不过，如果冲突得到解决，这种情况可能会改变。包括国际能源署在内的知名预测机构预计，如果波斯湾的石油供应全面恢复，全球石油市场将进入过剩状态，甚至可能在2027年面临潜在的供应过剩。

“欧佩克正在与石油地缘政治的重大变化作斗争，这看起来越来越像一场必败之战，”咨询公司欧亚集团能源与资源董事总经理亨宁·格洛伊斯坦表示。

他表示：“成员数量和产量的下降自然意味着该组织对全球市场的控制力正在减弱，尤其是在美国在供应侧获得了主动权的情况下。与此同时，中国已成为需求侧的关键摇摆角色。”

如果包括俄罗斯在内的更广泛集团OPEC+面临通过减产恢复平衡的压力，作为该组织实际领导者的沙特将承担更大的责任。沙特是否愿意扮演这一角色尚不明确。

“未来可能是一个前所未有的动荡时期，OPEC未来的角色和重要性可能被根本性地重新定义，”现为独立顾问的阿尔·里亚米表示。

### English Original

## OPEC’s Future Darkens With Venezuela Latest Nation to Mull Exit

OPEC’s decades-long hold over global oil markets is fraying again as founder member Venezuela becomes the second nation in a matter of months to consider an exit.

Caracas’s deliberations on whether to leave the organization it helped create more than six decades ago, as it cleaves ever closer to the US, may have negligible immediate impact on global oil supplies, crude traders say.

But coming shortly after the withdrawal of the [United Arab Emirates](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/uae-to-leave-opec-and-opec-next-month-to-pursue-new-strategy), and amid signs of discontent from fellow member Iraq, the potential move will inevitably spur doubts over whether the Saudi Arabia-led group can continue to hold together and influence oil prices.

Any ensuing breakup, if it did materialize, would increase the chances of alliance members competing with one another for customers and market share — a potential reprise of the brief but destructive price war that erupted in the early days of the 2020 pandemic.

But even if a price war could be avoided, a further fragmentation of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries would be widely felt. Oil markets would lose a global supply manager that steps in when surpluses emerge, leaving prices more liable to drop and the global petroleum industry more exposed to shocks.

“The very cohesion and credibility of OPEC could be at stake,” said Ali Al Riyami, former director general of oil and gas marketing in the energy ministry of Oman, a country that’s part of a wider of OPEC+ alliance. “The critical question is whether this marks the beginning of a broader wave of withdrawals.”

OPEC and its partners have seen their dominance eroded in recent years by the ascent of rivals like US shale drillers, Brazil and Guyana.

More recently, the war in Iran reduced the Saudis and other gulf nations to bystanders, forcing them to curb production, and transferred greater control to China as a player that balances oil markets, and the US, which ramped up exports. Russia’s clout has suffered as its exports were hit by its war against Ukraine.

“For OPEC, it is another sign of the group’s influence on the oil market waning, which could result in more volatile oil prices,” said Hamad Hussain, a climate and commodities economist at Capital Economics, said of Venezuela’s deliberations.

In the short term, the country’s departure has no major impact because its output has slumped in recent years due to sanctions and economic crises, leaving it exempt from OPEC’s agreements to cut production and system of quotas. Despite plans for potentials deals with the US, there’s little prospect of any significant production surge anytime soon.

But the blow to OPEC’s prestige, and the symbolism of such a departure, would be significant.

If Caracas followed the UAE in quitting, the two exits would remove more than 5 million barrels-a-day of OPEC’s output capacity, or about 17% of the amount held by core members at the beginning of the year. Venezuela also has vast among the world’s largest reserves, which could be unleashed in the coming decades with the help of America’s supermajors.

Venezuela is also widely seen as the country that played the biggest role among the five nations that created OPEC back in 1960, due to the tireless diplomatic efforts of its oil minister at the time, Juan Pablo Perez Alfonzo.

“That would be a big hit to OPEC,” said Jorge Leon, head of geopolitical analysis at Rystad Energy who previously worked at the OPEC secretariat. “Venezuela is a founding member.”

OPEC’s Vienna-based secretariat and Saudi Arabia’s energy ministry didn’t respond to requests for comment.

The UAE’s departure isn’t the first to happen in recent years.

Abu Dhabi announced its departure at the end of April, following years of frustration with output limits that prevented the country from deploying newly-expanded production capacity. Before the UAE, four countries left the organization in the preceding decade, including an acrimonious exit by Angola in 2024.

Iraq, which has also long resisted OPEC’s strictures, began to make restive noises shortly after the UAE’s move. Baghdad warned in June that it could consider leaving the organization if denied a higher limit as part of an audit being conducted on members’ capacities. The assessment is due to be completed at the end of next month.

As the Iran conflict forces the Saudis, Iraq and Kuwait to idle large volumes of output, there’s little for OPEC to do for now.

That could change though if the conflict is resolved. Prominent forecasters including the International Energy Agency project that global oil markets will tip into surplus if flows from the Persian Gulf resume fully, and even face a potential glut in 2027.

“OPEC is fighting what is starting to look like a losing battle against significant changes in the geopolitics of oil,” said Henning Gloystein, managing director for energy and resources at consultants Eurasia Group.

Its “declining memberships and volumes naturally means the group’s hold on global markets is waning, especially since the US is gaining agency on the supply-side,” he said. “Meanwhile, China has emerged as a key swing actor on the demand-side.”

If OPEC+, the wider group that also includes Russia, comes under pressure to restore balance by reducing output, there will be greater onus on the Saudis, as the group’s de facto leader, to shoulder the burden. Whether or not the kingdom has the appetite to play that role is unclear.

“What lies ahead is likely to be a period of unprecedented volatility, where OPEC’s future role and relevance may be fundamentally redefined,” said Al Riyami, who’s now an independent consultant.
