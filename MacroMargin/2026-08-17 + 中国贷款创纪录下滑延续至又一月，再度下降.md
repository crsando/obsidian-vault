### 中文译文

## 中国贷款创纪录下滑延续至又一月，再度下降

*摄影师：Raul Ariano/彭博*

彭博新闻，2026年8月14日下午4:42 GMT+8，*更新于2026年8月14日下午6:12 GMT+8*

### **要点**由彭博AI提供

- 借款人净偿还了5900亿元人民币的实体经济本币贷款，这是自2002年有数据以来的最高纪录。
- 社会融资规模增加了1.4万亿元人民币，其中几乎全部来自政府债券销售，总额达1.3万亿元人民币。
- 企业和家庭的中长期贷款均出现萎缩，反映出投资意愿和购房兴趣低迷。
中国一项新增贷款指标以新的纪录幅度收缩，政府债券未能提振整体信贷扩张，家庭和企业信心减弱促使他们回避债务。

根据中国人民银行周五公布的数据，借款人净偿还了5900亿元人民币（合875亿美元）的实体经济本币贷款，这是自2002年有数据以来的最高纪录。若将金融机构之间发放的信贷一并计算，整体贷款减少了3400亿元人民币，是经济学家预测降幅的三倍多。

社会融资规模（一项广义信贷指标）增加了1.4万亿元人民币，超过彭博调查经济学家的预期。但其中几乎全部来自政府债券销售，总额达1.3万亿元人民币。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b148159a389feb7a0a35dd8/3bc81cc9-0b14-81a6-af59-e05d62d03dbd-4dbef7a221c40e85d2968a13bf228e52.png)

曾经罕见的中国贷款收缩现在变得更加频繁，因为人们希望在经济增长放缓、工资停滞和投资回报低的环境中减轻债务负担。在最新数据发布之前，广义贷款指标在过去二十年中仅出现过两次下滑。

由于银行通常在第二季度末加大放贷力度，7月份的信贷活动往往会大幅下降。尽管如此，最新数据显示，即便考虑季节性因素后，潜在需求依然疲软。这就是一些经济学家一直呼吁政府加大借贷和支出以填补私营部门留下的缺口的原因。

“如果没有财政支持的大幅增加，银行贷款增长将继续放缓，”凯投宏观高级中国经济学家Leah Fahy在数据发布后的一份报告中写道。“降低贷款利率不足以将家庭贷款需求从低迷中拉出来。”

企业和家庭的中长期贷款均出现萎缩，反映出投资意愿和购房兴趣低迷。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b148159a389feb7a0a35dd8/3bc81cc9-0b14-810f-9f81-f12a3d7ce391-ad4935dbad9ab10e7dfef4ac18e083d3.png)

中国多年以来一直受困于贷款增长疲软，其[曾经强大的信贷周期](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-12/china-s-once-mighty-credit-cycle-is-a-fading-force-on-world-markets)曾推动全球市场，如今已不再是影响因素。

中央银行表示，这在一定程度上是因为经济正从房地产驱动的增长转向对贷款依赖较少的科技领域。近期，债券融资在新增信贷构成中首次[超过](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-18/china-s-tilt-to-bonds-from-loans-gives-pboc-broader-easing-tool)贷款，这得益于政府债券销售激增。

*“中国7月份信贷增长创历史新低，部分反映了北京从传统增长动力向新兴增长动力的转变。在缺乏强力政策支持的情况下，贷款密集型行业——特别是房地产和传统基础设施——的信贷需求继续萎缩。相比之下，高科技和新经济行业对债券和股权融资的依赖程度高于传统银行贷款。” — Eric Zhu*

中国人民银行（PBOC）对降息仍持谨慎态度，部分原因是担心加剧银行利润压力。自2025年5月以来，它一直维持政策利率不变，而彭博社上月调查的经济学家预计该基准利率今年全年将保持不变。

相反，央行专注于改善货币政策的传导机制。最近，它鼓励银行探索一种新的贷款定价方式。

多家银行[尝试](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/chinese-banks-break-with-lending-benchmark-in-pricing-new-credit)将新贷款与银行间利率挂钩，向一种能更好反映市场货币供需的新定价机制迈进。

## China’s Record Loan Slump Extends to Another Month With New Drop

*Photographer: Raul Ariano/Bloomberg*

By Bloomberg News, August 14, 2026 at 4:42 PM GMT+8, *Updated on August 14, 2026 at 6:12 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- Borrowers net repaid 590 billion yuan worth of local-currency loans extended to the real economy, the most in data going back to 2002.
- Aggregate financing increased 1.4 trillion yuan, with almost all of that coming from sales of government bonds, which totaled 1.3 trillion yuan.
- Both corporate and household longer-term loans shrank, reflecting weak investment appetite and interest in buying properties.
A measure of new loans in China contracted by a fresh record, with government bonds failing to lift up overall credit expansion as weakening confidence by households and businesses prompts them to shun debt.

Borrowers net repaid 590 billion yuan ($87.5 billion) worth of local-currency loans extended to the real economy, the most in data going back to 2002, according to data released by the People’s Bank of China on Friday. When counted together with credit issued between financial institutions, overall loans shrank by 340 billion yuan, more than triple the decline forecast by economists.

Aggregate financing, a broad measure of credit, increased 1.4 trillion yuan, more than forecast by economists polled by Bloomberg. But almost all of that came from sales of government bonds, which totaled 1.3 trillion yuan.

Once rare, contractions in Chinese lending have become more frequent because people are looking to reduce their debt burden in a slowing economy where wages are stagnating and returns on investment are low. The broader loans gauge has only slumped twice in the past two decades prior to the latest release.

Credit activity tends to fall sharply in July as banks typically ramp up lending at the end of the second quarter. Still, the latest figures show that underlying demand remains weak even after accounting for seasonal factors. That’s why some economists have been calling on the government to step up borrowing and spending to fill the gap left by the private sector.

“Without a significant ramp-up in fiscal support, bank loan growth will continue to slow,” Leah Fahy, senior China economist at Capital Economics, wrote in a note after the data release. “Lower lending rates won’t be enough to pull household loan demand out of the doldrums.”

Both corporate and household longer-term loans shrank, reflecting weak investment appetite and interest in buying properties.

China has suffered from sluggish lending growth for several years now, with its [once-powerful credit cycle](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-12/china-s-once-mighty-credit-cycle-is-a-fading-force-on-world-markets) that used to move global markets no longer a factor.

The central bank has said that’s in part due to the economy’s transition away from property-fueled growth toward tech sectors that rely less on loans. Bond financing recently [overtook](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-18/china-s-tilt-to-bonds-from-loans-gives-pboc-broader-easing-tool) loans in the mix of new credit for the first time, driven by surging sales of government debt.

*“China’s slowest-ever credit growth in July partly reflects Beijing’s shift from traditional to emerging growth drivers. Credit demand from loan-heavy sectors — specifically real estate and traditional infrastructure — continues to contract without aggressive policy support. High-tech and new-economy industries, by contrast, rely more heavily on bond and equity financing than conventional bank loans.” — Eric Zhu*

The PBOC remains cautious about lowering interest rate, partly for fear of exacerbating pressure on bank profits. It’s held its policy interest rate since May 2025, and economists surveyed by Bloomberg last month see the benchmark staying unchanged throughout this year.

Instead, the central bank has focused on improving the transmission of its monetary policy. Most recently, it encouraged banks to explore a new way to price their loans.

A number of lenders [experimented](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/chinese-banks-break-with-lending-benchmark-in-pricing-new-credit) with anchoring new loans to interbank rates, moving toward a new pricing mechanism that could better reflect supply and demand for money in the market.
