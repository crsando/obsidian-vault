## 中国在打击后重启自贸区债券市场

作者：[Janice Huang](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXlHy3rDuxA/janice-huang), [Pearl Liu](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWXVC4ff7OM/pearl-liu), [Shulun Huang](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWlV0NAHrz4/shulun-huang) 2026年8月12日 上午9:26 GMT+8 *更新于2026年8月12日 晚上10:28 GMT+8*

### **要点** 由 Bloomberg AI 提供

- 上海电气集团股份有限公司（Shanghai Electric Group Co.）在上海自贸区债券市场发行了15亿元人民币的三年期绿色票据，收益率为1.8%。
- 中国政策制定者希望企业更多地在境外发行人民币计价票据，但也设置了障碍，以遏制高风险债务的积累。
- 这笔新债券是上海电气自2020年以来首次在境外发行，目前正通过一家海外子公司销售，以符合自贸区的新规定。
上海自贸区债券市场迎来了自2023年底中国当局（中国各银行停止购买自贸区债券，打击城投公司）因地方政府过度借贷以来的首个非金融企业定价。

据一位不愿透露姓名的知情人士透露，由上海市政府支持的能源设备制造商[上海电气集团股份有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2727%20HK%20Equity)发行了15亿元人民币（约2.22亿美元）的三年期绿色票据，定价为：上海电气人民币15亿元三年期绿色自贸区担保债，收益率1.8%。这位知情人士补充说，所谓的“珍珠债券”收益率将为1.8%，而初步询价区间约为2.4%。

中国政策制定者希望企业通过各种机制（包括自贸区）在境外发行更多人民币计价票据，但他们也设置了障碍，以遏制高风险债务的积累。近几个月来，政府已[不鼓励](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/china-clamps-down-on-issuance-of-higher-yielding-offshore-debt)企业以较高收益率筹集资金，对海外借款的[审批](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-27/china-delays-foreign-debt-sales-with-100-billion-of-bonds-due)时间变长，并限制了[一种流行的贷款结构](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/china-curbs-loan-structure-used-distressed-developers)的使用。

“2023年暂停自贸区离岸债券发行是中国‘开放-收紧-再开放’周期的好例子，”富达国际亚洲固定收益主管Lei Zhu表示。“整体上，金融开放的大方向基本未变，但政策制定者在金融稳定风险开始积聚时调整了节奏。”

上海自贸区成立于2013年，最初允许国内外公司以任何货币出售离岸债券，不过该市场迄今为止一直由人民币债券主导。到2023年，它已成为负债累累的地方政府融资平台筹资的热门渠道，促使监管机构叫停国内银行购买明珠债券。

银行和证券公司去年在官员解除限制后恢复了使用明珠债券市场。根据彭博汇编的数据，在最新一笔交易之前，发行量比2025年同期增长超过五倍，达到5.12亿美元。这只是中国190亿美元市场的一小部分。

富达的Zhu表示，明珠债券可以为国际投资者提供更多安心，因为该市场完全由中国境内监管机构监管，他们只允许高质量发行人。她说，在近年来离岸投资者经历了一波房地产行业违约潮之后，这一点很重要。

有迹象表明，中国开放资本市场的努力正在取得成效。点心债券的发行量达到了今年以来的创纪录水平，而中国企业通过上海清算所发行的离岸债券销售也有所增加。外国实体在中国大陆发行人民币债券所使用的熊猫债券今年达到新高。

穆迪评级6月将上海电气（Shanghai Electric）上调一个档次至A3。

这笔新债券是该公司自2020年以来首次在离岸市场发行，通过境外子公司出售，以符合自贸区新规。据知情人士透露，该票据将由公司的香港子公司担保，并由上海电气提供维好协议。

## China Revives Free-Trade-Zone Bond Market After Crackdown

By [Janice Huang](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXlHy3rDuxA/janice-huang), [Pearl Liu](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWXVC4ff7OM/pearl-liu), and [Shulun Huang](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWlV0NAHrz4/shulun-huang)August 12, 2026 at 9:26 AM GMT+8*Updated on August 12, 2026 at 10:28 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

- Shanghai Electric Group Co. sold 1.5 billion yuan of three-year green notes, yielding 1.8%, in the Shanghai free-trade zone’s bond market.
- Chinese policymakers want companies to sell more yuan-denominated notes offshore, but have also erected barriers to stem a risky buildup of debt.
- The new bond is Shanghai Electric's first offshore sale since 2020 and is being sold through an overseas subsidiary to comply with new rules for the FTZ.
The Shanghai free-trade zone’s bond market had its first pricing by a nonfinancial firm since Chinese authorities China Banks Halt Buying Free-Trade-Zone Bonds in Blow to LGFVs on excessive borrowing by local governments in late 2023.

[Shanghai Electric Group Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2727%20HK%20Equity), an energy-equipment maker backed by the city, PRICED: Shanghai Electric CNY1.5b 3Y Green FTZ Guar. 1.8% 1.5 billion yuan ($222 million) of three-year green notes, according to a person familiar with the matter who asked not to be identified. The so-called pearl bond will yield 1.8%, compared with initial price talk of around 2.4%, the person added.

Chinese policymakers want companies to sell more yuan-denominated notes offshore through various mechanisms including the FTZ, though they’ve also erected barriers to stem a risky buildup of debt. In recent months, the government has [discouraged](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/china-clamps-down-on-issuance-of-higher-yielding-offshore-debt) firms from raising money at higher yields, taken longer to [approve proposals](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-27/china-delays-foreign-debt-sales-with-100-billion-of-bonds-due) for overseas borrowing and restricted use of a [popular loan structure](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/china-curbs-loan-structure-used-distressed-developers).

“The 2023 pause in FTZ offshore bond issuance is a good example” of “China’s open-tighten-reopen cycle,” said Lei Zhu, head of Asian fixed income at Fidelity International. “The overall direction toward greater financial opening has remained largely unchanged, but policymakers adjusted the pace when financial stability risks began to build.”

The Shanghai FTZ was set up in 2013 and initially allowed domestic and foreign companies to sell offshore bonds in any currency, though the market has so far been dominated by yuan debt. By 2023, it had become a popular avenue for debt-ridden local government financing vehicles to raise money, prompting regulators to halt purchases of pearl bonds by domestic banks.

Banks and securities firms resumed using the pearl-bond market last year after officials lifted restrictions. Issuance before the latest deal was up more than five-fold from the same point in 2025 at $512 million, according to data compiled by Bloomberg. That’s a tiny fraction of the country’s $19 billion market.

Pearl bonds can offer international investors more peace of mind because the market is fully regulated by onshore Chinese authorities who allow only high-quality issuers, according to Fidelity’s Zhu. That’s important after offshore investors in recent years experienced a wave of property-sector defaults, she said.

There are signs that China’s efforts to open its capital markets are paying off. Issuance of dim-sum bonds have reached a year-to-date record, while offshore bonds sales by Chinese companies linked to the Shanghai Clearing House have also risen. Panda bonds, used by foreign entities to sell yuan bonds in mainland China, reached a new high this year.

The new bond is the company’s first offshore sale since 2020 and is being sold through an overseas subsidiary to comply with new rules for the FTZ. The note will be guaranteed by the company’s Hong Kong subsidiary and supported by a keepwell deed from Shanghai Electric, the person familiar said.
