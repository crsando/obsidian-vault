### 中文译文

## 油轮为运出中东石油而拉长“隐形”时间

超级油轮为通过霍尔木兹海峡和曼德海峡而更长时间地“隐形”，加大了在伊朗战争早期磨练的战术力度。

在2月下旬冲突开始后的几周内，运载非伊朗原油的船只开始关闭应答器以避免被探测。当时，它们在通过霍尔木兹海峡时通常只会“隐形”几个小时。

如今，其中一些秘密行程可能持续一周或更长时间，油轮有时会出现在数千公里之外。这使得船舶追踪者难以查明期间发生了什么，而所谓的“穿梭贸易”的流行使这一问题更加复杂——在这种贸易中，石油就在霍尔木兹海峡外被转移到其他船只上。

这一趋势的一个近期例子是超级油轮“罗马尼亚繁荣”号：7月下旬，它在阿曼湾富查伊拉附近海域消失，10天后又在附近重新出现，观察者无法确定它是否装载着同样的原油货物，还是已将原油转移到另一艘船上，然后返回波斯湾装载新的货物。

船舶追踪数据显示，“罗马尼亚繁荣”号近日在富查伊拉和索哈尔附近海域闲置，但其应答器关闭了很长一段时间，使得很难知道它实际做了什么。来源：彭博

另一艘超大型原油运输船“亚历山大”号上月中旬在红海北部空载时消失，上周在印度附近出现时已部分载货。与此同时，超级油轮“科威特繁荣”号7月初在波斯湾满载原油时消失，10天后出现在印度尼西亚附近。

船只关闭应答器主要是为了隐藏位置并避免伊朗的攻击，由于运输利润丰厚，船东愿意冒险将货物运经危险咽喉要道。最近几周，阿联酋国有石油公司甚至已开始通过霍尔木兹海峡运输伊拉克石油。

Signal Ocean的高级市场分析师Maria Bertzeletou表示，这些隐蔽航行以及货运市场的其他动荡，“表明超大型油轮（VLCC）市场已经适应了不同的运营环境。”“已确认的油轮过境已转向隐蔽。”

油轮关闭应答器的策略随着美伊紧张局势的波动而时有增减。六月开始的临时和平协议导致更多船只[广播](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-23/hormuz-traffic-picks-up-as-more-tankers-cross-with-open-signals)其通行信息，但在德黑兰再次袭击船只后，黑暗航行增加。最近，在伊朗支持的胡塞武装组织宣布于七月中旬在红海封锁沙特阿拉伯石油后，这种做法进一步蔓延。

据 Signal Ocean 数据，在 7 月 14 日美国恢复对伊朗海上封锁至 8 月初期间，约 62% 的原油及成品油轮关闭应答器穿越霍尔木兹海峡。相比之下，此前这一比例约为一半。Signal Ocean 的数据未显示船只关闭应答器的时长。

### English Original

## Tankers Are Going Dark for Longer to Get Oil Out of Middle East

Supertankers are going dark for longer to get oil through the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb strait, doubling down on tactics honed in the earlier days of the Iran war.

Vessels carrying non-Iranian crude started turning off their transponders to avoid detection in the weeks following the start of the conflict in late February. At that time they would typically go dark for just a few hours while transiting Hormuz.

Now, some of these clandestine journeys can last a week or more, with tankers sometimes reappearing thousands of kilometers away. That’s making it tough for ship trackers to figure out what happened during the time, a problem that’s compounded by the popularity of the so-called [shuttle trade](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-08/aramco-adnoc-sneak-oil-through-hormuz-as-iran-menaces-strait), where oil is transferred onto other vessels just outside Hormuz.

A recent example of this trend was the supertanker Romania Prosperity: It vanished in waters off Fujairah on the Gulf of Oman in late July, and then reappeared nearby 10 days later, with observers unable to ascertain if it was carrying the same cargo of crude or had transferred it to another vessel and then gone back into the Persian Gulf to pick up a fresh shipment.

Ship-tracking data shows Romania Prosperity idling in waters off Fujairah and Sohar in recent days, but a lengthy period when it had its transponder turned off makes it hard to know what it actually did. Source: Bloomberg

Another very large crude carrier, the Alexandros, disappeared while empty in the northern Red Sea in the middle of last month before emerging partially laden near India last week. Meanwhile, supertanker Kuwait Prosperity vanished in the Persian Gulf loaded with crude in early July, before turning up 10 days later near Indonesia.

Vessels turn off their transponders primarily to mask their locations and avoid attacks by Iran, with shipowners ready to run the risk of taking cargoes through dangerous chokepoints due to [how profitable](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/sinokor-deal-sends-mideast-oil-tanker-benchmark-rate-surging) it is. In recent weeks, the United Arab Emirates state oil company has even started to move [Iraqi oil](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/uae-shuttles-oil-exports-from-iraq-through-the-strait-of-hormuz) through Hormuz.

The dark passages, alongside other upheavals in the freight market, “suggest that the VLCC market has adapted to a different operating environment,” said Maria Bertzeletou, a senior market analyst at [Signal Ocean](https://www.thesignalgroup.com/signal-ocean/signal-ocean-platform). “Confirmed tanker transits have shifted toward concealment.”

The tactic of tankers pushing turning off their transponders has ebbed and flowed as US-Iran tensions oscillated. An interim peace agreement that started in June resulted in more ships [broadcasting](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-23/hormuz-traffic-picks-up-as-more-tankers-cross-with-open-signals) their transits, but after Tehran began striking vessels again dark journeys increased. More recently, the practice has spread after the Iranian-backed Houthi militant group announced a blockade of Saudi Arabian oil in the Red Sea in mid July.

Some 62% of oil and product tankers crossed Hormuz dark between July 14, when the US reinstated its maritime blockade of Iran, and early August, according to Signal Ocean. That compares with around half before that. The Signal Ocean data doesn’t show how long ships are turning off their transponders for.
