### 中文译文

## 中国保险税报道引发保诚、汇丰股价暴跌

保诚集团、汇丰控股和渣打银行股价因一篇报道下跌，该报道称中国正扩大个人所得税征收范围，将香港保单的任何回报纳入其中。

北京和杭州的早期执法案例表明，中国税务机关已对离岸保单的股息支付和利息收益征收20%的税，从而堵住了一个长期存在的监管漏洞。彭博新闻社周三独家报道称，中国将税收网扩大至离岸保险。

保诚股价在伦敦交易中一度暴跌13%，创下自2020年3月以来最大盘中跌幅。汇丰和渣打股价下跌超过6.5%。保诚与[友邦保险集团（AIA Group Ltd.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1299%20HK%20Equity)是严重依赖中国大陆游客赴港购买保险的金融服务公司之一。

据一位知情人士透露，根据现行规定，离岸保险产品（包括人寿保险、投资连结保险和万能人寿保险）产生的投资回报在内地需要纳税。该人士补充说，由于数据可得性较差，全面执行该规则仍具挑战性。由于讨论的是私人事务，该人士要求匿名。

中国国家税务总局未回应在正常工作时间之外提出的置评请求。

杰富瑞金融集团（Jefferies Financial Group Inc.）分析师菲利普·凯特（Philip Kett）和德雷德·吴（Derald Goh）在研报中写道，对收益征收20%的税“使得在香港购买保单的增量回报因此降低，这可能合理地预期会对销量造成压力。”

杰富瑞分析师写道，尽管税收可能拖累未来销售，但“离岸保单被完全禁止的可能性较小。”

中国已加大力度加强对跨境财富的监管，并提高税收透明度，以弥补巨大的预算赤字。由于持续的房地产危机意味着地方政府不能再依靠土地出让或过度借贷为支出提供资金，他们面临财政收入压力。

除了对保险产品征税外，北京还对受到中国超级富豪青睐的信托结构进行了打击。他们还抵制所谓的红筹上市，即中国企业利用这种上市方式在海外融资，而不受当地税务官员的直接监管。中国还采取措施，通过离岸经纪商和香港银行账户遏制资本外流。

这些保险公司以及在该地区有风险敞口的其他金融公司，其股价在6月初大幅下跌，此前有报道称，一些银行暂停为内地客户开设香港账户，而这些账户可能被用于海外投资。

“中国据报对香港人寿保险保单免税回报的打击，对汇丰估值及年初至今相对于欧洲银行同业的优异表现背后的关键驱动因素之一构成了挑战，”彭博行业研究分析师弗朗西斯·陈（Francis Chan）和托马斯·诺策尔（Tomasz Noetzel）在一份研究报告中表示。

他们表示，人寿保险是汇丰增长最快的财富业务之一，第二季度手续费收入增长21%，上半年香港年度新保费增长26%。

### English Original

## Prudential, HSBC Shares Tumble on China Insurance Tax Report

Prudential Plc, HSBC Holdings Plc and Standard Chartered Plc shares dropped after a report that China is expanding its personal income tax to include any returns from insurance policies in Hong Kong.

Early enforcement cases in Beijing and Hangzhou indicate Chinese tax authorities are already levying a 20% tax on dividend payouts and interest earned from the offshore policies, closing a longstanding regulatory loophole, Exclusive: China Widens Tax Net to Offshore Insurancereported Wednesday.

Prudential shares plunged as much as 13% in London trading, marking their steepest intraday drop since March 2020. HSBC and Standard Chartered tumbled more than 6.5%. Prudential and [AIA Group Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1299%20HK%20Equity) are among financial services firms that rely heavily on visitors from mainland China to Hong Kong who buy insurance policies.

Under the current rules, investment returns generated by offshore insurance products, including life, investment-linked insurance and universal life, are subject to taxation on the mainland, according to a person familiar with the matter. Broad-based enforcement of the rule remains challenging due to poor data availability, the person added, asking not to be identified discussing a private matter.

China’s central tax bureau didn’t respond to requests for comment made outside normal business hours.

A 20% tax on returns “makes the incremental upside from buying a policy in Hong Kong therefore lower, which might be reasonably expected to weigh on volumes,” Jefferies Financial Group Inc. analysts Philip Kett and Derald Goh wrote in a note.

While taxes are likely to weigh on future sales, it’s “less likely that offshore insurance policies are banned entirely,” the Jefferies analysts wrote.

China has ramped up efforts to strengthen oversight of cross-border wealth and improve tax transparency to close a yawning budget deficit. Local governments are pressed for revenue as a protracted property crisis means they can no longer rely on land sales or excessive borrowing to fund their spending.

In addition to the tax on insurance products, Beijing has cracked down on trust structures favored by China’s ultra-wealthy. They’ve also pushed back against so-called red-chip listings, which Chinese companies use to raise money overseas without direct oversight of local tax officials. China has also moved to curb capital outflows through offshore brokers and Hong Kong bank accounts.

Shares of these insurance companies, alongside other financial firms exposed to the region, saw sharp declines earlier in June after reports that some banks suspended opening Hong Kong accounts for mainland Chinese clients, which could be used for overseas investments.

“China’s reported crackdown on tax-free returns from Hong Kong life insurance policies challenges one of the key drivers behind HSBC’s valuation and year-to-date outperformance versus European bank peers,” Bloomberg Intelligence analysts Francis Chan and Tomasz Noetzel said in a research note.

Life insurance was among HSBC’s fastest-growing wealth businesses, with fee income up 21% in the second quarter and Hong Kong annual new premiums up 26% in the first half, they said.
