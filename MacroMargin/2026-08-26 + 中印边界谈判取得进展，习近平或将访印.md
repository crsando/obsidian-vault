## 中印边界谈判取得进展，习近平或将访印

*印度拉达克地区中印边境附近公路上的一辆印度军车。摄影：Yawar Nazir/Getty Images*

彭博新闻社
2026年8月25日 晚上11:01 GMT+8 · *2026年8月26日 下午1:28 GMT+8 更新*

中国和印度在本周最新一轮高层会谈中同意寻求解决边界争端，双方正为习近平主席下月可能访问新德里做准备。

据中国官方[声明](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/wjbzhd/202608/t20260825_12010205.shtml)，中国外交部长王毅和印度国家安全顾问阿吉特·多瓦尔周二在北京就划定和管理边界举行了“深入”会谈。中国表示，双方将寻求“一揽子”解决方案，而印度表示，双方将继续致力于达成反映其长期利益的政治解决方案。

此次会谈凸显了这两个拥核邻国在2020年致命边界冲突后关系的逐步改善。然而，强调达成全面协议而非临时措施，表明突破仍然遥远，即便双方都寻求在其他领域扩大合作。

中印两国共享一条长达3488公里、未标记的边界，该边界近年来一直是紧张局势的根源。2020年6月的冲突造成双方士兵死亡，并将两国关系推向数十年来的最低点。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b148110a106c5411793478f/3c881cc9-0b14-81db-aab0-f5ea0b3e2e43-356afa465f1ab52a4bd5d522a2914570.png)

印度总理纳伦德拉·莫迪上次与习近平会晤是在2025年8月于天津举行的上海合作组织峰会期间。预计两位领导人下周都将出席在吉尔吉斯斯坦举行的今年上合组织会议。

习近平预计还将访问新德里，出席9月举行的金砖国家领导人峰会，这将是他自2019年以来首次访问印度。

自上次习近平与莫迪会晤以来，双方恢复了直航，印度也放宽了对中国企业投资的部分限制。然而，两国企业表示，他们在获取旅行签证方面仍面临限制。

印度本周对会谈的总结称，王毅和多瓦尔还讨论了在维护稳定、确保边境“和平与安宁”的同时扩大双边合作。

多瓦尔和王毅周二同意于2027年在印度举行下一轮会谈。

双方在2025年同意成立一个专家小组，探讨“早期收获”协议。该术语指的是达成有限、可实现成果的协议，而不是等待全面、最终的解决。

两国政府均未在周二会晤的说明中使用这一表述。中方反而重申了“一揽子”方案，这一措辞表明北京倾向于整体解决争端，而非达成逐步协议。

## China, India Border Talks Signal Progress Before Likely Xi Visit

*An Indian army vehicle on a road near the China border in Ladakh, India. Photographer: Yawar Nazir/Getty Images*

By Bloomberg News
August 25, 2026 at 11:01 PM GMT+8 · *Updated on August 26, 2026 at 1:28 PM GMT+8*

China and India agreed to pursue settlement of their border dispute in the latest round of high-level talks this week, as both sides lay the groundwork for a possible visit by President Xi Jinping to New Delhi next month.

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi and Indian National Security Advisor Ajit Doval held “in-depth” talks Tuesday in Beijing on demarcating and managing the border, according to an official Chinese [statement](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/wjbzhd/202608/t20260825_12010205.shtml). China said they would seek a “package” settlement, while India said they would continue working toward a political resolution reflecting their long-term interests.

The talks underscore a gradual improvement in relations between the nuclear-armed neighbors after a deadly 2020 border clash. Yet the emphasis on a comprehensive agreement, rather than interim steps, suggests a breakthrough remains distant even as both sides seek to expand cooperation elsewhere.

China and India share a 3,488-kilometer-long unmarked border that has been the source of tensions in recent years. The June 2020 clash killed soldiers on both sides and drove relations to their lowest point in decades.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi last met Xi in Tianjin in August 2025 at a summit of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization. Both leaders are expected to attend this year’s SCO gathering in Kyrgyzstan next week.

Xi is also expected to visit New Delhi for the BRICS leaders’ summit in September, which would be his first trip to India since 2019.

Since the last Xi-Modi meeting, both sides have restored direct flights, while India has relaxed some curbs on investment from Chinese companies. However, businesses in both countries say they still face restrictions on obtaining travel visas.

India’s summary of the talks this week said Wang and Doval also discussed expanding bilateral cooperation while maintaining stability and ensuring “peace and tranquility” along the border.

Doval and Wang agreed on Tuesday to hold the next round of talks in India in 2027.

The two sides agreed in 2025 to establish an expert group to explore an “early harvest” deal. That’s a term for an agreement that delivers limited, achievable results rather than waiting for a comprehensive, final settlement.

Neither government used that phrase in its account of Tuesday’s meeting. China instead reiterated the “package” approach, language that indicates Beijing’s preference for resolving the dispute as a whole instead of reaching incremental deals.
