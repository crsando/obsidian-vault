## AI繁荣正以前所未有的方式重塑美国经济

### AI已迅速成为巨大的经济因素，影响着从资本投资到你为iPhone支付的费用等方方面面

美国经济正越来越多地将筹码押注在人工智能上。

科技公司正花费数千亿美元来满足AI计算需求，并发行数十亿美元债务来助力这些采购。

数据中心的高速建设正在推动建筑支出、就业和市政收入增长。与此同时，由芯片制造商及其他受益于AI繁荣的公司股价上涨推动的股市反弹，带来了家庭财富的大幅增加，这有助于支撑消费者支出。

牛津经济研究院（Oxford Economics）经济学家迈克尔·皮尔斯（Michael Pearce）表示，综合来看，这些因素可能占美国近期经济增长的大约三分之一。

但经济从AI繁荣中获得的强劲动力同时也是脆弱之处：如果繁荣消退，会发生什么？

巴克莱（Barclays）经济学家乔纳森·米勒（Jonathan Millar）表示：“当前经济在很大程度上是由AI驱动的。很难想象没有这种推动力，我们还能拥有如此强的韧性。”

要精确衡量经济从人工智能中获得了多少动力并非易事，而且容易出错。即便如此，这种动力似乎相当可观。

上周发布的[国内生产总值报告](https://www.wsj.com/economy/u-s-economic-growth-slowed-to-1-5-in-second-quarter-003c464f?mod=article_inline)的细节显示，商业在人工智能相关领域的投资持续膨胀。该报告根据国家产出衡量经济增长。若以同样速度持续12个月，软件、数据中心建设以及计算机和通信设备的支出将达到约1.5万亿美元。而两年前的年度支出约为1万亿美元。

周一，美国商务部报告称，6月份数据中心建设支出按年率计算达到683亿美元，比去年同期增加215亿美元。同期，所有其他私人建筑支出——从住宅到购物中心再到医院——减少了1016亿美元。同样在周一，供应管理研究所7月份制造业报告中的评论指出，与人工智能相关的需求强劲。

并非所有科技相关支出都流向了人工智能——例如，其中一部分用于购买白领办公桌上的电脑。另一方面，人工智能建设也在向其他资本支出类别投入资金，如冷却系统和备用发电机。

投入建设的大部分产品（如存储芯片）依赖进口，因此部分人工智能支出并未直接提振美国产出，从而也未直接提振GDP。例如，今年前五个月，美国从台湾进口了约900亿美元的计算机设备、半导体和其他电子元件。而2024年前五个月这一数字约为200亿美元。

此外，通货膨胀正在蚕食人工智能支出的价值。芯片价格飙升意味着买家花同样的钱买到的东西不如以往。

即便如此，牛津经济研究院的皮尔斯估计，仅人工智能投资一项就支撑了近期国内生产总值增长近四分之一。

受人工智能提振的股市是另一大支撑来源。

据美联储数据，第一季度美国家庭净资产达到174万亿美元，较上年同期增加13万亿美元，主要归因于股票收益。此后，涵盖范围广泛的标普1500指数上涨约15%，因此目前家庭净资产可能还要高出数万亿美元。这些收益在很大程度上是由对AI的热情推动的。

当人们的净资产增加时，他们会更自由地消费。对这些财富效应的估计各不相同，但经济学家普遍认为，股市财富每增加1美元，人们的消费会增加几美分。持有大部分股票的高收入消费者的支出受益最大。

“如果没有这一投资热潮，我认为很明显经济将会降温，”皮尔斯表示。

诚然，AI可能正在吸走大量资源，以至于实际上在挤占经济的其他部分。例如，[数据中心建设所占用](https://www.wsj.com/business/ai-data-center-construction-is-boomingbut-not-much-else-is-6ae37b3e?mod=article_inline)——土地、材料、熟练劳动力——本来可能用于其他用途。

“你不能简单地剔除AI而让其他一切保持不变，”摩根大通经济学家迈克尔·费罗利表示。“与AI相关的活动和金融繁荣可能会挤出其他活动。”

AI也在助推通胀，削弱消费者的购买力。如果不是内存芯片价格跳涨，9月上市的iPhone 18 Pro [可能会更便宜](https://www.wsj.com/tech/personal-tech/apple-iphone-price-increase-e846d737?mod=article_inline)。

就目前而言，尽管近期一些AI相关股票遭到抛售，但AI支出热潮似乎仍将持续。据FactSet数据，分析师目前估计，在所谓五大超大规模企业——[Alphabet](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GOOGL)、亚马逊、Meta Platforms、微软和甲骨文——截至2029年的四年间，资本支出将接近4万亿美元，比他们大约一个月前的估计高出逾3000亿美元。

股市的真正麻烦可能会打乱这一局面。同样，为这一热潮提供资金的债务市场若出现问题，也可能打乱这一局面。巴克莱估计，五大超大规模企业和AI新进入者SpaceX今年的债券发行额将达到2850亿美元，而去年约为1090亿美元。

“人工智能触及的领域太多了，”巴克莱的米勒表示。“经济的很大一部分在某种程度上依赖于人工智能建设持续走强。”

### AI has rapidly become a huge economic factor while affecting everything from capital investment to how much you pay for an iPhone

By

The U.S. economy keeps putting more eggs in the artificial-intelligence basket.

Tech companies are spending hundreds of billions of dollars to meet AI computing needs and issuing billions of dollars of debt to help make those purchases.

The rapid data-center build-out is powering construction spending, hiring and municipal revenues. Meanwhile, a stock-market rally fueled by the rise in shares of chip makers and other companies benefiting from the AI boom has led to a massive increase in household wealth. That is helping to bolster consumer spending.

Combined, those factors are likely responsible for roughly one-third of the nation’s recent economic growth, according to Michael Pearce, an economist at Oxford Economics.

But the strength that the economy is drawing from the AI boom is also a vulnerability: What would happen if the boom fizzled out?

“It is very much an AI-driven economy right now,” said [Barclays](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/UK/XLON/BARC) economist Jonathan Millar. “It’s hard to imagine that we would be anywhere near as resilient without that impetus.”

Mapping out exactly how much strength the economy is drawing from AI is less than straightforward, and prone to error. Even so, it appears substantial.

Details of last week’s [gross domestic product report](https://www.wsj.com/economy/u-s-economic-growth-slowed-to-1-5-in-second-quarter-003c464f?mod=article_inline), which measures economic growth based on what the country produces, showed that business investment in AI-related categories continues to swell. Spending on software, data-center construction and computer and communications equipment, were it to keep the same pace for 12 months, came to about $1.5 trillion. That compared with an annual rate of about $1 trillion two years earlier.

On Monday, the Commerce Department reported that June construction outlays on data centers reached $68.3 billion, at an annual rate, up $21.5 billion from a year earlier. Outlays on all other private construction—everything from houses to shopping centers to hospitals—fell by $101.6 billion over the same period. Also Monday, comments included in the Institute for Supply Management’s July manufacturing report pointed to robust, AI-related demand.

Not all of tech-related spending went toward AI—some of it was devoted to buying computers that sit on white-collar workers’ desks, for example. On the other hand, the AI build-out is also pouring money into other capital-spending categories, like cooling systems and backup generators.

A chunk of what is going into the build-out, such as memory chips, is imported, so some AI spending isn’t directly boosting U.S. production, and therefore GDP. In the first five months of this year, for example, the U.S. imported about $90 billion in computer equipment, semiconductors and other electronic components from Taiwan. That compared with about $20 billion in the first five months of 2024.

Also, inflation is eating away at the value of AI spending. Surging chip prices mean buyers simply aren’t getting as much bang for their buck as they used to.

Even so, Oxford’s Pearce estimates that AI investment alone has been behind nearly a quarter of the growth in gross domestic product recently.

The AI-boosted stock market is the other big source of support.

In the first quarter, U.S. household net worth reached $174 trillion, according to the Federal Reserve, an increase of $13 trillion from a year earlier that was mostly due to stock gains. Since then, the broad S&P 1500 index has risen by about 15%, so net worth is likely trillions of dollars higher now. Those gains have been driven in large part by enthusiasm for AI.

When people’s net worth increases, they spend more freely. Estimates of these wealth effects vary, but economists generally think that for every dollar in stock-market wealth gained, people spend a few cents more. The spending from higher-income consumers, who hold the bulk of stocks, benefits the most.

“Without this investment boom, I think it’s pretty clear the economy would be running cooler,” Pearce said.

To be sure, AI might be sucking up so many resources that it is actually crowding out other parts of the economy. For example, resources that are being [taken up by data-center construction](https://www.wsj.com/business/ai-data-center-construction-is-boomingbut-not-much-else-is-6ae37b3e?mod=article_inline)—land, materials, skilled labor—might otherwise be put to other use.

“You can’t just take out AI and leave everything else unchanged,” said JPMorgan Chase economist Michael Feroli. “The activity and the financial exuberance associated with AI might be squeezing out activity.”

AI is also feeding into inflation, cutting into consumers’ spending power. The iPhone 18 Pro coming out in September [might cost less](https://www.wsj.com/tech/personal-tech/apple-iphone-price-increase-e846d737?mod=article_inline) if it weren’t for the jump in memory-chip prices.

For now, and despite recent selling in some AI-related stocks, the AI spending boom looks likely to continue. Analysts now estimate that capital spending at five of the so-called hyperscalers—[Alphabet](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/GOOGL), Amazon.com, Meta Platforms, Microsoft and Oracle—will come to nearly $4 trillion over the four years ending in 2029, according to FactSet. That is over $300 billion more than what they estimated about a month ago.

Real trouble in the stock market could disrupt that story. So, too, could problems in the debt markets that are helping fund the boom. Barclays estimates that bond issuance at the five big hyperscalers and AI entrant SpaceX will come to $285 billion this year, versus about $109 billion last year.

“AI is touching so many things,” said Millar, from Barclays. “A lot of the economy is kind of banking on continued strength from the build-out.”
