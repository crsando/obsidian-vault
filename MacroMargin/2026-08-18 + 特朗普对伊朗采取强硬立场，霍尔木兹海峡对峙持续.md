### 中文译文

## 特朗普对伊朗采取强硬立场，霍尔木兹海峡对峙持续

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普表示，他不会试图重启与伊朗已失效的停火协议，这使得中东不断发酵的冲突以及对霍尔木兹海峡控制权的争夺陷入僵局。

当被问及是否会寻求延长美伊6月签署的谅解备忘录时，特朗普回答“不”。该协议给予双方60天的时间窗口以谈判达成持久和平协议，技术上已于周一到期。

无论如何，停火协议曾多次被违反，零星冲突仍在继续，双方均已宣布该协议失效。

周二，布伦特原油一度上涨1.1%至每桶91.85美元，创下三周多来的最高水平，原因是霍尔木兹海峡迅速重新开放的希望变得渺茫。战前，全球五分之一的石油和天然气通过该海峡运输。随后油价回吐了大部分涨幅，交投于91美元附近。

特朗普要求恢复通过该水道的自由通行，而伊朗表示将与阿曼共同管理交通。

近日，在连接波斯湾与阿曼湾和阿拉伯海的航线上，多艘船只遭到袭击，交通量仅为战前的一小部分。周二，英国表示，一艘驶出海峡的船只被一枚射弹击中，机舱受损，造成一人伤亡。

伊朗与美国谈判的首席代表穆罕默德·巴盖尔·卡利巴夫周二告诉议员们，在美国解除港口封锁、释放冻结资产、取消石油制裁并停止所有军事行动（正如美国6月同意的那样）之前，霍尔木兹海峡不会重新开放。

“该政权愿意忍受巨大苦难，也愿意让民众承受巨大苦难，以维持权力，”战略与国际研究中心高级顾问马克·坎西安对彭博电视台表示。“我们正处于我所说的经济消耗战中，看谁能坚持得更久，看谁能迫使对方做出让步。”

战争于2月28日爆发，当时美国和以色列对伊朗发动空袭，并引发了席卷中东大部分地区的平行冲突。特朗普一再表示，他的首要目标是阻止伊斯兰共和国获得核武器，而伊朗官员否认在追求这一目标。

特朗普周一表示，美国对伊朗保持着影响力，并提到对其港口的海上封锁。他重申了将霍尔木兹宣布为美国领土的想法，坚称美国拥有完全控制权——这些言论与伊朗官员的言论直接矛盾。

伊朗和阿曼正在就如何管理霍尔木兹海峡进行谈判，但美国并未参与这些讨论。在接受福克斯新闻的电话采访时，特朗普警告说，如果“阿曼挡路，我们就把他们炸个稀巴烂”，但没有提供更多细节，这重复了他5月发出的[类似威胁](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-27/trump-not-satisfied-in-iran-talks-casting-doubt-on-quick-deal)。阿曼不是美国的正式军事盟友，但一直是密切的安全伙伴。

特朗普一直在努力寻找战争的出口，这场战争推高了汽油价格，在美国越来越不受欢迎，可能削弱共和党在11月中期选举中的支持率。他和他的官员承诺加大对伊朗的经济压力，迫使其屈服，但目前尚不清楚正在考虑哪些措施，而伊朗伊斯兰共和国已经承受了数十年的制裁。

战争破坏了伊朗的大部分工业产能，严重削减了其原油出口，并导致通货膨胀飙升。卡利巴夫承认伊朗公民有合理的经济不满，但表示他们应优先维护国家统一。

在该地区的其他地方，以色列军方近日发动了袭击，称目标分别是黎巴嫩南部的真主党和加沙的哈马斯，同时总部设在也门的胡塞叛军袭击了红海的船只。这三个激进组织都得到伊朗的支持。

与此同时，特朗普的女婿兼和平特使贾里德·库什纳（Jared Kushner）本周访问中东，试图加速美国斡旋的加沙停火，并与调解人以及以色列和哈马斯的领导人举行了会谈。

据一位了解会谈情况的官员透露，库什纳向以色列总理本雅明·内塔尼亚胡（Benjamin Netanyahu）保证，在哈马斯完全解除武装之前，以色列军队不会被强制撤离加沙。哈马斯则坚持要求以色列先从巴勒斯坦领土撤军，然后才放弃武器，尽管法新社周二援引该组织的话称，其仍致力于停火。

## Trump Takes Hard Line on Iran as Hormuz Standoff Drags On

US President Donald Trump said he won’t try to revive a lapsed ceasefire deal with Iran, leaving the simmering conflict in the Middle East and a tussle for control of the Strait of Hormuz stuck in limbo.

When asked if he would seek an extension to the memorandum of understanding the US and Iran signed in June, Trump replied “no.” The agreement, which gave the parties a 60-day window to negotiate a lasting peace deal, technically expired Monday.

The ceasefire had been repeatedly violated in any event, with intermittent clashes continuing, and both sides had declared it defunct.

Brent crude climbed as much as 1.1% to $91.85 a barrel on Tuesday — its highest level in more than three weeks — as prospects dimmed for a swift reopening of Hormuz, through which a fifth of the world’s oil and gas transited before the war. It later retraced most of those gains, trading around $91.

Trump is demanding that free passage through the waterway be restored, while Iran said it will govern traffic in conjunction with Oman.

Several ships have been attacked along the route linking the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea in recent days and traffic is a fraction of what it was prior to the war. On Tuesday, the UK said a vessel heading out of the strait was struck by a projectile that damaged its engine room, resulting in one casualty.

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran’s lead negotiator with the US, told lawmakers on Tuesday Hormuz won’t reopen before the US lifts a port blockade, releases frozen assets, removes oil sanctions and ceases all its military operations — as it agreed to do in June.

“The regime is willing to endure great hardship, they are willing to inflict great hardship on the population, in order to stay in power,” Mark Cancian, a senior adviser at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, told Bloomberg TV. “We are in what I call an economic war of attrition to see who can endure longer, to see who can force the other side to make concessions.”

Oil tankers and cargo vessels anchored off the coast of Oman in June. Photographer: Elke Scholiers/Getty Images

The war erupted on Feb. 28, when the US and Israel launched airstrikes against Iran and sparked parallel conflicts that have embroiled much of the Middle East. Trump has repeatedly said his primary objective is to prevent the Islamic Republic from obtaining a nuclear weapon, which the nation’s officials deny pursuing.

Trump on Monday said the US retains leverage over Iran, citing the naval blockade of its ports. He reiterated his idea of declaring Hormuz an American territory, insisting the US had total control — remarks directly at odds with those of Iranian officials.

Iran and Oman are negotiating a deal on how Hormuz should be managed, but the US isn’t part of those discussions. In a phone interview with Fox News, Trump warned that if “Oman gets in the way we’ll bomb the s—— out of them,” without giving any further details, repeating a [similar threat](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-27/trump-not-satisfied-in-iran-talks-casting-doubt-on-quick-deal) he made in May. Oman isn’t a formal US military ally, but has served as a close security partner.

Fighter jets on the USS Abraham Lincoln. Source: US Navy

Trump has struggled to find an off-ramp to a war that’s driven up gasoline prices and grown increasingly unpopular in the US, threatening to erode support for Republicans in November’s midterm elections. He and his officials have pledged to ratchet up economic pressure on Iran to force it to capitulate, but it’s unclear what measures are under consideration and the Islamic Republic has withstood decades of sanctions.

The war has damaged much of Iran’s industrial capacity, severely curtailed its crude exports and sent inflation skyrocketing. Ghalibaf acknowledged that Iranian citizens had legitimate economic grievances, but said they should prioritize maintaining national unity.

Elsewhere in the region, Israel’s military has staged strikes that it said targeted Hezbollah in southern Lebanon and Hamas in Gaza over recent days, while Yemen-based Houthi rebels have attacked ships in the Red Sea. All three militant groups are backed by Iran.

Meanwhile, Trump’s son-in-law and peace envoy Jared Kushner visited the Middle East this week to try and accelerate a US-brokered ceasefire in Gaza, holding talks with mediators and the leaders of Israel and Hamas.

Kushner assured Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu that his military won’t be compelled to leave Gaza until Hamas fully disarms, according to an official with knowledge of the talks. Hamas has insisted that Israel withdraw from the Palestinian territory before it gives up its weapons, although Agence France-Presse on Tuesday cited the group as saying it remained committed to the truce.
