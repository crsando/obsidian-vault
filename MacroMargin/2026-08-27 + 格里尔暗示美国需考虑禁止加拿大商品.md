### 中文译文

## 格里尔暗示美国需考虑禁止加拿大商品

特朗普政府最高贸易谈判代表表示，美国应考虑禁止部分加拿大商品，为华盛顿与渥太华之间的贸易争端增添了新的威胁。

美国贸易代表杰米森·格里尔周三在接受加拿大广播公司采访时表示：“加拿大已禁止销售酒类和烈酒。我们还没有禁止任何来自加拿大的商品。你知道，他们限制了我们可进口的汽车类型，还禁止某些商品和服务在各省采购。”

他说：“再说，我们从未实施过这些禁令，这相当极端。但也许我们需要这样做。”他办公室随后将这番话发布在X平台上。

格里尔在加拿大电视台的露面中，花了大量时间解释白宫对上周谈判的说法：谈判一度连续几天看似即将达成协议，却在周五深夜的最后期限前突然破裂。

对于谈判破裂的原因，各方说法不一。美国官员声称加拿大对美方开出的、给予任何国家的最佳条件不予接受，而渥太华则指责美方在最后关头做出改变，认为这些改变对其汽车工业和国家主权不利。

此后，美国对价值200亿美元的加拿大进口商品加征50%关税，约占加拿大对美出口总额的5%。

作为回应，总理马克·卡尼宣布，自9月8日起，同等金额的美国商品将被加征15%至50%的关税。他对美国总统唐纳德·特朗普的强硬立场，赢得了公众的广泛支持，民众准备承受与美国贸易战带来的经济冲击。

特朗普对北方邻国的攻势，加上加拿大计划中的报复，可能在国内引发反弹，加剧共和党在11月中期选举中的危险。

在接受CBC新闻采访时，格里尔表示，他与负责加美贸易的部长多米尼克·勒布朗的会谈仍然处于暂停状态。

“我想说我们现在没有开放的沟通渠道，”他说。“我和勒布朗部长关系良好，但现在我认为加拿大方面，你知道，正在讨论他们的下一步行动。”

## Greer Suggests US Needs to Consider Bans on Canadian Goods

The Trump administration’s top trade negotiator suggested the US ought to consider banning some Canadian goods, injecting another threat into a trade dispute between Washington and Ottawa.

“Canada has banned the sale of liquor and spirits. We haven’t banned anything from Canada. You know, they’ve capped the type of autos we can bring in, they’ve banned certain goods and services from procurement in the provinces,” US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said in an [interview](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/jamieson-greer-interview-canada-trade-9.7322114) with Canadian Broadcasting Corp. on Wednesday.

“Again, we’ve never done these bans, it’s quite extreme. But maybe we need to,” he said in remarks that his office later [posted](https://x.com/USTradeRep/status/2092753340710937010) on X.

Greer spent much of his appearance on Canadian television explaining the White House’s version of how talks last week, which for several days appeared [headed for a deal](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/trump-pauses-50-canada-tariffs-for-three-days-subject-to-a-deal-mszgvwa3), suddenly collapsed before a deadline late Friday.

Versions of the breakdown have differed, with US officials claiming Canada balked at the best terms offered to any country, while Ottawa blamed [last-minute changes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/lutnick-s-intervention-in-canada-talks-draws-praise-and-blame) deemed detrimental to its auto industry and national sovereignty.

Since then, US tariffs of 50% have been imposed on $20 billion in Canadian imports, or about 5% of the total.

In response, Prime Minister Mark Carney announced that similar amount of American goods will get [hit with duties](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/canada-s-carney-matches-trump-tariffs-with-50-tax-on-us-steel-furniture-dairy) ranging from 15% to 50% starting Sept. 8. His tough stance against President Donald Trump has been met with a groundswell of public support to hunker down for the economic fallout from a trade war with the US.

Trump’s offensive against the US’s northern neighbor, along with Canada’s planned retaliation, [risk a backlash](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/trump-s-trade-actions-give-republicans-another-midterm-worry) at home that deepens Republicans’ peril in November’s midterm elections.

In the CBC News interview, Greer indicated that his talks with Dominic LeBlanc, the minister responsible for Canada-US trade, remain suspended.

“I would say we don’t have open channels right now,” he said. “I have a good relationship with Minister LeBlanc, but right now I think the Canadian side is, you know, talking about their next steps.”
