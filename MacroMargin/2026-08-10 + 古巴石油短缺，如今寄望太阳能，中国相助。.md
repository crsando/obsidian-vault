## **古巴石油短缺，如今寄望太阳能，中国相助。**

转向可再生能源帮助这个岛屿挺过了来自华盛顿日益增加的压力。

哈瓦那

[古巴](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/cuba)燃料短缺，但不缺阳光。

随着这个加勒比岛屿的能源危机加深，中国提供的太阳能电池板在全国各地涌现，为饱受燃料短缺、基础设施破旧和几乎每日停电困扰的电网带来了一个出人意料的亮点。

根据追踪太阳能开发和卫星图像的数据库RenewAtlas，过去两年至少建成了41个中型和18个小型太阳能园区。政府于2024年[表示](https://www.pv-magazine-latam.com/2024/03/14/el-gobierno-cubano-asegura-que-instalara-92-parques-fotovoltaicos-por-un-total-de-2-gw-de-potencia/)，计划到2028年建造92个中型园区。

![Solar parks built in Cuba, Jan. 2024 to July 2026](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481ee9c71d49dbe903876/3b881cc9-0b14-8122-bb13-d6d37cdedfa6-94f8015a9f712e972f5fb277897921e7.jpg)

**2024年1月至2026年7月古巴建成的太阳能园区**

注：仅包含估计容量20兆瓦及以上的项目。排除图像不清晰的月份。

美国大学的古巴经济学家里卡多·托雷斯·佩雷斯表示，在2024年一系列全国性停电事件后，太阳能推广加速，部分原因是古巴从委内瑞拉进口的原油锐减。

但讽刺的是，尽管新增太阳能装机容量激增，古巴人每天仍有大部分时间无电可用。由于燃料供应不断减少，这个岛国的1000万居民几乎每天都遭遇停电，而在1月下旬，特朗普政府几乎阻止了所有运往[哈瓦那](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/havana)的能源运输后，这一危机进一步恶化。

新的太阳能园区在峰值出力时可提供约1,000兆瓦的发电能力，几乎是此前装机容量的五倍，也约占该国约3,200兆瓦典型峰值需求的三分之一。

政府表示，太阳能目前满足该国约10%的电力需求，高于去年年初的3%。

尽管尚不清楚哈瓦那为这些新建设项目支付了多少费用，但中国似乎是主要设备来源。

根据海关数据，北京方面（全球约80%太阳能电池板的制造商）在2025年向古巴运送了价值1.17亿美元的设备，而2023年仅为300万美元。数据还显示，中国今年还运送了更多电池储能装置。

古巴总理曼努埃尔·马雷罗·克鲁斯曾表示，这些项目部分由镍收入提供资金。政府的[2026年经济计划](https://www.minjus.gob.cu/sites/default/files/archivos/publicacion/2026-04/programa_economico_y_social_del_gobierno_2026ok.pdf)指出，来自中国的捐赠太阳能电池板总装机容量为320兆瓦。越南和日本也捐赠了少量设备。

来源：Ember

根据智库[美洲对话组织](https://www.thedialogue.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/A-Review-of-Chinese-Climate-Assistance-in-the-Caribbean-PubVer.pdf)的一份研究报告，中国已迅速增加对加勒比地区的与气候相关的捐赠和融资，该地区是北京从拉丁美洲获取原材料的关键中转区之一。过去二十年来，[中国已取代美国](https://www.wsj.com/world/china-xi-jinping-latin-america-acf6dbc1?mod=article_inline)成为该地区大多数主要经济体的主导贸易伙伴。

“中国通过与古巴的合作在整个地区获得了很多好感分，”研究古巴能源问题的得克萨斯大学的豪尔赫·皮尼翁说。

尽管新增的太阳能发电能力是受欢迎的补充，但几乎未能缓解古巴长期存在的能源问题。

由于燃料运输和国内石油产量多年来一直下降，古巴依赖进口的电网处于脆弱状态，甚至在美国切断委内瑞拉石油并威胁对向哈瓦那供应燃料的国家实施制裁之前就已如此。

古巴5月表示，其[燃料储备已耗尽](https://www.wsj.com/world/americas/protests-erupt-in-cuba-as-fuel-runs-out-8e577bc9?mod=article_inline)。特朗普政府此后[允许向岛上的私营公司运送一些燃料](https://www.wsj.com/world/u-s-eases-restriction-on-fuel-shipments-to-cuba-7909a6a7?mod=article_inline)。

该岛的能源基础设施也状况不佳。过去几十年，化石燃料发电厂不断老化，产出下降。根据托雷斯的[研究](https://cubastudygroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Without-Power-There-Is-No-Country.-Cuba%C2%B4s-Electricity-Generation-Crisis.pdf)，输电线老化导致电网约16%的电力损失。政府[无力偿债，无法升级](https://www.wsj.com/world/americas/cuba-struggles-to-revive-obsolete-power-grid-after-nationwide-blackout-6f714044?mod=article_inline)电网。

老化的电网也阻碍了新的太阳能园区。古巴能源部长4月表示，该国目前仅使用了已部署太阳能发电能力的一小部分，因为缺乏足够的电池存储来应对太阳能发电固有的波动。据官方媒体报道，7月安装了一块电池，另有3块计划安装。

除公用事业规模项目外，许多家庭和组织已安装屋顶太阳能系统。中国、西班牙和其他国家捐赠了数千块太阳能板用于个别建筑。但这些新增设施尚未在危机中产生实质性影响。

今年至少发生了六次全国性停电，以及无数持续数天的地方性停电。生活条件恶化引发[全国各地的抗议](https://www.wsj.com/world/americas/protests-erupt-in-cuba-as-fuel-runs-out-8e577bc9?mod=article_inline)。

托雷斯说：“如果没有太阳能园区，停电会更严重。但现实是，停电还是加剧了。”

## Cuba Is Low on Oil. Now, It’s Counting on Solar, With China’s Help.

## **Cuba Is Low on Oil. Now, It’s Counting on Solar, With China’s Help.**

The turn toward renewable energy has helped the island weather increased pressure from Washington

Havana

[Cuba](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/cuba) is running short of fuel, but not sunshine.

As the Caribbean island’s energy crisis has deepened, Chinese-provided solar panels have popped up across the country, offering an unlikely bright spot for a power grid plagued by fuel shortages, crumbling infrastructure and near-daily blackouts.

At least 41 midsize and 18 smaller-scale solar parks have been built over the past two years, according to RenewAtlas, a database that tracks solar developments and satellite imagery. The government [said](https://www.pv-magazine-latam.com/2024/03/14/el-gobierno-cubano-asegura-que-instalara-92-parques-fotovoltaicos-por-un-total-de-2-gw-de-potencia/) in 2024 that it plans to build 92 midsize parks by 2028.

**Solar parks built in Cuba, Jan. 2024 to July 2026**

Notes: Only includes projects with estimated 20 megawatts of capacity or more. Excludes months without clear imagery.

The solar push accelerated after a series of nationwide blackouts in 2024, in part because Cuba’s imports of crude oil from Venezuela collapsed, said Ricardo Torres Pérez, a Cuban economist at American University.

But in a cruel twist, Cubans are still spending much of their days without electricity despite the surge in new solar capacity. The island’s 10 million residents have suffered almost daily power outages as fuel supplies have dwindled, a crisis that worsened after the Trump administration blocked nearly all energy shipments to [Havana](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/havana) in late January.

The new solar parks could generate around 1,000 megawatts of capacity when operating at peak output, nearly five times what was previously installed and almost one-third of the country’s typical peak demand of about 3,200 megawatts.

Solar now meets about 10% of the nation’s power needs, up from 3% at the beginning of last year, the government said.

While it is unclear how much Havana is paying for the new construction, China appears to be the main source of equipment.

Beijing, the manufacturer of about 80% of the world’s solar panels, sent Cuba $117 million worth of equipment in 2025, up from $3 million in 2023, according to customs data. China has also sent more battery storage units this year, according to the data.

Cuban Prime Minister Manuel Marrero Cruz has said the projects were partially financed with nickel revenues. The government’s [2026 economic plan](https://www.minjus.gob.cu/sites/default/files/archivos/publicacion/2026-04/programa_economico_y_social_del_gobierno_2026ok.pdf) notes 320 megawatts worth of donated solar panels from China. Vietnam and Japan have also donated smaller amounts of equipment.

#### Chinese green technology exports to Cuba, quarterly

Source: Ember

China has rapidly increased climate-related donations and financing to the Caribbean, which is one of Beijing’s key transit zones for raw materials from Latin America, according to a research report from [the think tank Inter-American Dialogue.](https://www.thedialogue.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/A-Review-of-Chinese-Climate-Assistance-in-the-Caribbean-PubVer.pdf) Over the past two decades, [China has replaced the U.S.](https://www.wsj.com/world/china-xi-jinping-latin-america-acf6dbc1?mod=article_inline) as the dominant trading partner for most big economies in the area.

“China gets a lot of goodwill brownie points throughout the region with what they’re doing with Cuba,” said Jorge Piñón, who studies Cuban energy at the University of Texas.

Though the new solar capacity is a welcome addition, it has done little to ease Cuba’s chronic energy problems.

Since fuel shipments and domestic-oil production have declined for years, Cuba’s import-dependent grid was in a fragile state even before the U.S. cut off Venezuelan oil and threatened sanctions on countries supplying Havana with fuel.

Cuba said in May that it [ran out of fuel reserves](https://www.wsj.com/world/americas/protests-erupt-in-cuba-as-fuel-runs-out-8e577bc9?mod=article_inline). The Trump administration has since [allowed some fuel shipments](https://www.wsj.com/world/u-s-eases-restriction-on-fuel-shipments-to-cuba-7909a6a7?mod=article_inline) for private companies on the island.

The island’s energy infrastructure is also in poor shape. Fossil-fuel power plants have deteriorated over the past few decades, decreasing output. Power lines have degraded, causing the loss of about 16% of the grid’s electricity, according to [research by Torres](https://cubastudygroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Without-Power-There-Is-No-Country.-Cuba%C2%B4s-Electricity-Generation-Crisis.pdf). The government is [insolvent and unable to upgrade](https://www.wsj.com/world/americas/cuba-struggles-to-revive-obsolete-power-grid-after-nationwide-blackout-6f714044?mod=article_inline) the grid.

The aging grid has also hampered the new solar parks. The country is only using a fraction of the solar capacity it has deployed because it lacks enough battery storage to manage the fluctuations inherent in solar power, the Cuban energy minister said in April. One battery was installed in July and three more are planned, according to state media.

Besides utility-scale projects, many families and organizations have installed rooftop solar systems. China, Spain and other nations have donated thousands of panels for individual buildings. But the additions have yet to make a meaningful dent in the crisis.

There have been at least six nationwide blackouts this year, with countless localized dayslong power outages. Failing living conditions have sparked [protests across the country](https://www.wsj.com/world/americas/protests-erupt-in-cuba-as-fuel-runs-out-8e577bc9?mod=article_inline).

“Without the solar parks, the blackouts would be even worse,” said Torres. “But the reality is they’ve worsened nonetheless.”
