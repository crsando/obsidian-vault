## 特朗普在伊朗胜利遥不可及之际重返外交

## **特朗普在伊朗胜利遥不可及之际重返外交**

### 问题在于谈判能否最终重新开放霍尔木兹海峡，并为核谈判奠定基础

![U.S. President Donald Trump disembarks Marine One at Morristown Municipal Airport in New Jersey.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b181cc90b1481b19946e7c3853cf098/3b181cc9-0b14-8167-bd3d-fdda7f89ee24-25a5a95632732bc03d8d63c59fdb122d.jpg)

特朗普总统周六在社交媒体上写道，中东国家已要求他取消对伊朗的新一轮攻击，因为协议框架已经拟好。丹尼尔·霍伊尔/路透社

迈克尔·R·戈登

亚历山大·沃德

和

菲利普·韦格曼

Aug. 2, 2026 7:00 pm ET

- 特朗普总统在应中东国家要求取消计划中的军事进攻后，正寻求与伊朗进行外交接触。
- 美国计划于周一参与会谈，而伊朗和阿曼报告称，关于重新开放霍尔木兹海峡的谈判取得了进展。
- 这场持续六个月的冲突推高了油价，耗尽了石油储备，并自2月以来已导致18名美国军人死亡。
华盛顿——在数日威胁要对[伊朗](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran)发动新攻势后，特朗普总统再次试图通过外交手段实现他尚未通过战争达成的目标。

问题在于，特朗普的海湾伙伴能否在未来几天取得足够进展，以重新开放霍尔木兹海峡供航运通行（至少是暂时性的），并为最终核谈判奠定基础。

此前数月，在这两条战线上的努力均告失败，尽管周日出现了重新开放该水道的初步进展迹象。阿曼和伊朗表示，他们正在取得进展。

特朗普周日对记者表示，美国将于周一下午开始参与会谈。他说，他原本已准备好发动自二战以来最大规模的军事打击，但被美国盟友劝阻。

“我们当时已准备好行动，但当盟友要求取消时，你不得不有点说，‘好吧，再看看，’”他说。

然而，如果谈判像过去一样再次受挫，特朗普可能会再次陷入熟悉的循环，在搁置军事威胁后不久又重新提出。

“他现在正尝试通过外交解决方案来避免军事升级，但没有明确的道路，无论是外交还是军事手段，能让特朗普达到可以宣布胜利的地步，”约翰斯·霍普金斯大学的伊朗问题专家瓦利·纳斯尔（Vali Nasr）表示。

这一循环破坏了中东的稳定，并使特朗普政府的国内议程复杂化。最近几周，伊朗对该地区多个美国盟友发动了袭击，包括约旦、巴林和科威特，而其在伊拉克的代理人则向沙特阿拉伯发射了无人机。

周日，德黑兰展出的导弹。Atta KENARE/法新社/盖蒂图片社

伊朗实质上封锁霍尔木兹海峡的能力推高了汽油价格，并迫使美国和其他国家[动用它们的石油储备。](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/oil-executives-are-sounding-the-alarm-over-dwindling-stockpiles-ad0f6928?mod=article_inline) 与此同时，国会中的共和党人在距离中期选举仅三个月之际，敦促白宫更明确地阐述伊朗战争的目标，并解释战争将如何结束。

三名美国士兵在[伊朗导弹袭击中丧生](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-missile-struck-housing-for-u-s-troops-at-jordan-base-9f5b8120?mod=article_inline)，此次袭击针对他们在约旦的基地，这凸显了上述担忧，使自2月以来在行动中阵亡的军人总数达到18人。

当特朗普在2月底下令对伊朗发动袭击时，他是在有限的国际支持下开始这场战争的，民意调查显示许多美国人并不相信德黑兰构成了紧迫的核威胁。

特朗普试图安抚不安的公众，告诉他们汽油价格上涨不会持续太久，预测冲突可能在四到六周内结束。随着战争进入第六个月，他在国内外的支持基础已经缩减。

特朗普的支持者表示，他持续不断的威胁和明显的不耐烦，推动了一系列外交活动，这些活动可能促成一项期待已久的协议，最终结束这场战争。

特朗普周六晚在社交媒体发帖称，伊朗和其他中东国家正在恳求他取消攻击，因为“协议的框架”已经敲定。

特朗普在与沙特王储穆罕默德·本·萨勒曼交谈后发表了这一消息。特朗普周日表示，他问王储：“你是希望我们这样做，还是不希望？”特朗普称，王储回答说：“我们更希望达成协议，而非发动攻击，因为你不知道这些攻击会导致什么后果。”

但特朗普没有致电以色列总理本雅明·内塔尼亚胡，这位与他一同发动战争的坚定盟友，仍是总统耳边主张更多军事行动的最响亮声音。

周日出现了外交开放的最初迹象。

据伊朗国家媒体报道，伊朗外长阿巴斯·阿拉格希表示，与阿曼关于船只通过霍尔木兹海峡机制的谈判“已接近完成，处于最后阶段”。但未说明德黑兰是否准备永久放弃其向过境商船收取通行费的要求，以及这些谈判是否会促成该航道完全重新开放。

![Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in a black suit and white shirt.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b181cc90b1481b19946e7c3853cf098/3b181cc9-0b14-8148-b6e4-dca974266c2d-b8a0e8386f6c4385ee18fd0d3274af9d.jpg)

伊朗外长阿巴斯·阿拉格希。哈迪·米兹班/美联社

据利雅得方面的通报，阿拉格希还致电沙特外交大臣费萨尔·本·法尔汉亲王，讨论进一步的降级措施。

特朗普的批评者说，总统的突然转变反映了他对剩余军事和外交选择的不满，并不反映经过精心调整的战略。

“他的慌乱是对美国威慑力的一个沉重打击，”拜登政府时期担任五角大楼高级官员、并在巴拉克·奥巴马总统任内担任美国驻以色列大使的丹尼尔·夏皮罗说。“没有人相信他说的任何话——盟友不信，当然我们的伊朗对手也不信。”

这场冲突的最新篇章始于6月，当时美国与伊朗谈判达成了一份谅解备忘录，但随后因对如何解除霍尔木兹海峡航运限制的解读相互矛盾而破裂。

伊朗军队向多艘商船开火后，特朗普谴责了该国领导人。到7月初，他曾称赞为“非常聪明”的伊朗领导人，却被他嘲笑为“败类”，不再愿意与他们打交道。

随着外交降温，特朗普重新实施经济制裁，并指示美军恢复海上封锁。小规模空袭之后，特朗普[上周威胁](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-sours-on-diplomacy-vows-to-hit-iran-hard-2c7af868?mod=article_inline)要以桥梁和能源工厂等基础设施为目标的军事行动升级。

“我们只是想赢，”特朗普周五在美国军事行动看似即将发起之际说。“而且你知道，在某个时候，他们会说‘我们真的受不了了。’”

7月，特朗普与国务卿马尔科·卢比奥和国防部长皮特·赫格塞斯在内阁会议上。丹尼尔·豪尔/路透社

特朗普发表上述言论之际，[美国中央司令部正在准备选项](https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/the-u-s-commander-with-a-two-week-plan-to-escalate-strikes-in-iran-0ce32cb2?mod=article_inline)对伊朗的导弹、国防工业和其他地点发动大规模进攻。军事分析人士表示，美国有能力对伊朗实施惩罚性打击。

但伊朗试图先发制人，警告称可能对配合美国军事计划的阿拉伯海湾国家进行打击，引发外界担忧海湾能源基础设施可能遭到攻击。

随着军事行动的前景逼近，特朗普周二与内塔尼亚胡会面，后者一直将伊朗视为主要威胁。但以色列方面并未拥有最终发言权。

沙特国防大臣哈立德·本·萨勒曼次日与副总统万斯和特朗普会面。这些会面之前，沙特和美国对伊朗支持的伊拉克民兵组织进行了联合打击。据知情人士透露，沙特方面传递的信息是，伊朗的代理人已被削弱，现在到了缓和局势的时候。

随后在周六晚上，据沙特官方媒体和了解情况的沙特官员称，沙特王储在电话中跟进，敦促通过对话缓和地区紧张局势。

外交路径至少暂时让特朗普避免了大规模军事行动的风险。但外交本身也有风险，包括伊朗可能将特朗普的摇摆视为在谈判中最大化自身筹码的理由。

“谈判达成一项维护航行自由的解决方案不会很快，也不容易，”布鲁金斯学会外交政策副总裁、伊朗问题专家苏珊娜·马洛尼表示。“目前尚不清楚本届政府是否具备所需的精力和注意力。”

## Trump Pivots Back to Diplomacy as Iran Victory Eludes Him

## **Trump Pivots Back to Diplomacy as Iran Victory Eludes Him**

### The question is whether negotiations can finally reopen the Strait of Hormuz and lay the groundwork for nuclear talks

President Trump wrote on social-media Saturday that Middle East countries had asked him to cancel a new attack on Iran because the framework of a deal had been worked out. Daniel Heuer/Reuters

By

Michael R. Gordon

Alexander Ward

and

Philip Wegmann

Aug. 2, 2026 7:00 pm ET

- President Trump is pursuing diplomacy with Iran after calling off a planned military offensive at the request of Middle East countries.
- The U.S. is scheduled to join talks on Monday, while Iran and Oman report progress on negotiations to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.
- The six-month conflict has raised gas prices, depleted oil reserves, and resulted in the deaths of 18 U.S. servicemembers since February.
WASHINGTON—After days of threats to launch a new offensive against [Iran](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran), President Trump is once again trying to achieve through diplomacy what he has yet to accomplish through war.

The question is whether Trump’s Gulf partners can make enough headway in the days ahead to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to shipping, at least temporarily, and lay the groundwork for eventual nuclear talks.

Previous efforts on both fronts have failed for months, though there were initial signs of progress Sunday on reopening the waterway. Oman and Iran noted they were making progress.

Trump told reporters Sunday that the U.S. would engage in talks beginning Monday afternoon. He said that he had been prepared to launch the biggest military attack since World War II but had been talked down by U.S. allies.

“We were all set to go, but when the allies asked to call it off, you got to sort of say, ‘well, let’s see,’” he said.

Still, if talks falter as they have in the past, Trump could once again fall into a familiar cycle of reviving his military threats shortly after he shelved them.

“He is trying his hand now at a diplomatic solution to avoid military escalation. But there’s no clear path, either diplomatically or militarily, to get Trump to a place where he can declare victory,” said Vali Nasr, an Iran expert at Johns Hopkins University.

This cycle has destabilized the Middle East and complicated the Trump administration’s domestic agenda. Iran in recent weeks has carried out attacks on a number of U.S. allies in the region, including Jordan, Bahrain and Kuwait, while its proxies in Iraq have launched drones at Saudi Arabia.

Missiles on display in Tehran on Sunday. Atta KENARE/AFP/Getty Images

Iran’s ability to essentially shut down the Strait of Hormuz has pushed up the price of gasoline and forced the U.S. and other counties to [eat into their oil reserves.](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/oil-executives-are-sounding-the-alarm-over-dwindling-stockpiles-ad0f6928?mod=article_inline) Meanwhile, Republicans in Congress, with just three months until the midterm elections, have pressed the White House to better articulate the goals for the Iran war and explain how it will end.

That concern was underscored when three U.S. soldiers were [killed in an Iranian missile attack ](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-missile-struck-housing-for-u-s-troops-at-jordan-base-9f5b8120?mod=article_inline)against their base in Jordan, bringing the total to 18 service personnel killed in action since February.

When he ordered attacks on Iran in late February, Trump began the war with limited international support, and public opinion polls that showed many Americans hadn’t been convinced that Tehran presented an urgent nuclear threat.

Trump sought to reassure a restive public that rising gas prices wouldn’t last long, projecting that the conflict could be over in four to six weeks. His reservoir of support, abroad and at home, has shrunk as the war has entered its sixth month.

Trump’s supporters said that his drumbeat of threats and visible impatience had encouraged a flurry of diplomatic activity that could result in a long-sought agreement that will finally bring the war to a close.

Trump wrote in a social-media post Saturday night that Iran and other Middle East countries were imploring him to cancel his attack because the “perimeters of a deal” had been hammered out.

Trump delivered that message after speaking with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. Trump on Sunday said that he asked him, “Would you rather have us do this or not?” Trump said the crown prince replied, “We would much prefer a deal as opposed to an attack because you don’t know where these attacks lead.”

But Trump didn’t call Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, the staunch ally with whom he launched the war and remains the loudest voice in the president’s ear for more military action.

There were initial signs of a diplomatic opening Sunday.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said talks with Oman about a mechanism for ships to traverse the Strait of Hormuz was “nearing completion and in their final stages,” according to Iranian state media. Left unsaid was whether Tehran is prepared to permanently drop its demand that it should be able to collect tolls from transiting commercial vessels and if those negotiations would lead to a fully reopened waterway.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Hadi Mizban/AP

Araghchi also called his Saudi counterpart, Prince Faisal bin Farhan, to discuss further de-escalatory measures, according to a readout from Riyadh.

Trump’s critics say the president’s abrupt twists and turns reflect his unhappiness with his remaining military and diplomatic options and don’t reflect a carefully calibrated strategy.

“His flailing is a terrible blow to U.S. deterrence,” said Daniel Shapiro, a senior Pentagon official during the Biden administration who also served as U.S. ambassador to Israel under President Barack Obama. “No one believes anything he says—not allies and certainly not our Iranian adversaries.”

The latest chapter in the conflict began in June when the U.S. negotiated a memorandum of understanding with Iran only to see it collapse amid conflicting interpretations of how to unlock shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

After Iranian forces fired on several commercial vessels, Trump denounced the country’s leaders. By early July, Iranian leaders whom Trump had once praised as “very smart” were being derided as “scum” with whom he no longer wanted to engage.

As diplomacy receded, Trump reimposed economic sanctions and directed the U.S. military to resume its naval blockade. Small scale airstrikes were followed by Trump’s [threat last week t](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-sours-on-diplomacy-vows-to-hit-iran-hard-2c7af868?mod=article_inline)o escalate military action targeting infrastructure such as bridges and energy plants.

“We just want to win,” Trump said Friday as U.S. military action appeared imminent. “And you know at some point, they’re going to say, ‘We just can’t take it anymore.’”

Trump with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth at a cabinet meeting in July. Daniel Heuer/Reuters

Trump’s statement came [as U.S. Central Command prepared options](https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/the-u-s-commander-with-a-two-week-plan-to-escalate-strikes-in-iran-0ce32cb2?mod=article_inline) to launch a major offensive against Iran’s missiles, defense industry and other sites. Military analysts said the U.S. had the ability to carry out punishing attacks in Iran.

But Iran sought to head off the U.S. offensive by warning that it could lash out against Arab Gulf states that cooperated with U.S. military plans, spurring fears that energy infrastructure in the Gulf could come under fire.

As the prospect of military action approached, Trump met Tuesday with Netanyahu, who has long cast Iran as a major threat. But the Israelis didn’t have the last word.

Saudi Defense Minister Khalid bin Salman met the next day with Vice President JD Vance and with Trump. Those meetings followed joint Saudi and U.S. strikes against Iran-backed militias in Iraq. The Saudi message was that Iran’s proxies had been weakened and that it was now time to de-escalate, according to a person familiar with the meetings.

Then on Saturday night, the Saudi crown prince followed up during a phone call in which he urged dialogue to reduce escalation in the region, according to Saudi state media and Saudi officials familiar with the matter.

The diplomatic pathway has insulated Trump from the risks of major military actions, at least for now. But diplomacy carries its own risks, including that Iran may view Trump’s vacillation as a reason to try to maximize its leverage in the talks.

“Negotiating a resolution that preserves freedom of navigation won’t be quick or easy,” said Suzanne Maloney, vice president for foreign policy and an Iran expert at the Brookings Institution. “It’s not obvious that this administration has either the bandwidth or the attention span needed.”
