### 中文译文

## 特朗普削减美韩联合军演 称伊朗问题缺乏支持

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普已通知五角大楼“大幅减少”与韩国的联合军事演习，并表示他做出这一决定是因为对这位长期盟友未能在其与伊朗的战争中提供更多支持感到失望。

特朗普在唐纳德·J·特朗普（社交媒体账号）上说：“基于我与朝鲜的金正恩非常良好的关系，我对周日演习费用高昂且向朝鲜发出‘不恰当信号’这一事实感到不满。”他援引了他与金正恩的“非常良好的关系”。据首尔官员上周表示，一项为期10天、约1.8万名韩国士兵参加的联合演习原定于周一开始，旨在应对来自朝鲜的新威胁。

特朗普的宣布似乎让首尔感到意外。韩国国防部周一表示演习将按计划继续进行，但随后总统李在明的办公室表示，首尔和华盛顿将就细节密切协调。该办公室在一份声明中还表示，首尔正在与华盛顿就中东问题上做出“实际和军事贡献”的方式进行密切磋商。

与许多美国盟友一样，韩国拒绝介入美国与以色列对伊朗的战争，这场战争始于2月28日的空袭。特朗普此前曾抱怨欧洲盟友拒绝让美国使用其机场发动袭击。

特朗普在社交媒体帖子中表示，韩国拒绝“加入我们对伊朗伊斯兰共和国进行无核化的行动”。他说，虽然现在取消演习为时已晚，但演习规模将会缩减。

特朗普显然再次利用军事承诺作为筹码的决定，可能会引起日本和澳大利亚等其他亚太盟友的担忧。两国都与美国签有安全条约，但也选择置身于伊朗冲突之外。

“日本肯定会感到担忧，”彭博经济高级地缘经济分析师亚当·法拉尔表示。他曾担任美国国家安全委员会朝鲜半岛事务主任，在特朗普和拜登两届政府任职。“如果这是由伊朗问题驱动的，鉴于他们自身不愿介入，他们自己也会面临风险。”

“无论原因是什么，这种最后一刻的决定都会引发对美国对其亚洲盟友承诺的质疑，并可能影响威慑力，”法拉尔补充道。

日本外务省未立即回应置评请求。

据美国海军研究所称，特朗普宣布这一决定之际，美国在亚太地区唯一的航空母舰“乔治·华盛顿”号正被调离该地区前往中东。美国通常在该地区至少保留一艘航母，以帮助威慑中国和朝鲜。

特朗普的决定可能还受到双方关系中其他紧张点的影响。此前有媒体报道称，华盛顿正在施压首尔加速在美国的战略投资项目计划。

韩国在充实战略项目方面取得的进展不如日本明显，这些项目是3500亿美元投资承诺的一部分，而日本则做出了类似的5500亿美元承诺。两国都是在与美国进行贸易谈判时做出投资承诺的，以限制对其出口依赖型经济体征收关税。

特朗普缩减联合演习早有先例。尽管美韩已进行联合军演数十年，但在特朗普首个总统任期内，[这类演习曾缩减](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-06-18/u-s-south-korea-suspend-military-drill-planned-for-august)，当时他与金正恩进行谈判。这些谈判最终破裂，未就朝鲜核武器计划达成任何协议。

“这次的理由不太清晰。在俄罗斯的支持下，金正恩处于更有利的地位，并且迄今对接触兴趣寥寥，”法拉尔说。

特朗普的宣布引发了一个问题：名为“乙支自由之盾”的演习如何在短时间内缩减。美国军方官员曾将这些演习描述为对抗朝鲜能力的工具，这些能力在战场上得到磨炼，而平壤继续为俄罗斯在乌克兰的战争提供军事支持。

除了测试弹道导弹系统，朝鲜还接触了无人机等现代技术。

朝鲜已明确表示对军演的不满。周三，朝鲜在其东海岸发射了一枚弹道导弹，此前六天刚发射了一枚短程弹道导弹。周五，朝鲜誓言继续行使自卫权。

“以新水平的威慑应对新水平的威胁，是我们确保安全的一贯原则，”朝鲜外务省发言人在朝鲜官方中央通讯社发表的声明中表示。

尽管摩擦不断，韩国总统李在周六的光复节讲话中呼吁与朝鲜对话，并承诺采取措施缓和军事紧张，在半岛建立和平共处。李说，韩国将履行作为“和平缔造者”和朝鲜半岛稳定关系利益攸关方的角色。

周一，李的办公室对特朗普关于金正恩的言论作出积极回应。其在声明中表示，期待美朝领导人之间的友好关系能带来朝鲜半岛和平的“有意义的”对话。

中国表示欢迎特朗普社交媒体帖子后美朝恢复对话。“我们注意到了相关报道。维护半岛和平稳定，推动半岛问题政治解决，符合各方利益，”外交部发言人林剑在例行记者会上表示。

伊朗战争大幅推高了石油和天然气价格，给特朗普的共和党在中期选举前带来压力。美国表示正准备对德黑兰采取经济措施，试图在数月僵局谈判后迫使伊朗屈服。

尽管如此，朝鲜“对特朗普来说仍是未竟事业，他显然想再次与金正恩进行直接外交，”欧亚集团中国与东北亚高级分析师杰里米·陈（Jeremy Chan）表示。

“然而，恢复外交的基本障碍仍然是特朗普不愿承认朝鲜的核武器计划，”他说。

## Trump Reduces US-South Korea Drills, Citing Lack of Help on Iran

President Donald Trump told the Pentagon to “substantially reduce” joint military exercises with South Korea and indicated his decision to do so was driven by frustration the longtime US ally hadn’t provided more support for his war with Iran.

Trump said in a Donald J. Trump: Based on my very good relationship with Kim Jong Un, of North Korea, I am not happy with the fact that the Sunday the drills were costly and sent an “inappropriate signal” to North Korea, citing his “very good relationship” with its leader Kim Jong Un. A 10-day joint exercise involving some 18,000 South Korean troops was scheduled to begin Monday with the goal of addressing new threats from North Korea, officials in Seoul said last week.

Trump’s announcement appeared to catch Seoul by surprise. South Korea’s Defense Ministry said on Monday the drills would continue as planned, but later President Lee Jae Myung’s office said that Seoul and Washington would coordinate closely on the details. Seoul is also in close consultations with Washington on ways to make “practical and military contributions” on the Middle East issues, the office said in a statement.

South Korea, like many US allies, has declined to get involved in the US-Israeli war on Iran, which began with airstrikes on Feb. 28. Trump has previously complained about European allies refusing to let the US use their airfields to launch attacks.

In his social post, Trump said South Korea had declined to “join us in the Denuclearization of the Islamic Republic of Iran.” He said that while it was too late to cancel the exercises, they would be reduced.

US armored vehicles from the 2nd Infantry Division during a military exercise with the South Korean army in Yeoncheon, South Korea, in March. Photographer: SeongJoon Cho/Bloomberg

Trump’s apparent decision to again use a military commitment as leverage may cause concern among other Asia-Pacific allies such as Japan and Australia. Both have security treaties with the US, but have also opted to stay out of the Iran conflict.

“Japan will certainly be concerned,” said Adam Farrar, a senior geoeconomics analyst at Bloomberg Economics and former director on the US National Security Council covering Korea in both the Trump and Biden administrations. “If this was driven by Iran, they themselves are at risk, given their own unwillingness to get involved.”

“No matter what the reason is, this type of last-minute decision will raise questions about the US commitment to its Asian allies and potentially impact deterrence,” Farrar added.

Japan’s Foreign Ministry didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment.

Trump’s announcement came as the lone US aircraft carrier in the Asia-Pacific region, the USS George Washington, was being diverted from the region to the Middle East, according to the US Naval Institute. The US typically keeps at least one carrier in the region to help deter China and North Korea.

The decision by Trump may also be driven by other points of tension in the relationship. It comes following a media report that Washington is pressing Seoul to accelerate plans for strategic investment projects in the US.

South Korea has made less visible progress on fleshing out the strategic projects that form part of a $350 billion investment pledge than Japan has with a similar $550 billion commitment. Both countries made the investment pledges during trade negotiations with the US to limit tariffs on their export-dependent economies.

There is a precedent for Trump scaling back joint exercises. While the US and South Korea have conducted joint military drills for decades, they were [curtailed during Trump’s first presidency](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-06-18/u-s-south-korea-suspend-military-drill-planned-for-august) when he negotiated with Kim. Those talks collapsed without any agreement on North Korea’s nuclear weapons program.

“This time, the rationale is less clear. With Russian backing, Kim is in a stronger position and has so far shown little interest in engagement,” Farrar said.

Trump’s announcement raises the question of how the exercises, known as Ulchi Freedom Shield, might be reduced at short notice. US military officials have portrayed the drills as a means to counter North Korean capabilities that have been honed on the battlefield, as Pyongyang continues to provide military support for Russia in its war on Ukraine.

As well as testing ballistic missile systems, North Korea has gained exposure to modern technology such as drones.

North Korea has made its displeasure at the drills clear. On Wednesday it fired a ballistic missile off its east coast, six days after launching a short-range ballistic missile. On Friday it vowed to continue exercising its right to self-defense.

“It is our consistent principle of ensuring security to respond to a new level of a threat with a new level of a deterrent,” a spokesperson for North Korea’s Foreign Ministry said in a statement carried by the official Korean Central News Agency.

Despite ongoing friction, South Korean President Lee on Saturday called for dialogue with North Korea and pledged to take steps to reduce military tensions and establish peaceful coexistence on the peninsula in a Liberation Day address. South Korea will fulfill its role as a “peacemaker” and stakeholder in stable relations on the Korean peninsula, Lee said.

On Monday, Lee’s office responded positively to Trump’s comments about Kim. In its statement it said it expects the friendly relations between the leaders of the US and North Korea would lead to “meaningful” dialogue for peace on the Korean peninsula.

China said it would welcome renewed dialogue between the US and North Korea following Trump’s social post. “We have noticed relevant reports. Maintaining peace and stability in the peninsula and promoting the political solution of the peninsula issue fits the interest of all sides,” Foreign Ministry spokesman Lin Jian said at a regular press briefing.

The Iran war has sharply increased the price of oil and gas and put pressure on Trump’s Republican party heading into midterm elections. The US has said it is readying economic measures against Tehran, in an effort to force Iran to capitulate after months of stalled talks.

Nonetheless, North Korea “remains unfinished business for Trump, who clearly wants another go at direct diplomacy with Kim,” said Jeremy Chan, a senior analyst for China and Northeast Asia at the ⁠Eurasia Group.

“However, the fundamental obstacle to resumed diplomacy remains Trump’s unwillingness to acknowledge North Korea’s nuclear weapons program,” he said.
