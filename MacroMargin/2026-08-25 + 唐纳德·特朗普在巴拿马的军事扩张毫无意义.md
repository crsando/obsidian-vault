## 唐纳德·特朗普在巴拿马的军事扩张毫无意义

## 唐纳德·特朗普在巴拿马的军事扩张毫无意义

### 这正在扭曲保护该国运河的中立性

![photograph: u.s. air national guard photo](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b14814e8d5edd3a7c616fd9/3c781cc9-0b14-81d1-be1a-c4e104277fdb-168ceff85d274d2fa13df5d60b2bdb0d.jpg)

图片：美国空军国民警卫队照片

快艇在巴拿马运河入口附近拦截一艘可疑船只。士兵登船并押走坏人。在加勒比海岸，特种部队对叛军基地发动攻击。在首都巴拿马城郊区，支奴干和黑鹰直升机在上空呼啸而过。从8月3日到13日，巴拿马人目睹了近二十年来未曾见过的军事力量展示。

这次活动的名称是“巴拿马极限”（Panamax），一场由美国领导的多国军事演习，旨在演练应对[巴拿马运河](https://www.economist.com/the-americas/2026/07/16/the-panama-canal-is-growing-more-important)可能面临的威胁。自2003年以来大约每两年举行一次，过去13年一直以在得克萨斯州和佛罗里达州基地进行的模拟形式进行。今年它回到了巴拿马。超过1700名士兵（大部分是美国人，但得到来自17个（主要是拉丁美洲）国家的士兵支持）在全国各地进行了“动态行动”。

![map: the economist](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b14814e8d5edd3a7c616fd9/3c781cc9-0b14-8160-8a27-dd8fe068a7d7-3734e739d90112cfc0257f0e2011d635.png)

地图：《经济学人》

“我对桌面推演没有意见，但你无法复制这个，”战争部长皮特·赫格塞斯（Pete Hegseth）说，他穿着一件被汗水浸透的橄榄绿T恤。他说，运河是“关键地形。我们与巴拿马合作在这里驻扎的兵力比很长时间以来都要多。”

自唐纳德·特朗普第二次就任总统以来，美国重新对西半球产生了兴趣。它已在委内瑞拉进行军事干预，对古巴政权发起经济施压运动，欺凌加拿大，并要求控制格陵兰。特朗普先生多次威胁[要控制巴拿马运河](https://www.economist.com/video/CUAnDQnOrqd/ecQpgYuf)。对于负责拉丁美洲的美国联合司令部南方司令部（Southcom）来说，“确保美国不受阻碍地进入巴拿马运河是首要任务。”但美国的军事扩张主义与管辖运河的条约并不相容。而且其有效性远未得到保证。

赫格塞斯先生是在谢尔曼堡（Fort Sherman）发表上述讲话的，这是他在2025年4月上次访问该国期间宣布的三个美巴联合训练学校之一。在20世纪下半叶，数十万部队在谢尔曼堡接受了艰苦的丛林战训练，其中许多人是在被派往越南之前。1999年，“绿色地狱”（Green Hell）与另外13个美国军事设施一起关闭，这是1977年《运河中立条约》（Canal Neutrality Treaty）的条件，该条约禁止外国军事设施的存在。巴拿马政府表示，新版的“丛林学校”（Jungle School）并不代表外国军事基地。反对派政客称其重新开放是“伪装入侵”。

巴拿马宪法禁止该国拥有军队，但自2024年上台以来，总统何塞·劳尔·穆利诺（José Raúl Mulino）加强了边境控制部队 Senafront 和海军及空军 Senan。去年，巴拿马同意购买四架巴西战斗机。今年4月，它收到了从美国订购的首批监视无人机和拦截艇。据穆利诺先生称，加上港口扫描和网络安全设备，美国已向安全部队提供了价值超过1.74亿美元的技术。8月，政府启动了一个新的巴拿马安全部队联合指挥部，由运河收入资助，专门负责保护水道及其水文流域。

Panamax 也为美洲反卡特尔联盟（Americas Counter-Cartel Coalition，原称“美洲之盾”）的拉丁美洲成员提供了一个会面的机会。赫格塞斯先生表示，哥伦比亚以及洪都拉斯和危地马拉已同意允许美国在其领土上开展联合军事行动，“将打击行动带到陆地上的[指定的恐怖组织]。”（危地马拉政府否认已达成同意。）

随着哥伦比亚新总统阿贝拉多·德·拉·埃斯普列利亚（Abelardo de la Espriella）承诺大力打击犯罪集团，巴拿马正准备应对外溢效应。8月15日，Senafront 向该国南部边境派遣了700多名特种部队，以阻止黑帮通过分隔两国的森林茂密的达连隘口（Darién Gap）进入。“运河的敌人是毒品贩运，没有别人，”穆利诺先生说。

这不是美国的立场。特朗普政府认为，对运河构成最严重威胁的是中国，而不是贩毒集团。它希望将中国的活动从其能在拉丁美洲触及的所有关键基础设施上赶走。去年，特朗普政府推动巴拿马将中国运营商从运河两端的港口移除。据南方司令部（Southcom）称，这种“双重用途基础设施”可能被用于“全球紧急情况下的情报收集、网络漏洞或后勤拒止”。赫格塞斯先生将巴拿马视为“大北美”的一部分，也是“直接安全周边”的一部分。

![photograph: u.s. army photo](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b14814e8d5edd3a7c616fd9/3c781cc9-0b14-816c-8295-db859c7d77e4-104870adbb817c44d1195706159bd1e0.jpg)

照片：美国陆军

“巴拿马运河可能是西半球最重要的基础设施，”美国驻巴拿马大使凯文·卡布雷拉（Kevin Cabrera）表示。“如果你想中断我们的贸易，巴拿马运河就是破坏它的途径。”他说，美国与巴拿马之间的军事协调对于保卫这条水道至关重要。

但重启丛林学校对解决这些关切作用甚微。部队无法阻止其他国家的间谍活动或网络攻击，而且需要大量兵力才能防止无人机攻击或恐怖分子的破坏。

相反，美国更多的干预增加了运河的风险。巴拿马学童被教导，国家的中立是运河最好的防御，而运河对所有船只“在和平时期和战争时期”的开放一直是其在世界贸易中地位不断提升的关键。当地政治分析人士罗德里戈·诺列加（Rodrigo Noriega）表示，特朗普的重商主义威胁到了这种中立，运河成为目标的可能性正在增大。卡夫雷拉先生（Mr Cabrera）对此不以为然。他说：“国家和非国家行为体都不在乎法治。他们不在乎条约。”

巴拿马的外交官仍然认为这很重要。在过去一年里，他们重新努力扩大《中立条约》的签署国数量。奥地利、比利时、巴西、葡萄牙和瑞士已签署。已向日本、印度和其他12个国家发出了邀请。诺列加先生指出，中立不仅保护巴拿马运河免受实际攻击，还可能保护该国免受特朗普的欺凌。

## Donald Trump’s military expansion in Panama makes little sense

## Donald Trump’s military expansion in Panama makes little sense

### It is warping the neutrality which protects the country’s canal

Photograph: U.S. Air National Guard photo

Speedboats intercept a suspicious ship near the entrance to the Panama Canal. Troops board it and bundle the bad guys off. On the Caribbean coast special forces launch an assault on a rebel base. On the outskirts of Panama City, the capital, Chinook and Black Hawk helicopters buzz overhead. From August 3rd to the 13th, Panamanians witnessed a display of military prowess not seen in the country for almost two decades.

The occasion was “Panamax”, a multinational military exercise led by the United States to practise defending against possible threats to the [Panama Canal](https://www.economist.com/the-americas/2026/07/16/the-panama-canal-is-growing-more-important). Held roughly every two years since 2003, for the past 13 it has taken the form of a simulation run from bases in Texas and Florida. This year it returned to Panama. Over 1,700 troops, mostly American but supported by soldiers from 17 (mostly Latin American) countries, engaged in “kinetic operations” across the country.

Map: The Economist

“I’ve no issue with tabletop exercises but you can’t replicate this,” said Pete Hegseth, the secretary of war, dressed in a sweat-soaked olive green t-shirt. The canal, he said, is “a key piece of terrain. We’ve got more troops here in partnership with Panama than we’ve had in a very long time.”

The United States has taken a renewed interest in the western hemisphere since Donald Trump took office for the second time. It has intervened militarily in Venezuela, run an economic-pressure campaign against the Cuban regime, bullied Canada and demanded control of Greenland. And Mr Trump has made repeated threats [to take control of the Panama Canal](https://www.economist.com/video/CUAnDQnOrqd/ecQpgYuf). For Southcom, the US Joint Command focused on Latin America, “ensuring unfettered US access to the Panama Canal is a top priority.” But American military expansionism does not sit comfortably with the treaties that govern the canal. And its effectiveness is far from guaranteed.

Mr Hegseth was speaking at Fort Sherman, one of three American-Panamanian joint training schools he announced during his previous trip to the country in April 2025. In the second half of the 20th century hundreds of thousands of troops undertook gruelling jungle-warfare training at Fort Sherman, many of them in advance of being sent to Vietnam. “Green Hell” was closed in 1999, along with 13 other American military installations, as a condition of the 1977 Canal Neutrality Treaty, which forbade the existence of foreign military installations. The Panamanian government says the new incarnation of “Jungle School” does not represent a foreign military base. Opposition politicians call its reopening a “camouflaged invasion”.

Panama’s constitution bars the country from having an army, but since coming to power in 2024 president José Raúl Mulino has beefed up Senafront, the border-control force, and Senan, the naval and air service. Last year Panama agreed to buy four Brazilian fighter planes. In April it received the first of an order of surveillance drones and interceptor boats from the United States. Together with port-scanning and cybersecurity equipment, the United States has given technology worth more than $174m to the security forces, according to Mr Mulino. In August the government launched a new joint command of Panamanian security forces, funded by revenue from the canal, and dedicated to the defence of the waterway and its hydrographic basin.

Panamax was also an opportunity for the Latin American members of the Americas Counter-Cartel Coalition, formerly known as the “Shield of The Americas”, to meet. Mr Hegseth said that Colombia, along with Honduras and Guatemala, had agreed to allow joint American military operations in their territory “to bring the fight to the [designated terrorist organisations] on land.” (The Guatemalan government denies that it has agreed.)

With Colombia’s new president, Abelardo de la Espriella, promising an aggressive campaign against criminal groups, Panama is preparing to deal with the spillover. On August 15th Senafront sent over 700 special-forces troops to the country’s southern border in an effort to stop gangsters entering through the heavily forested Darién Gap, which separates the two countries. “The canal’s enemy is narcotrafficking, and no one else,” said Mr Mulino.

That is not the position of the United States. The Trump administration believes that China, not drug gangs, poses the most serious threat to the canal. It wants to drive Chinese activity away from all the critical infrastructure it can in Latin America. Last year the Trump administration pushed Panama to remove Chinese operators from the ports at each end of the canal. Such “dual-use infrastructure” could be used for “intelligence gathering, cyber vulnerability, or logistical denial during global contingencies”, according to Southcom. Mr Hegseth views Panama as part of “Greater North America” and part of the “immediate security perimeter”.

Photograph: U.S. Army photo

“The Panama Canal is probably the most important infrastructure in the western hemisphere,” says Kevin Cabrera, the American ambassador to Panama. “If you wanted to interrupt our commerce, the Panama Canal is the way to mess with that.” Military co-ordination between the United States and Panama is crucial to defending the waterway, he says.

But restarting Jungle School does little address these concerns. Troops cannot stop spying or cyberattacks by other states, and would be needed in great numbers to prevent drone attacks or sabotage by terrorists.

Instead, increased American meddling increases the risks to the canal. Panamanian schoolchildren are taught that their country’s neutrality is the canal’s best defence, while its openness to all vessels “in times of peace and in times of war” has been the key to its rising role in world trade. Rodrigo Noriega, a local political analyst, says Mr Trump’s mercantilism threatens this neutrality, and that the chances of the canal being targeted are growing. Mr Cabrera dismisses this. “State and non-state actors don’t care about the rule of law. They don’t care about treaties,” he says.

Panama’s diplomats still think it matters. Over the past year they have renewed efforts to bolster signatories to the Neutrality Treaty. Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Portugal and Switzerland have signed. Invitations have been extended to Japan, India and 12 other countries. Neutrality, Mr Noriega notes, offers Panama protection not just from physical attacks on its canal, but potentially from Mr Trump’s bullying.
