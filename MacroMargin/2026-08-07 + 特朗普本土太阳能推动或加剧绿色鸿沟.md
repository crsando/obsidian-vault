### 中文译文

## 特朗普本土太阳能推动或加剧绿色鸿沟

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普最近避开外国太阳能设备之举，是他重振美国制造业影响力的努力的一部分。这可能同时加剧美国与世界其他地区之间迅速扩大的绿色技术鸿沟。

特朗普宣布了一项计划，对进口的用于半导体和太阳能电池板的多晶硅[征收关税](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-signed-order-on-tariffs-trade-actions-on-polysilicon)并设定最低价格。这项征税是在联邦通信委员会[收紧对逆变器（太阳能发电的另一关键部件）的限制](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs)一周后出台的。

此类保护措施可以促进国内制造业的增长——但正如多个经济体数十年来所发现的那样，限制往往带来财务和技术成本。

短期内，这些限制措施将推高美国太阳能电池板的价格。由于多年来对进口产品征收关税，美国的价格已经是全球水平的两倍多；而在特朗普的第二任期内，他还推行了反可再生能源政策。

如果国内制造业不能迅速立足，随着世界其他国家在廉价的中国替代品上取得进展，美国在技术和电力方面可能会进一步落后。

华盛顿特区亚洲协会政策研究所中国气候中心主任李硕表示：“美国在如此迫切需要更多电力来发展高科技和人工智能之际，却在限制这种能源。这与你世界各地看到的太阳能市场截然不同。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b581cc90b1481709de8f716d54e431c/3b581cc9-0b14-813a-a67d-f1ed6ae40006-510b94c756d8737af387e24fca3a2235.png)

特朗普的指令将于12月4日生效，对包括硅片、光伏电池和太阳能组件在内的衍生产品征收15%的关税，并为每个产品设定了高于美国当前成本的最低价格。

例如，进口太阳能组件的新最低价格为38美分/瓦。根据彭博新能源财经（BloombergNEF）的数据，目前运往美国的组件价格为27美分/瓦，而全球平均价格为11美分。

特朗普自第一个任期以来一直推动美国工业复兴，但也在提供“胡萝卜”作为激励。如果企业承诺在2029年1月20日前开始在美国建设工厂以生产太阳能产品，它们可以避免更高的成本。

白宫发言人库什·德赛在一份声明中表示：“美国不能在多晶硅上依赖外国，多晶硅是支撑半导体和太阳能供应链的关键基础材料。”

德赛称：“特朗普总统承诺维护我们的国家和经济安全，除了将关键制造业回流美国外，特朗普政府仍致力于为家庭和企业释放美国本就丰富的可靠且负担得起的能源来源，从天然气到煤炭。”

支持制造业回流的人士——实际上就是把美国制造能力带回国——对这一消息表示欢迎。美国繁荣联盟主席乔恩·图米表示，全球进口壁垒可以终结企业为规避关税而在第三国设厂的规避行为。

该指令还适用于供应链的所有环节，这应有助于解决投资下游组装厂却发现仍依赖其他国家提供上游投入品的问题。

图米表示：“美国首次通过单一行动保护整个太阳能供应链。”

在太阳能供应链中，超高纯度多晶硅被制成方形锭，再切片成称为硅片的超薄方块。这些硅片被连接成电池片，再拼装成太阳能电池板。

摩根大通包括马克·斯特劳斯在内的分析师在研究报告中表示，从头构建完整供应链可能为美国带来巨大好处。它可能“降低价格、降低地缘政治风险，并改善该领域的长期投资者情绪。”

绿色技术一直是全球推动工业化的核心，美国也不例外。但随着对算力的争夺加速，清洁能源竞赛加剧了美国与愿意使用中国技术的经济体之间的鸿沟——一方受益于极低的价格和现成的先进技术，另一方则寻求自建产业链。

就在2012年，美国还是全球最大的多晶硅生产国。那一年，总统巴拉克·奥巴马发起了一场针对太阳能产品的关税战，导致北京对美国产品加征关税。作为回应，中国大力发展国内产业，如今控制着全球超过95%的多晶硅产量。

近年来美国取得了一些进展，目前拥有全球第三大太阳能组件组装产能。但据彭博新能源财经驻香港太阳能分析师谭又瑞（Youru Tan）表示，美国仍然依赖海外供应商提供电池片、硅片和多晶硅，这意味着即使美国太阳能制造业站稳脚跟，也可能难以追赶。

“美国的生产成本远高于中国，”谭又瑞表示，“而且大多数美国工厂依赖中国的设备。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b581cc90b1481709de8f716d54e431c/3b581cc9-0b14-81ef-84d1-f4f5fbac2a70-b8faf1fdb8bc673aee9a748e9ecf57f1.png)

新工厂的投资者将不得不权衡特朗普新指令所提供的支持，与那些给清洁能源长期需求带来不确定性的信息。

在第二任期迄今为止，特朗普的政策措施还包括终止太阳能安装税收抵免、削弱鼓励无排放电力的联邦政策以及绿色能源许可延迟，这些措施进一步阻碍了美国可再生能源的部署。

据Guggenheim证券分析师称，如果制造业繁荣未能实现，美国太阳能开发商将遭受组件价格大幅上涨的冲击，这将使太阳能系统总成本增加约12%。最终由谁来吸收增加的成本——是开发商的利润率还是更高的电价——仍未可知。

人工智能热潮应有助于用户应对短期风险。摩根大通表示，需求的上升和竞争性能源价格高于常态将抵消额外成本。根据彭博新能源财经的数据，即使美国支付的费用高于世界其他地区，太阳能仍然是美国最便宜的电力选择之一。

这还没有考虑不采用廉价组件的机会成本。从[尼日利亚](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-26/african-imports-of-chinese-solar-panels-surge-in-energy-shakeup)到[巴基斯坦](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-05-19/shadow-power-market-thrives-as-iran-war-leaves-pakistan-in-the-dark)的国家利用这一点，在短短几个月内从根本上重塑了其电力供应不足的电网。对菲律宾的太阳能电池板销售正在[激增](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-12/china-is-supercharging-a-rooftop-solar-boom-in-the-philippines)。即使是与中国贸易谈判代表在电动汽车和风电等其他清洁技术领域较量的欧洲，在太阳能方面也几乎举起了白旗。

“太阳能可以说是你能部署的最快的能源，在自由开放的市场中，它是最便宜的电力来源之一，”亚洲协会政策研究所的李说。“美国将无法享有这两项优势。”

## Trump’s Homegrown Solar Push Risks Widening Green Divide

President Donald Trump’s latest move to shun foreign solar power equipment is part of his effort to rebuild US manufacturing clout. It may simultaneously exacerbate the fast-widening green technology split between the US and the rest of the world.

Trump announced a plan to [impose tariffs](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-signed-order-on-tariffs-trade-actions-on-polysilicon) and minimum prices on imported polysilicon used in semicondictors and solar panels. The levy comes a week after the Federal Communications Commission [tightened curbs](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs) on inverters, another vital component of solar-powered energy.

Such protections can encourage the growth of domestic manufacturing — but as multiple economies have found over decades, restrictions often come at a financial and technological cost.

In the near term, curbs will drive up the price of solar panels in the US, a country that already pays more than double the global level thanks to years of tariffs on imports - and where Trump has pursued an anti-renewables policy during his second term.

If domestic manufacturing doesn’t establish a foothold quickly, it could put the US further behind in technological and power terms as the rest of the world presses ahead with cheap Chinese alternatives.

“The US is limiting this source of energy when the country so desperately needs more electricity to scale up high tech and AI,” said Li Shuo, director of the China Climate Hub at the Asia Society Policy Institute in Washington DC. “It looks so different from solar markets you’ll see anywhere else in the world.”

Trump’s directive, which takes effect Dec. 4, subjects derivatives including silicon wafers, photovoltaic cells and solar modules to 15% tariffs, and sets minimum price floors for each product that are higher than current costs in the US.

For example, the new minimum price for imported solar modules will be 38 cents per watt. That compares to 27 cents per watt for modules currently shipped to the US, according to BloombergNEF, while the global average is 11 cents.

Trump, who has pushed the revival of US industry since his first term in office, is also offering a carrot, however. If companies commit to starting construction of US factories to build the solar products by Jan. 20, 2029, they can avoid the higher costs.

“America cannot be reliant on foreign countries for the polysilicon that’s the key base material underpinning semiconductor and solar-power supply chains,” White House spokesman Kush Desai said in a statement.

“President Trump pledged to safeguard our national and economic security, and in addition to reshoring critical manufacturing back to the United States, the Trump administration remains focused on unleashing America’s already-abundant sources of reliable and affordable energy for families and businesses, from natural gas to coal,” Desai said.

Advocates for reshoring — effectively bringing home US manufacturing capability — have feted the news. According to Jon Toomey, president of the Coalition for a Prosperous America, global import barriers can put an end to circumvention, when companies set up facilities in third-party countries to avoid tariffs.

The directive also applies all parts of the supply chain, which should help solve the problem of investing in downstream assembly plants only to find they are still dependent on other countries for upstream inputs.

“For the first time, the United States is protecting the entire solar supply chain with a single action,” Toomey said.

In the solar supply chain, ultra-refined polysilicon is shaped into rectangular ingots, which are in turn sliced into ultra-thin squares known as wafers. Those wafers are wired into cells and pieced together to form solar panels.

Building a complete chain from the ground up could have enormous benefits for the US, JPMorgan analysts including Mark Strouse said in a research note. It could “reduce pricing, reduce geopolitical risks, and improve long-term investor sentiment for the space.”

Green technology has been at the heart of global efforts to boost industrialization, including in the US. But the clean-energy race, accelerating along with the scramble for computing power, has increased the divide between the US and economies open to using Chinese technology — with one side benefiting from rock-bottom prices and existing, advanced technology, and the other seeking to build its own.

As recently as 2012, the US was the world’s largest producer of polysilicon. That year, President Barack Obama kicked off a tariff war over solar products that saw Beijing slap duties on US production. China revved up its domestic industry in response and now controls more than 95% of global output of the material.

The US has made some strides in recent years, and the country now has the world’s third-largest capacity for assembling solar modules. Still, it remains reliant on overseas suppliers for cells, wafers and polysilicon, meaning it could struggle to catch up even if US solar manufacturing gains a foothold, according to Youru Tan, a BloombergNEF solar analyst in Hong Kong.

“US production is much more expensive than China,” Tan said. “Plus, most US factories rely on equipment from China.”

Investors in new factories will have to weigh the support offered from Trump’s new directive against messages that have created uncertainty around long-term appetite for clean energy.

So far in his second term, Trump’s policy moves also including ending tax credits for solar installations, eroding federal policies encouraging emission-free power and green energy permitting delays have done more to hold back the deployment of US renewables.

And if the manufacturing boom fails to materialize, US solar developers will be hit by a steep hike in module prices, which will amount to about a 12% hike to the overall cost of solar systems, according to Guggenheim Securities analysts. The question of who ends up absorbing the increased costs, whether it’s developer profit margins or higher power tariffs, remains open.

The AI boom should help users cope with short-term risks. Rising demand and higher-than-normal prices for competing sources of power will counter the extra cost, according to JPMorgan. Even while paying more than the rest of the world, solar remains among the cheapest options for electricity in the US, according to BloombergNEF data.

That doesn’t account for the opportunity cost of not embracing cut-price modules. Countries from [Nigeria](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-26/african-imports-of-chinese-solar-panels-surge-in-energy-shakeup) to [Pakistan](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-05-19/shadow-power-market-thrives-as-iran-war-leaves-pakistan-in-the-dark) have taken advantage to radically reshape their undersupplied power grids in a matter of months. Sales of solar panels to the Philippines are [booming](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-12/china-is-supercharging-a-rooftop-solar-boom-in-the-philippines). Even Europe, which is battling Chinese trade negotiators over other areas of clean tech like EVs and wind power, has all but waved the white flag when it comes to solar.

“Solar is arguably the fastest energy you can deploy, and in a free and open market it’s one of cheapest power sources,” said Asia Society Policy Institute’s Li. “The US is not going to enjoy those two advantages.”
