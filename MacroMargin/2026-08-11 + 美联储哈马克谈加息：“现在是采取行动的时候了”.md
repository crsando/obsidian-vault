[13:58] Beth Hammack：对任何央行而言，保留灵活性都是明智的。过去处于零利率下限时，前瞻指引曾是为经济增加宽松的有效工具。但在当前环境中，面对如此大的不确定性，我们需要最大限度的灵活性。说到“家庭争论”，我很喜欢的一点是，FOMC有19位来自不同生活背景、持有不同视角的成员，因此我们能够进行全面而深入的讨论。我从那些成长环境不同的人，尤其是一些学者那里学到很多；学术并非我的背景，但听取他们的观点非常有帮助。

[14:52] Jennifer Schonberger: There are some reports that Chairman Warsh floated the idea during the last policy meeting of scaling back the number of FOMC meetings to six from eight, and the other two could be used for broader discussions. Is that true, and what do you make of that idea?

[14:52] Jennifer Schonberger：有报道称，Warsh主席在上次政策会议上提出把FOMC会议从每年八次减至六次，另外两次用于更广泛的讨论。这是真的吗？你如何看待这个想法？

[15:13] Beth Hammack: Um, so I can't disclose anything that's discussed in an FOMC meeting. We will, of course, release our transcripts five years from now, and so there'll be some conversation there about what was said. Um, I think that we have a cadence of meetings that has been suitable for some period of time. I think it's always good to re-evaluate: Is it the right meeting cadence? Should it be more frequent, less frequent? Those are good conversations, and I know the chairman is focused on making sure that we're bringing in great insights. I think he's put together these five task forces to give us some outside perspectives. So he's engaged with some extremely well-respected experts, both from the academic and the business sectors, who can help give us insights about a number of different areas. Um, and ultimately they'll bring those observations to the FOMC, and the FOMC will make decisions whether we want to adopt them or not.

[15:13] Beth Hammack：我不能披露FOMC会议讨论的任何内容。五年后我们当然会公布会议记录，届时可以看到有关发言。现有会议节奏在一段时间内一直是合适的，但重新评估总是有益的：目前的开会频率是否合适？应该更频繁还是更少？这些都是值得讨论的问题。我知道主席希望确保我们吸收高质量见解。他设立了五个工作组，为我们引入外部观点，并邀请学术界和商界一些备受尊敬的专家，就多个领域提供见解。最终，这些观察会提交给FOMC，由FOMC决定是否采纳。

[16:22] Jennifer Schonberger: On those task forces, one of the areas that he's looking to explore is the Fed's inflation framework. And some on Wall Street have said that could be a way to redefine away inflation. And I'm curious, do you think the Fed needs a new inflation framework, or do you think that core PCE should remain the Fed's preferred measure of inflation?

[16:22] Jennifer Schonberger：在这些工作组中，他希望研究的一个领域是美联储的通胀框架。华尔街一些人认为，这可能成为通过重新定义来淡化通胀的方式。你认为美联储需要新的通胀框架吗？还是核心PCE应继续作为美联储偏好的通胀指标？

[16:51] Beth Hammack: I welcome the discussion, and I welcome doing deep analysis on it. Um, I did have a recent speech where I sort of broke down why the Fed had settled on 2% PCE inflation as our objective. Um, and I think if you go back and look at the history of it, there are a lot of good reasons why that brings in a lot of broader inputs into what's going on in the economy. And 2% is a good number because you want something above zero. If you're in a 0% inflation environment, that can be a very difficult environment. It usually means there's not a lot of growth happening in the economy, but you want it to be at a level where people aren't really factoring it into their decisions in a material way. Um, so I welcome the insights that we'll get from the task force that will help us think about what we should be using as that guide, if that's one of the things that they're looking at. Um, we in Cleveland have the Center for Inflation Research, and so our team spends a lot of time thinking about all the various metrics for inflation, and thinking about what are the different things that we're learning from each of those different indicators. Because again, we have one objective that we're held accountable to, but there's a myriad of other indicators that I look at to help me get a sense of where we're headed over the intermediate and longer term.

[16:51] Beth Hammack：我欢迎这项讨论，也欢迎对它开展深入分析。我最近曾在一次演讲中梳理美联储为何最终把2%的PCE通胀设为目标。回顾历史，这一选择有许多充分理由，因为它能够纳入经济运行中更广泛的因素。2%也是一个合适的数字，因为目标需要高于零。零通胀往往是非常困难的环境，通常意味着经济增长不足；但目标也应该低到不会实质影响人们日常决策。我欢迎工作组提供见解，帮助我们思考应当采用什么指标作为指引，如果这确实是他们研究的问题之一。克利夫兰联储设有通胀研究中心，我们的团队会花大量时间研究各种通胀指标，以及每项指标分别告诉了我们什么。我们只有一个需要对其负责的目标，但我还会观察大量其他指标，以判断经济在中期和长期可能走向哪里。

[18:12] Jennifer Schonberger: President Trump said that he is now considering—or there's a letter that the White House has sent to Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook telling her that he's considering removing her again. This comes, of course, after the Supreme Court ruled that the president needs to offer a certain amount of time for her to respond to allegations. What's your reaction to that?

[18:12] Jennifer Schonberger：特朗普总统表示，他正在考虑再次免去美联储理事Lisa Cook的职务；白宫已经致信告知她这一考虑。此前最高法院裁定，总统需要给她一定时间回应相关指控。你对此有何反应？

[18:43] Beth Hammack: Um, I think the Supreme Court went through and was very thoughtful in their decision-making. Um, they said that it's really important to maintain the independence of the Federal Reserve. I firmly believe that. I think having monetary policymakers who are free from political pressures, as we are, is important to make sure that we can set policy in a way that hits that dual-mandate objective as set by Congress, for both maximum employment and price stability. Um, and it's important that we're accountable. So we are delivering on that mandate as set for us by Congress, and we are accountable to Congress. And so I try to meet with different elected officials when I'm down in Washington, and you saw the chair was testifying in front of both the House and the Senate earlier this year. So the transparency, the accountability, the independence—all of those are really wrapped together.

[18:43] Beth Hammack：我认为最高法院经过了审慎的决策过程。法院指出，维护美联储独立性非常重要，我对此深信不疑。货币政策制定者像我们这样免受政治压力，才能确保政策实现国会赋予的双重使命，也就是最大就业和价格稳定。同时，问责也很重要。我们必须履行国会设定的使命，并对国会负责。因此，我去华盛顿时会尽量与不同的民选官员会面；今年早些时候，主席也在众议院和参议院作证。透明、问责和独立实际上是相互交织的。

[19:45] Jennifer Schonberger: Beth, thank you so much for your insights. So appreciate it.

[19:45] Jennifer Schonberger：Beth，非常感谢你分享这些见解。十分感谢。
