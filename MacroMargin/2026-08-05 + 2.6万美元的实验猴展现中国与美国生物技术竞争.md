## 2.6万美元的实验猴展现中国与美国生物技术竞争

### [技术](https://www.bloomberg.com/technology)

食蟹猴繁殖中心，实验猴与幼猴一同坐在笼内。*摄影师：Mladen Antonov/AFP/盖蒂图片社*

作者：[安柏·唐](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXDw-uykeeQ/amber-tong)2026年8月5日，上午7:00（GMT+8）*更新于2026年8月5日，下午2:05（GMT+8）*

### **要点** 由彭博AI提供

- 中国实验猴价格飙升，使新药研发试验复杂化，并增加了从周边国家进口灵长类动物的需求。
- 价格飙升由需求驱动，新型生物制剂与下一代疗法的涌现，使灵长类研究需求急剧上升。
- 供应紧张考验着企业快速验证候选药物并启动临床试验的能力，因为猴子在生物制剂及其他复杂药物的测试中不可或缺。
中国的实验猴价格飙升，使新药开发试验复杂化，并增加了从邻国进口灵长类动物的需求。

6月，一家国有实验室以每只17.8万元（约合26,294美元）的价格采购了40只食蟹猴，价格几乎是去年同期的两倍。据多位生物技术高管称，这种用于药物安全试验的小型灵长类动物在整个行业都出现了类似的价格飙升。由于猴子试验是某些药物获批的先决条件，供应紧张可能会给中国极快的药物研发速度带来延误。

行业高管和分析师表示，受监管的猴子养殖场供应大体稳定。价格飙升反而是由需求驱动的：新生物制剂和下一代疗法的激增，导致对灵长类动物研究的需求急剧上升，远超现有产能。

这种情况让人想起新冠疫情期间的猴子[短缺](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8236990/)，当时疫苗项目的激增带来了旺盛需求，推高了价格。然而，这次供应不匹配反映的是中国转型为创新药物研发的主要来源。如今，中国占全球创新药管线约三分之一，是临床试验的首要[目的地](https://www.bmj.com/content/392/bmj.s221)，并与渴望获得新疗法的美国和欧洲公司达成了重磅交易。

“这就像与英伟达谈判以获得其芯片，或者如果你是需要计算能力的LLM公司，就要与数据中心提供商协商产能，”下一代癌症疗法开发商Excalipoint Therapeutics联合创始人兼首席财务官朱杰伦表示。“每个人都有自己的方式。”

这种需求并非中国独有。在美国，每年约有[6万只](https://neprimateconservancy.org/primates-in-animal-studies/#:~:text=106%2C430*%20*%20FY%202024.%20*%20COLUMN%20B%3A,*%20COLUMN%20D%3A%20USED%2C%20PAIN%20MINIMIZED.%2026%2C055.)猴子用于研究，其中一些在2024年时售价超过5万美元。法国的国家科学研究中心正在建设新的繁育能力，因为灵长类动物的成本已升至每只[3万欧元](https://sciencebusiness.net/news/biodiversity/france-expands-primate-breeding-centre-name-scientific-sovereignty)。尽管如此，中国需求和价格的飙升反映出该国开发可能挑战全球制药公司的创新药物的快速步伐。

由于猴子更难获得，规划研究可能需要额外四个月，某些情况下甚至长达10个月，常驻中国的独立顾问哈里·贾韦莱宁（Harri Jarvelainen）表示，他为西方客户提供临床前工作咨询。由于中国监管机构要求用于毒理学试验的猴子至少3岁，他补充说：“一旦出现瓶颈，你无法轻易解决。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b381cc90b1481d88ef9f9d800aff0bd/3b381cc9-0b14-8136-89d6-e559ebfb61d4-1ee55fe4f22a07370553a16fc4d2d248.png)

供应紧张正在考验企业快速验证候选药物和启动临床试验的能力。猴子至关重要，以至于大型合同研究组织，如[药明康德](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2359%20HK%20Equity)、[康龙化成](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300759%20CH%20Equity)和[昭衍新药](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/603127%20CH%20Equity)，都拥有自己的繁育基地以确保供应。

这在生物制剂测试中尤为重要，生物制剂是一种更复杂的药物类型，对癌症和炎症等难治疾病具有前景。为了获得生物制剂试验的监管许可，公司通常必须提供猴子数据来证明可能的人类安全性。一个项目可能需要12到100只猴子。

### **人类替身**

猴子研究对于较新的技术仍然不可或缺，例如[抗体药物偶联物](https://www.globaldata.com/media/pharma/chinese-companies-lead-adc-development-pipeline-reveals-globaldata/)、[双特异性抗体](https://prescienthg.com/chinas-pd-l1-x-vegf-bispecifics-boom-as-pharmas-answer-to-the-coming-patent-cliff/)和[体内CAR-T](https://asianbiotechiesinabar.substack.com/p/asian-biotechies-in-a-bar-is-now) ——而这些正是中国研发和交易活跃的领域，瑞银证券中国医疗健康研究主管陈晨表示。由于灵长类动物与人类在生理上的相似性，它们是许多毒理学测试中最可靠的替代者，这些测试追踪药物在体内的移动，评估对各器官的反应，最关键的是，发现任何致命影响。

Excalipoint的朱先生表示，快速将候选药物从发现推进到临床一直是中企的主要竞争优势。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b381cc90b1481d88ef9f9d800aff0bd/3b381cc9-0b14-81f9-875f-c6a80dc25a7d-3beefe12df9fb496b98b8befd347c25b.png)

他表示，明年一只猴子的成本可能超过20万元人民币，而一两年前才略高于10万元。每家公司实际承担的价格最终取决于所需数量、紧急程度和供应商。

昭衍新药在7月预测上半年净利润将飙升13倍，这主要归功于其实验猴的货币价值激增。在2月份的一份报告中，[浙商证券](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/601878%20CH%20Equity)估计2026年至2028年间每年短缺1.5万至2万只猴子，而来自柬埔寨等地的进口（在因疫情长期中断后于今年恢复）预计最终将填补缺口。

与此同时，随着投资涌入以资助新项目，对有限名额的竞争可能会继续。根据商业情报平台[PharmaDJ](https://www.pharmadj.com/ssr/en/article/2074438297616187394)的数据，2026年上半年，中国私营生物技术公司筹集了超过34.9亿美元，高于2025年全年总额。

“好消息是，这显然是业务蓬勃发展的结果，”Jarvelainen说。

## $26,000 Lab Monkeys Show China’s Quest to Rival US Biotech

### [Technology](https://www.bloomberg.com/technology)

Laboratory monkeys with their babies sitting in their cage in the breeding centre for cynomolgus macaques.*Photographer: Mladen Antonov/AFP/Getty Images*

By [Amber Tong](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXDw-uykeeQ/amber-tong)August 5, 2026 at 7:00 AM GMT+8*Updated on August 5, 2026 at 2:05 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

- Prices of lab monkeys in China have surged, complicating new drug development trials and increasing the need for primate imports from neighboring countries.
- The spike in prices is driven by demand, with an explosion of new biologics and next-generation therapies creating a sharp rise in the need for primate studies.
- The supply crunch is testing the ability of firms to rapidly validate drug candidates and launch clinical trials, with monkeys being central to testing for biologics and other complex medicines.
Prices of lab monkeys in China have surged, complicating new drug development trials and increasing the need for primate imports from neighboring countries.

In June, a state lab procured 40 cynomolgus monkeys at 178,000 yuan ($26,294) each, nearly double the rate from a year ago. There have been similar price spikes for the small-sized primates, which are needed for drug safety trials, across the industry, according to several biotech executives. The supply squeeze could add delays to China’s breakneck speed of drug development, given monkey experiments are a prerequisite for certain drugs to win approvals.

Industry executives and analysts say supply from regulated monkey farms remain broadly stable. The spike in prices is instead driven by demand: an explosion of new biologics and next-generation therapies has created a sharp rise in the need for primate studies that far exceeds existing capacity.

The situation is reminiscent of monkey [shortages](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8236990/) during the Covid pandemic, when a rush of vaccine programs drove intense demand and sent prices up. This time, however, the supply mismatch reflects China’s transformation into a major source of novel drug development. China now accounts for roughly a third of the world’s innovative drug pipeline, is the [top destination](https://www.bmj.com/content/392/bmj.s221) for clinical trials, and has struck blockbuster deals with US and European firms eager to source new treatments.

“It’s like negotiating with Nvidia to secure its chips, or arranging for capacity with a data center provider, if you are a LLM company in need of computing power,” said Jielun Zhu, co-founder and chief financial officer at next-generation cancer therapy developer Excalipoint Therapeutics. “Everybody has their own way.”

The demand is not unique to China. In the US, where about [60,000 monkeys](https://neprimateconservancy.org/primates-in-animal-studies/#:~:text=106%2C430*%20*%20FY%202024.%20*%20COLUMN%20B%3A,*%20COLUMN%20D%3A%20USED%2C%20PAIN%20MINIMIZED.%2026%2C055.) are used every year in research, some were selling for more than $50,000 as recently as 2024. France’s National Centre for Scientific Research is building new breeding capacity as costs of primates rose to [€30,000](https://sciencebusiness.net/news/biodiversity/france-expands-primate-breeding-centre-name-scientific-sovereignty) each. Still, the surge in demand and prices in China reflects the rapid pace of the country’s push to develop innovative drugs that could challenge global pharmaceutical companies.

With monkeys harder to source, planning studies can take an additional four months — and in some cases, as long as 10 months, said Harri Jarvelainen, a China-based independent consultant who advises Western clients on preclinical work. Because Chinese regulators require monkeys used in toxicology testing to be at least three years of age, “once a bottleneck develops, you can’t fix it just like that,” he added.

The supply crunch is testing the ability of firms to rapidly validate drug candidates and launch clinical trials. Monkeys are so central that large contract research organizations, such as [WuXi AppTec Co Ltd](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2359%20HK%20Equity), [Pharmaron Beijing Co Ltd](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300759%20CH%20Equity) and [Joinn Laboratories](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/603127%20CH%20Equity), have their own breeding farms to secure access.

This is especially important in testing for biologics, a more complex type of medicines that carry promise for tough-to-treat diseases such as cancer and inflammation. To get regulatory clearance for biologics trials, companies often must provide monkey data to demonstrate likely human safety. A single program could require anywhere from a dozen to 100 monkeys.

### **Human Surrogates**

Monkey studies remain indispensable for newer technologies such as [antibody-drug conjugates](https://www.globaldata.com/media/pharma/chinese-companies-lead-adc-development-pipeline-reveals-globaldata/), [bispecifics](https://prescienthg.com/chinas-pd-l1-x-vegf-bispecifics-boom-as-pharmas-answer-to-the-coming-patent-cliff/) and [in vivo CAR-T](https://asianbiotechiesinabar.substack.com/p/asian-biotechies-in-a-bar-is-now) – areas of intense R&D and dealmaking in China, said Chen Chen, head of China healthcare research at UBS Securities. Their physiological similarity to humans makes primates the most reliable surrogates for many toxicology tests, which track a drug’s movement through the body, assess reactions on various organs and – most critically – flag any fatal effects.

Bringing drug candidates from discovery to the clinic quickly has been a major competitive edge for Chinese companies, Excalipoint’s Zhu said.

The cost for a single monkey might exceed 200,000 yuan next year, he said, compared to a little more than 100,000 yuan just a year or two ago. But the price borne by each company ultimately depends on the number needed, urgency and vendor.

In July, Joinn Laboratories forecast a 13-fold jump in net profit for the first half of the year, thanks in large part to the surging monetary value of its research monkeys.** **In a February report, [Zheshang Securities Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/601878%20CH%20Equity) estimated an annual shortage of 15,000 to 20,000 monkeys between 2026 and 2028, with imports from places like Cambodia, which resumed this year after a lengthy pause stemming from the pandemic, expected to help eventually plug the gap.

In the meantime, competition for the limited spots are likely to continue as investments pour in to fund new projects. Private Chinese biotechs raised more than $3.49 billion in the first half of 2026, a larger sum than in all of 2025, according to business intelligence platform [PharmaDJ](https://www.pharmadj.com/ssr/en/article/2074438297616187394).

“The good news is that it’s clearly a result of booming business,” said Jarvelainen.
