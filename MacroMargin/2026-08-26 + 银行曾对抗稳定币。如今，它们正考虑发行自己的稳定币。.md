## **银行曾对抗稳定币。如今，它们正考虑发行自己的稳定币。**

### 随着银行体系外的公司发行代币，高管们处于守势

和

Vicky Ge Huang

2026年8月26日 上午10:59（美国东部时间）

![Bank of America sign and parking lot with palm trees.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b14812e8208c0f13167812b/3c881cc9-0b14-8126-a962-cc9b367e6534-54bdb13a14fec24a15247dfa7bfdb63f.jpg)

美国银行是正在推进稳定币项目的金融机构之一。图片来源：Reuben J. Brown/《华尔街日报》

- 随着Visa、贝莱德、谷歌和DoorDash等非银行公司进入市场，银行正摩拳擦掌，准备发行自己的稳定币。
- 包括美国银行和富国银行在内的十多家金融机构组成的团体，正在推进一个全球稳定币项目。
- 州银行家协会联盟计划于2027年上半年推出BankChain Alliance，这是一个支持稳定币的区块链平台。
大银行和小银行都已开始对拥有自己的稳定币的想法表现出兴趣，尽管一些高管早先曾怀疑其需求不会很大。

过去一年，一些银行高管曾对稳定币的需求不屑一顾，银行业界针对那些希望提供类似银行存款的稳定币的加密公司 [发起了一场游说战](https://www.wsj.com/podcasts/the-journal/big-banks-vs-big-crypto/4118c930-3b19-457b-9719-3cf8875ff6dc?mod=article_inline)。相反，银行联手 [推出了代币化存款系统](https://www.wsj.com/finance/banking/jpmorgan-citi-and-big-banks-plan-new-tokenized-deposit-system-to-answer-crypto-6b2d696b?mod=article_inline)，以应对支付领域的快速变化。

稳定币是与美元或其他货币挂钩的数字代币，而代币化存款只是传统货币以数字代币形式呈现。

但现在，多家银行已开始考虑是否需要这两者，即便只是为了防御性举措，因为一些高管担心稳定币可能侵蚀其业务。Visa、[贝莱德](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/BLK)、谷歌和[DoorDash](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/DASH)等大型非银行公司已采取行动，[涉足稳定币市场](https://joinopenstandard.com/)，该市场一直被Tether和Circle主导。

据知情人士透露，[摩根大通](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/JPM)最近评估了推出自己的稳定币的可能性。另一位人士表示，这些讨论尚未成熟，目前没有正在积极开发的产品。这家美国最大的银行已经拥有一种名为JPM Coin的代币化存款，以及自己的区块链。

摩根大通的一位女发言人表示：“虽然我们没有发行稳定币的计划，但根据客户需求和监管环境的变化，我们当然会在未来评估所有选择。”

许多银行专注于合资企业，尽管一些银行最终可能既参与联盟，又拥有自己的稳定币。

据知情人士透露，包括[美国银行](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/BAC)、[富国银行](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/WFC)和桑坦德银行在内的十多家金融机构一直在推进一项面向全球的稳定币项目。

据其中一些人士称，这些银行已讨论了一项稳定币计划，该稳定币将专注于其商业业务，并覆盖美元，随后是欧元，最终覆盖七国集团的其他货币，其用例可能因地区而异。

较小的银行也在推进类似的举措。周二，一个由州银行家协会组成的联盟公布了其计划，将推出一个由银行构建、拥有和治理的区块链平台。该平台受联邦住房贷款银行模式启发，旨在服务各种规模的银行。目前，约39个州银行家协会代表约3000家银行参与了这项工作。

该技术平台名为“BankChain Alliance”，将用于资金管理、供应链金融和现金管理。预计将于2027年上半年推出，并支持代币化存款和稳定币。

BankChain Alliance临时主席、佛罗里达银行家协会首席执行官凯西·克兰宁格表示：“这绝对是我们愿景的一部分，即确保我们提供银行希望利用的服务和能力。”

加密货币公司Anchorage Digital首席执行官内森·麦考利表示，该公司正在推进十几个稳定币项目，其中一部分直接来自银行和银行主导的财团。

银行一直与加密货币行业就稳定币是否可以向持有者提供奖励展开激烈交锋。银行认为，收益可能引发存款从银行流失，并在《清晰法案》中反对这一点，该法案是一项旨在为所有数字资产建立首个全面监管框架的里程碑式措施。

《华尔街日报》此前报道，大型银行今年宣布计划推出代币化存款网络。银行倾向于选择代币化存款而非稳定币，因为代币化存款本质上就是传统银行存款在区块链上以数字代币形式呈现。

这意味着代币化存款保留了相同的信用风险特征、监管预期和会计处理方式，使银行更容易在现有监管框架内提供基于区块链的支付。它们还将存款保留在银行体系内。

稳定币的支持者表示，稳定币的设计目的是在不同公共区块链（如以太坊和索拉纳）之间无缝转移。代币化存款通常在私有区块链或围墙花园中发行，一家银行的网络不一定与另一家银行的网络相连。

批评者表示，稳定币缺乏强有力的用例，并且没有银行存款所拥有的那种保险，一些人称其为“寻找问题的解决方案”。

布鲁金斯学会高级研究员亚伦·克莱因表示：“如果目标是实现可编程货币，那么代币化存款似乎有可能在不需构建全新金融架构的情况下解决这个问题。”

更多加密公司[正寻求自己成为银行](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/national-trust-charters-fintech-cypto-b3d3e443?mod=article_inline)。近期，特朗普家族的旗舰加密企业World Liberty Financial表示，其信托公司[已获得货币监理署(OCC)的初步有条件批准](https://www.wsj.com/finance/trump-familys-world-liberty-financial-gets-preliminary-approval-to-launch-a-bank-dd518a2b?mod=article_inline)可成为银行。若获得最终批准，World Liberty Trust将发行、赎回并保护USD1——World Liberty去年发行的40亿美元锚定美元稳定币。

OCC负责人乔纳森·古尔德上周在怀俄明州杰克逊霍尔举行的怀俄明区块链研讨会上表示：“在提交给OCC审议的商业计划中，涉及和整合支付稳定币等正变得越来越常见。”

## Banks Fought Against Stablecoins. Now They Are Considering Launching Their Own.

## **Banks Fought Against Stablecoins. Now They Are Considering Launching Their Own.**

### Executives are on defensive as companies outside of banking system launch coins

By

and

Vicky Ge Huang

Aug. 26, 2026 10:59 am ET

Bank of America is among a group of financial institutions that have been moving forward on a stablecoin venture. Reuben J. Brown for WSJ

- Banks are warming up to launching their own stablecoins as nonbank companies like Visa, BlackRock, Google and DoorDash enter the market.
- A group of over a dozen financial institutions, including Bank of America and Wells Fargo, is moving forward on a global stablecoin venture.
- A consortium of state bankers associations plans to launch BankChain Alliance, a blockchain platform supporting stablecoins, in the first half of 2027.
Banks large and small have started to warm up to the idea of their own stablecoins, despite early doubts from some executives that there will be much demand for them.

Some bank executives over the past year had shrugged off the need for stablecoins and the industry [waged a lobbying battle](https://www.wsj.com/podcasts/the-journal/big-banks-vs-big-crypto/4118c930-3b19-457b-9719-3cf8875ff6dc?mod=article_inline) against crypto firms that wanted to offer stablecoins that would act like bank deposits. Instead, banks banded together [to launch a tokenized deposit system](https://www.wsj.com/finance/banking/jpmorgan-citi-and-big-banks-plan-new-tokenized-deposit-system-to-answer-crypto-6b2d696b?mod=article_inline) as an answer to the rapid changes in the world of payments.

Stablecoins are digital tokens pegged to the U.S. dollar or other currencies, while tokenized deposits are just traditional money represented as digital tokens.

But now a number of banks have started to consider whether there could be a need for both, even if only as a defensive play, as some executives worry stablecoins could encroach on their businesses. Big nonbank companies such as Visa, [BlackRock](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/BLK), Google and [DoorDash](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/DASH) have moved [to get involved in the stablecoin market](https://joinopenstandard.com/), which has been dominated by Tether and Circle.

[JPMorgan Chase](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/JPM) recently evaluated whether it could launch its own stablecoin, according to people familiar with the matter. Those conversations have been preliminary and there is no active product under way, another person said. The largest U.S. bank already has a tokenized deposit called JPM Coin and its own blockchain.

“While we have no plans to issue a stablecoin, depending on customer demand and the evolution of the regulatory landscape, we would of course evaluate all options in the future,” a JPMorgan spokeswoman said.

Many banks are focused on joint ventures, though some could end up both in consortia and with stablecoins of their own.

A group of more than a dozen financial institutions including [Bank of America](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/BAC), [Wells Fargo](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/WFC) and Santander has been moving forward on a stablecoin venture that would reach across the globe, according to people familiar with the matter.

The banks have discussed plans for a stablecoin that would be focused on the commercial side of their businesses and cover the dollar, followed by the euro and eventually other Group of Seven currencies, according to some of the people, with use cases that could vary by region.

Smaller banks are working on similar initiatives, too. On Tuesday, a consortium of state bankers associations unveiled their plans to launch a blockchain platform built for, owned and governed by banks. Inspired by the Federal Home Loan Bank model, the platform is meant for banks of all sizes. Currently, some 39 state bankers associations representing about 3,000 banks are involved in the effort.

Called the BankChain Alliance, the tech platform would be used for treasury management, supply-chain finance and cash management. It is expected to launch in the first half of 2027 and support both tokenized deposits and stablecoins.

“That is definitely part of our vision in terms of making sure that we’re providing the services and capabilities that banks would want to take advantage of,” said Kathy Kraninger, interim chair at the BankChain Alliance and chief executive of the Florida Bankers Association.

Crypto firm Anchorage Digital is seeing more than a dozen stablecoin projects in its pipeline, with a subset of those coming directly from banks and banks-led consortia, according to CEO Nathan McCauley.

Banks have been in a heated [battle with the crypto industry](https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/crypto-industry-notches-win-in-washington-after-brawl-with-banks-21e53443?mod=article_inline) over whether stablecoins can offer rewards to holders. Banks have argued that yield could trigger deposit flight from banks and fought against it in the Clarity Act, a landmark measure intended to establish the first comprehensive regulatory framework for all digital assets.

Major banks announced this year plans [to launch a tokenized deposit network](https://www.wsj.com/finance/banking/jpmorgan-citi-and-big-banks-plan-new-tokenized-deposit-system-to-answer-crypto-6b2d696b?mod=article_inline), The Wall Street Journal previously reported. Banks tend to favor tokenized deposits over stablecoins because they are simply traditional bank deposits represented as digital tokens on the blockchain.

That means tokenized deposits retain the same credit-risk profile, regulatory expectations and accounting treatments, making it easier for banks to offer blockchain-based payments within an existing regulatory framework. They also keep deposits within the banking system.

Proponents of stablecoins say they are designed to move seamlessly across different public blockchains like Ethereum and Solana. Tokenized deposits typically are issued on private blockchains or walled gardens where one bank’s network isn’t necessarily connected to that of another bank.

Critics say there aren’t strong use cases for stablecoins, which lack the type of insurance that bank deposits have, with some calling them a solution looking for a problem.

“If the desire is to have programmable money, then tokenized deposits seem to have the potential to solve that problem without requiring a whole new financial architecture,” said Aaron Klein, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution.

More crypto firms [are seeking to become banks themselves](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/national-trust-charters-fintech-cypto-b3d3e443?mod=article_inline). Recently, World Liberty Financial, the Trump family’s flagship crypto venture, said its trust company [received preliminary conditional approval](https://www.wsj.com/finance/trump-familys-world-liberty-financial-gets-preliminary-approval-to-launch-a-bank-dd518a2b?mod=article_inline) from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, or OCC, to become a bank. Upon final approval, World Liberty Trust will issue, redeem and safeguard USD1, the $4 billion dollar-backed stablecoin that World Liberty launched last year.

“It is becoming ordinary course to involve and integrate payment stablecoins, etc., in the business plans that we are now seeing presented to the OCC for consideration,” Jonathan Gould, the head of the OCC, said at the Wyoming Blockchain Symposium in Jackson Hole, Wyo., last week.
