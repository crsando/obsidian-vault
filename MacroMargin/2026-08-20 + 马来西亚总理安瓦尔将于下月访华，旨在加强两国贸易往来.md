### 中文译文

## 马来西亚总理安瓦尔将于下月访华，旨在加强两国贸易往来

马来西亚总理安瓦尔·易卜拉欣计划于下月访问中国——这是他一年多来首次访华——这个东南亚国家希望深化与北京的贸易联系。

据知情人士透露，安瓦尔预计将于9月底应总理李强之邀对中国进行为期三天的访问，行程包括上海和杭州，不包括北京。

消息人士向《南华早报》表示，他的行程仍在最后敲定中，但这位马来西亚领导人预计将出席全球数字贸易博览会，并在访问期间与李强举行会谈。

该博览会是是中国最重要的数字经济平台之一，定于9月23日至27日在华东城市杭州举行。

这些消息人士因未获授权公开谈论此事而要求匿名。

此次访问是马来西亚为加强与最大贸易伙伴中国的关系所做努力的一部分。

中国国家主席习近平去年4月访问了马来西亚。四个月后，安瓦尔访问了中国北方港口城市天津，出席上海合作组织峰会，随后在北京参加纪念二战结束80周年的盛大阅兵式。

习近平和安瓦尔在阅兵式期间举行了会谈，中国领导人将这两个亚洲邻国描述为“具有共同愿景的好朋友”。两国承诺深化战略协调，加强合作。

安瓦尔于十月在吉隆坡会见了李强，李强总理表示，中国始终将马来西亚视为周边外交的优先方向。

安瓦尔在周一接受半岛电视台采访时关于台湾的言论引起了全球关注，并得到了北京的支持。当被问及对北京对该岛采取侵略行动的看法时，他说：“我认为台湾是中国的一部分，就这样。”

他并没有明确表示是否会谴责北京对台湾使用武力，而是表示，如果马来西亚的一个省份试图脱离，他会使用武力来“保护国家的主权和统一”。

北京将台湾视为中国的一部分，如有必要将通过武力实现统一。美国与大多数国家一样，不承认这个自治岛屿的独立地位，但反对任何以武力夺取它的企图，并且依照法律有义务向其提供武器。

台湾外交部拒绝了安瓦尔的言论，并敦促这位马来西亚领导人“坚持民主、自由、人权和法治等普世价值观，尊重人民的意志”。

安瓦尔于周四为其言论辩护，称马来西亚一直奉行一个中国政策，并将继续坚持这一政策。“当然，我们希望在可能的情况下寻求和平道路，”马来西亚媒体援引他的话说。

当被问及安瓦尔的言论时，北京外交部周三表示，北京“赞赏”这些言论以及马来西亚总理在坚持一个中国原则问题上的“坚定立场”。

外交部发言人林剑表示：“坚持一个中国原则是人心所向，也是正确之举。国际社会对一个中国原则的承诺不会被动摇。”

“让我再次强调，世界上只有一个中国，台湾是中国领土不可分割的一部分。中国必须也必将实现统一。这一点不会改变。”

## Malaysia’s Anwar to visit China next month with an eye on building trade ties

Malaysian Prime Minister [Anwar Ibrahim](https://www.scmp.com/topics/anwar-ibrahim) is planning a visit to China next month – his first in over a year – as the Southeast Asian country looks to deepen trade ties with Beijing.

According to people familiar with the planning, Anwar is expected to make a three-day visit to China in late September at the invitation of Premier Li Qiang, with stops in Shanghai and Hangzhou. The trip does not include a stop in Beijing.

His itinerary was still being finalised, sources told the South China Morning Post, but the Malaysian leader was expected to attend the Global Digital Trade Expo and hold talks with Li during the trip.

The expo, one of China’s most important digital economy platforms, is scheduled to run from September 23 to 27 in the eastern Chinese city of Hangzhou.

The sources spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorised to comment publicly on the matter.

The visit builds on Malaysia’s efforts to strengthen relations with China, its largest trading partner.

Chinese President Xi Jinping [visited Malaysia](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3306646/xi-jinping-arrives-malaysia-trumps-tariffs-push-china-cosy-neighbours) in April last year. Four months later, Anwar visited China’s northern port city of Tianjin for the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit before attending a major military parade in Beijing to mark the 80th anniversary of the end of World War II.

Xi and Anwar held talks on the sidelines of the military parade, with the Chinese leader describing the two Asian neighbours as “good friends with a common vision”. The countries pledged to deepen strategic coordination and strengthen cooperation.

Anwar met Li in October in Kuala Lumpur, where the premier said China had always regarded Malaysia as a priority in its neighbourhood diplomacy.

Anwar’s comments on Taiwan during a Monday interview with Al Jazeera drew global attention – and support from Beijing. When asked for his views on Beijing’s aggression towards the island, he said, “I see Taiwan as part of China, period.”

He stopped short of saying whether he would condemn Beijing’s use of force against Taiwan, saying instead that if a Malaysian province sought to break away, he would use force to “protect the sanctity and unity of the nation”.

Beijing sees Taiwan as part of China to be reunited by force if necessary. The US, like most countries, does not recognise the self-ruled island as independent but is opposed to any attempt to seize it by force and is legally bound to supply it with weapons.

Taiwan’s foreign ministry rejected Anwar’s remarks and urged the Malaysian leader to “uphold universal values such as democracy, freedom, human rights and the rule of law, and respect the will of the people”.

Anwar defended his comments on Thursday, saying that Malaysia had maintained its one-China policy and would continue to uphold it. “Of course, we want to seek a peaceful path whenever possible,” he was quoted by Malaysian media as saying.

Asked about Anwar’s comments, the foreign ministry in Beijing said on Wednesday that Beijing “commends” the remarks and the Malaysian prime minister’s “steadfast position” on the one-China principle.

“Upholding the one-China principle is where public opinion trends and the right thing to do. The international community’s commitment to the one-China principle is not to be shaken,” foreign ministry spokesman Lin Jian said.

“Let me stress once again that there is but one China in the world, and Taiwan is an inalienable part of China’s territory. China must and will achieve reunification. This will not be stopped.”
