### 中文译文

## 印度寻求中国河流数据，西藏特大大坝引发担忧

> 中国西藏自治区林芝市墨脱县雅鲁藏布江某河段的鸟瞰图。

> *摄影：李林/中国新闻社/VCG/Getty Images*

作者：[Sudhi Ranjan Sen](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUpqObttH3w/sudhi-ranjan-sen)
2026年8月7日下午5:03（GMT+8）

[印度](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1504Z:IN)正在敦促[中国](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PRCH%20CH%20Equity)恢复共享河流流量数据，因为北京在雅鲁藏布江上修建全球最大大坝引发的担忧日益加剧，这条河流经印度进入孟加拉国。

据[外交部周五的一份声明](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases?dtl%2F41635%2F36th_Meeting_of_the_Working_Mechanism_for_Consultation_and_Coordination_on_IndiaChina_Border_Affairs_August_06_2026=)，新德里重申需要尽早举行跨境河流专家级机制会议，并强调分享上游河流项目细节的重要性。

印度在新德里举行的[中印边境事务磋商和协调工作机制](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases?dtl%2F41635%2F36th_Meeting_of_the_Working_Mechanism_for_Consultation_and_Coordination_on_IndiaChina_Border_Affairs_August_06_2026=)下的边境会谈中向中方官员提出了这一问题，该机制是处理边界争端的军事外交对话。双方还回顾了为稳定关系和解决边界长期分歧所做的努力。

据[彭博新闻社](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/5350Z:US)[报道](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-16/why-china-s-167-billion-mega-dam-project-in-tibet-is-so-controversial)，中国正在西藏山区建造全球最大的水电站大坝，估计耗资约1.2万亿元人民币（合1780亿美元）。该项目可能影响当地环境和下游国家，包括印度。

雅鲁藏布江流经印度进入孟加拉国，为数百万人供水。该大坝可能还需约十年才能完工。印度政府去年8月告诉[议员](https://www.sansad.in/getFile/annex/268/AU3287_qYFiZe.pdf?utm=)，中国自2023年6月以来一直没有分享这条河的水文数据，这条河在印度被称为布拉马普特拉河。

另外，两国同意在争议边境地区维持和平与安宁，该部在声明中表示，并补充说，他们将继续利用“现有的外交和军事渠道”进行沟通，以避免边界沿线出现“误解和误判”。

这两个亚洲邻国拥有长达3488公里的未划定边界，近年来导致了对峙和紧张局势。

这两个拥有核武器的邻国之间的关系在2020年6月一场导致士兵死亡的边界冲突后冻结了五年，目前正逐步恢复。去年八月，印度总理纳伦德拉·莫迪在天津会见了中国国家主席习近平，这标志着两国关系解冻。

## India Seeks China River Data as Tibet Mega-Dam Raises Concerns

> Aerial view of a section of the Yarlung Tsangpo River in Medog County, Nyingchi, Tibet Autonomous Region of China.

> *Photographer: Li Lin/China News Service/VCG/Getty Images*

By [Sudhi Ranjan Sen](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUpqObttH3w/sudhi-ranjan-sen)
August 7, 2026 at 5:03 PM GMT+8

[India](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1504Z:IN) is pressing [China](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PRCH%20CH%20Equity) to resume sharing water-flow data as concerns mount over Beijing’s construction of the world’s largest dam on the Yarlung Tsangpo, a river that flows through India into Bangladesh.

New Delhi reiterated the need for an early meeting of the Expert Level Mechanism on trans-border rivers and stressed the importance of sharing details of upstream river projects, according to a [statement](https://www.mea.gov.in/press-releases?dtl%2F41635%2F36th_Meeting_of_the_Working_Mechanism_for_Consultation_and_Coordination_on_IndiaChina_Border_Affairs_August_06_2026=) from the Ministry of External Affairs on Friday.

India raised the issue during the border talks with Chinese officials in New Delhi under the Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination on India-China Border Affairs, a diplomatic-military dialogue on managing the boundary dispute. The two sides also reviewed efforts to stabilize relations and address longstanding differences along the border.

China is constructing the world’s largest hydroelectric dam in the mountainous region of Tibet at an estimated cost of around 1.2 trillion yuan ($178 billion). The project is likely to affect the local environment and downstream countries, including India, [Bloomberg News](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/5350Z:US) [reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-16/why-china-s-167-billion-mega-dam-project-in-tibet-is-so-controversial).

The Yarlung Tsangpo flows through India into Bangladesh, supplying water to millions of people. The dam is likely to take about a decade to complete. The Indian government told [lawmakers](https://www.sansad.in/getFile/annex/268/AU3287_qYFiZe.pdf?amp%3Butm=) last August that China had not shared hydrological data on the river, known as the Brahmaputra in India, since June 2023.

Separately, the two countries agreed to maintain peace and tranquility along the disputed border, the ministry said in the statement, adding that they would continue using “existing diplomatic and military channels” to communicate and “avoid misunderstanding and miscalculations” along the boundary.

The Asian neighbors share a 3,488-kilometer-long unmarked border, which has led to military standoffs and tensions in recent years.

Ties between the nuclear-armed neighbors are gradually recovering after a five-year freeze following a June 2020 border clash that killed soldiers. Prime Minister Narendra Modi met Chinese President Xi Jinping in Tianjin last August, signaling a thaw in ties.
