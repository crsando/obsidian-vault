## 台湾起诉九人涉嫌向中国走私AI服务器

## **台湾起诉九人涉嫌向中国走私AI服务器**

起诉涉及将搭载英伟达芯片的超微服务器转运至他处

作者：[王若宇](https://www.wsj.com/news/author/joyu-wang?mod=WSJ_article_author)

2026年8月24日 东部时间上午9:32

![An Nvidia chip on a board at Computex 2026.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b14812fb263d74451f95f8d/3c681cc9-0b14-8177-a909-dd3f1fd1de93-ce7e249e9c2798d7ffb259c783a867bc.jpg)

中国企业寻求通过后门渠道获取英伟达芯片。图片来源：Vernon Yuen/Nexpher Images/ZUMA Press

台北——[台湾](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/taiwan)检方起诉九人，其中包括[英伟达](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/NVDA)和[超微计算机](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/SMCI)的前员工，指控他们协助向中国运送先进人工智能服务器。台湾正试图限制技术进入大陆。

起诉书指控其中八人犯有背信和伪造等罪行。另有一人因涉嫌从涉案公司挪用资金而被起诉。

华盛顿正[试图阻止中国获取](https://www.wsj.com/articles/u-s-restricts-semiconductor-exports-in-bid-to-slow-chinas-military-advance-11665155702?mod=article_inline)最新的英伟达芯片，并已实施出口管制。这迫使一些中国企业通过后门渠道寻求芯片。台湾制造先进计算组件，正面临寻找打击方式的压力。

港口城市基隆的检察官周一表示，其中两名被起诉者为了获得从超微（Super Micro）购买130台配备高端英伟达（Nvidia）芯片的AI服务器的批准，做出了虚假陈述，包括声称这些硬件将留在台湾的一处设施内。

检察官称，被告声称这些服务器不会出售或出口给受美国制裁的实体，且硬件将遵守美国出口管制法规。

根据起诉书，一名超微员工泄露了这家美国公司的内部审查程序，并要求一名英伟达员工帮助确保服务器配额。

检察官指控，这名英伟达员工知晓该计划，并向管理人员保证该笔销售的现场检查已通过。检察官称，这促进了服务器销售获批，尽管台湾的设施缺乏所需的机架空间、电力和带宽。

最终，有74台服务器经香港和印度尼西亚运往中国。台湾海关官员截获了56台原定运往日本的服务器。

检察官称，这些被告“纯粹受利润驱使”，且完全了解出口管制法规，却“合谋……出口高端服务器，推高了企业的合规成本”。“这严重损害了我国的国际形象。”

调查始于5月。当调查在6月[升级](https://www.wsj.com/tech/taiwan-steps-up-probe-into-ai-hardware-smuggling-2baa6e40?mod=article_inline)时，超微表示正在与台湾及其他全球司法辖区的当局合作，以保护其技术和知识产权。

超微和英伟达周一未回应就起诉书置评的请求。

## Taiwan Charges Nine in Connection With Smuggling of AI Servers to China

## **Taiwan Charges Nine in Connection With Smuggling of AI Servers to China**

Indictments involve diversion of Super Micro servers equipped with Nvidia chips

By [Joyu Wang](https://www.wsj.com/news/author/joyu-wang?mod=WSJ_article_author)

Aug. 24, 2026 9:32 am ET

Chinese companies have sought access to Nvidia chips through backdoor routes. Vernon Yuen/Nexpher Images/ZUMA Press

TAIPEI—[Taiwan](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/taiwan) prosecutors charged nine people, including former employees of [Nvidia](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/NVDA) and [Super Micro Computer](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/SMCI), with aiding the shipment of advanced artificial-intelligence servers to China, as [Taiwan](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/taiwan) tries to limit technology reaching the mainland.

The indictment charges eight of the individuals with crimes including breach of trust and forgery. One individual was charged in connection with allegedly siphoning funds from a company involved in the case.

Washington is [trying to block China from getting access](https://www.wsj.com/articles/u-s-restricts-semiconductor-exports-in-bid-to-slow-chinas-military-advance-11665155702?mod=article_inline) to the latest Nvidia chips and has imposed export controls. That has pushed some Chinese companies to seek the chips through backdoor routes. Taiwan, which manufactures advanced computing components, is under pressure to find ways to clamp down.

Two of those charged made false claims to secure approval for the purchase of 130 AI servers from Super Micro equipped with high-end Nvidia chips, including by saying the hardware would remain at a facility in Taiwan, prosecutors in the port city of Keelung said Monday.

Defendants said the servers wouldn’t be sold or exported to U.S.-sanctioned entities and that the hardware would comply with U.S. export-control regulations, prosecutors said.

According to the indictments, a Super Micro employee leaked the U.S. company’s internal vetting procedures and asked an Nvidia employee to help secure server quotas.

Prosecutors alleged that the Nvidia employee was aware of the plan and assured managers that on-site inspections for the sale had cleared. This facilitated approval of the sale of the servers, though the Taiwan facility lacked the required rack space, power and bandwidth, the prosecutors said.

In the end, 74 of the servers were shipped to China through Hong Kong and Indonesia. Taiwan customs officials intercepted 56 servers, which were intended for shipment to Japan.

The defendants, “motivated solely by profit” and with full knowledge of export control regulations, “colluded…to export high-end servers, driving up compliance costs for enterprises,” prosecutors said. “This is gravely damaging to our country’s international image.”

The investigation began in May. When it [escalated in June](https://www.wsj.com/tech/taiwan-steps-up-probe-into-ai-hardware-smuggling-2baa6e40?mod=article_inline), Super Micro said that it was cooperating with authorities in Taiwan and other global jurisdictions to protect its technology and intellectual property.

Super Micro and Nvidia didn’t respond to requests for comment on the indictments Monday.
