## 杰克逊霍尔会议可能进一步巩固美联储与财政部正在形成的默契

![The theme of the Federal Reserve’s symposium at Jackson Hole in Wyoming this year is ‘Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy’ © Bloomberg](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A3d6c44d9-b83a-4864-bf9d-03a240e14103?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

今年美联储在怀俄明州杰克逊霍尔研讨会的主题是“金融创新：对支付和政策的启示” © Bloomberg

[**安德鲁·惠芬**](https://www.ft.com/andrew-whiffin)

发布于4小时前

随着各国央行官员在夏季假期结束时齐聚杰克逊霍尔，凯文·沃什将于周五发表讲话。这将是自7月份有争议的利率决定以来，我们首次听到这位美国联邦储备委员会新主席的发言，也提供了一个机会，来回应自那以来国债市场中出现的一些担忧。

今年研讨会的主题是“金融创新：对支付和政策的启示”。此前《天才法案》于去年成为法律，预示着稳定币时代的正式开启。该法案承诺带来一场金融革命，加速支付、结算和清算，如果它颠覆了传统的银行存款模式，可能对美联储产生重大影响。

更紧迫的问题是，沃什是否会回应长期国债收益率的上升，一些人将此归咎于他的沟通风格。在通胀目标连续五年多超调之后，沃什未能就如何让通胀回到目标水平提出可信的计划。期限溢价——投资者因持有较长期债券而要求的额外收益率——因此上升。

美国银行全球研究美国利率策略主管马克·卡巴纳表示：“沃什对降低通胀的‘坚定承诺’对市场来说还不够。考虑到持续未能实现目标，我们需要听到美联储降低通胀计划的具体细节。”

沃什此前还曾表示，收益率上升是好事，是市场在美联储决定取消官方前瞻性指引后自行理清问题的迹象。

这种观点现在看来与美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特不一致。上周财政部进行计划外的债券回购增加干预后，他表示“我们认为收益率并未反映基本面”。

![图片](https://www.ft.com/content/undefined)

美国金融管理两大分支之间的紧张关系加剧，这让人有充分理由对沃什有所期待。任何冲突都对沃什缩减美联储资产负债表的计划不利。债券市场的动向表明，这只会进一步加大收益率的上行压力。如果他继续推进，与财政部的协调就必不可少，或许要通过一项新的正式[“协议”](https://www.ft.com/content/6589d251-efa7-41d8-8c06-cb958c77b95c)。

这使得杰克逊霍尔成为沃什履行自身义务的理想之地。像许多其他央行行长那样，对其反应函数提供一些见解，并承认利率可能需要上升，或许会有所裨益。

#### 财政部的“扭曲操作”

对收益率曲线长端说几句安抚的话，并不是贝森特长期内对美联储主席的唯一需求。美国赤字支出预计今年将达到2万亿美元，约占GDP的6%，长期预测显示未来十年几乎没有偏离，尽管政府声称增长和关税意味着赤字已达到峰值。

上周的回购反映了政府在债务管理方面一个更大的转变，这一转变已暗中进行了一段时间。财政部表示，从9月起，其购买长期限、流动性较差债券的数量将翻倍。每季度回购量可能额外增加160亿美元，但这在美国公共债务中只是沧海一粟。不过，财政部的买入信号暂时压住了收益率。

这些购买将通过新的短期债务或国库券来融资。从操作机制看，这一举措被称为从长端转向短端的“扭曲”操作。

财政部已在进行一项规模大得多的扭曲操作，该机构一直在定期拍卖较长期限债券，并增加了国库券发行以满足新的借款需求。预计今年将额外发行5000亿美元的新国库券，如果较长期限债券的拍卖量不增加，到2028年这一数字将超过1万亿美元。

将美国债务负担向短期端转移，使美联储在维持低利率方面承担更大责任。如果沃什能够通过生产率驱动的增长和低通胀的组合来实现低利率，那么对短期国债的更高依赖可能节省数万亿美元的利息支出。

然而，目前的问题是，由于中东冲击，今年大部分时间里整体物价指标一直在上升，一些政策制定者推动加息。市场认为年底前加息的可能性为78%。

将美国国债发行转向短期端的另一个问题是需求。贝森特寄希望于稳定币来帮助解决这个问题；发行人有义务购买美国债务，预计将从目前约3000亿美元的水平快速增长。预计在杰克逊霍尔会议上将听到更多相关消息。

#### 资产负债表如何配合

直到最近，美联储一直在通过增加国库券供应来帮助财政部。在去年回购市场动荡之后，美联储一直在“储备管理购买”（RMPs）的名义下购买国库券。原本预计今年将购买2000亿至3000亿美元的国库券，扩大其资产负债表，并将储备保持在“充足”水平，以促进回购市场的平稳运行。

然而，几周前，纽约联储表示，8月中旬至9月中旬期间的储备管理购买将为零。“目前融资条件宽松，这解释了美联储为何停止储备管理购买。但我预计今年晚些时候购买将恢复，因为美联储将继续随着经济增长和更广泛的银行贷款而扩大其资产负债表，”美国银行的卡巴纳表示。

长期来看，美联储将购买多少国库券仍有待讨论，沃什的工作组预计将在年底前提出缩减资产负债表的建议。

加拿大帝国商业银行美国利率策略主管迈克尔·克洛赫蒂表示，“量化紧缩最早可能在明年年底开始。第一步将是对流动性规则提出修改建议。这些修改应足以降低储备需求，使其远低于充足区间，并允许美联储减少其资产。”

美联储仍持有约1.6万亿美元的十年期或更长期限的国债，未来任何将这些债券重新投放市场的努力都将意味着收益率面临进一步上行压力。沃什周五在多大程度上试图压低长期收益率，可能暗示美联储最终真正发布激进资产负债表公告的可能性有多大。

## Jackson Hole could further formalise emerging Fed-Treasury accord

The theme of the Federal Reserve’s symposium at Jackson Hole in Wyoming this year is ‘Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy’ © Bloomberg

[**Andrew Whiffin**](https://www.ft.com/andrew-whiffin)

Published4 hours ago

Kevin Warsh will speak on Friday as central bankers gather at Jackson Hole at the end of the summer break. It will be the first time we have heard from the new US Federal Reserve chair since July’s contested interest rate decision and offers an opportunity to address some of the concerns in Treasury markets since then.

The theme of this year’s symposium is “Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy”. It follows the Genius Act, which became law last year and heralded the official start of the stablecoin era. The act promises a financial revolution that speeds up payments, settlements and clearing, and could have big implications for the Fed if it disrupts the traditional bank deposit model.

More immediate is whether Warsh will address the rise in long Treasury yields, which some have blamed on his communication style. Warsh has failed to offer a credible plan on how to get inflation back to target after more than five years of overshooting. Term premium — the additional yield an investor wants in exchange for holding longer-dated debt — has risen as a result.

Mark Cabana, head of US rates strategy at Bank of America Global Research, said: “Warsh’s ‘resolute commitment’ to lower inflation is not good enough for the market. We need to hear details of the Fed’s plan to get inflation down given the persistent failure to meet its target.”

Warsh has also previously said that rising yields were a good thing and a sign that the market was figuring things out for itself after the Fed’s decision to remove official forward guidance.

That view now appears to be at odds with US Treasury secretary Scott Bessent, who said “we believe that the yields don’t reflect the underlying fundamentals” after the Treasury intervened with an unscheduled increase in bond buybacks last week.

Tension between the two branches of US financial administration is high and a good reason to expect something from Warsh. Any conflict bodes ill for Warsh’s plans to shrink the Fed’s balance sheet. And action in the bond market shows this would only add further upward pressure to yields. If he goes ahead, co-ordination with the Treasury is a must, perhaps via a new formal [“accord”](https://www.ft.com/content/6589d251-efa7-41d8-8c06-cb958c77b95c).

This makes Jackson Hole perfect for Warsh to contribute to his end of the bargain. Some insight into his reaction function and an acknowledgment that rates may need to rise, as many other central bankers have done, would probably do some good.

#### The Treasury ‘twist’

A few soothing words for the long end of the yield curve are not the only thing Bessent will need from the Fed chair in the longer term. US deficit spending is expected to hit $2tn this year or about 6 per cent of GDP, and long-term projections show little deviation over the next decade, despite claims from the administration that growth and tariffs mean the deficit has peaked.

Last week’s buybacks reflect a bigger shift in debt management from the administration that has been going on in the background for a while. The Treasury said it would double the amount of longer-dated, less liquid bonds it purchases as of September. Volumes could total an extra $16bn of buybacks per quarter and are a drop in the ocean of US public debt. But buy signals from the Treasury have kept a lid on yields for now.

The purchases will be funded with new shorter-term debt or Treasury bills. Mechanically, the move is being described as a “twist” operation from the long end to the short.

A much bigger twist operation is already well under way at the Treasury, which has been holding regular auctions of longer-dated debt and has increased issuance of T-bills to meet new borrowing requirements. An extra $500bn in new bills is expected to be issued this year and, without an increase in longer-dated auction amounts, that figure will surpass $1tn in 2028.

Shifting the US debt burden closer to the front end places a greater onus on the Fed to keep rates low. If Warsh can deliver low rates from a mix of productivity-driven growth and low inflation, a higher reliance on bills could save trillions of dollars in interest payments.

The problem right now, though, is that headline measures of prices have been moving up for much of this year because of the Middle East shock, with some policymakers pushing for higher rates. The market gives a 78 per cent chance of a rate rise by the end of the year.

Another problem with shifting Treasury issuance to the short end is demand. Bessent is betting on stablecoins to help here; issuers are obliged to buy US debt and rapid growth is predicted from levels of about $300bn today. Expect to hear more about this at Jackson Hole.

#### How the balance sheet fits in

Until very recently, the Fed has been helping out the Treasury with the increase in bill supply. After last year’s turmoil in repo markets, it had been buying T-bills under the heading of Reserve Management Purchases (RMPs). It had been expected to buy between $200bn and $300bn of bills this year, growing its balance sheet and keeping reserves within the “ample” level that facilitates smooth repo market functioning.

A few weeks ago, however, the New York Fed said RMPs would be zero between mid-August and mid-September. “Funding conditions are easy right now, which explains why the Fed has stopped RMPs. But I expect purchases to resume later this year as the Fed continues to grow its balance sheet in line with growth in the economy and broader bank lending,” says BofA’s Cabana.

How much Fed bill buying there will be in the longer term remains up for debate, with Warsh’s task force expected to present recommendations for a smaller balance sheet by the end of the year.

Michael Cloherty, head of US rates strategy at Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce, said “quantitative tightening could begin as early as the end of next year. The first step would be making the proposed changes to liquidity rules. Those should lower reserve demand enough to move well within the ample range and allow the Fed to reduce its assets.”

The Fed still holds about $1.6tn of 10-year or longer Treasury bonds, and any future effort to put these back to the market would mean further upward pressure on yields. How hard Warsh tries to talk longer yields down on Friday could be a signal of how probable it is that a radical Fed balance sheet announcement ever actually arrives.
