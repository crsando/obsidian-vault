### 中文译文

## 法院裁定：清算人可因中国房地产巨头倒闭在全球范围内追责普华永道

香港的这项裁决有可能威胁四大会计师事务所的合伙网络模式

[**Arjun Neil Alim**](https://www.ft.com/arjun-neil-alim) 在香港，[**Stephen Foley**](https://www.ft.com/stephen-foley) 在纽约

法官裁定，已倒闭的中国房地产集团恒大（Evergrande）的清算人可以在全球范围内就审计失职追究普华永道（PwC）的责任，此案将考验四大会计师事务所的合伙网络模式。

香港法官周三允许[恒大](https://www.ft.com/stream/e6b30a32-d0fa-4316-b172-ff5f48b18574)的清算人向普华永道国际（PwC International，该网络的全球伞形组织）及其负责审计恒大的香港和中国内地附属公司提起疏忽诉讼。

高等法院副法官Patrick Fung写道：“至少可以说，[普华永道]国际确实对[恒大]负有注意义务。至关重要的是，应当进行文件披露和质询，我相信这将有助于进一步厘清此案。”

清算人正寻求从这三个实体获得约84亿美元的损害赔偿，此前多个监管机构发现，普华永道香港和中国内地的审计师在签署恒大财务报表时未能达到专业标准。

这项裁决对来自Alvarez & Marsal的清算人Eddie Middleton和Tiffany Wong来说是一场胜利，鉴于在中国大陆为债权人追回恒大资产的难度，他们已将大量法律精力集中在追究普华永道的疏忽责任上。

恒大曾是中国最大的房地产开发商之一，于2021年违约，负债约3000亿美元。后来被发现，在破产前夕欺诈性地虚增收入。

今年4月，普华永道香港同意支付13亿港元（合1.66亿美元），以了结与当地监管机构就恒大相关索赔的纠纷。2024年，其中国内地关联公司在北京市财政局发现普华永道员工“隐瞒甚至纵容”该房地产集团的欺诈行为后，被处以4.41亿元人民币（合6600万美元）罚款。

与传统跨国公司不同，四大会计师事务所采用网络结构，使各国的审计事务所在法律上保持独立。它们由一个全球伞形组织协调，该组织维护品牌并推行共同标准。

这一制度旨在遵守不同国家的审计法规，并保护每一家成员所免受其他国家审计失败带来的法律责任。

香港的这一裁决可能将财务和法律风险输出给普华永道网络的其他部分，其中最大的成员所，包括美国和英国的成员所，承担了普华永道国际的大部分资金成本。

该伞形组织通常不盈利，也不持有大量资产。

恒大的清算人认为，普华永道国际应为其在香港和中国内地的成员所的过失承担责任，这些过失使得该房地产集团尽管财务状况不佳，仍能在2017年至2020年间支付60亿美元的股息。

清算人正就56亿美元向普华永道国际追索。

普华永道国际辩称，其不应成为本案的当事方，因为恒大不是其客户。

四大会计师事务所的网络结构成功地将大多数审计丑闻隔离在个别成员事务所内部，但也存在例外。

2009年，美国一家法院裁定，德勤全球可能因其意大利成员事务所的客户乳业集团帕玛拉特的欺诈行为而被起诉。2011年，普华永道国际参与了与印度萨蒂扬计算机服务公司倒闭相关的投资者索赔和解。

恒大集团的清算人欢迎法院的裁决，并表示将继续“为债权人的利益追讨资产”。

普华永道全球网络的一位发言人表示，他们不同意法院的裁决，并正在“评估我们的法律选项”。

“[普华永道国际]是普华永道网络内部的协调实体，从未向恒大提供任何服务，也与该公司没有任何关系，”该发言人称。“[普华永道国际]确信针对其的索赔没有法律依据。”

*补充报道：托马斯·黑尔（上海）*

### English Original

## Liquidators can pursue PwC globally over Chinese property giant’s collapse, court rules

Hong Kong decision has potential to threaten Big Four firms’ partnership network model

[**Arjun Neil Alim**](https://www.ft.com/arjun-neil-alim) in Hong Kong and [**Stephen Foley**](https://www.ft.com/stephen-foley) in New York

Liquidators of the collapsed Chinese property group Evergrande can pursue PwC globally for audit failings, a judge has ruled, in a case that will test the Big Four accounting firms’ partnership network model.

A Hong Kong judge on Wednesday allowed [Evergrande](https://www.ft.com/stream/e6b30a32-d0fa-4316-b172-ff5f48b18574)’s liquidators to pursue their negligence case against PwC International, the network’s global umbrella organisation, alongside its Hong Kong and mainland China affiliates, which were responsible for auditing Evergrande.

“It is at least arguable that . . . [PwC] International did owe a duty of care to [Evergrande],” wrote deputy High Court judge Patrick Fung. “It is crucial that there should be discovery of documents and interrogatories administered, which I believe will throw more light on the case.”

The liquidators are seeking about $8.4bn in damages from the three entities after multiple regulators found PwC auditors in Hong Kong and mainland China failed to meet professional standards when they signed off on Evergrande’s financial statements.

The ruling is a victory for Eddie Middleton and Tiffany Wong, the liquidators from Alvarez & Marsal who have focused much of their legal attention on pursuing PwC for negligence, given the difficulty of recovering Evergrande assets in mainland China for creditors.

Evergrande, which was one of China’s largest property developers, defaulted in 2021 with about $300bn in liabilities. It was later found to have fraudulently inflated revenue in the run-up to its collapse.

In April, PwC Hong Kong [agreed to pay HK$1.3bn ($166mn)](https://www.ft.com/content/29ecdb51-0b86-4c42-a101-e344c811d088?syn-25a6b1a6=1) to settle Evergrande-related claims with local regulators. Its mainland affiliate in 2024 [paid a Rmb441mn ($66mn) fine](https://www.ft.com/content/f4e0a831-d738-4bb1-98f8-6cff6a27a1ed?syn-25a6b1a6=1) after the finance ministry in Beijing found PwC staff had “concealed or even condoned” fraud at the property group.

Unlike traditional multinational companies, the Big Four accounting firms use a network structure that keeps national audit firms legally separate. They are co-ordinated by a global umbrella organisation that maintains the brand and imposes common standards.

The system is designed to comply with differing national audit regulations and to shield each member firm from legal liability due to audit failures in other countries.

The Hong Kong ruling threatens to export financial and legal risks to the rest of the PwC network, where the largest member firms, including those in the US and the UK, shoulder most of the funding costs for PwC International.

The umbrella organisation does not typically make profits or hold significant assets.

Evergrande’s liquidators have argued that PwC International should be held liable for the failings of its member firms in Hong Kong and mainland China, which allowed the property group to pay out $6bn in dividends between 2017 and 2020 despite its financial situation.

The liquidators are pursuing PwC International for $5.6bn.

PwC International argued that it should not be party to the case because Evergrande was not its client.

The Big Four’s network structure has successfully kept most audit scandals isolated to individual member firms, but there have been exceptions.

In 2009, a US court ruled that Deloitte Global could be sued over fraud at dairy group Parmalat, a client of its Italian member firm. In 2011, PwC International was part of a settlement of investor claims related to the collapse of India’s Satyam Computer Services.

Evergrande’s liquidators welcomed the court’s decision and said they would continue to “pursue recoveries for the benefit of creditors”.

A spokesperson for PwC’s global network said they disagreed with the court’s decision and were “evaluating our legal options”.

“[PwC International] is the co-ordinating entity within the PwC network and has never provided any services to Evergrande or had any relationship with the company,” said the spokesperson. “[PwC International] is confident that the claims against it have no merit.”

*Additional reporting by Thomas Hale in Shanghai*
