## 特朗普未能松动中国对拉美的经济掌控

作者：Mie Dahl 和 Colum Murphy，2026年8月24日，下午5:00（GMT+8）

### **彭博AI要点**

- 安德烈斯·桑塔马里亚·加里多称北京的商业外交咄咄逼人、务实且难以忽视，中国企业更便宜、更执着且更容易合作。
- 中国的经济分量使其不可替代，无论美国的态度日益强硬，中国在整个拉美的投资规模仍然太大、太根深蒂固，该地区政府难以轻易摆脱。
- 如果被迫疏远与中国的关系，拉美国家将面临几乎无法承受的经济代价，因此它们不得不为了国家利益和人民福祉而维持平衡。
作为代表哥伦比亚各城市协会的负责人，安德烈斯·桑塔马里亚·加里多可以近距离观察[中国的经济策略](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-02-10/us-china-trump-s-focus-on-west-hands-xi-economic-edge-in-asia)在地方层面的实施。

在他位于波哥大市中心的办公室里，摆放着这座城市红色地铁列车的模型——该项目由中资主导的联合体承建。桑塔马里亚称北京的商业外交咄咄逼人、务实且难以忽视。

他说，中国企业更便宜、更执着且更容易合作。它们还愿意绕过国家政府，与州长、市长、当地商业团体和市政官员打交道。*Asocapitales* 执行董事表示，其结果是它们越来越[将美国同行甩在身后](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2023-08-09/ford-shuts-brazil-electric-car-factory-as-lula-turns-to-china-s-byd)。

“对中国将会有技术开放，因为他们确实更先进，”桑塔马里亚说，他列举了自动驾驶汽车、机器人和无人机作为中国专长的具体领域。

桑塔马里亚的经历触及了唐纳德·特朗普及其扩大美国对西半球控制计划所面临困境的核心。虽然总统可以指出通过从阿根廷到秘鲁的一系列结盟领导人[在确保美国政治影响力方面取得了重大进展](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-01-15/trump-backs-argentina-ally-with-us-capital-as-he-confronts-venezuela-with-force)，但他[建立美国经济主导地位](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-23/trump-takes-anti-china-cursade-to-chile-ahead-of-latin-america-summit-in-miami)的努力却证明要困难得多。

原因之一是，中国多年来在拉美建立的投资布局，从港口到能源和矿业，规模庞大且根深蒂固，该地区政府无法仅仅因为美国政府的突发奇想就将其抛弃。与此同时，北京通过向电动汽车、电池和数据中心等领域的高价值链迈进，成功保持了领先华盛顿几步的优势。

这就是为什么厄瓜多尔总统丹尼尔·诺沃亚——特朗普的盟友，全力投入与美国的安全伙伴关系——刚刚结束对中国的为期一周的访问，包括与中国国家主席习近平会晤。诺沃亚访问中国以支持厄瓜多尔的太阳能发电和数字转型，通过中国人工智能驱动的气象系统扩大太阳能发电能力，并支持厄瓜多尔应对厄尔尼诺气候现象。

Photographer: Andres Martinez Casares/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481d88618ebaee3c801b0/3c681cc9-0b14-818a-869d-d7759bc78935-60dca2a54788f2005c0a50257326c257.png)

中国国家主席习近平（右）8月18日在北京人民大会堂签署仪式后与厄瓜多尔总统丹尼尔·诺沃亚握手。摄影师：Andres Martinez Casares/Getty Images

这表明，尽管美国施加政治压力，特朗普所谓“唐罗主义”的经济层面仍未实现。

墨西哥驻华大使赫苏斯·塞亚德表示，与北京的经济联系总体上“未受到”特朗普西半球霸权计划的影响。“这一切仍停留在政治层面，”他说，同时强调了中国在贸易、技术、投资和创新方面的影响力。

“对我们来说，保护与美国的关系非常重要，”塞亚德在北京的视频采访中说。“但我们不会通过与中国对抗来做到这一点。”

### **门罗主义**

遏制中国对战略资产主导地位的努力一直是特朗普版门罗主义的核心。在[第二任期就职演说](https://www.whitehouse.gov/remarks/2025/01/the-inaugural-address/)中，特朗普声称中国正在运营巴拿马运河，并断言美国“正在夺回它”。国防部长皮特·赫格塞斯随后[表示](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2025/04/sunday-shows-president-trumps-america-first-trade-policies-in-action/)，“中国的影响力不能控制我们的后院。”

这份33页的美国[国家安全战略](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-National-Security-Strategy.pdf)于11月发布，在专门针对西半球的章节中将这一理论正式化，阐述了政府“恢复美国优势地位”的目标。该战略谈到培养在移民和犯罪问题上与其政策优先事项一致的“地区支持者”，同时将“非半球竞争者”拒之门外。

特朗普可以为其议程指出一些明显的政治成就，从将尼古拉斯·马杜罗从委内瑞拉抓走，到孤立古巴，再到在阿根廷中期选举前夕通过一笔200亿美元的货币互换来支持哈维尔·米莱总统。在一年多的时间里，拉美七个国家举行总统选举，特朗普支持的候选人全部获胜，最近一次是在哥伦比亚。

![The Chancay megaport in Chancay, Peru. Photographer: Marco Garro/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481d88618ebaee3c801b0/3c681cc9-0b14-8164-9c70-d7282ef0dff8-686bec67cae7774480a5623b5303f3aa.png)

### 秘鲁钱凯的钱凯巨型港口。摄影师：Marco Garro/彭博社

但除了挑战少数几个中国旗舰项目，包括总部位于香港的长和集团在巴拿马运河的存在，以及秘鲁耗资13亿美元的[钱凯巨型港口](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trade-war-brazil-steel-billionaires-take-fight-with-china-to-peru)，美国遏制中国经济扩张的努力成效有限。

“中国的经济分量使其不可替代，无论美国日益胁迫的姿态如何，”中国人民大学国际发展研究所副所长崔守军表示，该研究所与中国共产党关系密切。

最近一波与特朗普结盟的领导人带来了亲美言论，但“经济现实始终在抵消意识形态转向”，他说，将特朗普的施压策略与中国对该地区的“互利”做法进行对比。

民调似乎证实了这一点。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481d88618ebaee3c801b0/3c681cc9-0b14-818a-82f1-e8893a1eec8f-49664d009c99e990d0086fdc7a9d6221.png)

当然，近年来商业重心向中国转移是惊人的。与拉丁美洲的货物贸易从2000年的略高于140亿美元增长至2024年的逾5000亿美元。中国已成为南美最大贸易伙伴（拉美洞察：特朗普的“门罗主义”遇上中国的影响力），即便华盛顿在墨西哥、中美洲和加勒比大部分地区仍占主导地位。

尽管北京对美国意图大加抨击，但中国并未因此从拉美退缩。但这并不是说中国无视美国的关切。

据驻北京的政治风险分析师、来自厄瓜多尔的克里斯蒂安·雷耶斯称，中国正变得更加谨慎，保护现有资产，并在寻求新机会时更具适应性。这需要从更易暴露风险的政府间交易转向私营部门渠道。

它也在摆脱引人注目的基础设施项目，转而通过北京已苦心经营数十年的行业中的低调交易推进。

位于巴西亚马孙地区、距州首府马瑙斯以北约200英里的皮廷加锡矿显示，中国如何继续深化其关键矿产——全球洞察：中国稀土掌控难以打破（1）——通过将自身定位在技术性强、偏远且更难成为外交争端点的资产上。

2024年11月，当秘鲁的Minsur [同意](https://www.mtaboca.com.br/paginas/detalhes-noticia.aspx?ID=30&amp%3Btitulo=Minera%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20Taboca%20anuncia%20acordo%20de%20venda)出售 [Mineracao Taboca](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/3008535Z:BZ)时，中国国有企业CNMC出价高于竞争对手，以约3.4亿美元收购该公司。除了皮廷加的锡业务外，该公司还获得了获取更广泛战略矿产和稀土元素的潜在渠道。

一位评估过Mineracao Taboca的巴西投资者（因讨论机密交易谈判而要求匿名）表示，按常规条件来看，该资产缺乏吸引力，称其为一个接近盈亏平衡的锡业务，负有重大的环境责任，需要巨额开支且回报前景有限。

CNMC愿意承担风险。1月，该矿商宣布了一项1亿美元的计划，以现代化运营并扩大生产，从而加深了对Pitinga的押注。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481d88618ebaee3c801b0/3c681cc9-0b14-8174-8045-f084b6951121-0ec34314fe819df8d3d8bcb1c9ac0a62.png)

相比之下，据美洲对话组织亚洲与拉丁美洲项目高级顾问Margaret Myers称，特朗普政府缺乏对该地区的连贯经济参与战略。

“中国在该地区的影响力源于多种因素，尤其是贸易，”她说，“当然，领导人在做出政治决策时会考虑到其最大的贸易伙伴。”

中国在南美主要市场的电网、配电和发电资产中根基深厚。在智利，中国企业控制着[超过一半](https://www.tearline.mil/public_page/chinese-control-of-critical-energy-infrastructure-in-chile)的输配电；而在秘鲁首都利马，[两家中国国有企业](https://www.csis.org/analysis/power-moves-how-chinas-energy-investments-provide-durable-influence-south-america)目前为该市1000万居民供电。

随着人工智能、数据中心以及交通和工业电气化推动全球电力竞争加剧，这正变得更加具有战略意义。中国最近成立了[世界人工智能合作组织](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/china-s-ai-ascendance-gives-xi-a-stage-and-a-security-dilemma)，巴西、委内瑞拉、尼加拉瓜和古巴是其29个创始成员国之一。

![Two Chinese state-owned firms now distribute electricity to Lima’s 10 million residents. Photographer: Christian Vinces/iStockphoto/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481d88618ebaee3c801b0/3c681cc9-0b14-8175-bce7-e271c50028f7-f03105585990d36a7e9ce2602b808eb0.png)

两家中国国有企业现在为利马的1000万居民供电。摄影师：Christian Vinces/iStockphoto/Getty Images

在美国将重心缩小到所谓的战略资产和关键基础设施之际，中国正向华盛顿较少关注的其他领域多元化发展，如电池、金融服务和消费品。在拉美实力雄厚的制药、航空航天和机械也是重点。与此同时，中国汽车制造商按价值计算约占该地区市场20%的份额。

特朗普的保护主义转向在[巴西产生了意想不到的效果](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-20/brazil-election-lula-seen-as-frontrunner-over-flavio-bolsonaro)，增强了中国在拉美最大经济体的吸引力，巴西是该地区两个抵制特朗普施压的主要国家之一，另一个是墨西哥。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481d88618ebaee3c801b0/3c681cc9-0b14-812a-9251-c6a5c0c0e99a-aa90359a33d39c5879a48030d701ce75.png)

中国的推进往往可以刻意低调，但它向该地区各消费经济体的进军却声势浩大、丰富多彩且越来越难以回避。

在巴西，比亚迪在每个州、每个首府和主要城市都设有经销店，并投入巨资赞助黄金时段电视节目、电视剧和足球赛事。巴西人的手机正变得越来越中国化：在线购物有Shein、AliExpress、Temu和TikTok Shop，网约车有滴滴的99，外卖有99Food和美团的Keeta。

中国还通过赠送礼物来换取好感。在纳伊布·布克尔的萨尔瓦多首都圣萨尔瓦多，一座崭新的国家图书馆在总统府附近拔地而起，起重机正在施工建设一座5万座的足球场，工人们也在为附近的会展中心平整场地——这些全部是中国捐赠的。

![The new National Library of El Salvador (BINAES) in San Salvador was built with the support of donations from China. Photographer: Carlos Barrera/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c681cc90b1481d88618ebaee3c801b0/3c681cc9-0b14-8126-9cfc-c2dad525e878-7f83502aabda707323cc9270ef4eb0e0.png)

位于圣萨尔瓦多的萨尔瓦多新国家图书馆（BINAES）是在中国捐赠支持下建成的。摄影师：卡洛斯·巴雷拉/彭博社

美国主导的[美洲之盾](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/hegseth-says-colombia-approves-joint-military-operations-with-us)联盟——其成员包括阿根廷、玻利维亚、智利、厄瓜多尔、萨尔瓦多、圭亚那和巴拿马——对各国政府具有吸引力，是加深与华盛顿地区安全合作的工具。但所有成员仍依赖中国的贸易、融资或投资。

中国企业胜过本土生产商仍有可能引发不满情绪。

墨西哥政府批准对来自中国等未签署自由贸易协定的国家的1400多个产品类别征收最高达50%的关税。前墨西哥驻华大使豪尔赫·瓜哈尔多表示，此举反映了墨西哥产业界[要求采取行动](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/mexico-weighs-tougher-trade-rules-for-china-as-us-talks-grind-on)的呼声，而非美国的压力。

“该地区对这一去工业化威胁反应迟缓，”瓜哈尔多说，“而美国很快就明白了。”

秘鲁驻华大使卡洛斯·巴斯克斯表示，如果被迫疏远与中国的关系，拉丁美洲国家最终将面临“几乎难以承受的经济成本”。

他说：“为了维护我们的国家利益和人民福祉，我们不得不保持平衡。”如果完全与任何一个超级大国结盟，“你就会输。”

## Trump Fails to Loosen China’s Economic Grip on Latin America

By Mie Dahl and Colum MurphyAugust 24, 2026 at 5:00 PM GMT+8

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

- Andrés Santamaría Garrido describes Beijing's commercial diplomacy as aggressive, practical — and hard to ignore, with Chinese firms being cheaper, more persistent and easier to work with.
- China's economic weight makes it irreplaceable, regardless of the US's increasingly coercive posture, with China's investment profile across Latin America remaining too big and entrenched for the region's governments to simply cast off.
- Latin American countries face almost unbearable economic costs if compelled to back away from their relationship with China, and are forced to maintain a balance for the benefit of their national interests and their people.
As head of an association representing cities across Colombia, Andrés Santamaría Garrido has a front-row seat to observe [China’s economic playbook](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-02-10/us-china-trump-s-focus-on-west-hands-xi-economic-edge-in-asia) at municipal level.

Sitting in his downtown Bogota office containing a model of the city’s red metro train, a project being built by a Chinese-led consortium, Santamaría described Beijing’s commercial diplomacy as aggressive, practical — and hard to ignore.

Chinese firms are cheaper, more persistent and easier to work with, he said. They’re also willing to go around national governments to deal with governors, mayors, local business groups and municipal officials. The result, said the executive director of *Asocapitales*, is that they are increasingly [leaving their US counterparts behind](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2023-08-09/ford-shuts-brazil-electric-car-factory-as-lula-turns-to-china-s-byd).

The first line of the Bogota Metro, built by a Chinese-led consortium, under construction in Bogota. Photographer: Sergio Acero/AFP/Getty Images

“There will be a technological opening to China, because they are simply more advanced,” said Santamaría, listing autonomous vehicles, robotics and drones as particular areas of Chinese expertise.

Santamaría’s experience gets to the heart of a dilemma for Donald Trump and his plans to extend US control over the Western Hemisphere. While the president can point to [significant inroads](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-01-15/trump-backs-argentina-ally-with-us-capital-as-he-confronts-venezuela-with-force) in securing US political influence through a string of aligned leaders from Argentina to Peru, his drive to [establish American economic dominance](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-23/trump-takes-anti-china-cursade-to-chile-ahead-of-latin-america-summit-in-miami) is proving far harder to achieve.

One reason is that China’s investment profile from ports to energy and mining, built over years across Latin America, remains too big and entrenched for the region’s governments to simply cast off on a whim of the US administration. At the same time, Beijing has managed to stay several steps ahead of Washington by moving up the value chain into fields such as electric vehicles, batteries and data centers.

It’s why Ecuador’s President Daniel Noboa, a Trump ally who’s all-in on [the security partnership](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-04-08/ecuador-s-daniel-noboa-counts-on-us-security-alliance-to-achieve-law-and-order) with the US, has just wrapped up a weeklong visit to China, including a meeting with President Xi Jinping. Noboa China to Support Ecuador in Solar Power, Digital Transformation to expand solar-power generation and support for Ecuador’s response to the El Niño climate phenomenon via a Chinese AI-driven meteorological system.

China’s President Xi Jinping, right, shakes hands with Ecuador’s President Daniel Noboa after a signing ceremony at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing, on Aug 18. Photographer: Andres Martinez Casares/Getty Images

It’s an illustration of how, for all the US political pressure, the economic dimension of Trump’s so-called Donroe Doctrine remains unfulfilled.

Economic ties with Beijing have in general “not been affected” by Trump’s plans for hemispheric hegemony, said Mexico’s Ambassador to China Jesús Seade. “That all remains in the political sphere,” he said, while highlighting China’s influence in trade matters, technology, investment and innovation.

“For us, it is very important to protect the relationship with the United States,” Seade said in a video interview from Beijing. “But we don’t do that by picking fights with China.”

### **Donroe Doctrine**

The push to curb Beijing’s dominance over strategic assets has always been at the core of Trump’s version of the Monroe Doctrine. In his [inaugural second-term address](https://www.whitehouse.gov/remarks/2025/01/the-inaugural-address/), Trump claimed that China was operating the Panama Canal and asserted that the US was “taking it back.” Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth followed up by [saying](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2025/04/sunday-shows-president-trumps-america-first-trade-policies-in-action/) that “Chinese influence cannot control our own backyard.”

The 33-page US [National Security Strategy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-National-Security-Strategy.pdf) released in November codified the doctrine in a section devoted to the Western Hemisphere laying out the administration’s goal to “restore American preeminence.” It talks of cultivating “regional champions” who share its policy priorities on migration and crime, while shutting out “non-Hemispheric competitors.”

Trump can point to some clear political successes for his agenda, from grabbing Nicolás Maduro out of Venezuela and isolating Cuba to shoring up President Javier Milei with a $20 billion currency swap immediately before Argentina’s midterms. In little more than a year, seven Latin American countries have held presidential elections with Trump-supporting candidates winning all of them, most recently in Colombia.

### The Chancay megaport in Chancay, Peru. Photographer: Marco Garro/Bloomberg

But beyond challenging a handful of flagship Chinese projects, including Hong Kong-based CK Hutchison’s presence on the Panama Canal and the $1.3 billion [megaport of Chancay](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trade-war-brazil-steel-billionaires-take-fight-with-china-to-peru) in Peru, US efforts to curb China’s economic expansion have had limited success.

“China’s economic weight makes it irreplaceable, regardless of the US’s increasingly coercive posture,” said Cui Shoujun, deputy director at the Institute of International Development Studies at Renmin University, which has close ties to China’s Communist Party.

The recent wave of Trump-aligned leaders has generated pro-US rhetoric, but “economic realities have consistently neutralized ideological shifts,” he said, contrasting Trump’s pressure tactics to China’s “mutually beneficial” approach to the region.

Polling appears to bear that out.

Certainly, the commercial shift toward China of recent years has been dramatic. Goods trade with Latin America has grown from just over $14 billion in 2000 to more than $500 billion in 2024. While Beijing has railed against US intentions, China isn’t retreating from Latin America in response. But that’s not to say that China is ignoring US concerns.

It’s becoming more cautious, protective of existing assets, and adaptive in how it pursues new opportunities, according to Christian Reyes, a Beijing-based political risk analyst originally from Ecuador. That entails a shift from more exposed government-to-government deals and toward private-sector channels.

It’s also moving away from headline-grabbing infrastructure projects to advancing through quieter deals in sectors Beijing has spent decades cultivating.

When Peru’s Minsur [agreed](https://www.mtaboca.com.br/paginas/detalhes-noticia.aspx?ID=30&amp%3Btitulo=Minera%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20Taboca%20anuncia%20acordo%20de%20venda) to sell [Mineracao Taboca](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/3008535Z:BZ) in November 2024, Chinese state entity CNMC outbid rivals, paying about $340 million for the company. Along with tin operations at Pitinga, the company gained potential access to a broader set of strategic minerals and rare-earth elements.

One Brazilian investor who evaluated Mineracao Taboca and asked not to be identified discussing confidential deal talks said the asset looked unattractive on conventional terms, describing it as a tin business close to break-even, with significant environmental liabilities, heavy spending needs and limited return prospects.

CNMC was willing to take the risk. In January, the miner deepened its bet on Pitinga with the announcement of a $100 million plan to modernize operations and expand production.

By contrast, the Trump administration lacks a coherent economic engagement strategy for the region, according to Margaret Myers, senior adviser to the Asia and Latin America Program at the Inter-American Dialogue.

“China’s influence in the region derives from a wide set of factors, especially trade,” she said. “Of course leaders make political decisions with their top trade partner in mind.”

China’s foothold is deep in electricity grids, distribution and generation assets in major South American markets. In Chile, Chinese companies control [more than half](https://www.tearline.mil/public_page/chinese-control-of-critical-energy-infrastructure-in-chile) of power transmission and distribution, while in Peru’s capital, Lima, [two Chinese state-owned firms](https://www.csis.org/analysis/power-moves-how-chinas-energy-investments-provide-durable-influence-south-america) now distribute electricity to the capital’s 10 million residents.

That’s becoming more of a strategic presence as the global race for power intensifies, driven by artificial intelligence, data centers and the electrification of transport and industry. China recently set up the [World AI Cooperation Organization](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/china-s-ai-ascendance-gives-xi-a-stage-and-a-security-dilemma), with Brazil, Venezuela, Nicaragua and Cuba among its 29 founding members.

Two Chinese state-owned firms now distribute electricity to Lima’s 10 million residents. Photographer: Christian Vinces/iStockphoto/Getty Images

While the US is narrowing its focus to what it calls strategic assets and critical infrastructure, China is diversifying into other sectors Washington has paid less attention to, like batteries, financial services and consumer products. Pharmaceuticals, aerospace and machinery which are all strong in Latin America are also a focus. Chinese automakers meanwhile account for some 20% of the region’s market in value terms.

Trump’s protectionist turn has had an [unintended effect in Brazil](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-20/brazil-election-lula-seen-as-frontrunner-over-flavio-bolsonaro), bolstering China’s appeal in Latin America’s largest economy and one of two main regional holdouts — along with Mexico — against his pressure to fall in line.

Where China’s advance can often be deliberately quiet, its push into consumer economies across the region is loud, colorful and increasingly hard to avoid.

In Brazil, BYD, which has a dealership in every state, capital and major city, has poured money into prime-time television, telenovelas and soccer sponsorships. Brazilians’ phones are looking increasingly Chinese: Shein, AliExpress, Temu and TikTok Shop in online shopping, Didi’s 99 in ride-hailing, and 99Food and Meituan’s Keeta in food delivery.

China also charms its way in through gifts. In San Salvador, the capital of Nayib Bukele’s El Salvador, a gleaming national library rises near the presidential palace, while cranes work on a 50,000-seat soccer stadium and crews prepare the ground for a nearby convention center — all Chinese donations.

The new National Library of El Salvador (BINAES) in San Salvador was built with the support of donations from China. Photographer: Carlos Barrera/Bloomberg

The US-led [Shield of the Americas](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/hegseth-says-colombia-approves-joint-military-operations-with-us) coalition — whose members include Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guyana and Panama — is attractive to governments as a vehicle for deeper regional security cooperation with Washington. Yet all its members continue to depend on Chinese trade, financing or investment.

There is still a risk of resentment fueled by Chinese firms outcompeting domestic producers.

Mexico’s government approved tariffs of as much as 50% on more than 1,400 product categories from countries like China that are not party to free-trade agreements. Jorge Guajardo, a former Mexican ambassador to China, said the move reflected [demands for action](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/mexico-weighs-tougher-trade-rules-for-china-as-us-talks-grind-on) from Mexican industry rather than US pressure.

“The region has been slow to understand this deindustrialization threat,” Guajardo said. “The US understood it quickly.”

Ultimately, Latin American countries face “almost unbearable economic costs” if compelled to back away from their relationship with China, said Carlos Vasquez, Peru’s ambassador to China.

“We are forced to maintain a balance for the benefit of our national interests and our people,” he said. Align completely with either superpower, and “you’re going to lose.”
