### 中文译文

## 为什么中国平价AI让硅谷担忧

### [科技](https://www.bloomberg.com/technology)解读

*插图：Nico H. Brausch*

作者 [Saritha Rai](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASxkTfI9dW8/saritha-rai) 2026年8月18日 12:01 PM GMT+8

### **要点**

- 中国人工智能公司正在开发几乎可以与领先AI模型媲美的系统，且无需最强大的硬件，这挑战了美国的主导商业模式。
- 中国正在押注“开放权重”AI软件，即向开发者公开内部参数，供其分享、研究和调整，以促进AI在全国经济中的快速采用。
- 中国AI模型更低的成本基础和不断提升的性能，可能使美国巨头更难宣称其服务物有所值，并可能导致大量用户转向中国的替代品。
中国人工智能公司在财力上无法与美国竞争对手匹敌，美国政府又剥夺了他们训练AI模型所需的最尖端芯片。然而，中国正在侵蚀美国在该领域的领先地位。

中国的开发者，如[DeepSeek](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2544189D:CH)、[阿里巴巴集团控股有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9988%20HK%20Equity)和月之暗面（Moonshot），一直专注于设计能够在不需要最强大硬件的情况下，性能几乎与领先人工智能模型持平的系统。中国正在押注“开放权重”人工智能软件——即内部参数可供开发者共享、研究和调整——以此推动人工智能在全国经济中的快速应用。这一切都对美国以投资数十亿美元并让用户为最强大、专有的人工智能技术支付高额费用为基础的主导商业模式构成了挑战。

以下是关于中国人工智能以及它对OpenAI、[Anthropic PBC](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1892140D:US)和其他美国巨头构成多大威胁的须知信息。

### **效率**

美国实施的出口管制限制了中国获取美国设计的芯片，这些芯片比中国同类芯片快约20%，功耗低多达30%，因为它们能在每片硅片上集成更多晶体管。中国的科技界试图通过开发更加“压缩”的人工智能软件来克服这一障碍。这意味着在实现与表现最佳的美国人工智能模型相当的结果时，所需的计算步骤更少。

实现这一目标的一种方法是采用一种称为“专家混合”的技术。当用户向DeepSeek或阿里巴巴的Qwen等中国聊天机器人发出提示时，模型不需要调动其整个“神经网络”来生成响应（这一过程称为推理）。相反，它会激活称为“专家”的专门子网络，这些专家仅使用该软件可用计算容量的一小部分。

OpenAI和Anthropic等美国实验室的模型在单次交互中可处理的信息量以及响应的复杂程度方面往往具有优势。但为了实现这一点，它们会激活模型中更多的可用“神经元”，从而消耗更多的计算能力。据DeepSeek发布的性能数据显示，在其旗舰模型DeepSeek-V4-Pro中，大约3%的参数在任意给定时刻实际参与工作。同样，月之暗面的旗舰模型Kimi K3在运行时，同时使用的参数不足4%。
2025年1月下旬，在DeepSeek展示了新模型R1之后，全球科技股大幅下跌，该模型以看似美国人工智能聊天机器人开发成本的一小部分提供了相当的性能。如今，尽管OpenAI、Anthropic和谷歌仍提供世界上性能最佳的人工智能模型，但根据LiveBench的大语言模型排名，月之暗面的Kimi K3和阿里巴巴的Qwen 3.8 Max已跻身前八。而且它们的价格便宜得多：截至8月15日，OpenAI的GPT-5.6 Sol每百万输出标记（人工智能模型响应用户提示时处理的数据单位）收费30美元，是DeepSeek V4-Pro在高峰时段每百万标记3.96美元价格的约10倍。

Anthropic已[指控](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)DeepSeek、月之暗面以及另一家中国人工智能实验室MiniMax进行“工业规模的蒸馏攻击”——利用2.4万个欺诈账户非法提取其专有Claude模型的功能以获取优势。此后，Anthropic、OpenAI和谷歌一直在合作，试图[杜绝](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-06/openai-anthropic-google-unite-to-combat-model-copying-in-china)此类行为。DeepSeek、月之暗面和MiniMax未回复寻求评论的电子邮件。

### **开放架构**

新一代人工智能模型的工作原理是摄取人类记录的各种信息，以识别数据中的模式，并记录音节、词语、声音或像素之间最常见的关系。由此，它们构建出庞大的神经网络，能够解析书面提示语的组成部分，并根据训练数据返回最有可能的有用回应。

领先的美国平台不会公布ChatGPT、Claude或Gemini等人工智能模型的参数，因此第三方人工智能开发者要么无法复制这些模型，要么需要为此付费。虽然中国人工智能公司对用于训练系统的数据同样可能守口如瓶，但许多公司已经将模型训练过程中被赋予数值并不断调整的参数——即所谓的权重——开放下载。这使得大学、初创企业和规模较小的科技公司能够在这些模型的基础上进行构建和调整，以便更好地执行特定任务，且无需额外费用。

这种方法实际上是以众包形式开展国家人工智能研发，降低了维护和更新模型的成本。此外，模型灵活性的增强促进了人工智能在各行各业的采用。

DeepSeek发布R1模型权重后，该模型迅速得到应用。开发者已针对金融、医疗和中文任务制作了R1的多个版本。阿里巴巴的Qwen系列人工智能模型[下载量已突破](https://x.com/alibaba_cloud/status/2014213940100595726)30亿次，成为下载量最广的开源人工智能模型系列，超过了马克·扎克伯格旗下[Meta平台公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/META%20US%20Equity)的Llama平台。阿里巴巴表示，Qwen已在全球催生了超过30万个衍生人工智能模型。

中国地方政府一直在资助开放权重模型，并鼓励涌现出托管数千个模型和数据集的人工智能社区，这些模型和数据集作为公共资产，可供当地企业共享使用。中国政府将这一旨在扩大技术在经济中采用（即使这会削减个别科技公司的利润）的方法视为软实力工具。中国政府还积极支持诸如2025年7月《全球人工智能治理行动计划》等倡议，这些倡议旨在让低收入国家的软件开发人员更容易获得人工智能模型。

### **能源**

由于训练和运行人工智能服务消耗大量电力，它们的快速应用正在给全球能源网络带来压力。在一些国家，安装所需的新电站、太阳能和风电场、输电线路、变电站和变压器来应对需求激增，正被证明是一项艰巨的任务。

这在中国已被证明不是什么大问题，因为中国的发电能力增长速度快于其他国家。在中国的某些地区，例如内蒙古，来自可再生能源的充足且廉价的电力，已经促使建设了数百个专门用于训练人工智能模型的数据中心。

中国政府正在对本土人工智能公司的电费设定上限，以使它们获得相对于外国竞争对手的竞争优势。国家为国内一些最大型的数据中心补贴一半的电费——条件是它们只使用中国芯片。

### **行业**

在世界大部分地区，人工智能的应用推广在商业服务领域最为强劲：自动化客户关系、简化业务流程，并在金融、法律和医疗保健等行业提高生产力。中国的国家主导方式则强调通过在商业、制造业和物流中嵌入人工智能来提升国家生产力。

这或许并不令人意外：美国经济主要以服务业为基础。中国是世界主要的制成品生产国，包括电动汽车和其他低碳技术，同时是电子商务强国和新兴的机器人技术领导者。根据国际机器人联合会的数据，近年来中国安装的新工业机器人占全球一半以上，因为中国制造商正在实现检测、装配和物流自动化。中国政府已明确表示，其目标不是打造规模最大、最富有的人工智能公司，而是利用人工智能获得技术优势，以加速经济发展。

美国公司和学术机构在推进人工智能基础技术的长期基础科学研究方面处于领先地位。中国的人工智能社区则更专注于积累已部署人工智能产生的大规模数据集，并将其反馈以改进模型。汽车制造商比亚迪已在其电动汽车装配线中嵌入人工智能，以缩短周期时间并减少材料浪费。它记录了电池故障减少40%，电池寿命提高20%，并将此[归功于](https://evmagazine.com/news/how-chinas-byd-is-using-ai-to-scale-global-ev-manufacturing-2025)基于人工智能的质量保证。

[苹果公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AAPL%20US%20Equity)的中国iPhone组装合作伙伴[富士康](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/601138%20CH%20Equity)已部署人工智能驱动的计算机视觉来检测缺陷并优化生产线的能源使用。该公司表示，这使部分工厂的能源消耗降低了两位数百分比，而通过人工智能进行的自动化质量检查[降低了缺陷率](https://www.foxconn.com/en-us/press-center/events/csr-events/533)。电子商务巨头阿里巴巴的人工智能系统目前处理大部分常规客服。

中国政府正在鼓励甚至为在制造业、物流和服务任务中部署人工智能机器人提供资金，以应对因劳动年龄人口缩减而日益严重的人力短缺。北京官员正谨慎推进，考虑到突然全面转向自动化可能导致大规模裁员并引发社会动荡的风险。中国一家法院[裁定](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-02/chinese-court-rules-firms-can-t-lay-off-workers-on-ai-grounds)，公司不能仅仅为了用人工智能系统取代员工而解雇员工。

### **国家战略**

即使中国的科技创业者想加入美国人工智能巨头为追逐巨额利润而展开的算力军备竞赛，也并非确信他们能够做到。中国国家主席习近平在2020年至2023年打击国内为所欲为的亿万富翁时，已明确表示他不赞同炫富。他希望国家的资本家服务于中国共产党的最高目标——稳定且创造就业的经济增长。

作为交换，习近平政府一直在打造一个全国性生态系统，为国内人工智能初创企业提供科学人才、廉价资本和商业机会。2017年的《新一代人工智能发展规划》协调了科研机构与私营部门的激励措施。大学负责推动研究，企业负责将成果商业化。

中国还正在[调动](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-09/china-bets-on-ai-stocks-as-it-races-against-us-for-chip-tech-dominance)其28万亿美元的股票和债券市场，以缩小美国在人工智能领域最大的优势之一——获取资本——，加快科技企业IPO，鼓励债券发行，并将长期资金引向具有战略意义的重要企业。

中国最新的国家级人工智能计划“AI Plus”于2025年启动，旨在将该技术嵌入经济和社会各个领域。小学生正在学习编程和组装人工智能模型。地方政府已建设补贴性人工智能园区，企业可获得资本、便捷获取丰富的模型训练数据以及有保障的采购机会。数家中国人工智能初创企业由顶尖大学的教授或校友在科学突破后创立，并迅速被纳入政府轨道。人工智能开发商[知识图谱科技股份有限公司（智谱AI）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2513%20HK%20Equity)由清华大学教授唐杰和李涓子于2019年创立，并被鼓励与国家目标保持一致。该公司获得了政府客户以及北京中关村科学城创新发展和成都地区政府等国家基金的支持，并受益于国家支持的算力资源和其他有利于国内人工智能领军企业的政策。

据彭博行业研究12月发布的深度报告，所有这些政府支持已迫使中国人工智能公司与国家要务保持一致，因此该技术的大规模部署将优先于利润。

### **拓展海外**

北京政府将中国可嵌入各行业的开放权重模型视为国家资产，类似于公路、电网或电信网络。这一资产可出口到其他国家，以促进对中国技术的依赖。

中美AI平台正在东南亚、中东和非洲地区竞争，在这些地区，中国实体获取政府、企业或个人数据的可能性所引发的担忧少于西方国家。

中国科技巨头阿里巴巴、[华为技术有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH)和[腾讯控股有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity)正在这些快速增长的市场中为其云平台寻求客户。他们的计划是通过提供客户开始使用中国AI所需的一切来削弱美国竞争对手：平台本身（如Qwen或DeepSeek）、低成本的中国云计算、互联网连接以及必要的硬件。他们还调整了中国AI模型以适应本地语言并遵守国家法规。中国政府则通过“一带一路”倡议及其他项目向其中许多国家提供融资，为这些企业铺平了道路。

这一做法似乎正在奏效：根据AI模型市场OpenRouter的数据，2025年11月，中国生成式AI模型约占全球市场份额的15%，而一年前仅为约1%。

这一成功正在激励一些美国公司进行调整：8月初，Meta [推出](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/meta-releases-muse-glimmer-ai-model-people-can-run-on-their-laptop)了一款名为Muse Glimmer的新可下载AI模型，可在个人电脑上运行。该模型的权重将公开发布。“我们的目标应该是让美国开源模型成为全球最佳，”扎克伯格在随公告发布的一篇文章中表示。

### **对美国AI的威胁？**

目前，中国大型企业进入美国和欧洲市场的机会相当有限。与数据隐私和国家安全相关的规则和法规，使得那里的客户——尤其是政府实体和大公司——被劝阻使用中国云服务。

这并没有阻止美国大型云服务提供商，如[微软公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MSFT%20US%20Equity)和[亚马逊公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AMZN%20US%20Equity)，向客户提供中国AI模型，并在美国及中国以外的其他地方的AI服务器上托管这些模型。由于中国平台采用开放权重方法，它们被提供为美国模型更灵活、适应性更强的替代品。而且由于它们在响应用户提示时往往使用较少的处理能力，它们可能便宜得多。

中国和美国的人工智能公司都在为运行其平台所需的计算能力投入巨资。它们从阿里云、谷歌、亚马逊网络服务或微软等云运营商处购买这种数据处理能力作为服务。对人工智能基础设施的巨额投资基于这样的预期：这些平台将逐渐成为工作生活中不可或缺的一部分，从而使公司能够大幅提高向用户收取的费用。目前，没有人能确定这笔收入何时甚至能否开始覆盖所有支出。

对于MiniMax等中国人工智能公司来说也是如此——它们正烧掉大量现金向阿里巴巴支付计算能力费用——OpenAI、Anthropic以及其他出手阔绰的美国玩家也是如此。阿里巴巴今年[发布了](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-02/alibaba-unveils-third-closed-source-ai-model-in-focus-on-profit)多个封闭专有的人工智能平台，同时还有其开放权重模型，这可能表明中国玩家也感受到了提高人工智能投资回报的压力。从8月16日起，DeepSeek将[提高](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/deepseek-increases-prices-for-ai-services-by-multiple-times)其旗舰V4模型的价格，此前该公司可能进行首次公开募股。新的高峰时段定价将比当前水平高出四倍以上。

在8月5日发布的通知中，DeepSeek[宣布](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/deepseek-plans-significant-price-increase-for-its-ai-services)将对其人工智能服务实施大幅涨价。据彭博[报道](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/deepseek-mulls-new-funding-weeks-after-7-billion-round-ft-says)，该公司正处于大规模融资推进过程中，并已开始筹备最早于今年进行的首次公开募股。

美国人工智能投资者担心的是，中国模型较低的成本基础和不断提升的性能将使美国巨头更难声称其服务应获得高价，而且西方市场的大量用户将搁置对数据安全的疑虑，[选择](https://sifted.eu/articles/europe-startups-use-deepseek-news)中国替代产品。

“美国盟国不会以任何官方方式使用DeepSeek，”彭博智库高级分析师Robert Lea表示。“但在这些注重成本的时代，如果DeepSeek提供了GPT 90%的功能，那么消费者可能会与政府持不同看法。”

## Why China’s Affordable AI Is a Worry for Silicon Valley

### [Technology](https://www.bloomberg.com/technology)Explainer

*Illustration: Nico H. Brausch*

By [Saritha Rai](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASxkTfI9dW8/saritha-rai)August 18, 2026 at 12:01 PM GMT+8

### **Takeaways**

- Chinese artificial intelligence companies are developing systems that can perform almost as well as leading AI models without needing the most powerful hardware, challenging the dominant US business model.
- China is making a bet on "open-weight" AI software, where internal parameters are made available for developers to share, study, and tweak, to foster rapid adoption of AI across the national economy.
- The lower cost base and improving performance of Chinese AI models may make it harder for US giants to claim their services justify premium prices, and could lead to significant numbers of users opting for Chinese alternatives.
Chinese artificial intelligence companies can’t match the financial firepower of their American rivals, and the US government has deprived them of the most cutting-edge chips to train their AI models. Yet China is encroaching on US leadership in the field.

Chinese developers such as [DeepSeek](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2544189D:CH), [Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9988%20HK%20Equity) and Moonshot have focused on devising systems that perform almost on a par with the leading AI models without needing the most powerful hardware. And China is making a bet on “open-weight” AI software — where the internal parameters are made available for developers to share, study and tweak — as a way to foster rapid adoption of AI across the national economy. All this challenges the dominant US business model that’s predicated on investing billions and getting users paying top dollar for the most powerful, proprietary AI technology.

Here’s what to know about Chinese AI and how much of a threat it poses to OpenAI, [Anthropic PBC](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1892140D:US) and other US giants.

### **Efficiency**

Export controls imposed by Washington restrict China’s access to US-designed chips that are around 20% faster and consume as much as 30% less power than their Chinese competitors as they squeeze more transistors onto each sheet of silicon. China’s tech community has sought to overcome this handicap by developing AI software that is more “compressed.” This means fewer steps of calculation are required to achieve outputs comparable to those of the best-performing US AI models.

One way this is achieved is by employing a technique known as mixture of experts. When a user prompts a Chinese chatbot such as DeepSeek or Alibaba’s Qwen, the model doesn’t need to mobilize its entire “neural network” to generate a response (the process known as inference). Instead, it activates specialized subnetworks known as experts that engage only a fraction of the software’s available computational capacity.

The models of US labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic often have the edge when it comes to the sheer volume of information they can handle in a single interaction and the sophistication of their responses. But to achieve this, they activate far more of a model’s available “neurons” and thereby consume a lot more computing power. With DeepSeek’s flagship model, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, about 3% of its parameters are actually doing work at any given moment, according to performance data released by the company. Likewise, Moonshot’s flagship, Kimi K3, operates with less than 4% of its parameters at a time.
Global technology stocks tumbled in late January 2025 after DeepSeek demonstrated a new model, R1, that offered comparable performance to US AI chatbots at seemingly a fraction of their development cost. Today, while OpenAI, Anthropic and Google still offer the world’s best-performing AI models, Moonshot’s Kimi K3 and Alibaba’s Qwen 3.8 Max now feature among the top eight, according to LiveBench’s large language model rankings. And they are far cheaper: OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol costs $30 per million output tokens (the units of data processed by AI models in response to a user prompt) as of Aug. 15 — around 10 times more than DeepSeek’s V4-Pro, at $3.96 per million tokens during peak hours.

Anthropic has [accused](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks) DeepSeek, Moonshot and another Chinese AI lab, MiniMax, of “industrial-scale distillation attacks” — illegally extracting capabilities from its proprietary Claude model using 24,000 fraudulent accounts to gain an edge. Since then, Anthropic, OpenAI and Google have been collaborating to try to [stamp out](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-06/openai-anthropic-google-unite-to-combat-model-copying-in-china) such practices. DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMax did not respond to emails seeking comment.

### **Open architecture**

The new crop of AI models work by ingesting all kinds of information recorded by humans to spot patterns in the data and record the most common relationships between syllables, words, sounds or pixels. From this, they build a vast neural network that can break down the components of a written prompt and send back the most probable useful response based on the training data.

The leading US platforms don’t publish the parameters of AI models such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, so third-party AI developers are either unable to replicate them or they need to pay for that privilege. While Chinese AI companies can be just as secretive about what data they’ve used to train their systems, many have made the parameters assigned as numerical values and tweaked during the training of their AI models — known as weights — available for download. This allows universities, startups and smaller tech firms to build upon and adjust the models so they can better perform specific tasks at no extra cost.

This approach effectively crowdsources national AI research and development, lowering the cost of maintaining and updating the models. And the added model flexibility eases the adoption of AI across a swath of industries.

The DeepSeek artificial intelligence logo. Photographer: Andrey Rudakov/Bloomberg

R1 saw rapid uptake after DeepSeek released the model’s weights. Developers have produced versions of R1 for finance, medicine and Chinese language tasks. Alibaba’s Qwen family of AI models [surpassed](https://x.com/alibaba_cloud/status/2014213940100595726) 3 billion downloads by mid-August, making it the most widely downloaded open-source AI model family, ahead of the Llama platform of Mark Zuckerberg’s [Meta Platforms Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/META%20US%20Equity) Alibaba said Qwen has spawned more than 300,000 derivative AI models worldwide.

Regional governments in China have been funding open-weight models and encouraging the emergence of AI communities that host thousands of models and datasets as communal assets that can be shared for the benefit of local companies. China’s government sees this approach, designed to broaden the technology’s adoption across the economy even if it cuts into the profits of individual tech firms, as a tool of soft power. It’s an active sponsor of initiatives such as the July 2025 Global AI Governance Action Plan that seek to make AI models more accessible to software developers in lower-income nations.

### **Energy**

As training and operating AI services consumes massive amounts of electricity, their rapid adoption is straining energy networks around the world. In some countries, it’s proving a struggle to install the new power stations, solar and wind farms, transmission lines, substations and transformers required to handle the surge in demand.

This is proving to be less of a problem in China, which has been adding generation capacity faster than other countries. In some Chinese regions, such as Inner Mongolia, abundant and cheap electricity from renewable sources has led to the construction of hundreds of data centers dedicated to training AI models.

China’s government is capping electricity bills for its AI companies to give them a competitive advantage over foreign rivals. The state is subsidizing half of the electricity costs of some of the country’s biggest data centers — as long as they use solely Chinese chips.

A mock-up of an AI data center on display at the SK Hynix booth at the China International Import Expo (CIIE) in Shanghai on Nov. 6. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg

### **Industry**

In much of the world, the push for AI adoption is most intense in business services: automating customer relations, streamlining business workflows and accelerating productivity in sectors such as finance, law and healthcare. China’s state-directed approach places the emphasis on raising national productivity by embedding AI in commerce, manufacturing and logistics.

That’s perhaps not a surprise: The US economy is largely services-based. China is the world’s dominant producer of manufactured goods, including electric vehicles and other low-carbon technologies, an e-commerce powerhouse and an emerging leader in robotics. China installed more than half of the world’s new industrial robots in recent years, according to the International Federation of Robotics, as Chinese manufacturers automated inspection, assembly and logistics. The government has made clear its goal is not to create the biggest, wealthiest AI companies, but to use AI to gain a technological edge to accelerate economic development.

US companies and academic institutions lead in the basic, long-term scientific work aimed at advancing the foundational technology behind AI. China’s AI community is more focused on accumulating massive data sets from deployed AI that can be fed back to improve models. Carmaker BYD has embedded AI in its EV assembly lines to shorten cycle times and reduce material waste. It’s documented a 40% decrease in battery faults and a 20% improvement in battery longevity that it [attributed](https://evmagazine.com/news/how-chinas-byd-is-using-ai-to-scale-global-ev-manufacturing-2025) to AI-enabled quality assurance.

[Apple Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AAPL%20US%20Equity)’s Chinese iPhone assembly partner, [Foxconn](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/601138%20CH%20Equity), has deployed AI-driven computer vision to detect defects and optimize energy use on production lines. It said this resulted in a double-digit percentage reduction in energy consumption at some sites, while automated quality inspections carried out with AI [cut defect rates](https://www.foxconn.com/en-us/press-center/events/csr-events/533). E-commerce giant Alibaba’s AI systems now handle most routine customer service.

The government is encouraging, and even providing funding for, the deployment of AI-enabled robots in manufacturing, logistics and service tasks as a way to overcome growing manpower shortages due to a shrinking working-age population. Officials in Beijing are proceeding with caution, given the risk that a sudden, wholesale shift to automation might lead to mass layoffs and cause a wave of social unrest. A Chinese court [ruled](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-02/chinese-court-rules-firms-can-t-lay-off-workers-on-ai-grounds) that companies cannot terminate employees just to replace them with artificial intelligence systems.

### **National Strategy**

Even if China’s tech entrepreneurs wanted to join the computing-power arms race being pursued by the American AI giants in search of giant profits, it’s not certain that they could. Chinese President Xi Jinping already made clear his disapproval of ostentatious wealth when he cracked down on the country’s free-wheeling billionaires from 2020 to 2023. He wants the nation’s capitalists to serve the Chinese Communist Party’s overriding objective — steady, job-creating economic growth.

In exchange, Xi’s government has been creating a national ecosystem to supply the country’s AI startups with scientific talent, cheap capital and commercial opportunities. A 2017 New Generation AI Development Plan aligned incentives across scientific institutions and the private sector. Universities would drive research and companies would commercialize their breakthroughs.

China is also [mobilizing](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-09/china-bets-on-ai-stocks-as-it-races-against-us-for-chip-tech-dominance) its $28 trillion stock and bond markets to narrow one of America’s biggest advantages in AI — access to capital — fast-tracking tech IPOs, encouraging bond issuance and steering long-term funding toward strategically important companies.

The country’s latest state AI initiative, AI Plus, launched in 2025, aims to embed the technology across the economy and society. Primary-school children are learning to code and put together AI models. Local governments have built subsidized AI parks, and firms receive capital, easy access to a wealth of data for model training and guaranteed procurement opportunities. Several Chinese AI startups were founded by professors or alumni of elite universities following scientific breakthroughs and then quickly brought into the government’s orbit. AI developer [Knowledge Atlas Technology JSC Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2513%20HK%20Equity), or Zhipu AI, was founded by Tsinghua professors Tang Jie and Li Juanzi in 2019 and was encouraged to align with national goals. It secured government customers and backing from state funds such as Beijing Zhongguancun Science City Innovation Development and the Chengdu regional government, and benefited from state-supported computing resources and other policies favoring domestic AI champions.

All that state backing has compelled Chinese AI companies to align with national imperatives, so wide-scale deployment of the technology is set to take precedence over profits, according to a Bloomberg Intelligence deep dive report published in December.

### **Expanding abroad**

The government in Beijing views Chinese open-weight models that can be embedded across industries as a national asset, akin to roads, power grids or telecommunication networks. This asset can be exported to other countries to foster dependence on Chinese technology.

Chinese and US AI platforms are competing in regions of Southeast Asia, the Middle East and Africa where the possibility of Chinese entities accessing government, corporate or personal data stirs fewer concerns than in Western countries.

Chinese tech giants Alibaba, [Huawei Technologies Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH) and [Tencent Holdings Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity) are seeking customers for their own cloud platforms in some of these fast-growing markets. Their plan is to undercut US rivals there by offering everything a customer needs to start using Chinese AI: the platform itself, such as Qwen or DeepSeek; low-cost, China-based cloud computing; an internet connection; and the necessary hardware. They’ve adapted Chinese AI models to handle local languages and adhere to national regulations. And the Chinese government has sought to smooth their path by extending financing to many of those countries under its Belt and Road Initiative and other programs.

The approach appears to be working: Chinese generative AI models accounted for about 15% of global market share in November 2025, up from around 1% a year earlier, according to AI model marketplace OpenRouter.

That success is inspiring some US companies to adapt: In early August, Meta [introduced](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/meta-releases-muse-glimmer-ai-model-people-can-run-on-their-laptop) a new, downloadable AI model called Muse Glimmer that can run on a personal computer. The model’s weights will be made public. “Our goal should be for American open source models to be the best globally,” Zuckerberg said in an essay accompanying the announcement.

### **Threat to American AI?**

For now, the big Chinese players’ access to the US and European markets is fairly limited. Rules and regulations to do with data privacy and national security mean customers there — especially government entities and large corporations — are discouraged from using Chinese cloud computing services.

This hasn’t stopped big US cloud providers such as [Microsoft Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MSFT%20US%20Equity) and [Amazon.com Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/AMZN%20US%20Equity) from offering Chinese AI models to their customers and hosting the models on AI servers within the US and elsewhere outside China. The Chinese platforms are being offered as a more flexible and adaptable alternative to US models because of their open-weight approach. And because they tend to use less processing power in responding to user prompts, they can be a lot cheaper.

Both Chinese and US AI companies are spending heavily on the computing power needed to run their platforms. They buy that data-crunching capacity as a service from cloud operators such as Alibaba, Google, Amazon Web Services or Microsoft. The huge investments being made in AI infrastructure are predicated on the expectation that these platforms will gradually become so indispensable to working life that the companies will be able to significantly increase the fees they charge users. For now, no one can say with any certainty when, or even if, that revenue will begin to cover all the spending.

That’s as much the case for Chinese AI companies such as MiniMax — which is burning through large amounts of cash to pay Alibaba for computing capacity — as it is for OpenAI, Anthropic and other big-spending American players. Alibaba this year [released](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-02/alibaba-unveils-third-closed-source-ai-model-in-focus-on-profit) several closed, proprietary AI platforms alongside its open-weight models, in a possible sign that Chinese players are also feeling the pressure to boost returns on their AI investments. Beginning Aug. 16, DeepSeek is [raising the prices](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/deepseek-increases-prices-for-ai-services-by-multiple-times) for its flagship V4 models ahead of a potential initial public offering. New peak-hour pricing will be more than four times higher than current levels.

In a notice posted on Aug. 5, DeepSeek [announced](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/deepseek-plans-significant-price-increase-for-its-ai-services) it will implement a significant price increase across its AI services. The company is in the middle of a massive fundraising push and has begun preparations for an initial public offering as soon as this year, Bloomberg [has reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/deepseek-mulls-new-funding-weeks-after-7-billion-round-ft-says).

The concern for investors in American AI is that the lower cost base and improving performance of Chinese models will make it harder for the US giants to claim their services justify premium prices, and that significant numbers of users in Western markets will put aside any misgivings over data security and [opt for](https://sifted.eu/articles/europe-startups-use-deepseek-news) the Chinese alternatives.

“US-allied countries are not going to be using DeepSeek in any official way,” said Robert Lea, a senior analyst at Bloomberg Intelligence. “But in these cost-conscious times, if DeepSeek offers 90% of the functionality of GPT, then consumers might take a different view to governments.”
