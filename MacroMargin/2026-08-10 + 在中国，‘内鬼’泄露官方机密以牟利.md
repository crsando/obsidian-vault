### 中文译文

## 在中国，‘内鬼’泄露官方机密以牟利

### 习近平的执法者正在追捕被指控泄露未宣布清洗行动的党内人士

Chun Han Wong

Aug. 9, 2026 10:00 pm ET

![Silhouette of a person with glasses against a red Communist Party of China flag.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b148161ba29f8577fbc1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-81a1-8ca3-c13a2f056600-fdaf903d21e530e3c2b915ed8077e02f.jpg)

中共当局对泄密行为表示警觉，同时试图追查泄密者。Kevin Frayer/Getty Images

- 中国当局正在打击共产党内部人员，他们在社交媒体上泄露即将进行的反腐清洗行动的秘密。
- 这些泄密帮助嫌疑人销毁证据、逃避逮捕，同时让外部人士得以把握时机进行股票交易。
- 中共在2023年处分了超过7800名行为不当的纪检人员，北京正寻求根除内部泄密。
- 中国当局正在打击在社交媒体上泄露有关即将到来的反腐清洗信息的共产党内部人士。
中国领导人习近平的纪律审查行动中的机密信息不断在网上浮出水面，这无视了一场已使数百万人陷入困境的打击行动——同时也凸显出腐败和不忠在共产党内部仍然根深蒂固。

据官方媒体报道，随着党每年拘留数以万计的官员，中国社交媒体上出现了一个关于腐败八卦的地下市场，神秘用户暗示谁将下一个被清洗——以换取费用和点击量。

此类爆料可以帮助嫌疑人逃脱指控或逃避逮捕，同时使外部人士能够进行时机精准的交易——这是中国版的对美国政治内部人士利用Kalshi和Polymarket等预测市场牟利的担忧。

党的领导机关在试图追查泄密者的同时，已对这一现象表示警惕。反腐调查的爆料也引发了对习近平打击行动有效性的质疑，自2012年底以来，已有超过700万人因受贿、违抗政策和泄露机密等不当行为受到纪律处分。

“少数官员违规泄露信息，”官方新华社主办的杂志《半月谈》在最近的一篇报道中说，“这些数据随后通过网络群组转卖——经过多人之手——从而形成一条隐蔽的非法供应链。”

报道称，利用此类泄露，一些社交媒体账号“已经通过发布简历或使用谐音和暗语等方式‘预言’政府官员的倒台”，这些方式暗示了嫌疑人的身份。这些隐晦的帖子旨在规避保密规则，因为官员的姓名和简历通常不被视为敏感信息。

据《半月谈》报道，一些牟利者通过出售社交媒体聊天室的访问权来将这些泄露信息货币化，聊天室中分享各种线索，费用从几十元到几百元不等，也就是从几美元到约120美元。

其他人则试图提高浏览量以赚取报酬。该杂志称：“有些人甚至夸大腐败细节、捏造丑闻来吸引流量。”

该杂志称，在一个案例中，调查人员发现南方城市贵阳的一名党内官员与六名亲属合谋，运营九个[微信](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/HK/XHKG/700)社交媒体账号，发布有关即将进行的晋升和清洗的暗示。

中国政治体系的不透明使得涉及正在进行的调查的线索极具价值。如果提前得到通知，嫌疑人及其同伙可以销毁证据、转移非法资产并逃跑。当局可能被迫过早拘留嫌疑人，从而削弱其法律案件。党内人士可以在游说即将出现的职位空缺时抢占先机。投资者可以做空可能受到即将到来的清洗影响的股票。

中国研究人员表示，腐败调查可能引发市场对被视为与嫌疑人有关联或受到行业整顿影响的公司进行抛售。中国最大的商业银行之一[招商银行](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/CN/XSHG/600036)在2022年该党宣布对银行前行长田惠宇进行调查后，[股价暴跌](https://www.wsj.com/articles/china-merchants-bank-dives-amid-watchdog-probe-into-ex-ceo-11650858685?mod=article_inline)。

一群中国学者在2021年的一项研究中写道：“腐败调查显著增加了被调查高级官员所在或曾任职省份的上市公司股价暴跌风险，尤其是在这些官员被调查期间。”

维也纳大学中国政治与法律讲师李玲（Ling Li）表示：“反腐败调查高度保密，鉴于反腐败在中国精英政治中的核心作用，出于政治好奇心、商业利益或风险管理的动机，对内部信息的需求持续存在。”

李玲表示：“选择性和过早地披露调查信息可能被用来向潜在行贿者发出信号，表明一些党内人士愿意用特权信息换取私利。”为了将风险降至最低，泄密者“仅限于发布高度暗示性的帖子，这些帖子本身并未越过任何明显的法律界限。”

中共长期以来一直在应对腐败的内部人员——包括反腐监察官员——他们泄露腐败调查的秘密以换取贿赂。2019年，中共重组了其最高纪律监督机构，以确保不同的监察小组分别负责日常监督、调查和内部审理，旨在防止任何人掌握可能被利用谋取私利的过度权力。

然而，北京方面仍不断抱怨其执法人员中存在“内鬼”和害群之马。2023年，中共对7800多名纪律监察官员实施了正式处分，这是习近平领导下报告的年度总数最高的一次。

尽管如此，[习近平拒绝](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/chinas-xi-is-resurrecting-maos-continuous-revolution-with-a-twist-ed5ec610?mod=article_inline)在官僚体系中提高透明度和采取更多制衡措施，一些专家表示，这些措施对于更廉洁的治理是必要的——但会削弱威权控制。

![Chinese President Xi Jinping attending the ceremony for the 105th anniversary of the Communist Party of China.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b148161ba29f8577fbc1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-81c2-b715-c18a20038715-41f7249fb2daaca98b5a25a4e9de1301.jpg)

习近平的清洗巩固了他作为中国几十年来最具主导力的领导人的权威。Maxim Shemetov/Reuters

北京方面对腐败调查泄漏的审查日趋严格，这与[美国类似调查](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/flurry-of-suspicious-oil-trades-worth-800-million-triggers-regulatory-probe-71e959ce?mod=article_inline)相呼应。在美国，监管机构正在审查在预测市场投注热潮中，利用特朗普政府内部信息[涉嫌牟利](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/white-house-teleprompter-operator-investigated-for-alleged-bets-on-trump-speeches-bc95eeff?mod=article_inline)的[行为](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/u-s-soldier-in-maduro-raid-is-charged-with-making-bets-on-leaders-ouster-535caf49?mod=article_inline)。

在《半月谈》报道之后，其他官方媒体纷纷谴责内部泄密并要求严厉打击——例如加强审查算法和严厉处罚。

四川省官方党报在一篇评论中表示：“反腐不能被利用，必须遏制‘炒作腐败’的乱象。”

习近平的清洗巩固了他作为[中国几十年来最具主导力的领导人](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/xi-jinping-leadership-tactics-china-318b3413?mod=article_inline)的权威。2012年习近平上台时发起反腐运动，此后已成为[一场长期运动](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/xis-enforcers-are-hunting-down-officials-who-consult-mystics-and-borrow-too-much-4f41a315?mod=article_inline)，让官员保持忠诚和警惕。中共[去年处分了98.3万人](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/xis-enforcers-punished-nearly-a-million-in-2025and-chinas-leader-wants-more-019e1c65?mod=article_inline)，是记录在案的最高年度总数。

在社交媒体上，即将到来的清洗的泄露往往以隐蔽形式出现。一些帖子使用与目标官员姓名发音相似的名称或短语，或直接发布该官员的简历。

2025年7月，一个可译为“洋澜湖知情者”的微信账号（指湖北省的一个地方）发布了一篇简短帖子，附有湖北省前副省长、曾任省会武汉市长周先旺的照片和简历。两天后，类似帖子出现在视频分享平台抖音（TikTok的中国版）上，发布者为来自东南省份广东的名为“广东子豪兄弟”的用户。

抖音帖子出现后的第二天，党的最高内部监督机构宣布，周因涉嫌严重不当行为正在接受调查。该党于1月以涉嫌腐败为由将周开除党籍，此后检察官已对其提起刑事诉讼。

“子豪兄弟”随后提供了更多线索，有些比正式公告提前数天甚至数周。11月下旬，该账号发布了一张湖南省退休资深官员叶红专的照片，并配有一个茶杯表情符号——似乎暗示叶被“请去喝茶”，这是审讯的委婉说法。大约一周后，当局宣布对叶展开调查。

然后在3月，“子豪兄弟”发布了隐晦的帖子，展示了三名官僚的姓名和照片——广东省湛江市的两名高级市政官员，以及南部省份贵州省一家国有支持的妇女联合会的前主席。在随后的几周内，党的当局宣布对每名官员进行调查。

用户称赞“子豪兄弟”的准确线索，其中最新的一条是在7月，涉及一名广东官员，几乎在宣布调查前两周。然而，当局认为这些泄露证明“内鬼”在兜售秘密以牟利。

该党遏制此类泄露的努力已成为一场猫捉老鼠的游戏。一名调查人员告诉《半月谈》，当局缺乏法律依据来调查或处罚那些仅仅在社交媒体上发布官员简历的人。当监管机构关闭一些违规微信账号时，运营者干脆迁移到抖音等其他平台，《半月谈》称。

## **In China, ‘Moles’ Are Leaking Official Secrets for Profit**

### Xi’s enforcers are hunting down party insiders accused of tipping off unannounced purges

By

Chun Han Wong

Aug. 9, 2026 10:00 pm ET

Secrets from Chinese leader Xi Jinping’s disciplinary purges keep surfacing online, defying a crackdown that has ensnared millions of people—and highlighting how deeply embedded graft and disloyalty remain within the Communist Party.

With the party detaining tens of thousands of officials each year, an underground market for corruption gossip has emerged on Chinese social media, where shadowy users drop hints on who’s getting purged next—in return for fees and clicks, state media said.

Such tipoffs can help suspects beat charges or evade arrest while enabling outsiders to make well-timed trades—a Chinese twist on concerns in the U.S. about political insiders cashing in on prediction markets such as Kalshi and Polymarket.

Party authorities have voiced alarm about the phenomenon while trying to hunt down leakers. The corruption-probe tipoffs also raise questions about the effectiveness of Xi’s crackdown, which has disciplined more than seven million people for misconduct such as taking bribes, defying policies and divulging secrets since late 2012.

“A small number of officials leak information in violation of regulations,” Banyuetan, a magazine published by the official Xinhua News Agency, said in a recent report. “This data is then resold through online groups—passing through multiple hands—thus forming a covert and illicit supply chain.”

Using such leaks, some social-media accounts “have been ‘predicting’ the downfall of government officials in advance using methods such as posting résumés or using homophones and coded language” that hint at a suspect’s identity, said the report. These cryptic posts are meant to skirt secrecy rules, since officials’ names and résumés generally aren’t considered sensitive.

Some profiteers monetize these leaks by selling access to social-media chat rooms where tips are shared, with fees ranging from a few dozen yuan to several hundred yuan, according to Banyuetan—that is, from a few dollars to about $120.

Others try to boost views to earn payouts. “Some even exaggerate corruption details and fabricate scandals to attract traffic,” the magazine said.

In one case, investigators found a party bureaucrat in the southern city of Guiyang who colluded with six relatives to operate nine [WeChat](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/HK/XHKG/700) social-media accounts that published hints about forthcoming promotions and purges, the magazine said.

The opacity of China’s political system makes tipoffs on ongoing probes highly valuable. With early notice, suspects and their associates can destroy evidence, spirit away illicit assets and flee. Authorities may be forced to detain suspects prematurely, weakening their legal case. Party insiders could gain a head start in lobbying for imminent job vacancies. Investors can short stocks that would likely be affected by a coming purge.

Chinese researchers say corruption probes can trigger a market selloff on companies seen as linked to a suspect or affected by an industry crackdown. [China Merchants Bank](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/CN/XSHG/600036), one of the country’s largest commercial lenders, [saw its share price plunge](https://www.wsj.com/articles/china-merchants-bank-dives-amid-watchdog-probe-into-ex-ceo-11650858685?mod=article_inline) in 2022 after the party announced an investigation against the bank’s former president, Tian Huiyu.

“Corruption investigations significantly increase the stock-price-crash risk for listed firms in provinces where investigated senior officials work or have worked during the period when these officials were investigated,” a group of Chinese academics wrote in a 2021 study.

“Anticorruption investigations are conducted with a high degree of secrecy, and given the central role of anticorruption in China’s elite politics, there is persistent demand for insider information from individuals motivated by political curiosity, commercial interests, or risk management,” said Ling Li, a Chinese politics and law lecturer at the University of Vienna.

“The selective and premature disclosure of investigative information might have been used to signal to potential bribers” that some party insiders are willing to trade privileged information for private gain, Li said. To minimize risks, the leakers “confined themselves to publishing highly suggestive posts that, in themselves, did not cross any obvious legal line.”

The party has long grappled with venal insiders—including antigraft inspectors—who leak secrets about corruption probes in return for bribes. In 2019, the party restructured its top disciplinary watchdog to ensure that separate groups of inspectors handle routine supervision, investigations and internal trials, aiming to prevent anyone from wielding excessive authority that can be exploited for personal gain.

Yet Beijing has continued to bemoan the presence of “moles” and bad apples among its enforcers. The party imposed formal penalties on more than 7,800 discipline inspectors in 2023, the highest annual total reported under Xi.

Regardless, [Xi has refused](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/chinas-xi-is-resurrecting-maos-continuous-revolution-with-a-twist-ed5ec610?mod=article_inline) to allow greater transparency and adopt more checks and balances in the bureaucracy, which some experts say are needed for cleaner governance—but would undermine authoritarian control.

Xi Jinping’s purges have entrenched his authority as China’s most dominant leader in decades. Maxim Shemetov/Reuters

Beijing’s tightening scrutiny on graft-probe leaks mirrors [similar inquiries in the U.S.](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/flurry-of-suspicious-oil-trades-worth-800-million-triggers-regulatory-probe-71e959ce?mod=article_inline), where regulators are examining [suspected profiteering](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/white-house-teleprompter-operator-investigated-for-alleged-bets-on-trump-speeches-bc95eeff?mod=article_inline) on [information from within](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/law/u-s-soldier-in-maduro-raid-is-charged-with-making-bets-on-leaders-ouster-535caf49?mod=article_inline) the Trump administration amid a betting boom on prediction markets.

Following the Banyuetan report, other state media rushed to denounce insider leaks and demand a crackdown—such as by strengthening censorship algorithms and issuing stern punishments.

“Anticorruption efforts mustn’t be exploited, and the chaotic practice of ‘hyping up corruption’ must be curbed,” the official party newspaper of Sichuan province said in a commentary.

Xi’s purges have entrenched his authority as [China’s most dominant leader](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/xi-jinping-leadership-tactics-china-318b3413?mod=article_inline) in decades. Launched to curb corruption when Xi took power in 2012, they have since become [a permanent campaign](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/xis-enforcers-are-hunting-down-officials-who-consult-mystics-and-borrow-too-much-4f41a315?mod=article_inline) that keeps officials loyal and on their toes. Party [authorities disciplined 983,000 people](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/xis-enforcers-punished-nearly-a-million-in-2025and-chinas-leader-wants-more-019e1c65?mod=article_inline) last year, the highest annual total on record.

On social media, leaks of impending purges often appear in subtle forms. Some posts use names or phrases with a similar pronunciation to the targeted official’s name, or simply post the official’s résumé.

In July 2025, a WeChat account, whose name can translate as “Yanglan Lake Insider,” referring to a place in Hubei province, published a brief post featuring a photograph and résumé of Zhou Xianwang, a former vice governor of Hubei and onetime mayor of its capital, Wuhan. A similar post appeared two days later on the video-sharing platform Douyin, the Chinese equivalent of TikTok, from a user named “Brother Zihao from Guangdong,” the southeastern province.

The day after the Douyin post appeared, the party’s top internal watchdog announced that Zhou was under investigation for alleged severe misconduct. The party expelled Zhou in January for alleged corruption, and prosecutors have since opened criminal proceedings against him.

“Brother Zihao” followed up with more tips, some coming days or even weeks ahead of the formal announcement. In late November, the account posted an image of Ye Hongzhuan, a retired veteran official in Hunan province, along with a teacup emoji—seemingly suggesting that Ye had been “invited to tea,” a euphemism for an interrogation. Authorities announced a probe against Ye roughly a week later.

Then in March, “Brother Zihao” published cryptic posts featuring the names and images of three bureaucrats—two senior municipal officials in Guangdong’s Zhanjiang city and a former chairwoman of a state-backed women’s federation in the southern province of Guizhou. Party authorities announced probes against each official over subsequent weeks.

Users praised “Brother Zihao” for the accurate tips, the most recent including one in July about a Guangdong official nearly two weeks before a probe was announced against him. Authorities, however, see such leaks as proof that “moles” were peddling secrets for profit.

The party’s attempts to plug such leaks have become a game of cat and mouse. One investigator told Banyuetan that authorities lack legal grounds to probe or penalize people who merely publish officials’ résumés on social media. When regulators shut down some offending WeChat accounts, the operators simply migrated to other platforms such as Douyin, Banyuetan said.
