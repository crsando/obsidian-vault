纽约，8月3日（路透社）——路透社在7月31日，即最近一次政策会议两天后，采访了威廉姆斯。以下为采访全文实录，为清晰和背景理解略作编辑。相关报道请[点击此处](https://www.reuters.com/business/feds-williams-expects-inflation-ease-says-fed-will-act-if-it-doesnt-2026-08-03/)。

Michael Derby:

迈克尔·德比：

I wanted to start out talking about inflation because that was obviously one of the key issues with the FOMC meeting. I've been to your speeches and followed along with what you've been talking about inflation. Now the war is back on again in some capacity, what are you thinking about the outlook for inflation right now? You know you had this idea that it was going to moderate over time, but do you still feel that way?

我想先从通货膨胀谈起，因为这显然是联邦公开市场委员会（FOMC）会议的关键议题之一。我参加过你的演讲，也一直关注你关于通胀的论述。现在战争又在某种程度上卷土重来，你目前对通胀前景有何看法？你曾认为通胀会随着时间的推移而缓和，现在你还是这样认为吗？

John Williams:

约翰·威廉姆斯：

I would, I still find it useful to kind of separate the three categories of factors that are causing inflation to be elevated, and I'll come back to one of them, is definitely the conflict in the Middle East.

我会的，我仍然觉得可以对导致通胀上升的三类因素进行区分，这样是有帮助的。其中一个因素，我稍后再谈，肯定就是中东冲突。

But I'll start with the tariffs. I think what we've seen on the tariffs is that probably the vast majority or large part of the effects of the tariffs that are in place have now passed through into to prices in the U.S. economy. That's I think consistent with the kind of, all the research we've done on this and the, and the data we've seen. So I think that those effects, which have been pretty sizable on inflation, we should not see large effects from tariffs on inflation rates in the coming months anymore. Assuming that the constellation of tariffs doesn't significantly change, I would say that on that, the newly announced tariffs, which really were replacing some of the expiring tariffs, on net, this is a relatively modest increase in the average tariff rate. So it doesn't really change my view on the effects of tariffs fundamentally. I think that those effects have mostly played out in the data we've already seen.

但我会先从关税谈起。我认为，从关税方面我们看到的情况是，现有关税的影响可能已经大部分或很大一部分传导到了美国经济的物价中。我认为这与我们在这方面做过的所有研究以及我们看到的数据是一致的。因此，我认为这些对通胀影响相当大的效应，在未来几个月内不会再看到关税对通胀率的重大影响。假设关税组合没有重大变化，我要说的是，新宣布的关税实际上是在替代一些即将到期的关税，净效果而言，平均关税率的增幅相对温和。所以这并没有从根本上改变我对关税影响的看法。我认为这些影响大多已经在我们已经看到的数据中体现出来了。

In terms of the conflict in Middle East clearly is a situation that does change day to day, week to week, and so we've seen the situation lead to higher oil prices and the closing of the Strait, and so that's just something that obviously will affect energy and commodity prices for a while. The futures markets, and some experts, still expect that there will be a resolution to that that leads to the reopening of this trade or reopening of trade in energy and other commodities, and so therefore expects those commodity energy prices to come down later in the year, we'll have to wait and see. I would still say that's part of my, if you will, my base forecast. But that's one I don't hold of energy prices ...

关于中东冲突，局势显然每天都在变化，每周都在变化，我们已经看到这一局势导致油价上涨和海峡关闭，因此这显然会在一段时间内影响能源和大宗商品价格。期货市场和一些专家仍然预计，这一问题将得到解决，从而重新开放这一贸易，或者说重新开放能源和其他大宗商品的贸易，因此预计这些大宗商品和能源价格将在今年晚些时候回落，我们拭目以待。我仍然想说，这是，如果你愿意的话，我的基准预测的一部分。但这是我对能源价格不持有的……

Derby: Moderating?

德比：正在放缓吗？

Williams: Moderating somewhat, or basically having essentially already peaked. Now it's just a question of you over what time period do they moderate, and what price do they come to? So I think, from my point of view on this, I'm not putting a great deal of certainty or confidence in any particular forecast of energy prices. But I don't anticipate, at least based on what's happening so far in my base case, that we're going to see continued inflationary push in the second half of the year or the next year from the from the conflict in the Middle East. But that's something that obviously could change depending on circumstances. But definitely relative to some of my earlier comments, oil prices are higher, and I think the path of future path of oil prices is also somewhat higher.

威廉姆斯：有所放缓，或者说基本上已经见顶。现在的问题是，它们会在一段时期内以多快的速度放缓，以及价格会回落到什么水平？所以，从我的角度看，我对任何特定的能源价格预测都不是很有把握或信心。但根据目前的情况，在我的基准情形中，我预计不会因为中东冲突而在今年下半年或明年看到持续的通胀压力。但这显然可能根据情况而改变。但相较于我之前的一些评论，油价确实更高了，而且我认为未来油价的路径也有所上移。

But the third category, which I think is important, is the effects of very strong demand related to AI. We can talk more about that. But I think that's just one area that I'm watching carefully. It's not a big driver of inflation right now, but is clearly something that we're seeing, certain categories of goods and being pushed up by the very very strong demand that's right now at pace and supply.

但第三个类别，我认为很重要，是与人工智能相关的非常强劲需求的影响。我们可以进一步讨论这一点。但我认为这只是我密切关注的一个领域。它目前不是通胀的主要驱动因素，但显然我们正在看到，某些类别的商品正被当前非常强劲的需求以及供应状况推高。

In some of your speeches, you talked about how the energy market was priced for a benign outcome, and one thing I've been watching is this inventory story and the oil doomers, their forecasts have not come to fruition so far. But there's this idea that the longer this ‌grinds on, we grind down through the inventories, that eventually there could be just a really bad situation. So I know it's hard to second guess markets, but do think markets are appropriately priced given this inventory situation, or do you think they're underpricing the risk that exists out there for energy?

在你的一些演讲中，你谈到了能源市场如何为良性结果定价，而我一直在关注的一件事是库存情况，以及那些看空油价的人，他们的预测到目前为止尚未实现。但有一种观点认为，这种僵持持续得越久，我们逐渐消耗库存，最终可能会陷入非常糟糕的局面。所以我知道很难对市场进行二次猜测，但你认为在当前库存情况下，市场定价是否合理，还是说他们认为能源领域存在的风险被低估了？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

First of all, I do think that the market, and I think all experts recognize, there's a lot of uncertainty about these issues, about kind of how this will play out. So, are they underpricing the risk? I can't really speak to that.

首先，我确实认为市场，以及我认为所有专家都认识到，这些问题存在很多不确定性，关于这将如何发展。那么，他们是否低估了风险？我实在无法对此发表看法。

I will say one thing that when you talk to people, when I talk to people in the markets, I mean, they're very aware of all the inventory issues. We started, actually before the conflict started, we had a pretty high level of oil inventories around the world, which I think lessens some of the effects that we saw early on. We've definitely seen developments where countries have tapped into strategic reserves. We've also seen other factors that I think have lessened somewhat the impact of the closing of the strait on world oil prices because either more supplies come from other areas, or demand has actually come down in some areas, so I think that's been one of the mitigating factors there.

我要说一点，当你与人交谈时，当我与市场人士交谈时，我的意思是，他们非常清楚所有的库存问题。实际上，在冲突开始之前，我们就已经看到全球石油库存处于相当高的水平，我认为这减轻了我们在早期看到的一些影响。我们确实看到了一些国家动用战略储备的情况。我们还看到了其他因素，我认为这些因素在一定程度上减轻了海峡关闭对世界油价的影响，因为要么其他地区的供应增加，要么某些地区的需求实际上有所下降，所以我认为这是其中的一个缓解因素。

I think that when you talk to people in the financial markets, especially around why are they not as worried as you might expect about the running out of inventories is, I think they often point to, well this is understood and at some point this would be, would lead to very very high oil prices and things and that would be part of an argument why various parties would want to come to a resolution of this and I would say on both sides. I mean, the cost to the various countries involved in this goes up if we get to that point.

我认为，当你与金融市场人士交谈时，尤其是关于他们为何不像你可能预期的那样担心库存耗尽的问题，我认为他们往往会指出，这一点已经被理解，而且在某个时点，这将导致油价大幅上涨，而这将成为各方为何想要解决这一问题的部分理由，我认为双方都是如此。我的意思是，如果到了那个地步，各相关国家的成本都会上升。

But again, those are just points of view that market participants make. So I think the thing that I do draw from this is they're fully aware of all the data and how much the inventories are, how much is out of ships and things. It's really, I think they're very focused on how does this, how is the conflict resolved, what's the timeline for reopening trade in oil and commodities. And then, what we did see when earlier in the year when this trade was reopened, we actually saw, I would say, a more rapid kind of normalization of movement of ships through, energy prices came down quite a bit.

但同样，这些只是市场参与者的观点。所以我认为我从中得出的结论是，他们完全了解所有数据、库存有多少、船上有多少货物等等。我认为他们确实非常关注冲突如何解决、石油和大宗商品贸易重新开放的时间表。而今年早些时候，当这一贸易重新开放时，我们确实看到，我想说，船舶通行的正常化速度更快，能源价格大幅下降。

So I think that there's also some confidence that if this can be resolved, we can maybe get back to normal maybe a bit quicker than some people had previously thought. Again, these are highly uncertain things, and I think everyone recognizes that. But I think that's kind of how the markets are looking at, recognizing that eventually these inventory issues will become much more front and center.

所以我认为也存在一些信心，即如果这个问题能够解决，我们或许能比一些人先前认为的更快恢复正常。再次强调，这些都是高度不确定的事情，我想每个人都认识到这一点。但我认为这就是市场的看法，认识到最终这些库存问题将变得更加突出和核心。

So if you had to sum up the inflation risk outlook, has it gotten worse since, say, the June FOMC meeting, or is it about in the in the same area?

那么，如果必须总结一下通胀风险前景，自6月FOMC会议以来是恶化了，还是大致持平？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

It's about, in my view, of course, I'm always speaking from my perspective. It's, I think similar to the June FOMC over these six weeks. My view on the economic outlook actually hasn't changed that much more generally.

在我看来（当然，我总是从我的角度发言），我认为与6月FOMC时的情况相似，在这六周里。我对经济前景的看法总体上其实没有太大变化。

I think, one, is if you go back to the June time frame, that was actually a time where oil prices were actually still pretty elevated. So the situation has, it's changed in between, but we're a similar situation. I would say that I think in terms of what we've seen in terms of the tariffs and also the inflation data, I think are more are consistent with kind of what I was, kind of had in my baseline forecast. So basically, it's similar to what I had seen in June, recognizing the great deal of uncertainty.

我想，第一，如果你回顾6月的时间框架，那实际上是油价仍相当高的时期。所以情况在此期间发生了变化，但我们处于类似的情况。我想说，就我们看到的关税和通胀数据而言，我认为这更符合我基线预测中的预期。所以基本上，这与我在6月看到的情况相似，同时认识到存在很大的不确定性。

So that would get us back to 2% inflation in 2028. I believe that was your last comment on that.

所以这将使我们在2028年回到2%的通胀率。我相信这是你最后一次对此发表评论。

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

I think again in my base case, and there's, we can talk about where things can go wrong in that base case, but assuming that energy prices have basically peaked and will come down gradually or come down over time, assuming that the tariff effects have also peaked and we won't see those affecting inflation rates in the future, and assuming that we keep the economy or the economy remains in a good macroeconomic balance in terms of the labor market and more generally, I think that some of the big drivers that pushed up inflation over the past 18 [months], year and a half or so will not be at play as much, and then some of the disinflationary forces that we've been seeing also kind of in the background, like lower housing costs, that that will continue to support a disinflation.

我认为，再次在我的基准情景中，而且我们可以讨论该基准情景中可能出错的地方，但假设能源价格已基本见顶并将逐步回落或随时间下降，假设关税影响也已见顶，未来不会再影响通胀率，并且假设我们保持经济或经济在劳动力市场及更广泛方面保持良好宏观平衡，我认为过去18个月（大约一年半）推动通胀上升的一些主要驱动因素将不再那么明显，而且我们一直看到的某些反通胀力量，比如较低的住房成本，也将继续支持反通胀。

Goods inflation can get back to rates that are kind of more normal around zero, or even slightly negative. And then, watch core services inflation, excluding housing, also to gradually come back down. So I think that that base case is still there in terms of, it's not really focused on energy prices because that's going to depend on what happens in the Middle East and that can change. But I am quite honestly focused quite a bit on what are we seeing in the core inflation data over the next several months, and is that consistent with a kind of a run rate of inflation moving towards 2% and really on a disinflationary path consistent with us achieving our 2% inflation goal on a sustained basis by 2028.

商品通胀可以回到更接近正常水平的零附近，甚至略微负值。然后，观察不包括住房的核心服务业通胀，也将逐渐回落。所以我认为这个基准情景仍然存在，它并不真正聚焦于能源价格，因为这取决于中东局势，而那可能会变化。但说实话，我非常关注未来几个月核心通胀数据的情况，以及这是否与通胀向2%移动的运行速度一致，并且确实处于与我们在2028年实现2%通胀目标的可持续发展道路相符的反通胀路径上。

I'm sure, as you know, this morning your colleagues were putting out their dissenting statements explaining why they thought Fed should raise rates, should have raised rates on Wednesday, and [Minneapolis Fed leader] Neel Kashkari even talked about the idea of maybe even talking about a gradual path of rate rises. So what is your reason why, given inflation being as above target, being above target for as long as it's been, why did you not you argue ⁠for the Fed, why didn't the Fed raise rates on Wednesday? What's the case against raising rates right now?

我相信，如你所知，今天早上你的同事们发表了他们的异议声明，解释为什么他们认为美联储应该加息，本应在周三加息，而明尼阿波利斯联储行长尼尔·卡什卡利甚至谈到了也许应该讨论逐步加息路径的想法。那么，考虑到通胀高于目标且已持续了这么长时间，你为何不为美联储辩护？为何美联储周三没有加息？现在反对加息的理由是什么？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

Well, and of course I'm speaking from my own my own perspective, I strongly supported the decision of the committee, and there was strong support for that. I think that, from my perspective, the labor market is, been stable. Growth has been solid, but not more than solid. I mean, GDP growth over the last year was 2%, it's a little less than that in the first half of this year. So we're not seeing the economy show any signs of overheating in terms of labor market, in terms of growth. We're seeing economy that's growing more or less at trend, and that's why the unemployment rate has been near constant for about a year.

嗯，当然，我要从我的个人角度来说，我强烈支持委员会的决定，而且该决定得到了强有力的支持。我认为，从我的角度看，劳动力市场一直稳定。经济增长稳健，但也不算过热。我的意思是，过去一年GDP增长率为2%，今年上半年略低于这个水平。所以我们没有看到经济在劳动力市场或增长方面显示出任何过热迹象。我们看到经济增长大致处于趋势水平，这就是为什么失业率大约一年来一直保持近乎不变。

So I think that's, from my point of view, we're not seeing signs that I think are adding to inflation from where the overall economy is. I think that the big drivers of inflation, the primary drivers of inflation over the last year and a half, really have been from these specific sets of circumstances around the tariffs, around some of the uncertainty, geopolitical events, and obviously the kind of conflict in the Middle East.

所以我认为，从我的角度看，我们并没有看到整体经济方面会增加通胀的迹象。我认为过去一年半里，通胀的主要驱动因素实际上来自围绕关税、一些不确定性、地缘政治事件，以及显然中东冲突等这些特定情况。

In my base forecast, we should see those factors diminish in terms of the fact of inflation, and we should see the underlying disinflationary trends, which I think were present earlier and are present in the data, to continue. Now that's a-- and I think monetary policy currently is well positioned in where we are today to support that disinflationary path.

在我的基准预测中，这些因素对通胀的影响应该会减弱，而我们认为早期已经存在并体现在数据中的潜在反通胀趋势应该会继续。现在这是一个——我认为当前货币政策在当前形势下的定位良好，能够支持这条反通胀路径。

I think that that's why the case is, we have is really very forward looking. It's not looking backwards, but it's looking, where is the economy today? Where is the economy going, is likely to be in the next year or two? Given that monetary policy takes, it takes time to have its full effects on the economy, and as I've said, my forecast personally is for inflation to come down in the second half of this year and come down further next year. And so it's really about thinking about what's the right monetary policy, given where the economy is headed. I think that's how I view the case for that.

我认为这就是为什么我们考虑的情况是非常前瞻性的。它不是回顾过去，而是要看经济如今处于什么位置，未来一两年可能走向何方？鉴于货币政策需要时间才能对经济产生全面影响，而且正如我所说，我个人的预测是通胀将在今年下半年回落，并在明年进一步回落。所以，这实际上是考虑在经济发展方向下什么是正确的货币政策。我认为这就是我对此的看法。

And obviously, there's scenarios where that doesn't happen, my base case doesn't happen, and we could get different factors that drive inflation higher or even lower. Or factors that affect the overall health of the economy and the labor market, and that would, you can change the outlook and change kind of the assessment of what the appropriate monetary policy is.

I think what's important is not about, do the markets come into a meeting thinking, well, I think the Fed's going to do this or that. But what I care about, that kind of uncertainty may just be normal, what I care about is that everybody understands our absolute very clear determination to achieve our 2% inflation goal. That we're obviously ready to make decisions in order to support the achievement of that. And we're committed on achieving our goals and doing our very best to accomplish that, and the fact that there would be uncertainty about well what are you going to do at the next meeting, I don't think that's a problem.

所以简而言之，我的意思是，我并不担心市场参与者说，你知道，他们可能会朝这个或那个方向走，或者对某个特定决定可能存在不确定性。我认为重要的不是市场在开会时是否认为，嗯，我认为美联储会这样或那样做。但我关心的是，这种不确定性可能只是正常的，我关心的是每个人都理解我们实现2%通胀目标的绝对明确的决心。我们显然准备做出决定以支持实现这一目标。我们致力于实现我们的目标，并尽最大努力完成它，而事实上，对于下一次会议你会做什么存在不确定性，我不认为这是个问题。

Has there been any change in the Fed's commitment to achieving 2%, has it gotten stronger since Chair Warsh has returned, or has it been fairly consistent all the way along?

美联储对实现2%目标的承诺是否有任何变化？自从沃什主席回归以来，它是否变得更加强烈，还是自始至终都相当一致？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

I think that our commitment on the FOMC has been to achieving the 2% goal, has been consistently very strong. I think it's, it has, given that the context of the situation we're in, where inflation has been too high for too long, I think it is important that not only we, we've made very clear that this is absolutely fundamental to achieve that, achieve both of our of our goals. So I think it's important to emphasize that and to, then of course make sure that we actually achieve that. But my personal view is that that commitment has been, has been there.

我认为FOMC对实现2%目标的承诺一直非常坚定。考虑到我们所处的环境，通胀过高已经持续太久，我认为重要的是，我们不仅要明确表示这对实现我们的两个目标都是绝对根本的。所以我认为强调这一点很重要，然后当然要确保我们真正实现这一目标。但我的个人观点是，这一承诺一直存在。

Do you think there was any sense in which financial markets were losing, or financial markets, the public in general were losing confidence the Fed was going to get inflation to 2%

你认为是否存在金融市场正在失去，或者金融市场和公众普遍对美联储将通胀降至2%失去信心的感觉？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

Well, it's very hard for me to answer the public's confidence in ⁠achieving something.

嗯，我很难回答公众对实现某事的信心。

You've got the Survey of Consumer Expectations!

你有消费者预期调查！

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

Yeah, we have the SCE. Thank you. We have market measures of inflation compensation and inflation swaps. We have the surveys, like the survey of consumer expectations, surveys of economists, other surveys, and then we talk to people.

是的，我们有SCE（消费者预期调查）。谢谢。我们有衡量通胀补偿和通胀互换的市场指标。我们有各种调查，比如消费者预期调查、经济学家调查和其他调查，然后我们还会与人交谈。

I talk to a lot of business people, community leaders, and market participants. I think that, I think people have their perspectives, their views. I think that we even see this in surveys, where when inflation got high, the surveys didn't really show, or the market measures didn't show, a big movement in longer run inflation expectations, but they did show some movement up a few years back in kind of near and medium term inflation expectations.

我与许多商界人士、社区领袖和市场参与者交谈。我认为，人们都有自己的视角和观点。我认为我们甚至可以在调查中看到这一点：当通胀走高时，调查或市场指标并没有显示出长期通胀预期的大幅变动，但确实在几年前显示出近期和中期通胀预期有所上升。

So we're watching this very carefully, and clearly there were some commentators who have expressed the view, I still remember this, when we were at 4% inflation, where people said you wouldn't get it back to two, and I said, yes, we will. And then when it got closer, between two and three back a couple years ago, I think that people said, kind of that view changed. So my view is it's hard for me to know. People have their views and perspectives and about this. I do think that, I go back to how I talked about financial markets, financial markets come to their own conclusions. Participants come to their own views about this.

所以我们正在非常仔细地观察这一点。显然，一些评论员曾表示——我仍记得——当通胀率达到4%时，人们说你不会让它回落到2%，而我说，是的，我们会。然后，当通胀率在几年前降至2%到3%之间、更接近目标时，我认为人们的观点有所改变。所以我的看法是，我很难知道。人们对此有自己的看法和观点。我确实认为，我回到我如何谈论金融市场——金融市场会得出自己的结论。参与者对此有自己的看法。

But what matters for us is that we're just laser focused on achieving our maximum employment price stability goals. And importantly, part of that is getting inflation back to 2.0% on a sustained basis.

但对我们来说重要的是，我们只是高度专注于实现最大就业和价格稳定的目标。重要的是，其中一部分是让通胀持续回到2.0%。

I will say on this, I really have not sensed a view that the credibility of the FOMC had in any way faltered or changed over time. Not only do we see that in inflation expectations, kind of longer term inflation expectations, I think that credibility is there, and it continues to be there. And so that that's not the issue that I see. I think that what you did see is when inflation was high, people kind of questioning, well, how long does it take for you to get inflation back down more than anything.

关于这一点，我要说的是，我真的没有感觉到联邦公开市场委员会（FOMC）的信誉有任何动摇或随时间改变。我们不仅在通胀预期（即长期通胀预期）中看到了这一点，我认为信誉是存在的，而且会继续存在。所以这不是我看到的问题。我认为你确实看到的是，当通胀居高不下时，人们更多是在质疑你们需要多长时间才能让通胀回落。

So I know you're limited about what you can say but Chair Warsh says he wants to have a family fight at FOMC meetings. Has there been any substantive change in how you guys talk about monetary policy, and I know you're limited in what you can say about deliberations, but is the tenor of the debate changing inside meetings in any way you can address?

所以我知道你能说的有限，但沃什主席说他想在FOMC会议上进行一场家庭争吵。你们讨论货币政策的方式有没有任何实质性变化？我知道你在讨论中能说的有限，但你能谈谈会议内部辩论的基调是否正在改变吗？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

I think we are having excellent meetings where people are all 19 participants are coming having thought carefully and thoroughly and deeply about all the issues that we're facing, all the great deal of uncertainty. So I think that this is a period of time where we're facing a lot of factors that are influencing the economy that also influence inflation and growth and the labor market. So people are bringing a lot of really good perspectives and differences of views, and bringing different kinds of research and analysis. It helps us think about that.

我认为我们正在进行非常出色的会议，19位与会者都经过深思熟虑，对我们面临的所有问题和巨大不确定性进行了深入思考。因此，我认为我们正处于一个面临许多影响经济、通胀、增长和劳动力市场的因素的时期。人们带来了许多非常好的观点和不同意见，以及各种研究和分析。这有助于我们思考这些问题。

So I think that's a really healthy for us. I think that it fits in to have that very open and I would say open-minded debate and discussion around these issues, that we're trying to understand, ⁠including AI and all of that. So that's good. Of course, we also are all looking forward to the various task forces coming and bringing some outside perspectives to I think hopefully bring some new ideas, but also shape some of the debates and discussions around these big issues.

所以我认为这对我们非常有益。我认为，围绕这些问题进行非常开放、可以说是开明的辩论和讨论是合适的，我们试图理解这些问题，包括人工智能等所有内容。这很好。当然，我们也期待各个工作组带来一些外部视角，我希望这能带来一些新想法，同时也能塑造围绕这些重大问题的辩论和讨论。

So I feel like that we're having those discussions, definitely creating that space to have I think a very healthy debate, and I cannot overstate how there's a big debate about how do we best think about things. There's a debate about how do we best achieve our goals, but there's absolutely no debate about the importance of achieving maximum employment and price stability. I mean, everybody is united and committed to that. We have to get this job done as best we can, we listen to each other, we learn from each other, and figure out as best we can the way forward. Knowing that every six to eight weeks we get back together, and we're going to do that again. So I think we're in a very, very healthy, good place on that.

所以我觉得我们正在进行这些讨论，确实为我认为非常健康的辩论创造了空间。我怎么强调都不为过，关于如何最好地思考问题存在重大辩论，关于如何最好地实现我们的目标也存在辩论，但在实现最大就业和物价稳定这一点上绝对没有辩论。我的意思是，每个人都团结一致并致力于此。我们必须尽最大努力完成这项工作，我们相互倾听，相互学习，并尽可能找出前进的道路。我们知道每六到八周我们会再次相聚，我们还会这样做。所以我认为我们在这一点上处于非常非常健康、良好的状态。

Various Fed reports have talked about elevated asset prices for an extended period of time, and that seems to come up again and again. I'm curious what you see in terms of bubble risks in the AI sector now that leverage is now starting to become part of it, and there is a lot of uncertainty about how this is all going to play out. And leverage is obviously where things, that starts to get into the mix and that's when stuff could happen. Do you see any notable risks in the AI investment, like a bubble type of situation?

美联储的多份报告都提到资产价格在一段较长时间内处于高位，这个话题似乎一再出现。我很好奇，既然杠杆现在开始成为其中的一部分，而且这一切将如何发展存在很大的不确定性，您如何看待人工智能领域的泡沫风险？显然，杠杆开始介入其中，事情可能就会发生。您是否看到人工智能投资中存在任何显著风险，比如泡沫式的情况？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

I don't. I don't see this as a bubble kind of situation. I guess maybe that word, maybe, is I think not the help very helpful here. But I think what we're seeing is a very high level of excitement, enthusiasm around new technology, around AI, and how that can increase productivity, how it can generate new products, new ways of doing things, and I think that's very real. And I'm definitely in the camp that thinks this is a general purpose technology that will be transformative, and it's going to take years and years of investment and lots of innovation and kind of new thinking around this to fully realize those benefits of new technologies.

不，我不认为这是泡沫式的情况。我想这个词在这里可能不是很有帮助。但我认为我们看到的是对新科技、对人工智能的高度兴奋和热情，以及它如何提高生产力、如何创造新产品和新做事方式，我认为这些都是非常真实的。我绝对属于那种认为这是一种通用技术、将带来变革的人，而且需要年复一年的投资和大量创新以及新的思路才能充分实现这些新技术的好处。

I think from the financial market point of view, investors are trying in real time to solve a almost intractable problem, and that is how big are the benefits of AI going to prove to be. Who are going to be the companies that end up coming up with the best models, the most useful models, and how much are they going to be able to charge for those? There's so much activity going on with large language models, small language models, competition from various companies here and abroad. So I think that when I think about the question you're asking, is I think there is a lot of well-founded enthusiasm to be investing at large scale, and that this is going to be transformative for the U.S. economy.

我认为从金融市场的角度来看，投资者正在实时解决一个几乎棘手的问题，那就是人工智能的收益最终会有多大。哪些公司最终会推出最好的模型、最有用的模型，以及它们能为此收取多少费用？大型语言模型、小型语言模型，以及国内外各家公司的竞争都非常激烈。所以，当我在思考你提出的问题时，我认为投资者有充分的理由大规模投资，这将对美国经济产生变革性影响。

But of course, whenever you have investors and people thinking about well, is it going to be this model that's going to work? Is it going to be this? Is who's going to come out ahead on this? There's risks.

但当然，当投资者和人们思考：会是这个模型成功吗？会是这个吗？谁会在其中胜出？这时就会有风险。

The introduction of leverage?

杠杆的引入？

Williams:

威廉姆斯：

So the leverage, I guess, this is not the kind of leverage you think about with the housing crisis, where it's like I think, at least so far, that you know where, it's that you're right now, a lot of it is really about debt financing along with equity financing. These are, most of these businesses have very high earnings, so I'm not as worried about the financial stability from the leverage right now. But I do think that you could have and likely to have at some point in markets, as well, we've seen it even in recent weeks. Kind of reassessing who, how big how big the effects of these going to be. Who's going to come out ahead? Who might be affected negatively? And I think that's a normal thing. So I that the idea that there's going to be some volatility in the equity markets as we see how this plays out, I think it just comes with a highly innovative fast-changing world there, and we've seen that in the past.

所以杠杆，我想，这不是你在住房危机时想到的那种杠杆，我认为至少到目前为止，你知道，它更多是关于债务融资和股权融资。这些企业大多盈利很高，所以我现在并不那么担心杠杆带来的金融稳定问题。但我确实认为，在某个时候市场上也可能出现重新评估，我们在最近几周也看到了。重新评估这些影响会有多大，谁会胜出？谁可能受到负面影响？我认为这是正常的。所以我认为随着事态发展，股市出现一些波动，这伴随着一个高度创新、快速变化的世界而来，我们过去也见过这种情况。
