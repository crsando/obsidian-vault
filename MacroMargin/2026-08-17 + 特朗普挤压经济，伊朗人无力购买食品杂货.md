## 特朗普挤压经济，伊朗人无力购买食品杂货

### [经济](https://www.bloomberg.com/economics)

战争已使伊朗人的购买力荡然无存。*摄影师：Fatemeh Bahrami/Anadolu/Getty Images*

作者：[Golnar Motevalli](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARwAJnhPlhE/golnar-motevalli)2026年8月14日 下午5:59 GMT+8*更新于2026年8月14日 晚上8:53 GMT+8*

### **要点**

- 美国、以色列和伊朗之间的冲突改变了伊朗人的消费习惯，由于成本飙升和近80%的通胀率，许多人开始使用信贷购买食品杂货。
- 伊朗人越来越多地使用无息信贷和先买后付计划来购买包括食品在内的基本必需品，而弱势和贫困的伊朗人约占人口的70%。
- 预计伊朗经济今年将萎缩6.1%，通胀率创纪录地达到70%，该国面临滑入全面战时经济的风险，可能出现食品、燃料、药品和工业原料短缺。
自德黑兰创业十多年来，Nazanine 经历了美国制裁、接二连三的货币崩盘和两场战争。这是她第一次不得不使用信用卡购买食品杂货。

自2月底美国和以色列开始轰炸伊朗以来，这位56岁的老人被迫裁员，并放弃所有非必要开支以应对不断上涨的成本。独居让她稍微轻松一些，但有时仍然入不敷出。

“有些日子我一点钱都没有，”纳扎宁（Nazanine）说，由于与外国媒体交谈的敏感性，她要求不透露自己的姓氏和公司细节。“分期购买每周的日用品真的很有帮助。”

![People shop for food and basic necessities at a bazaar in Tehran on June 1. Photographer: Abedin Taherkenareh/EPA/Shutterstock](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b14816a8dcce43231106282/3bc81cc9-0b14-81f5-b77a-ea6444d2cc21-8c5ad02382c4c948743c28fbc1268359.png)

6月1日，人们在德黑兰的一个集市上购买食品和基本必需品。摄影师：阿贝丁·塔赫肯纳雷/EPA/Shutterstock

这场冲突正在改变伊朗人的消费习惯，在近80%的通胀和今年贬值近30%的货币下，他们的购买力已经蒸发。数十年的制裁、腐败和管理不善早在战争前就已扼杀了经济，但美以的打击加剧了困境，摧毁了关键基础设施，并造成数千平民死亡。

战争开始近六个月，美国总统唐纳德·特朗普正押注，对伊斯兰共和国加强经济压力将在军事力量失败之处取得成功。他对伊朗最繁忙的海港维持了数月之久的海上封锁，而两国在霍尔木兹海峡控制权上仍陷入僵局。美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特（Scott Bessent）周四在接受Newsmax采访时表示，美国正准备宣布“前所未见”的新经济措施。

### 延伸阅读：[美国准备对伊朗实施前所未有的“经济孤立”计划](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-to-roll-out-economic-isolation-plan-for-iran-next-week)

伊朗多年来一直设法应对经济孤立，但经济学家警告称，这场冲突可能将这个拥有9000万人口的国家拖入历史上最严重的经济危机之一。

在伊朗首都各处，商店橱窗贴满了宣传无息信贷和先买后付计划的标语。这些计划多年来一直受到家具或厨房电器等耐用品的欢迎，但据官方媒体报道和彭博新闻社在该国采访的近十二人透露，自战争以来，随着零售商将这一选项扩展到包括食品在内的基本必需品，其使用量激增。大多数人因担心报复而要求匿名。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b14816a8dcce43231106282/3bc81cc9-0b14-813f-8052-fb5bebfea576-9252b8fd87ffbc2b737869e082552fb3.png)

总部位于华盛顿的昆西研究所（Quincy Institute）非常驻研究员哈迪·卡哈尔扎德（Hadi Kahalzadeh）估计，弱势和贫困的伊朗人已占全国人口的约70%。

他说：“分期购买食品和日常必需品，是通货膨胀从宏观经济问题演变为日常生活结构的最明显迹象之一。”

这对伊朗构成了挑战，因为伊朗正在反击特朗普的威胁和要求。在美以打击开始前两个月，里亚尔汇率暴跌引发了全国范围的暴力抗议，这些抗议演变为对伊斯兰共和国领导层的重大挑战。据联合国最高人权官员称，随后的镇压导致数千人死亡，司法部门继续惩罚抗议者，自3月以来已处决了与骚乱有关的[27人](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168081)。

Source: Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b14816a8dcce43231106282/3bc81cc9-0b14-810a-98cb-edf33213fe81-52da9c445e27400166501a722a295e1a.png)

1月8日，德黑兰因通胀飙升和里亚尔崩溃爆发示威活动。来源：Getty Images

[国际货币基金组织](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/13347Z:US)在4月表示，预计伊朗经济今年将[萎缩6.1%](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2026/april/english/text.pdf)，为90年代中期以来最快，通胀率达到创纪录的70%——即使对于世界上通胀率最高的国家之一来说，这也高得惊人。伊朗政府估计，战争前六周对经济造成了[2700亿美元的损失](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-29/iran-hosts-reconstruction-investment-event-amid-sign-of-us-deal)。

德黑兰的商户老板纳扎宁（Nazanine）表示，令人瞠目的价格上涨给家庭带来了巨大压力，但超市目前仍库存充足。在今年的穆哈兰姆月（伊斯兰什叶派日历中最神圣的月份之一）期间，她没有看到任何短缺迹象，人们在全市分发大量免费食品，作为传统的*nazry*供奉的一部分。

制裁和日益恶化的国际孤立迫使伊朗[强化经济](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/how-iran-s-economy-is-enduring-trump-s-war-us-blockade)以抵御外部威胁，在美欧寻求禁止进口其石油、伊朗被切断与国际银行联系之际，转向亚洲。

菲利普斯大学（Philipps-Universität）经济学教授穆罕默德·礼萨·法尔扎内甘（Mohammad Reza Farzanegan）说：“随着时间的推移，伊朗企业和政府已经找到了替代市场、中介、支付机制和运输路线。例如，石油出口已越来越多地转向中国。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b14816a8dcce43231106282/3bc81cc9-0b14-812c-9b29-c24ba2c57471-d1cfd52aedd07789fb8c51427227ff89.png)

伊朗表示，货物运输继续无视美国扼制其的努力，但出口水平仅为特朗普重返白宫前的一小部分。

### 阅读更多：[伊朗和美国不知道如何打赢他们的长期战争：评论](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-iran-war-why-neither-side-knows-how-to-win)

在这个传统上为中东最大现金经济体之一的国家，分期付款和信贷激增本身就标志着该国正在适应新一轮经济困境。

马哈茂德在德黑兰经营一家销售音视频设备的小企业，他表示分期付款和信贷对零售商留住客户至关重要。他也经常使用它们购买必需品。

“分期付款计划中包含的利息几乎抵消了通胀预期，”马哈茂德说，他不愿在对外国媒体发言时透露全名，“卖家对按当前市场价格销售更有信心，这也让现金流得以流动。”

伊朗仍是中东工业化程度最高的经济体之一，其人均国内生产总值与埃及、摩洛哥和突尼斯大致相当。

“伊朗的经济在纸面上可能看起来并不吸引人，但它设法为其民众提供了最低水平的社会福利，”驻德黑兰的能源分析师丹尼尔·拉赫马特（Danial Rahmat）表示。

鉴于迄今为止其具备的战争生存能力，伊斯兰共和国很可能已经得出结论：它“可能没有面包和水，但至少仍能封锁霍尔木兹海峡，”他补充道。

Photographer: Ali Saeedi/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b14816a8dcce43231106282/3bc81cc9-0b14-8121-b88a-fba5e4b87a89-d31c59b9d5eba5cbd072c78feb91749b.png)

8月10日，伊朗阿巴斯港附近霍尔木兹海峡锚泊的船只。摄影师：Ali Saeedi/Getty Images

经济一直是一场激烈政治辩论的核心，这场辩论主要由强硬派发起，辩论焦点是：既然与特朗普的所有谈判努力都导致伊朗遭到轰炸，伊朗官员是否还应继续与美国接触。

像阿亚图拉阿里·阿拉菲这样的知名强硬派神职人员已明确告诉官员不要重启外交。在阿亚图拉阿里·哈梅内伊被引发战争的美国和以色列空袭杀害后，阿拉菲曾担任临时最高领袖。

据半官方的塔斯尼姆通讯社报道，阿拉菲上个月说：“不要继续谈判。官员们不应以经济问题和害怕战争代价及基础设施遭打击为借口，继续走与异教徒谈判的道路。”

### 延伸阅读：[伊朗派系在哈梅内伊缺席情况下就战争目标争吵不休](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/iranian-factions-squabble-over-war-aims-as-khamenei-looks-to-assert-control)

卡哈扎德认为，伊朗很可能将资源转向军事用途，并实际上把负担转嫁给民众。那些有助于规避外部压力的机制——如国家控制、非正规贸易或减少进口——反而损害了经济和家庭福祉。

卡哈扎德说：“这些措施有助于避免立即崩溃，但也给我们留下高通胀、失业和贫困的后果。”他将这种整体影响比作“韧性陷阱”。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b14816a8dcce43231106282/3bc81cc9-0b14-8157-b5e5-df49ca3cdabe-33fd6634e9018eebca469574fca1ed6e.png)

本世纪初，伊朗是中东最大的经济体。现在，它落后于沙特阿拉伯或阿拉伯联合酋长国等许多邻国。按名义价值计算，人均GDP有所下降。

马吉德·礼萨·哈里里（Majid Reza Hariri）是商人，也是总部位于德黑兰的伊朗-中国商会会长。他警告称，美国的封锁比战争更具破坏性。他在周一发表于半官方的KhabarOnline上的一次采访中表示，陆路和铁路路线永远无法弥补伊朗依赖且一直在失去的海上贸易。

他表示，官员们绝不能允许经济像适应制裁那样去适应封锁。

尽管伊朗具有很强的韧性，但它面临自1980年伊拉克入侵以来首次陷入全面战时经济的风险。

法尔扎内甘（Farzanegan）表示，长期的海上封锁很可能导致“食品、燃料、药品、工业投入品或外汇的严重短缺”，并可能扩大配给和价格管制。

## Iranians Struggle to Afford Groceries as Trump Squeezes Economy

### [Economics](https://www.bloomberg.com/economics)

The war has evaporated Iranians' purchasing power.*Photographer: Fatemeh Bahrami/Anadolu/Getty Images*

By [Golnar Motevalli](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARwAJnhPlhE/golnar-motevalli)August 14, 2026 at 5:59 PM GMT+8*Updated on August 14, 2026 at 8:53 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways**

- The conflict between the US, Israel, and Iran has transformed the spending habits of Iranians, with many using credit to buy groceries due to surging costs and nearly-80% inflation.
- Iranians are increasingly using interest-free credit and buy-now-pay-later plans to purchase basic necessities, including food, with vulnerable and poor Iranians making up some 70% of the population.
- The Iranian economy is expected to shrink 6.1% this year, with inflation at a record 70%, and the country risks being tilted into a full wartime economy, with potential shortages of food, fuel, medicine, and industrial inputs.
In a little over a decade since starting her business in Tehran, Nazanine has weathered US sanctions, successive currency collapses and two wars. This is the first time she’s had to use credit to buy groceries.

Since the US and Israel began bombing Iran in late February, the 56-year-old has been forced to lay off staff and forgo any non-essential spending to cope with surging costs. Living alone makes it easier but, sometimes, she still falls short.

“There are days when I don’t have any money,” Nazanine said, asking that her last name and the details of her company be withheld due to the sensitivity of speaking with foreign media. “It really helps to buy my weekly shopping in installments.”

People shop for food and basic necessities at a bazaar in Tehran on June 1. Photographer: Abedin Taherkenareh/EPA/Shutterstock

The conflict is transforming the spending habits of Iranians, whose purchasing power has evaporated under nearly-80% inflation and a currency that’s lost almost 30% of its value this year. Decades of sanctions, corruption and poor management strangled the economy well before the war, but US-Israeli strikes have deepened the hardship, destroyed key infrastructure and killed thousands of civilians.

Almost six months since the start of the war, US President Donald Trump is betting that intensifying economic pressure on the Islamic Republic will succeed where military force has failed. He has maintained a months-long naval blockade on Iran’s busiest sea ports, as the two countries remain deadlocked over control of the Strait of Hormuz. In an interview with Newsmax on Thursday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US is preparing to announce new economic measures "like have never been seen."

### Read More: [US Readies Unprecedented ‘Economic Isolation’ Plan for Iran](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-to-roll-out-economic-isolation-plan-for-iran-next-week)

Iran has managed to navigate economic isolation for years, but economists warn that the conflict may drag it into one of the worst economic crises in the history of the nation of 90 million people.

Across Iran’s capital, shop windows are covered in signs advertising interest-free credit and buy-now-pay-later plans. Popular for years for durable goods like furniture or kitchen appliances, their use has surged since the war as retailers extended the option to basic necessities, including food, according to state media reports and almost a dozen people Bloomberg spoke to in the country. Most asked to remain anonymous for fear of reprisals.

Vulnerable and poor Iranians already make up some 70% of the population, estimates Hadi Kahalzadeh, a non-resident fellow at the Washington-based Quincy Institute.

“Buying food and everyday necessities in installments is one of the clearest signs that inflation has moved from a macroeconomic problem into the structure of daily life,” he said.

This poses a challenge for Iran as it pushes back against Trump’s threats and demands. Two months before US and Israeli strikes began, a slump in the rial triggered violent nationwide protests that evolved into a major challenge to the Islamic Republic’s leadership. Thousands were killed in the ensuing crackdown and the judiciary has continued to punish protesters, [executing 27 people](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168081) linked to the unrest since March, according to the United Nations’ top human rights official.

Demonstrations triggered by soaring inflation and the collapse of the rial in Tehran on Jan. 8. Source: Getty Images

The [International Monetary Fund](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/13347Z:US) said in April that it expected Iran’s economy to [shrink 6.1% this year](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2026/april/english/text.pdf), the fastest pace since the mid-90s, with inflation at a record 70% — steep even for a country with one of the world’s highest rates. The Iranian government estimates the first six weeks of the war caused [$270 billion worth of damage](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-29/iran-hosts-reconstruction-investment-event-amid-sign-of-us-deal) to the economy.

Nazanine, the business owner in Tehran, says eye-watering price rises are putting huge pressure on families but supermarkets remain, as yet, well stocked. During this year’s month of Muharram, one of the holiest in the Shia Muslim calendar, she saw no sign of shortages, with people distributing large amounts of free food throughout the city as part of traditional *nazry* offerings.

Sanctions and worsening international isolation have forced Iran to [steel its economy](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/how-iran-s-economy-is-enduring-trump-s-war-us-blockade) against external threats, pivoting to Asia as the US and the EU sought to ban imports of its oil and it was cut-off from international banks.

“Over time, Iranian firms and the government have found alternative markets, intermediaries, payment mechanisms, and transport routes,” said Mohammad Reza Farzanegan, economics professor at Philipps-Universität. “Oil exports, for example, have increasingly been redirected toward China.”

Iran has said shipments continue to defy efforts by the US to throttle them, but export levels are a fraction of what they were before Trump returned to office.

### Read More: [Iran and the US Don’t Know How to Win Their Long War: Essay](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-iran-war-why-neither-side-knows-how-to-win)

The surge in payment plans and credit in what’s traditionally been one of the Middle East’s biggest cash-based economies is itself a sign of the country adapting to a fresh cycle of economic hardship.

Mahmoud, who runs a small business selling audio and video equipment in Tehran, said installments and credit have been vital for retailers to retain customers. He too uses them frequently to buy essential goods.

“The interest built into installment plans pretty much offsets inflationary expectations,” Mahmoud, who didn’t want to be identified by his full name when speaking to foreign media, said. “Sellers feel more confident selling at current market prices, which keeps cash flow moving.”

Iran remains one of the most industrialized economies in the Middle East, its gross domestic product per capita roughly on par with that of Egypt, Morocco and Tunisia.

“Iran’s economy may not look attractive on paper, but it’s managed to provide a minimum level of social welfare to its population,” Tehran-based energy analyst Danial Rahmat said.

It’s likely that given its ability to survive the war so far, the Islamic Republic has reached the conclusion that it “may not have bread and water, but at least it can still close the Strait of Hormuz,” he added.

Anchored ships in the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Bandar Abbas, Iran, on Aug. 10. Photographer: Ali Saeedi/Getty Images

The economy has been at the heart of a fierce political debate, mostly waged by hardliners, over whether Iranian officials should keep engaging with the US when, they say, all efforts to negotiate with Trump have resulted in Iran being bombed.

High profile hardline clerics like Ayatollah Ali Arafi, who stood as interim Supreme Leader after Ayatollah Ali Khamenei was killed by the US-Israeli strikes that started the war, have explicitly told officials not to restart diplomacy.

“Do not continue the negotiations,” Arafi said last month, according to the semi-official Tasnim news agency. “Officials should not, on the pretext of economic problems and fear of the costs of war and striking infrastructure, continue the path of negotiations with infidels any further.”

Kahalzadeh believes Iran is likely to redirect resources to military efforts and effectively shift the burden onto the population. The very mechanisms that help bypass external pressure — such as state control, informal trade or reduced imports — undermine the economy and household welfare.
