### 中文译文

## 太平洋岛国图瓦卢担忧中国导弹试验引发海啸

太平洋岛国会议未能就批评一个月前的中国导弹试验达成共识，凸显了该地区日益尖锐的地缘政治紧张局势。

太平洋岛国论坛18个成员国的外长今日在斐济举行会议，但据一位因事涉政治敏感而要求匿名的知情人士透露，由于基里巴斯和瑙鲁拒绝签署，会议未能就试验一事发表声明。

根据论坛规则，所有声明须经一致同意。该人士称，官员们将在本月底于帕劳举行的领导人会议前继续讨论此事。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b581cc90b14813b8255e3dfeb3d6528/3b581cc9-0b14-81a4-be2a-c547911a06e3-05cc6465eebc67820c09a161eb38a6c6.png)

自7月6日中国从潜艇上发射可携带核弹头的导弹以来，该地区国家一直在讨论如何回应此次试验，其模拟弹头落在低洼岛国图瓦卢附近。

新西兰外长温斯顿·彼得斯在会后对记者表示，有两个国家利用需要达成共识这一规则，代表某个“外部伙伴”行使了事实上的否决权。他没有指明是哪些国家。

他说：“我们非常明确地表示，我们认为外部人士不应告诉内部人士——我们是在这里生活了数千年的人——我们应该做什么。”

彼得斯表示，这两个拒绝同意声明的国家没有为其立场提供任何理由或逻辑依据，并补充说他仍希望在领导人会议上能达成共识。他没有指明是哪些国家。

### **闯入民宅**

周五早些时候，图瓦卢一名高级官员将此次试验比作闯入民宅，并补充说，这使人们担心它会引发海啸。图瓦卢外交事务高级官员帕苏纳·图阿加（Pasuna Tuaga）周五在论坛会议间隙表示，中国没有就此次试验发出警告，也没有说它会落在距离图瓦卢如此近的地方。

“你自己想象一下，这就像有人闯进了你的家，”他说。“想象一下，如果是真的呢？这正是我们在正式会议上向论坛提出的问题：我们是否要等到被真导弹击中后才发表声明？”

图阿加表示，测试模拟弹头不是中国对待[太平洋地区](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-07/china-s-icbm-tests-point-to-new-normal-rattling-the-pacific)应有的方式，并呼吁该论坛的18个成员国集体谴责一个月前的那次发射。“我们必须发表声明，因为我们不想被任何大国恐吓，”他说，同时强调自己是以个人身份而非外交、劳工和贸易部高级官员的身份发表上述言论。

据图阿加称，他的国家试图与北京讨论这个问题，但毫无进展。他说，图瓦卢已请求澳大利亚、法国和美国在附近海域搜寻这枚模拟弹头。

虽然在此次试验后，太平洋岛屿论坛没有发表官方声明，但澳大利亚、新西兰等个别国家已批评此举。

在今天的会议开始前，彼得斯表示，太平洋岛国错失了就此次试验点名批评中国的机会，并表示新西兰将推动就此事发表联合声明。

“我们将拭目以待，看这场辩论中是逻辑占上风，还是辩论早已被那些抱持先入为主且不会改变立场的人所决定，”彼得斯告诉记者。

彼得斯不愿透露哪些国家阻碍了联合声明，尽管其中一些国家与中国有密切的经济或安全联盟。这些国家包括基里巴斯、巴布亚新几内亚和所罗门群岛。

澳大利亚外交部长黄英贤周五上午在电台讲话中重申，这次试验具有“破坏稳定”的性质。

“太平洋岛国论坛的大多数成员确实对中国弹道导弹试验表示了关切，”她说。“我希望中国能够注意到太平洋地区领导人和部长们对那次试验作出的非常明确的回应。”

### English Original

## Tiny Island Nation Tuvalu Feared Tsunami From China Missile Test

A meeting of Pacific Island nations has failed to reach consensus on criticizing a Chinese missile test a month ago, underscoring the geopolitical tensions that are becoming starker in the region.

The foreign ministers from the 18 Pacific Island Forum members met today in Fiji, but couldn’t agree on issuing a statement on the test after Kiribati and Nauru refused to sign on, a person familiar with the matter said, asking not to be named because the matter was politically sensitive.

All statements need to be unanimous, according to the rules of the Forum. Officials will continue to discuss the matter ahead of the meeting of national leaders meeting in Palau at the end of the month, the person said.

The countries of the region have been discussing how to respond to the test since China launched the nuclear-capable missile from a submarine on July 6, with the dummy warhead splashing down close to Tuvalu, the low-lying island nation.

Speaking to reporters after the meeting, New Zealand Foreign Minister Winston Peters said two countries used the need for consensus as an effective veto on behalf of an “outside partner.” He didn’t identify the countries.

“We make it very clear that we do not think that outsiders should be telling the insiders — that’s us who’ve been here for thousands of years — what we should do,” he said.

Peters said the two countries that refused to agree to the statement provided no reason or logic to support their position, adding he is still hopeful consensus can be reached at the leaders’ meeting. He didn’t identify the countries.

### **Home Invasion**

Earlier on Friday, a senior Tuvalu official likened the test to a home invasion, adding that it caused people to fear it would trigger a tsunami. China gave no warning on the test and didn’t say it would land so close to Tuvalu, Pasuna Tuaga, a senior Tuvalu foreign affairs official, said Friday on the sidelines of the Forum meeting.

“Just imagine it yourself, it was like someone invaded your home,” he said. “Just imagine if it was a real one? That’s the same question that we have put to the forum during the official meeting, do we have to wait until we are hit by a real one and then issue a statement?”

Tuaga said that testing dummy warheads was not how China should be [treating the Pacific](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-07/china-s-icbm-tests-point-to-new-normal-rattling-the-pacific), and called on the 18 members of the Forum to collectively condemn the launch a month ago. “It is important for us to make a statement because we don’t want to be intimidated by any powers,” he said, while emphasizing that his comments were in a personal capacity and not as a senior official in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Labour and Trade.

According to Tuaga, his nation tried to discuss the issue with Beijing, but got nowhere. Tuvalu has asked Australia, France and the US to search for the dummy warhead in the nearby waters, he said.

While there has been no official statement from the Pacific Islands Forum following the test, individual countries such as Australia and New Zealand have criticized the move.

Ahead of today’s meeting, Peters said Pacific Island nations had missed an opportunity to call out China for the test and said New Zealand would be pushing for a joint statement on the matter.

“We’ll see whether logic prevails in this debate, or whether the debate has already been settled by people having a predetermined position from which they will not move,” Peters told reporters.

Peters wouldn’t be drawn over which countries were the roadblock to a joint statement, although a number of the nations have close economic or security alignment with China. They include Kiribati, Papua New Guinea and Solomon Islands.

Australian Foreign Minister Penny Wong speaking on radio Friday morning reiterated that the test had been “destabilizing.”

“The majority of the Pacific Islands Forum did express concerns over China’s ballistic missile test,” she said. “I hope China does take note of the very clear response from leaders and ministers in the Pacific about that test.”
