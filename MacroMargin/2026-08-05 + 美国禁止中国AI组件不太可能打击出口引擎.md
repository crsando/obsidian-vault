### 中文译文

## 美国禁止中国AI组件不太可能打击出口引擎

美国可能禁止中国数据中心组件，这有可能给两国脆弱的贸易休战带来压力，但不太可能损害中国的出口引擎。

华盛顿正在考虑的限制措施将针对光收发器——这些组件在数据中心内部和之间以高速传输数据。6月份，中国向美国出口了6160万美元的此类产品，占其全球出货量的8.7%，但仅占其整体出口的一小部分，从而限制了任何限制措施对整体经济的影响。

美国此前是中国收发器的最大目的地，但自去年唐纳德·特朗普重返白宫以来，出货量急剧下降。马来西亚（其中[数据中心投资](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/ai-and-chips-are-turning-malaysia-into-asia-s-growth-standout)激增）已取代美国，当月接收了2.6亿美元的中国光收发器出口。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b381cc90b1481e5aa4dfed22af939b2/3b381cc9-0b14-8130-adbd-dbfe424fff13-e9cc299d1ec3499abd8006f9163bb6f5.png)

这一转变表明，中国企业可能通过向其他快速增长的市场销售来缓冲美国限制措施的影响。2026年上半年，光收发器的海外出货量同比增长了27%。

彭博社周二援引知情人士的话报道，美国联邦通信委员会正在起草一项命令，禁止中国制造的数据中心组件。路透社[报道](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/us-drafting-ban-on-china-data-center-components-reuters-says)早些时候称，该政府机构正在考虑禁止进口中国光收发器的新型号。

中国光设备类股周三下跌，[中际旭创股份有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300308%20CH%20Equity)在深圳一度下跌14%，随后收窄跌幅。美国、欧洲和日本的供应商股价上涨，表明投资者预期拟议中的限制措施将损害中国企业，同时使海外竞争对手受益。

不过，澳大利亚和新西兰银行集团有限公司大中华区经济学家Vicky Zhou表示，禁令对中国快速增长的人工智能相关出口影响有限，去年这类出口占中国整体出口的近23%。

Zhou表示，其他主要的人工智能相关产品，包括芯片和计算机，面临的美国禁令威胁可能较小，因为中国企业主要生产销往东南亚的低端型号。今年早些时候，这些产品对中国出口增长的贡献接近[一半](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/china-s-exports-imports-soar-faster-than-forecast-amid-ai-rush)。

周表示：“美国加强对中国人工智能限制的趋势非常明显，从半导体到人工智能服务，现在又到了硬件。”但“中国的AI出口将继续保持稳健，受到更高价格的支持。”

中国执政共产党的官方报纸周三警告称，将对美国不断扩大的技术限制采取反制措施，这给两国脆弱的贸易休战增添了新的压力来源。

就美国联邦通信委员会(FCC)上周宣布将收紧对部分外国制造的机器人和电源逆变器的限制一事，《人民日报》指责美国搞“单边霸凌”。中国在这些领域是占主导地位的制造商。

尽管该报没有提及对数据中心组件的拟议限制，但其警告凸显出，华盛顿方面不断扩大的对华技术打压可能使特朗普和习近平在中国领导人下月预计访美之前稳定关系的努力变得更加复杂。

![US President Donald Trump and his Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping tour Zhongnanhai Garden in Beijing last May. (Photo by Evan Vucci-Pool/Getty Images) Photographer: China Pool/Getty Images AsiaPac](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b381cc90b1481e5aa4dfed22af939b2/3b381cc9-0b14-8139-9bd5-f3a3722cb44e-5973f16a3de9f412f716b08d6bc17e46.png)

该报称：“美国以‘非歧视’或‘国家安全’为借口的措施，本质上是对中国企业和产品的歧视性对待和打压。”这篇评论署名“钟声”，这是“中国之声”的中文谐音，常用于传达北京在外交政策上的观点。

禁令将加剧美中在人工智能发展方面的脱钩，而业界正期待两国定于9月举行的以人工智能为重点的对话。

彭博行业研究分析师肖恩·陈（Sean Chen）表示，中国供应商控制着高速收发器市场一半以上的份额，这使得主要云计算公司在短期内几乎没有替代选择，并可能软化拟议的政策。

陈表示，中国企业或许还能通过在东南亚的工厂发货来规避限制，这取决于FCC如何定义中国光收发器。

“该提案凸显了日益加剧的地缘政治紧张局势，有利于苏州TFC等受政策风险影响较小的上游和中游中国供应商。” —— 肖恩·陈

美国联邦通信委员会还在考虑其他措施，以防止被列入黑名单的中国公司连接到美国网络，众议院中国特别委员会在周二的一份[报告](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/chinese-telecom-hack-exposed-data-centers-house-report-to-say)中表示。该委员会发现，监管漏洞使得三家被禁止直接连接美国网络的中国电信公司仍能保持与美国互联网基础设施的联系。

《人民日报》列举了美国联邦通信委员会早先针对中国电信和视频监控设备、无人机和路由器采取的行动，称这些行动是抑制中国技术、遏制中国工业发展努力的一部分。

“美方的这一举动与中美关系当前稳定改善的势头背道而驰，”该报援引习近平与特朗普5月在北京举行的峰会称。“如果美方一意孤行，中国将采取坚决的反制措施。”

## US Ban on Chinese AI Components Unlikely to Dent Export Engine

A potential US ban on Chinese data center components risks straining the countries’ fragile trade truce, but is unlikely to hurt China’s export engine.

The restrictions under Washington’s consideration would target optical transceivers — components that transmit data at high speeds within and between data centers. China exported $61.6 million of the products to the US in June, accounting for 8.7% of its worldwide shipments but only a fraction of its overall exports, limiting the wider economic impact of any restrictions.

The US was previously the biggest destination for Chinese transceivers, but shipments have fallen sharply since President Donald Trump returned to the White House last year. Malaysia, where [investment in data centers](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/ai-and-chips-are-turning-malaysia-into-asia-s-growth-standout) has surged, has taken its place, receiving $260 million of Chinese optical-transceiver exports that month.

That shift illustrates how Chinese companies may be able to cushion the impact of US curbs by selling into other fast-growing markets. Overseas shipments of optical transceivers rose 27% in the first half of 2026 from a year earlier.

The Federal Communications Commission is drafting an order to ban Chinese-made data center components, Bloomberg reported Tuesday, citing a person familiar with the matter. Reuters [reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/us-drafting-ban-on-china-data-center-components-reuters-says) earlier that the government agency was considering barring imports of new models of Chinese optical transceivers.

Chinese optical-equipment stocks slid on Wednesday, with [Zhongji Innolight Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300308%20CH%20Equity) falling as much as 14% in Shenzhen before paring the decline. US, European and Japanese suppliers rallied, suggesting investors expect the proposed restrictions to hurt Chinese companies while benefiting overseas rivals.

Still, a ban would have a limited effect on China’s fast-growing AI-related exports, which accounted for nearly 23% of its overall shipments last year, according to Vicky Zhou, Greater China economist at Australia and New Zealand Banking Group Ltd.

Other major AI-related products, including chips and computers, may face less threat from US bans because Chinese companies mainly produce lower-end models sold in Southeast Asia, Zhou said. The products contributed to [nearly half](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/china-s-exports-imports-soar-faster-than-forecast-amid-ai-rush) of China’s export growth earlier this year.

“The trend of US stepping up restrictions on Chinese AI is very clear, from semiconductors to AI services and now hardware,” Zhou said. But “China’s AI exports will continue to be solid, supported by higher prices.”

The official newspaper of China’s ruling Communist Party warned Wednesday of countermeasures against widening US technology curbs, adding a fresh source of strain to the countries’ fragile trade truce.

In a commentary, the People’s Daily accused the US of “unilateral bullying” over the FCC’s [announcement](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs) last week that it would tighten restrictions on some foreign-made robots and power inverters, sectors in which China is a dominant manufacturer.

While the newspaper didn’t mention the proposed restrictions on data center components, its warning underscored the potential for Washington’s expanding campaign against Chinese technology to complicate efforts by Trump and Xi Jinping to stabilize relations before the Chinese leader’s expected US visit next month.

“The US measures, under the pretext of ‘non-discrimination’ or ‘national security,’ are in essence discriminatory treatment and suppression targeting Chinese enterprises and products,” the newspaper said. The commentary was credited to Zhong Sheng, a Chinese homonym for “Voice of China” that’s often used to convey Beijing’s views on foreign policy.

A ban would reinforce the US and China’s decoupling in AI development, as industries await the upcoming [AI-focused dialogue](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/china-seeks-clarity-from-us-on-ai-talks-as-sanction-threats-loom) between the two countries scheduled in September.

Chinese suppliers control more than half of the high-speed transceiver market, leaving major cloud-computing companies with few near-term alternatives and potentially softening the proposed policy, according to Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Sean Chen.

Chinese companies may also be able to circumvent the restrictions by shipping products through factories in Southeast Asia, depending on how the FCC defines a Chinese optical transceiver, Chen said.

“The proposal underscores simmering geopolitical tensions, favoring upstream and midstream Chinese suppliers such as Suzhou TFC that are less exposed to policy risks.” — Sean Chen.

The FCC is also considering other steps to prevent blacklisted China-based companies from connecting to US networks, the House Select Committee on China said in a Tuesday [report](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/chinese-telecom-hack-exposed-data-centers-house-report-to-say). The committee found that regulatory gaps had allowed three Chinese telecommunications companies barred from directly connecting to US networks to retain links to America’s internet infrastructure.

The People’s Daily listed earlier FCC actions targeting Chinese telecommunications and video-surveillance equipment, drones and routers, describing them as part of an effort to suppress Chinese technology and contain the country’s industrial development.

“This move by the US side runs counter to the current momentum of stabilization and improvement in China-US relations,” the newspaper said, citing the May summit in Beijing between Xi and Trump. “If the US side is bent on going its own way, China will take resolute countermeasures.”
