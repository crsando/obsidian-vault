### 中文译文

## 英伟达携首批H200芯片销售重返中国市场

### **要点**

- 英伟达在最近一个季度向中国客户出售了少量H200芯片，销售额占该芯片制造商数据中心营收的比例不到1%。
- 由于北京当局的反对，该公司未能将美国政府许可证允许的全部H200芯片销往中国，并为过剩的H200库存计提了4亿美元费用。
- 尽管近期有销售，但英伟达仍继续预计来自中国的数据中心和AI芯片业务收入为零。这些销售是自2025年初以来首批向中国出售的AI芯片。
[英伟达公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity)表示，在最近一个季度向中国客户出售了少量H200芯片，尽管这些人工智能处理器对全球第二大经济体的出货量未达到美国总统唐纳德·特朗普批准的总量。

英伟达周三在[财报](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/nvidia-estimate-topping-forecast-fails-to-wow-investors)中表示，截至7月26日的三个月内，面向中国买家的H200销售额占该芯片制造商数据中心营收的比例不到1%。该公司补充称，由于北京当局的反对，其未能将1月份获得的美国政府许可证所允许的全部H200芯片销往中国。过去六个月，由于该产品需求下降，该公司为过剩的H200库存计提了4亿美元费用。

该公司表示，这些芯片销售以及其他非AI产品线（包括与电脑游戏相关的硬件）帮助其在中国的销售额达到78.8亿美元，几乎是上年同期的两倍。该公司未具体说明在中国销售了多少H200芯片，也未给出收入总额。

在随业绩发布的前瞻中，英伟达表示仍继续预计来自中国的数据中心和AI芯片业务收入为零。自2022年以来，由于华盛顿政策制定者担心该技术可能有助于北京军方，美国对向中国客户销售先进AI硬件的限制不断升级，该公司一直疲于应对。这些措施实际上已将英伟达挡在了中国AI芯片市场之外。

虽然这只是该公司整体收入的一小部分，但周三公布的H200出货量标志着自2025年初约6000万美元的H20芯片运往中国以来，首次向中国销售AI芯片。H20芯片旨在符合早先的美国出口限制，但同年4月被特朗普禁止向中国销售。总统后来推翻了这一决定，但中国告知其企业不要购买H20芯片。

去年，经过数月的游说，英伟达首席执行官黄仁勋说服特朗普允许公司将其Hopper产品线中的H200芯片出售给中国的获批买家，条件包括向美国支付25%的费用。7月，美国商务部工业与安全局副部长杰弗里·凯斯勒表示，向中国客户销售的H200芯片数量“微不足道”，但没有提供具体细节。

H200芯片目前比英伟达最新的Rubin处理器落后两代，但在中国市场上与本土竞争对手的产品相比仍具竞争力，这些对手包括[华为技术有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH)，华为与[中芯国际集成电路制造有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/981%20HK%20Equity)合作制造芯片。两家公司都受到美国限制的打击，例如禁止中芯国际获得光刻机等关键工具的措施。

数月来，英伟达已获得美国工业与安全局的批准，可向中国客户销售H200芯片，但由于中国是否会允许其科技公司完成采购的问题悬而未决，这些销售一直停滞不前。在5月访问北京后的回程途中，特朗普向记者表示，中国领导人习近平尚未批准购买H200芯片，因为他想支持本国芯片制造商。

尽管黄仁勋随行，此行还包括在阿拉斯加的空军一号加油停留处进行的一次临时会面，但英伟达在中国的AI销售并未取得突破，并于5月告知投资者，该公司未在中国售出任何H200芯片。虽然特朗普表示芯片并非其北京峰会的重点，但当下月习近平访问华盛顿时，英伟达在中国的销售情况可能会在两国领导人会谈中再次被提及。更广泛而言，特朗普表示他计划提出人工智能话题。

## Nvidia Steps Back Into China Market With First H200 Chip Sales

### **Takeaways**

- Nvidia sold a small number of its H200 chips to customers in China during the most recent quarter, with sales making up less than 1% of the chipmaker's data center revenue.
- The company hasn't been able to sell the full amount of H200s to China permitted by a US government license due to objections from authorities in Beijing, and took a $400 million charge for excess H200 inventory.
- Nvidia is continuing to project zero revenue for data center and AI chip business originating from China, despite the recent sales, which mark the first AI chip sales to China since early 2025.
[Nvidia Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity) said it sold a small number of its H200 chips to customers in China during the most recent quarter, though shipments of the artificial intelligence processors to the world’s second-largest economy fell short of the total permitted with the blessing of US President Donald Trump.

Sales of H200s to Chinese buyers made up less than 1% of the chipmaker’s data center revenue for the three months ended July 26, Nvidia said Wednesday in its [earnings report](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/nvidia-estimate-topping-forecast-fails-to-wow-investors). The company added that it hasn’t been able to sell the full amount of H200s to China permitted by a US government license granted in January, owing to objections from authorities in Beijing. It took a $400 million charge over the past six months for excess H200 inventory because of diminished demand for the product.

Those chip sales, along with other non-AI product lines, including hardware related to computer gaming, helped generate $7.88 billion in sales from China, nearly double from a year earlier, the company said. It didn’t specify how many H200s it sold in the country, nor did it give a revenue total.

In the outlook accompanying its results, Nvidia said it was continuing to project zero revenue for data center and AI chip business originating from China. Since 2022, the company has been grappling with escalating US restrictions on sales of its advanced AI hardware to Chinese customers, spurred by concerns by policymakers in Washington that the technology could aid Beijing’s military. The US measures have effectively blocked Nvidia from the AI chip market in China.

While a fraction of the company’s overall revenue, the H200 shipments reported Wednesday nonetheless mark the first AI chip sales to China since roughly $60 million of its H20 chips were shipped there in early 2025. The H20s, designed to comply with earlier US export curbs, were barred by Trump from sale to China in April of that year. The president would later reverse his decision, though China would tell its companies not to buy the H20 chips.

Jensen Huang Photographer: Kiyoshi Ota/Bloomberg

Last year, after months of lobbying, Nvidia Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang persuaded Trump to allow the company to sell the H200s, part of its Hopper product line, to approved buyers in China, subject to conditions that include a 25% fee paid to the US. In July, Undersecretary of Commerce for Industry and Security Jeffrey Kessler said that a “trivial” number of the H200 chips had been sold to Chinese customers, but offered few specifics.

The H200 chip is now two generations behind Nvidia’s latest Rubin processors, but they’re still competitive in the Chinese market against products from local rivals including [Huawei Technologies Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH), which makes chips with [Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/981%20HK%20Equity) Both companies have been hit by US restrictions, such as measures barring SMIC from accessing crucial tools like lithography machines.

For months, Nvidia had US approvals from the Bureau of Industry and Security to sell H200s to Chinese customers, but those sales had stagnated with questions looming about whether China would allow its tech companies to complete the purchases. Following his trip to Beijing in May, Trump told reporters on the way back to Washington that Chinese leader Xi Jinping had not cleared H200 purchases yet because he wanted to support his country’s own chipmakers.

Despite Huang joining the trip, which involved a last-minute meetup at an Air Force One refueling stop in Alaska, Nvidia saw no breakthrough in its China AI sales and told investors in May that it had not sold any H200s there. Though Trump said chips weren’t a focus of his summit in Beijing, Nvidia’s sales in China could come up again during talks between the two leaders when Xi visits Washington next month. More broadly, Trump has said he plans to bring up the topic of artificial intelligence.
