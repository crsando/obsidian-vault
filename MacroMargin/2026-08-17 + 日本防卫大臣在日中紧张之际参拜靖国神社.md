### 中文译文

## 日本防卫大臣在日中紧张之际参拜靖国神社

### **彭博AI要点**

- 日本防卫大臣小泉进次郎于日本在第二次世界大战中投降81周年之际参拜了靖国神社，该神社被视为日本战时军国主义的象征。
- 中国和韩国谴责此次参拜行为，中国表示“强烈谴责”这一举动，韩国则敦促日本领导人展现“通过行动对过去的真诚悔悟”。
- 小泉表示，他参拜神社是为了表达哀悼和敬意，并重申了永不再战、继续履行日本作为致力于和平国家的责任以推进其和平道路的承诺。
日本防卫大臣参拜了一座被日本邻国视为其战时军国主义象征的神社，在日中两国关系紧张数月后，此举招致中国的谴责。

小泉进次郎经常参拜靖国神社，周六，即日本在第二次世界大战最后阶段投降81周年之际，他前往参拜。据共同通讯社报道，首相高市早苗计划不参拜，而是献上祭品。韩国也谴责了这一行为。

近年来，大多数首相和内阁大臣都避免参拜这一争议场所。小泉此次参访正值东京与北京关系持续紧张之际，此前高市早苗去年在议会提出，中国入侵台湾可能为日本出兵提供法律依据。

靖国神社供奉着数百万日本战争亡灵，被周边国家，尤其是中国和韩国，视为日本过去军国主义的象征。

中国外交部在一份声明中表示，中方[“强烈谴责”](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/china-protests-japan-pm-defense-minister-s-yasukuni-actions)这些举动。官员称，靖国神社是日本军国主义的象征和精神工具，并补充说神社供奉着14名被定罪的甲级战犯。

韩国外交部表示，韩国政府对参拜靖国神社或向其供奉祭品的政客行为表示谴责，并敦促日本领导人“通过行动展现对过去的真正悔悟”。据韩联社报道，两名在韩日本官员被召见，并提出抗议。

韩国外交部称：“我们强调，这是基于两国间信任建立面向未来的韩日关系的关键基础。”

[在社交媒体平台 X 上的一篇帖子中](https://x.com/shinjirokoizumi?lang=ja)，小泉表示，他参拜神社是为了表达哀悼和敬意。他写道：“我还重申了永不再战的誓言，以及继续履行责任的决心，继承战后日本作为和平国家坚定不移走过的道路。”这是他作为防卫大臣的首次参拜。

高市近年来经常参拜靖国神社，包括去年纪念日。她没有参加今年春季祭典。她关于台湾的言论引发了中国方面的反弹，包括贸易限制和旅行警告，使这两个亚洲经济体在国防、外交和贸易等问题上对峙数月。

中国还敦促日本正视其战时历史，并与军国主义“彻底决裂”。北京表示，正确理解历史是日本与邻国关系的政治基础，也是其致力于和平发展的重要考验。

## Japan Defense Minister Visits Yasukuni During China Tensions

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- Japan's defense minister Shinjiro Koizumi visited Yasukuni shrine, a symbol of Japan's wartime militarism, on the 81st anniversary of Japan's surrender during World War II.
- China and South Korea condemned the visit, with China saying it "strongly condemns" the move and South Korea urging Japanese leaders to demonstrate "genuine remorse regarding the past through their actions".
- Koizumi said he visited the shrine to offer his condolences and express respect, and renewed his pledge never to wage war again and to continue fulfilling Japan's responsibility to carry forward its path as a nation committed to peace.
Japan’s defense minister visited a shrine seen by Japan’s neighbors as a symbol of its wartime militarism, drawing condemnation from China following months of tension between the countries.

Shinjiro Koizumi, a regular visitor to Yasukuni shrine, paid his respects on Saturday, the 81st anniversary of Japan’s surrender during the final days of World War II. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi was planning to skip a visit and made an offering instead, according to the Kyodo news agency. South Korea also condemned the actions.

Most prime ministers and cabinet ministers have avoided visiting the controversial site in recent years. Koizumi’s visit comes as tensions between Tokyo and Beijing remain strained after Takaichi suggested in parliament last year that a Chinese invasion of Taiwan could provide legal grounds for Japan to deploy troops.

Yasukuni, which honors millions of Japan’s war dead, is viewed by neighboring countries, particularly China and South Korea, as a symbol of Japan’s past militarism.

China “[strongly condemns](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/china-protests-japan-pm-defense-minister-s-yasukuni-actions)” the moves, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs said in a statement. Officials said the shrine is a symbol and spiritual tool of Japanese militarism, adding that it enshrines 14 convicted Class-A war criminals.

South Korea’s government said it deplored the acts of politicians who visited or made offerings to the shrine, and urged Japanese leaders to demonstrate “genuine remorse regarding the past through their actions,” according to a foreign ministry statement. Two Japanese officials in South Korea were summoned and a protest was lodged, Yonhap News Agency reported.

“We emphasize that this is a crucial foundation for building a future-oriented Korea-Japan relationship based on trust between the two nations,” the South Korean foreign ministry said.

[In a post](https://x.com/shinjirokoiz?lang=ja) on the social-media platform X, Koizumi said he visited the shrine to offer his condolences and express respect. “I also renewed my pledge never to wage war again and my determination to continue fulfilling our responsibility to carry forward the path that postwar Japan has steadfastly pursued as a nation committed to peace,” he wrote. It was his first visit as defense minister.

Takaichi had been a regular visitor to the shrine in recent years, including on the anniversary last year. She did not visit the shrine for this year’s spring festival. Her comments on Taiwan have triggered a backlash from China, including trade curbs and travel warnings, putting the two Asian economies at loggerheads for months on issues spanning defense, diplomacy and trade.

China also urged Tokyo to confront its wartime history and “completely break” with militarism. Beijing said a proper understanding of history is the political foundation of Japan’s relations with neighboring countries and an important test of its commitment to peaceful development.
