## 三星和SK海力士测试中国芯片工具以对冲美国风险

- 韩国存储芯片巨头在其中国工厂测试中微公司（AMEC）设备
- 企业寻求应急方案，以防美国限制影响西方工具的服务

8月5日（路透社）- 三星电子和SK海力士正在评估中国中微半导体设备（AMEC）的芯片制造设备，可能用于其中国工厂。三位知情人士称，这两家韩国公司正在对冲美国出口管制收紧的风险。

这两家存储芯片制造商大约两年前开始测试中微的刻蚀设备，其中两位消息人士称，当时华盛顿是否会继续允许他们将美国芯片制造工具进口到中国的不确定性正在加剧。

三星[（005930.KS），打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/005930.KS)表示，尚未测试中微设备用于其中国工厂，也未考虑这样做。

SK海力士[（000660.KS），打开新标签页](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/000660.KS)表示，尚未测试中微设备用于中国。

尽管评估尚未就更广泛部署做出决定，但这些评估为这家总部位于上海的公司提供了获得一些全球领先芯片制造商验证的难得机会。

更广泛地说，这些试验突显了美国技术管控的核心悖论：旨在限制北京半导体雄心的措施，正在为中国的竞争对手创造机会，使其在中国运营的外资晶圆厂中立足。

由于此事敏感，所有消息人士均拒绝透露身份。

中微公司和负责执行美国政府出口管制的美国商务部产业安全局（BIS）未立即回应置评请求。

### 收紧出口管制

美国商务部于2023年将三星和SK海力士的中国工厂指定为“经认证的最终用户”（VEU），允许其无需获得单独许可证即可进口某些受管控的美国设备。

华盛顿于2025年撤销了VEU授权，随后授予这些芯片制造商一份[年度许可证](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/samsung-wins-us-annual-approval-chipmaking-tool-shipments-china-source-says-2025-12-30/)，以便其在2026年向中国工厂运送芯片制造设备。

消息人士称，即便如此，两家公司仍担心未来的限制可能会从新设备扩展到已安装在其中国工厂的西方工具的维护、维修或更换。

消息人士补充称，因此，这些芯片制造商将中国供应商作为备用，以维护和升级现有生产线，而非扩大在中国的制造产能。

三星在西安运营其NAND闪存芯片工厂，SK海力士在大连拥有NAND设施，在无锡拥有一座DRAM存储芯片工厂。

他们在中国的工厂严重依赖美国公司供应的蚀刻工具，包括应用材料公司[(AMAT.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/AMAT.O)和泛林集团[(LRCX.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/LRCX.O)。

### 官方批准

对于中微公司（AMEC）和中国新兴的一批半导体设备制造商来说，获得三星或SK海力士的认可将等同于强有力的商业背书。

尽管中国设备制造商在先进光刻和某些检测系统方面仍落后于海外竞争对手，但他们在蚀刻、沉积、清洗和平坦化等领域缩小了差距，且成本通常显著更低。

据研究公司TechInsights副主席丹·哈奇森（Dan Hutcheson）称，中国工具的成本可比成熟外国供应商的同类设备低20%至30%。

消息人士称，中微公司的设备已被包括NAND生产商长江存储科技有限责任公司（YMTC）在内的中国领先芯片制造商使用，这让三星和SK海力士更有信心认为部分系统已足够成熟，可以进行测试。

中国供应商的崛起可能对包括应用材料公司（Applied Materials）、泛林集团（Lam Research）和KLA（[KLAC.O](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/KLAC.O)）在内的主导设备制造商，以及长期控制晶圆制造市场关键领域的日本和欧洲竞争对手构成长期挑战。

中国仍是这些公司的重要市场。应用材料公司报告称，2025财年在中国收入85.3亿美元，占总销售额的30%。

中国供应商的任何突破仍将面临障碍，包括漫长的认证流程、较小的服务网络、知识产权问题以及来自华盛顿的潜在政治压力。

目前尚不清楚韩国芯片制造商是否会因安全和知识产权风险而在国内工厂安装中国设备。

然而，美国的出口管制为中国设备行业创造了肥沃的机遇。德意志银行估计，北方华创[(002371.SZ), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/002371.SZ)、中微公司、拓荆科技和盛美半导体在2026年各自的营收都将超过10亿美元。

该银行估计，今年中国预计280亿美元的晶圆制造设备市场中，这些公司合计可能占据25%至30%的份额。若排除光刻和计量设备，中国供应商的份额可能接近40%。

- A win would give Chinese suppliers a major global customer endorsement

Aug 5 (Reuters) - Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are evaluating chipmaking equipment from China's Advanced Micro-Fabrication Equipment (AMEC) for possible use at their Chinese factories, three people familiar with the matter ‌said, as the South Korean firms hedge against the risk of tighter U.S. export controls.

The memory chipmakers began testing AMEC etching equipment about two years ago, when uncertainty was mounting over whether Washington would continue allowing them to import U.S. chipmaking tools into China, two of the sources said.

Samsung [(005930.KS), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/005930.KS) said it has not tested AMEC equipment for use at its China factory and had not considered doing so.

SK Hynix [(000660.KS), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/000660.KS) said it has not tested AMEC tools for use in China.

While the evaluations have yet to result in decisions on wider deployment, they offer the Shanghai-based company a rare chance to secure validation from some of the world's leading chipmakers.

More broadly, the trials underscore a paradox at the heart of U.S. ⁠technology controls: measures designed to constrain Beijing's semiconductor ambitions are creating opportunities for Chinese rivals to gain a foothold in foreign-owned fabs operating in China.

All sources declined to be identified due to the sensitivity of the matter.

AMEC and the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), which enforces the U.S. government's export controls, did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

### TIGHTENING EXPORT CONTROLS

The U.S. Commerce Department designated Samsung and SK Hynix's Chinese factories as "validated end users" (VEU) in 2023, allowing imports of certain controlled U.S. equipment without obtaining individual licences.

Washington revoked the VEU authorization in 2025 and later granted an [annual license](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/samsung-wins-us-annual-approval-chipmaking-tool-shipments-china-source-says-2025-12-30/) to the chipmakers to bring in chip manufacturing equipment to their facilities in China for 2026.

Even so, both companies remain wary that future restrictions could extend beyond new equipment to the servicing, repair or replacement of Western tools already installed at their Chinese plants, the sources said.

As a result, the chipmakers are keeping Chinese suppliers in reserve as a potential way to maintain and upgrade existing production lines, rather than expand manufacturing capacity in China, the sources added.

Samsung operates its NAND flash memory chip plant in Xian, and SK Hynix has NAND facilities in ‌Dalian and ⁠a DRAM memory chip plant in Wuxi.

Their China factories rely heavily on etching tools supplied by U.S. firms including Applied Materials [(AMAT.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/AMAT.O) and Lam Research [(LRCX.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/LRCX.O).

### STAMP OF APPROVAL

For AMEC and China's emerging crop of semiconductor-equipment makers, winning approval from Samsung or SK Hynix would amount to a powerful commercial endorsement.

While Chinese equipment makers continue to trail overseas rivals in advanced lithography and some inspection systems, they have narrowed the gap in areas such as etching, deposition, cleaning and planarisation, often at significantly lower cost.

Chinese tools can cost 20% to 30% less than comparable equipment from established foreign suppliers, according to Dan Hutcheson, vice chair of research firm TechInsights.

AMEC ⁠equipment is already used by leading Chinese chipmakers, including NAND producer Yangtze Memory Technologies Co (YMTC), giving Samsung and SK Hynix greater confidence that some systems are mature enough for testing, the sources said.

The rise of Chinese suppliers could pose a longer-term challenge to dominant equipment makers including Applied Materials, Lam Research and KLA [(KLAC.O), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/KLAC.O), as well as Japanese and European rivals that have long controlled key segments of the wafer-fabrication market.

China remains ⁠an important market for those companies. Applied Materials reported $8.53 billion in China revenue in fiscal 2025, equal to 30% of total sales.

Any breakthrough for Chinese suppliers would still face hurdles, including lengthy qualification processes, smaller service networks, intellectual-property concerns and potential political pressure from Washington.

It is also unclear whether Korean chipmakers would install Chinese equipment at domestic factories because of security and intellectual-property risks.

Yet ⁠U.S. export controls have helped create a fertile opening for China's equipment industry. Deutsche Bank estimates that Naura Technology [(002371.SZ), opens new tab](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/002371.SZ), AMEC, Piotech and ACM Research will each generate more than $1 billion in revenue in 2026.

Together, they could capture 25% to 30% of China's projected $28 billion wafer-fabrication equipment market this year, the bank estimated. Excluding lithography and metrology, Chinese suppliers' share could approach 40%.
