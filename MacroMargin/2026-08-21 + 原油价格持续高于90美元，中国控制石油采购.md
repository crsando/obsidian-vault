## 原油价格持续高于90美元，中国控制石油采购

### [行业](https://www.bloomberg.com/industries)能源

作者：[Rong Wei Neo](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXhJVqT3jL4/rong-wei-neo) 2026年8月21日 12:59 PM GMT+8

### **要点** 由彭博 AI 提供

### **要点**

- 由于油价坚挺，中国炼油商将原油采购量控制在低于此前水平
- 预计采购量将上升，但即使是最乐观的估计，第四季度的采购量也将仅为每日990万桶，仍低于去年的水平
- 进口速度将取决于多种因素，包括中东货盘的回归以及北京对能源安全的看法
坚挺的油价正促使中国炼油商将原油采购量维持在低于伊朗战争前高位的水平，尽管分析师和行业咨询机构估计进口量正逐步回升至每日1000万桶

过去几个月，中国的购买一直是分析师试图理解石油市场波动的焦点。在战争最严重的几个月里，这个全球最大石油进口国通过减少燃料运输并[动用](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-10/china-taps-commercial-oil-stockpiles-to-help-weather-gulf-shock)充足的商业库存，帮助将原油价格保持在远低于早期灾难性预测的水平。现在的问题是，它是否会恢复到2025年的热情。

这可能是一个遥远的前景。

根据Rystad Energy、Energy Aspects和FGE NexantECA的数据，从2026年第三季度到第四季度，每日购买量应增加多达120万桶，随着部分中东供应恢复和炼油厂开工率提高，购买量将继续回升。

然而，即使是最乐观的估计也认为第四季度购买量仅为每日990万桶，仍远低于去年触及的每日1200万至1300万桶的水平，当时中国正在建立库存并支撑全球价格。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c381cc90b14811ba43feb503f38a265/3c381cc9-0b14-8187-8188-c871cb5c75fe-5b83e17b81a7338b3848e73b1306fc94.png)

最明显的因素是价格。去年，原油平均价格低于70美元，而目前价格高于90美元，足以抑制需求。

但还有其他因素，包括难以获得合适的原油。许多中国炼油厂是为加工中东中质含硫原油而设计的，而那里的供应仍然昂贵且稀缺。买家争相购买[伊拉克](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/chinese-refiners-snap-up-iraqi-oil-as-more-supplies-exit-hormuz)和[沙特](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/china-buys-saudi-crude-in-rare-tender-as-imports-stay-muted)原油，但在[阿布扎比国家石油公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TAQA%20UH%20Equity)的最新招标中没有获得任何船货。

Energy Aspects分析师孙佳男表示，从巴西和安哥拉增加购买可能不足以完全取代这些失去的供应，即失去的中东供应。

“随着战争持续和中东供应仍受干扰，高油价正在阻止库存等更大的需求驱动因素出现，”孙表示，并补充说，国内燃油价格上限导致炼油厂无法将更高的原油成本完全转嫁给消费者，从而限制了它们支付高价的意愿。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c381cc90b14811ba43feb503f38a265/3c381cc9-0b14-816d-8ce5-e4690f2799f5-22f6f2f105061e2cefd6bcfce26f45d7.png)

然后还有一个挥之不去的问题：在战争期间，随着更多中国司机转向电动汽车，甚至卡车也走向绿色，一些需求是否已经完全消失——这些是长期趋势。

对北京来说，好消息是中国仍有相当大的缓冲。根据数据情报公司Kpler的数据，原油库存已从5月份的峰值下降近8%，但商业和战略储备合计仍维持在约11.6亿桶。即便是商业库存，尽管已被用于吸收战争冲击的很大一部分，也仍能为炼油厂提供回旋空间。

进口速度将部分取决于霍尔木兹海峡和中东货物的回归情况，包括FGE NexantECA在内的预测都考虑了一定程度的复苏。目前，尽管有更多石油通过该水道，但流量仍仅为战前水平的一小部分。

这还将取决于该行业的整体健康状况。从周日开始，中国国有炼油厂将发布首批数据，因为行业巨头将公布中期财务收益，详细说明价格对燃料销售和上游收入的影响。

中石化将率先公布业绩，财报将于8月23日发布。

最后，时机和规模最终可能取决于北京对能源安全（一个关键优先事项）的看法，因为波斯湾冲突仍在持续。仅此一点就可能决定库存管理和最低储备要求的重大变化。

“能源安全将支撑中国对长期冲突的战略回应，”Rystad Energy石油市场副总裁林烨（Lin Ye）表示。

## China Keeps Oil Buying in Check as Crude Prices Stay Above $90

### [Industries](https://www.bloomberg.com/industries)Energy

By [Rong Wei Neo](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXhJVqT3jL4/rong-wei-neo)August 21, 2026 at 12:59 PM GMT+8

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

### **Takeaways**

- China's refiners are keeping crude purchases below previous levels due to resilient oil prices.
- Purchases are expected to rise, but even the most bullish estimates put them at 9.9 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, still below last year's volumes.
- The speed of imports will depend on factors including the return of Middle East cargoes and Beijing's view of energy security.
Resilient oil prices are prompting China’s refiners to keep crude purchases below the lofty levels seen before the Iran war, even as analysts and industry consultancies estimate imports are edging back toward 10 million barrels a day.

China’s buying has been a focus for analysts trying to understand oil market movements over the past months. By reducing fuel shipments and [tapping](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-10/china-taps-commercial-oil-stockpiles-to-help-weather-gulf-shock) ample commercial stockpiles through the worst months of the war, the world’s top oil importer helped to keep crude prices far below early, catastrophic forecasts. The question now is whether it will go back to the enthusiasm of 2025.

That may be a distant prospect.

Purchases should rise as much as 1.2 million barrels a day from the third quarter into the last three months of 2026, according to Rystad Energy, Energy Aspects and FGE NexantECA, continuing to recover as some Middle East flows resume and refinery runs increase.

Yet even the most bullish estimates put purchases at just 9.9 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter — still far below volumes of 12 million to 13 million touched last year, when China was building inventory and supporting global prices.

The most obvious factor here is price. Last year, the average crude price was below $70 — as opposed to current levels above $90, high enough to cool demand.

But there are others, including difficulties in securing the right crude. Many Chinese refineries are set up to run medium sour grades from the Middle East, where supplies remain costly and scarce. Buyers have rushed for[ Iraqi](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/chinese-refiners-snap-up-iraqi-oil-as-more-supplies-exit-hormuz) and [Saudi](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/china-buys-saudi-crude-in-rare-tender-as-imports-stay-muted) barrels but received no cargoes in the latest tender awarded by [Abu Dhabi National Oil Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TAQA%20UH%20Equity)

Increased purchases from Brazil and Angola may not be enough to fully replace those lost flows, lost Middle Eastern flows, said Jianan Sun, an analyst at Energy Aspects.

“With the prolonged war and Middle East flows still disrupted, high crude prices are preventing larger demand drivers like stockpiling from taking place,” said Sun, adding domestic fuel price caps prevent refiners from fully passing higher crude costs to consumers, limiting their willingness to pay up.

Then there is the lingering question of whether some demand has disappeared entirely during the war, as more Chinese drivers switch to electric vehicles, and even trucks go green — long-running secular trends.

The good news for Beijing is that China still has a sizable buffer. Crude inventories have fallen nearly 8% from their May peak but remain at about 1.16 billion barrels across commercial and strategic reserves, according to data intelligence firm Kpler. Even just commercial stockpiles, used to absorb much of the war shock, can still provide refiners with room for maneuver.

The speed of imports will depend in part on the Strait of Hormuz, and the return of Middle East cargoes, with forecasts including those from FGE NexantECA factoring in a degree of recovery. For now, while more oil is getting through the waterway, flows are still a fraction of prewar levels.

It will also depend on the wider health of the sector. A first snapshot from China’s state-owned refiners is due from Sunday onwards, as the industry’s heavyweights report interim financial earnings, providing detail on what prices have done for fuel sales and for upstream revenue.

Sinopec will be the first to report, with earnings due on Aug. 23.

Finally, timing and scale may ultimately depend on Beijing’s view of energy security — a key priority — as the conflict in the Persian Gulf drags on. That alone could determine significant changes to inventory management and minimum stockpile requirements.

“Energy security will underpin China’s strategic response to an extended conflict,” said Lin Ye, vice president of oil markets at Rystad Energy.
