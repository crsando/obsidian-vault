> 中国风力涡轮机制造商明阳虽在英国海上风电遭国家安全审查受阻，仍积极拓展欧洲市场。
> •明阳欧洲首席执行官霍雷肖·埃弗斯表示将在欧洲长期发展，并已从维斯塔斯、西门子歌美飒等竞争对手挖角高管，将研发职能转移至欧洲。
> •英国政府以国家安全风险为由拒绝明阳海上风电项目，使其在苏格兰建厂计划受阻。
> •欧盟已启动补贴调查，并引入规则限制中国产涡轮机参与公共补贴项目。
> •明阳与英国Octopus Energy合作，探索陆上风电安装计划，并强调中国政府无法获取数据或远程关闭。

### 中文译文

## 中国风力涡轮机制造商瞄准欧洲，尽管面临政治障碍

![Ming Yang is among the highest-profile of several turbine makers that have helped turn China into the world’s biggest wind market © Bloomberg](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A9a162591-d24c-4325-8efc-1f40ac893d2a?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

明阳（Ming Yang）是几家风力涡轮机制造商中知名度最高的之一，这些制造商帮助中国成为全球最大的风电市场 © Bloomberg

中国最大风力涡轮机制造商之一的欧洲首席执行官表示，该公司将在欧洲长期发展——尽管其涡轮机因国家安全原因被英国海上风电市场拒之门外。

霍雷肖·埃弗斯（Horatio Evers）曾任德国化工巨头巴斯夫能源部门负责人，他于9月被上海上市的明阳聘用，负责领导公司向欧洲扩张。他已开始从竞争对手公司挖角高管，将研发职能转移到欧洲，尝试建立本地制造，并定期出现在行业活动中，以赢得人心和信任。

“这不是一次性的尝试，我们在这里所做的，”他在接受英国《金融时报》采访时表示。“明阳是全球第三大风力涡轮机制造商，他们需要多元化的产品组合——我们将进入世界上最具吸引力和最大的市场；这显然是欧洲、东南亚等。”

他已聘请维斯塔斯全球海上风电法律主管科琳娜·勃兰特（Corinna Brandt）担任欧洲总法律顾问，这是今年最引人注目的招聘之一，此外还有西门子歌美飒前海上风电技术开发主管彼得·沃尔夫（Peter Wolf）和瓦腾福前海上风电资源主管萨沙·埃布斯洛（Sascha Erbslöh）。

该公司在上海的市值为239亿元人民币（合36亿美元），同时还在伦敦交易全球存托凭证。它是最知名的几家风力涡轮机制造商之一，这些制造商帮助中国成为全球最大的风电市场，并正在向全球扩张。

上月，总部位于中国的三一重能宣布其在欧洲的首台大型风力涡轮机，是位于西班牙的6.25兆瓦型号，为食品企业Grupo Jorge供电。

但迄今为止，中国在欧洲风力涡轮机行业的存在感很小：根据全球风能理事会这一贸易组织的数据，去年中国制造商向欧洲供应了446兆瓦的涡轮机，不到当年新增总量的3%。

他们要想取得进展将面临艰巨任务：欧洲政界人士和行业担心本国非常成功的风电领军企业（如维斯塔斯和西门子歌美飒）会输给中国的绿色科技巨头，后者已经摧毁了欧洲的太阳能产业。

他们还担心所谓的网络安全风险以及中国政府可能对总部位于中国的公司施加的影响，即使是像明阳这样并非国有的公司也不例外。

今年5月，英国政府表示，在海上项目中使用明阳的风力发电机组存在国家安全风险，无法支持其使用，这挫败了该公司在苏格兰建设一座15亿英镑工厂的计划。

英国没有详细说明作出这一决定的理由，但英国《金融时报》此前曾报道，特朗普政府曾提出担忧。明阳董事长张传卫[随后警告](https://www.ft.com/content/3e07f0e2-b255-48ee-9f02-298805f9cbf6?syn-25a6b1a6=1)英国政府，这项投资存在“政治化”风险。

在欧洲大陆，欧洲已启动补贴调查，并正在引入多项规则，限制中国产涡轮机参与获得公共补贴的项目。

一些行业高管呼吁限制[“非西方”](https://www.ft.com/content/dd5c42b9-ff1f-4532-aee5-bdf838aece4d?syn-25a6b1a6=1)涡轮机制造商向欧盟销售。

去年8月，明阳还与开发商Luxcara“经双方同意”分道扬镳。Luxcara曾选择该公司作为德国一个海上风电项目的首选供应商，但该交易受到审查，最终改用西门子歌美飒。

然而，明阳已与英国最大的家庭能源供应商Octopus Energy建立了合作关系。后者由知名企业家兼政府顾问Greg Jackson领导。两家公司正在探索在英国安装陆上涡轮机的计划，Octopus正在设计一个系统，将涡轮机的访问、数据和固件置于英国管理之下。

明阳表示，中国政府无法获取其在欧洲的涡轮机数据，也无法使用“关闭开关”使其失效。埃弗斯表示，他希望英国首相换成安迪·伯纳姆可能会带来机会，重新审视其海上产品的立场。

## Chinese wind turbine manufacturers target Europe despite political obstacles

Ming Yang is among the highest-profile of several turbine makers that have helped turn China into the world’s biggest wind market © Bloomberg

The European chief executive of one of China’s largest wind turbine manufacturers says the company is in the continent for the long haul — despite its turbines being shut out of the UK’s offshore market on national security grounds.

Horatio Evers, the former head of German chemicals giant BASF’s energy division, was hired by Shanghai-listed Ming Yang in September to lead its expansion into the continent. He has set about poaching executives from rival companies, moving R&D functions to Europe, trying to establish local manufacturing, and regularly appearing at industry events as he tries to win hearts and minds.

“This is not a one-time try, what we are doing here,” he told the FT in an interview. “Ming Yang is the third-biggest [wind turbine] manufacturer in the world and they need to diversify their portfolio — we are going into the most attractive and biggest markets in the world; this is clearly Europe, south-east Asia, and so on.”

He has hired Vestas’s head of legal of global offshore wind, Corinna Brandt, as general counsel for Europe, among the highest-profile of his recruits this year, alongside Siemens Gamesa’s former head of technical offshore development, Peter Wolf, and Vattenfall’s former head of offshore wind resource, Sascha Erbslöh.

The company, which has a market cap of Rmb23.9bn ($3.6bn) in Shanghai and also trades global depositary receipts in London, is among the highest-profile of several turbine makers that have helped turn China into the world’s biggest wind market and are now expanding globally.

Last month, China-headquartered Sany Renewable Energy announced its first large wind turbine in Europe, a 6.25 megawatt model in Spain supplying electricity to food business Grupo Jorge.

But so far China’s presence in Europe’s wind turbine industry is small: last year, Chinese manufacturers supplied 446MW of turbines to Europe, according to figures from the Global Wind Energy Council trade group, or less than 3 per cent of the total added during the year.

They have an uphill task to gain ground: European politicians and industry are fearful about their own highly successful wind champions, such as Vestas and Siemens Gamesa, losing out to China’s green tech giants which have already laid waste to Europe’s solar industry.

They are also worried about alleged cyber security risks and the potential influence of the Chinese state on China-headquartered companies, even those such as Ming Yang, which are not state owned.

In May, the UK government said there were national security risks associated with the use of Ming Yang’s wind turbines in offshore projects and it could not support their use, thwarting the company’s plans for a £1.5bn factory in Scotland.

The UK did not elaborate on its reasons for the decision but the FT had previously reported that the Trump administration had raised concerns. Zhang Chuanwei, Ming Yang’s chair, [subsequently warned](https://www.ft.com/content/3e07f0e2-b255-48ee-9f02-298805f9cbf6?syn-25a6b1a6=1) the UK government about the risks of “politicisation” of the investment.

On the continent, Europe has launched subsidy probes and is introducing several rules that would restrict Chinese-made turbines from projects that receive public subsidies.

Some industry executives have called for restrictions on [“non-western”](https://www.ft.com/content/dd5c42b9-ff1f-4532-aee5-bdf838aece4d?syn-25a6b1a6=1) turbine makers selling into the EU.

In August last year, Ming Yang also parted ways “by mutual agreement” with developer Luxcara, which had picked the Chinese company as preferred supplier for an offshore wind project in Germany but drew scrutiny over the deal and ultimately switched to Siemens Gamesa.

Ming Yang has forged a tie-up, however, with Octopus Energy, the UK’s largest household energy supplier, led by high-profile entrepreneur and government adviser Greg Jackson. The two companies are exploring plans to install onshore turbines in Britain and Octopus is designing a system that would keep the turbines’ access, data and firmware under British management.

Ming Yang says the Chinese government would not be able to access data from its turbines in Europe or use a “kill switch” to disable them. Evers said he hoped the change of UK prime minister to Andy Burnham might open up an opportunity to review the position on its offshore products.
