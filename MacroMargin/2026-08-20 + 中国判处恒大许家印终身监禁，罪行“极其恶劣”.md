### **要点**

- 中国恒大集团创始人许家印被判处终身监禁，这标志着中国企业史上最戏剧性的兴衰故事之一画上句号。
- 许家印与涉及恒大的其他56人一同被判刑，其中包括他的儿子们。他的财产将被没收，其公司被合计罚款158.2亿元人民币。
- 许家印在中国法庭上对包括非法吸收公众存款和集资诈骗在内的指控认罪，当局认定他的崛起建立在欺骗之上，包括虚增资产和隐瞒负债。
[中国恒大集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3333%20HK%20Equity)创始人许家印被判处终身监禁，为中国企业史上最戏剧性的兴衰故事之一画上句号。

这位名誉扫地的房地产大亨曾是亚洲第二大富豪，在中国长期的房地产繁荣期间，他将恒大打造成了企业过度扩张的典型代表。但在2021年违约后帝国轰然倒塌，他落入了最终导致其垮台的官方调查法网。

许家印在法庭上明显苍老，头发花白，他与涉及恒大的56名其他人员一同被判刑，其中包括他的儿子许智健和许腾鹤。其资产将被没收，相关公司合计被罚款158.2亿元人民币（约24亿美元）。

此举为多年来中国最成功的商业故事之一拉上了帷幕。许家印在恒大扩张期间大肆举债，将其变成全球负债最重的开发商，同时将这家房地产巨头转型为一个庞大集团。

但中国当局认定，他的崛起建立在欺骗之上。他们表示，许家印实施了大规模金融欺诈，包括虚增资产和隐瞒负债。

今年4月，许家印在中国法庭上对非法吸收公众存款和集资诈骗等指控[认罪](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-14/china-evergrande-s-billionaire-founder-pleads-guilty-to-fraud)。

官方媒体新华社报道称：“犯罪数额特别巨大，情节特别恶劣，造成极其重大的经济损失，社会危害极其严重。”并补充说，恒大和许家印“必须依法严惩”。

恒大和许家印还被判犯有欺诈发行证券、违反信息披露规定以及行贿罪。此外，创始人被指控通过股利机制侵占公司资产。

这一判决并未结束围绕恒大的众多法律纠纷。该公司目前正处于清盘程序中，国内外大批债权人正在仔细审查其资产，希望收回部分沉重损失。

### **白手起家**

许家印（Hui）于1958年出生于河南省，在贫困中长大。受到邓小平经济改革计划前景的鼓舞，这位木匠之子于1992年辞去了一家钢铁公司的工作，后来开始在广东省会广州开发房地产。他随后于1996年创立了恒大，并乘着中国前所未有的房地产繁荣浪潮崛起。

恒大，在中文中意为“永久和伟大”，依靠大量借贷推动增长，成为同行中最大的美元债务借款人，并一度成为全国销售额最大的开发商。在其鼎盛时期，该公司在280个城市建造了1300多个项目。在房地产财富的强力推动下，其业务还扩展到瓶装水、职业足球和电动汽车等行业。

长期以来，恒大通过[动员中国内地和香港的富豪](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-08-29/rich-friends-who-helped-evergrande-tycoon-now-count-their-losses)购买公司股票和债券，避开了流动性恐慌。许家印还向自己的供应商和散户投资者寻求资金支持。但自2020年以来，北京对房地产行业的[打击](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-05-12/how-china-s-property-developers-got-into-such-a-mess-quicktake)限制了其借贷能力，实际上切断了他与信贷市场的联系。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481049c11c16ec11c39e4/3c281cc9-0b14-811b-a5df-fd5fb1828915-2795eba8690f1eed8f83b547fb8de6dd.png)

许家印受到指控，此前中国当局已对企业大亨们采取了一系列严厉行动。

[安邦集团控股公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1237583D:CH)前董事长吴小晖于2018年因欺诈和挪用公款被[判处](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-05-10/anbang-s-wu-sentenced-to-18-years-in-jail-for-10-billion-fraud)18年监禁。同年，中国华融资产管理股份有限公司董事长赖小民在腐败[丑闻](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-04-30/china-huarong-s-journey-from-safe-bet-to-bad-news-a-timeline)中被捕，后于2021年因包括贿赂在内的指控被执行死刑。几个月后，[海航集团有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HNAGRZ%20CH%20Equity)董事长陈峰与首席执行官谭向东因未明确罪行被[拘留](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-09-24/china-s-troubled-hna-group-says-chairman-ceo-detained-by-police)。

恒大在2021年底发生债务[违约](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-12-09/evergrande-defaults-for-first-time-as-china-debt-strains-spread)。在重组尝试失败后，于2024年在香港被下达清盘[令](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-29/what-s-next-for-china-evergrande-after-liquidation-order)，标志着该行业崩溃的重大升级。同年晚些时候，中国内地一家法院受理了针对其旗下一家主要境内子公司提出的[清盘申请](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-19/china-court-accepts-liquidation-hearing-against-evergrande-unit)。

许家印于2023年被中国警方带走，并处于所谓的“指定居所监视居住”之下——这是一种警察行动，正式程度低于拘留或逮捕。除了公司文件证实许家印被控制外，中国当局此后一直对其状况缄默不语。

2024年，中国监管机构指控恒大主要境内子公司通过提前确认销售收入，虚增收入超过5600亿元人民币，这一欺诈行为远超瑞幸咖啡公司的案件。随后，中国暂停了恒大长期审计师普华永道会计师事务所的运营六个月，并处以罚款。

许家印的已知财富大部分来源于他在恒大的控股股权以及自2009年公司在香港上市以来从公司获得的现金分红。根据彭博的计算，得益于公司慷慨的派息，许家印在过去十年中已入账超过70亿美元。

## China Sentences Evergrande’s Hui to Life for ‘Heinous’ Crime

China Sentences Evergrande Founder Hui to Life in Prison

WATCH: Hui Ka Yan has been sentenced to life in prison. Trista Xinyi Luo has the details.*Source: Bloomberg*

By Bloomberg NewsAugust 20, 2026 at 12:16 PM GMT+8*Updated on August 20, 2026 at 6:07 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

### **Takeaways**

- China Evergrande Group’s founder Hui Ka Yan has been sentenced to life in prison, bringing an end to one of the most dramatic rise and fall stories in China’s corporate history.
- Hui was sentenced alongside 56 others involved in Evergrande, including his sons, and his assets will be confiscated, and his companies were fined a combined 15.82 billion yuan.
- Hui pleaded guilty in a Chinese court to charges including illegally taking public deposits and fundraising fraud, with authorities concluding that his rise was built on deception, including inflating assets and concealing liabilities.
[China Evergrande Group](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3333%20HK%20Equity)’s founder Hui Ka Yan has been sentenced to life in prison, bringing an end to one of the most dramatic rise and fall stories in China’s corporate history.

The disgraced property tycoon, once Asia’s second richest man, had turned Evergrande into a poster child of corporate excess during China’s long real estate boom. But after his empire came crashing down following a 2021 default, he was caught in a dragnet of official investigations that ultimately caused his downfall.

Hui, who [appeared](https://h.xinhuaxmt.com/vh512/share/13248733?docid=13248733&newstype=1001&d=13527d4) visibly aged in court with greying hair, was sentenced alongside 56 others involved in Evergrande, including his sons Xu Zhijian and Xu Tenghe. His assets will be confiscated, and his companies were fined a combined 15.82 billion yuan ($2.4 billion).

The move draws a curtain on what for years was one of China’s great success stories. Hui had binged on debt during Evergrande’s expansion, turning it into the world’s most indebted developer as he transformed the property giant into a sprawling conglomerate.

But Chinese authorities concluded that his rise was built on deception. They said Hui had perpetrated a massive financial fraud that included inflating assets and concealing liabilities.

In April, Hui had [pleaded guilty](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-14/china-evergrande-s-billionaire-founder-pleads-guilty-to-fraud) in a Chinese court to charges including illegally taking public deposits and fundraising fraud.

“The amount involved in the crime is exceptionally large, the circumstances are particularly heinous, exceptionally heavy economic losses have been caused, and the harm to society is extremely grave,” a report from state media outlet Xinhua wrote, adding that Evergrande and Hui “must be severely punished in accordance with the law.”

Evergrande and Hui were also found guilty of fraudulently issuing securities, violating information disclosure rules as well as bribery. Meanwhile, the founder was accused of embezzling company assets through dividend mechanisms.

The sentence doesn’t bring an end to the numerous legal fights surrounding Evergrande. The company is now in liquidation, with an army of creditors inside and outside of China poring over its assets in the hopes of recovering some of their heavy losses.

### **Rags to Riches**

Hui, also known as Xu Jiayin in Mandarin, was born in Henan province in 1958 and grew up in poverty. Encouraged by the prospect of Deng Xiaoping’s economic reform plan, the son of a wood cutter quit his job at a steel firm in 1992 and later began developing property in Guangzhou, the provincial capital of Guangdong. He then founded Evergrande in 1996 and rode the wave of China’s unprecedented real estate boom.

Evergrande, which means “permanence and greatness” in Chinese, relied on heavy borrowing to fuel its growth, becoming the largest dollar-debt borrower among its peers and for a time the country’s [biggest](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2017-07-25/evergrande-s-half-year-profit-to-triple-as-property-prices-soar) developer by sales. During its prime, the firm built more than 1,300 projects across 280 cities. Supercharged by real estate riches, it also expanded its reach into industries ranging from bottled water to professional soccer and electric vehicles.

For a long time, Evergrande skirted liquidity scares by [tapping tycoons](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-08-29/rich-friends-who-helped-evergrande-tycoon-now-count-their-losses) in China and Hong Kong to buy the firm’s stock and bonds. Hui also turned to his own suppliers and retail investors for financial backing. But Beijing’s [crackdown](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-05-12/how-china-s-property-developers-got-into-such-a-mess-quicktake) on the property sector since 2020 capped its borrowing capacity, effectively cutting Hui off from credit markets.

Hui’s charges follow a long list of harsh actions by Chinese authorities against corporate magnates.

[Anbang Group Holdings Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1237583D:CH)’s former Chairman, Wu Xiaohui, was [sentenced](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-05-10/anbang-s-wu-sentenced-to-18-years-in-jail-for-10-billion-fraud) to 18 years in prison for fraud and embezzlement in 2018. That same year, China Huarong Asset Management Co. Chairman Lai Xiaomin was arrested amid a corruption [scandal](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-04-30/china-huarong-s-journey-from-safe-bet-to-bad-news-a-timeline) before being executed in 2021 on charges including bribery. [HNA Group Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HNAGRZ%20CH%20Equity) Chairman Chen Feng was [detained](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-09-24/china-s-troubled-hna-group-says-chairman-ceo-detained-by-police) a few months later along with Chief Executive Officer Tan Xiangdong for unspecified crimes.

Evergrande [defaulted](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-12-09/evergrande-defaults-for-first-time-as-china-debt-strains-spread) on its debt in late 2021. Following failed restructuring attempts, it was given a winding up[ order](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-29/what-s-next-for-china-evergrande-after-liquidation-order) in Hong Kong in 2024, marking a significant escalation in the unraveling of the sector. Later that year, a mainland Chinese court accepted a [liquidation application](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-19/china-court-accepts-liquidation-hearing-against-evergrande-unit) filed against one of its major onshore units.

Hui was [taken away ](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-09-27/evergrande-s-billionaire-chairman-hui-is-under-police-surveillance)by Chinese police in 2023 and held under so-called residential surveillance — a type of police action that falls short of formal detention or arrest. Apart from a company filing confirming Hui being put under control, Chinese authorities had remained tight-lipped about his status since then.

In 2024, Chinese regulators [accused](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-19/china-accuses-evergrande-of-78-billion-fraud-among-worst-ever) Evergrande’s main onshore unit of inflating more than 560 billion yuan of revenue by recognizing sales in advance, an alleged fraud that dwarfs that of [Luckin Coffee Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-04-29/credit-suisse-dream-client-becomes-nightmare-in-luckin-scandal) and SEC Case Against Arthur Andersen Partners Dismissed by Judge China then suspended the operations of [PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/10182Z:US), Evergrande’s long-time auditor, for six months and imposed fines.

Much of Hui’s known wealth was derived from his controlling stake in Evergrande and the cash dividends he’s received from the company since its 2009 listing in Hong Kong. Hui had pocketed more than $7 billion over the past decade, thanks to the firm’s generous payouts, according to Bloomberg calculations.
