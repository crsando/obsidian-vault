> 📌 **核心摘要**
>
> 人民币国际化面临最艰难的投资攀登，行政推动无法让世界自愿持有人民币。
> •文章将人民币国际化分为贸易、借贷、投资三个难度递增的阶段，中国在前两项取得进展，投资阶段刚刚开始。
> •中国通过贸易结算和银行信贷推动人民币国际化，人民币占中国货物贸易比重自2018年翻倍，并成为全球第二大贸易融资货币。
> •外国机构持有中国国债面临流动性差、对冲工具稀缺、资金汇回摩擦等问题，外国持有在岸债券不足总量的2%。
> •2025年6月陆家嘴论坛推出多项措施：央行流动性支持机制、香港中国国债期货、Omniclear离岸结算存管机构，旨在完善基础设施。
> •但回购便利仅限官方，央行酌情权存在政治风险，网络效应难以复制，且在岸收益率过低，人民币资产吸引力有限。
> •人民币成为储备货币需要让渡控制权，而这与北京拒绝放弃控制相矛盾；全球经济分裂虽有利，但行政推动无法回答根本问题。

**🔑 关键词**：人民币国际化、储备货币、资本账户

> 💡 **政策解读与建议**
>
> 主旨
> 人民币国际化的核心挑战在于让世界自愿持有人民币资产，而非仅用于支付；行政推动无法替代市场信心与控制权的让渡。
> 
> 指出问题
> 1. 投资阶段尚未启动，外国持有在岸债券不足2%，且央行储备占比约2%持平。
> 2. 在岸流动性差、对冲工具稀缺、资金汇回存在摩擦。
> 3. 在岸收益率降至全球最低之列，缺乏吸引力。
> 4. 控制权让渡与北京拒绝放弃控制存在矛盾。
> 
> 主要观点
> 1. 人民币国际化分贸易、借贷、投资三阶段，前两者靠行政推动取得成效，投资阶段是真正考验。
> 2. 美元武器化推动人民币贸易融资份额提升，但持有意愿取决于深度交易、质押融资和自由对冲的能力。
> 3. 2025年6月陆家嘴论坛的措施（央行流动性支持、香港国债期货、Omniclear）是首次连贯的基础设施建设，但均为次优选择。
> 4. 官方回购便利、央行酌情权、网络效应等制约因素依然严峻；经济上收益率过低，仅剩分散化动机。
> 
> 政策建议
> 短期完善基础设施：加快在岸市场深度与流动性建设，丰富对冲工具，改善资金汇回便利。
> 中期提升资产吸引力：在可控范围内提高收益率或创新产品，避免单边依赖分散化需求。
> 长期需让渡控制权：明确资本账户开放路径和市场价格形成机制，建立可信的规则承诺，以说服官方和非官方持有者。
> 并行推进离岸市场：支持香港离岸人民币产品创新，利用Omniclear等设施构建平行结算网络。

### 中文译文

## 人民币最艰难的一英里才刚刚开始

![Whether the renminbi becomes a currency the world chooses to save in — rather than one it is merely paid in — is a question an administrative push cannot answer © Bloomberg](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A794cb38a-fc03-4c7c-a52e-c7815c9008e5?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

人民币能否成为世界选择储值的货币——而不仅仅是用于支付的货币——是行政推动无法回答的问题 © Bloomberg

[**Alicia García-Herrero**](https://www.ft.com/stream/ffd32e18-ecf4-4068-b111-d0436ddbb6e5)

发布于16小时前

*作者是法国外贸银行(Natixis)亚太区首席经济学家，布鲁盖尔研究所(Bruegel)高级研究员*

关于人民币全球崛起的争论通常搞错了顺序。它把国际化视为一次单一的攀登，以储备货币地位为顶峰。

实际上，有三个难度迥异的独立攀登：贸易、借贷，以及最艰难的投资。15年来，中国在前两项上取得了很大进展。最后一项——说服外国持有人民币作为投资——才刚刚开始，去年6月在上海举行的陆家嘴论坛为未来之路[奠定了基础](https://www.ft.com/content/cab6c916-1127-4254-a68e-62d66bb37e85?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。

正统路径是通过资本账户：开放市场，出现对外赤字，让非居民积累本币。但中国不能也不会走这条路。因此，它通过其他方式推动人民币走出去——贸易结算和本国银行提供的信贷。

两者都奏效了。自2018年以来，人民币在中国货物贸易中的占比大约翻了一番，该货币目前在全球贸易融资中排名第二，超过欧元——这既得益于美元的武器化，也得益于中国作为全球最大大宗商品买家的影响力。融资紧随其后：人民币成为亚洲最便宜的融资货币之一，中国银行一直在增加对海外客户的贷款。

但持有一种货币是另一回事。一种货币成为储备资产，不是在被使用时，而是在被自愿持有时——因为以其计价的资产可以在景气和不景气时深度交易、质押融资和随意对冲。这就是为什么美国国债处于体系的核心。

![图片](https://www.ft.com/content/undefined)

持有中国国债的外国机构并未得到这种安逸。在岸流动性远低于美国国债市场，对冲工具稀缺，资金汇回仍面临储备管理者无法忽视的摩擦。这种迟疑体现在数据上：外国持有的在岸债券不足总量的2%，低于3%以上的峰值，人民币央行储备占比也在2%左右且持平。

6月论坛上公布的措施正是要解决这些问题：这是在不触及可兑换性的情况下，构建缺失基础设施的首次连贯尝试。中国人民银行6月宣布的面向外国和国际货币当局的流动性支持机制，模仿了美联储的类似结构。

此外，香港将推出中国国债期货，填补了让大型全球资产管理公司一直观望的对冲工具缺口。总部位于香港的新兴证券存管机构Omniclear专注于离岸人民币结算，可能成为Euroclear的替代选择。每一项都解决了真正阻碍持有（而不仅仅是使用）人民币的障碍，可能促进其作为投资货币的使用。

然而，制约因素依然严酷，每一项修复都是次优选择。回购便利仅向官方部门开放。而且，由发行央行酌情提供的支持机制，其有效性取决于这种酌情权——那些因美元被用作政策工具而进行多元化配置的储备管理者，不会看不到一种货币的政治属性，而发行这种货币的央行拥有大得多的干预空间。平行的结算轨道在其他人使用之前毫无分量；使Euroclear和美元基础设施不可或缺的网络效应无法通过立法凭空创造。

而经济因素指向错误方向：在岸收益率已降至如此之低，以至于中国债券如今提供全球最低的回报之一，因此对任何投资者都缺乏吸引力。剩下的理由仅仅是分散化。

这正是北京的赌注——对至少部分官方持有者而言，远离美元的意愿将超过这些摩擦。在边际上，这可能是对的。但这遇到了该项目核心的矛盾。贸易计价可以通过行政手段设计，信贷可以通过法令扩张；但对人民币资产的真实持有需求无法如此。这需要让渡控制权——对价格、对退出、对资本账户本身的控制——并说服他人相信，当控制变得不便时，这种控制不会被重新主张。但当然，控制正是北京拒绝放弃的东西。

前进方向毋庸置疑，日益分裂的全球经济正在为北京做大量工作。但人民币是否会成为世界选择用以储蓄的货币——而非仅仅被用于支付的货币——是一个行政推动无法回答的问题。这是最艰难的一英里，而且才刚刚开始。

### English Original

## The renminbi’s hardest mile is only now beginning

Whether the renminbi becomes a currency the world chooses to save in — rather than one it is merely paid in — is a question an administrative push cannot answer © Bloomberg

[**Alicia García-Herrero**](https://www.ft.com/stream/ffd32e18-ecf4-4068-b111-d0436ddbb6e5)

Published16 hours ago

*The writer is chief economist for Asia Pacific at Natixis and senior research fellow at Bruegel*

The debate about the renminbi’s global rise usually gets the sequencing wrong. It treats internationalisation as a single ascent, with reserve-currency status at the summit.

In practice there are three separate climbs of very different difficulty: trade, borrowing and, hardest of all, investment. China has made much progress on the first two over 15 years. The last — persuading foreigners to hold renminbi as an investment — has only now started, and last June’s Lujiazui Forum in Shanghai [set the stage](https://www.ft.com/content/cab6c916-1127-4254-a68e-62d66bb37e85?syn-25a6b1a6=1) for the way forward.

The orthodox path runs through the capital account: open your markets, run external deficits, let non-residents accumulate your currency. But China cannot and will not take that route. So it has pushed the renminbi outward by other means — trade settlement and credit extended by its own banks.

Both have worked. The renminbi share of China’s goods trade has roughly doubled since 2018 and the currency now ranks second in global trade finance, ahead of the euro — driven as much by the weaponisation of the dollar as by China’s leverage as the world’s largest commodity buyer. Funding has followed: the renminbi became one of Asia’s cheapest funding currencies and Chinese banks have been increasing lending to overseas clients.

But holding a currency is a different act. A currency becomes a reserve asset not when it is used but when it is willingly kept — because the assets denominated in it can be traded deeply, financed against and hedged at will, in good times and bad. That is why US Treasuries sit at the centre of the system.

A foreign institution holding Chinese government bonds has had neither comfort. Onshore liquidity is far thinner than the US Treasury market’s, hedging instruments are scarce, and repatriating proceeds still runs into frictions a reserve manager cannot ignore. The reluctance shows in the data: foreign holdings of onshore bonds are under 2 per cent of the total and down from a peak above 3 per cent and central bank reserves in renminbi are around 2 per cent as well as flat.

This is what measures unveiled at the June forum are meant to address: the first coherent attempt to build the missing plumbing without touching convertibility. A liquidity backstop for foreign and international monetary authorities announced by the PBoC in June models a similar structure to the Federal Reserve.

In addition, Hong Kong is launching Chinese government bond futures, closing the gap in hedging facilities that has kept large global managers on the sidelines. And a nascent securities depository based in Hong Kong, Omniclear focuses on offshore renminbi settlements, which could become an alternative to Euroclear. Each tackles a real obstacle to holding, rather than merely using, the currency, potentially fostering its use as investment currency.

Yet the constraints remain unforgiving, and every fix is a second-best. The repo facility is open only to the official sector. And a backstop supplied at the discretion of the issuing central bank is only as good as that discretion — and reserve managers diversifying because the dollar was turned into an instrument of policy will not be blind to the politics of a currency whose issuer has far more room to intervene. A parallel settlement rail carries no weight until others use it; the network effects that make Euroclear and the dollar’s plumbing indispensable cannot be legislated into existence.

And the economics point the wrong way: onshore yields have fallen so far that Chinese bonds now offer some of the world’s lowest returns, so they hold little appeal for any investor. What is left is the case for diversification alone.

That is Beijing’s bet — that the desire to move away from the dollar will outweigh these frictions for at least some official holders. At the margin it may be right. But it runs into the contradiction at the heart of the project. Trade invoicing can be engineered and credit extended by fiat; genuine demand to hold renminbi assets cannot. It requires ceding control — over prices, over exit, over the capital account itself — and convincing others the control will not be reasserted the moment it becomes inconvenient. But, of course, control is what Beijing refuses to give up.

The direction of travel is not in doubt, and a fracturing global economy is doing much of Beijing’s work for it. But whether the renminbi becomes a currency the world chooses to save in — rather than one it is merely paid in — is a question an administrative push cannot answer. It is the hardest mile, and it has only just begun.
