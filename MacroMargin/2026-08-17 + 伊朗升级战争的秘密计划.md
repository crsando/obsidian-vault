### 中文译文

## 伊朗升级战争的秘密计划

![An anti-US billboard in central Tehran.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14810a969ec7d6ede74db2/3bf81cc9-0b14-8119-91f8-d8de1dfe7917-ddd99ce4699d4e0a277e8209d1760df1.jpg)

德黑兰市中心的反美广告牌。ABEDIN TAHERKENAREH/EPA/SHUTTERSTOCK

6月中旬，特朗普总统与[伊朗](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran)签署了一份谅解备忘录后，政府官员们纷纷出面争取支持，希望这项协议能重新开放霍尔木兹海峡，并开始逐步结束战争。

据伊朗和阿拉伯官员称，伊朗强硬派领导人在德黑兰密会，提出了一个不同的计划。在他们看来，这项协议很可能只是美国和以色列为缓解全球经济压力、为日后更大规模攻击争取时间的一种尝试。他们没有寄希望于谈判，而是利用过去两个月为一场更大的战斗做准备。

他们的努力包括：让强大的伊斯兰革命卫队更多地控制国家正规军，任命在与[伊拉克](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iraq)的战争和过去国内镇压中经验丰富的强硬老兵担任关键职务，扩大国内反间谍行动，并提高导弹和无人机的产量。领导层迅速掌握了主动权，袭击船只以加强伊朗对霍尔木兹海峡的控制，并将战场扩大到红海——沙特阿拉伯一直利用红海来绕过伊朗对波斯湾的封锁。

![Strikes by Iran-backed Houthi rebels on the city of Moktah, Yemen, on Aug. 9 killed at least 11 people and damaged the city’s port.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14810a969ec7d6ede74db2/3bf81cc9-0b14-819c-b529-ff98324722e5-e25f27aa27b7294b0b81f1f973c7d53c.jpg)

8月9日，伊朗支持的胡塞叛军对也门穆克塔市的袭击造成至少11人死亡，并破坏了该市的港口。Khaled Ziad/AFP/Getty Images

阿拉伯情报官员已经收集到证据——包括伊朗与也门、伊拉克等国民兵盟友之间的通讯——表明伊朗强硬派领导层内部发生了战略转变，准备让部队扩大战争，提高美国的代价，知情官员如是说。伊朗领导人日益讨论在敌方领土上的进攻行动，这让科威特等较弱的海湾国家感到震惊。

他们的首要目标是造成足够的痛苦，以确保伊朗在[去年的12天战争](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-iraq-war-lessons-israel-244acefe?mod=article_inline)和持续冲突中所遭受的那种攻击不再重演。

“伊朗国内也普遍认为，主要战争尚未开始，”德黑兰国防分析师、与伊朗政府关系密切的穆罕默德·哈桑·桑塔拉什说。“迄今为止我们看到的情况，越来越被解读为‘切香肠’战术——在更大对抗之前，通过有限、渐进的升级来削弱对手能力。”

伊朗的备战表明，华盛顿和德黑兰对战争这一阶段的看法存在巨大分歧。这种分歧促使伊朗领导人采取强硬谈判立场，不愿达成协议，这始终令特朗普感到困惑，他[称他们疯了](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/inside-trumps-oval-office-decision-to-ditch-the-iran-ceasefire-b70a8b8b?mod=article_inline)并指责他们撒谎。

#### 谅解备忘录签署后的打击行动

伊朗的备战状态也凸显了双方之间严重的信任缺失，这很可能使双方无法在短期内达成持久协议以结束冲突。

伊朗的表态中不乏虚张声势。该国的军事和经济基础已遭到美国和以色列空袭的重创。包括伊朗总统马苏德·佩泽什基安在内的更为务实的领导层成员多次警告，伊朗需要通过谈判达成一项结束战争并解除制裁的协议，否则将面临经济崩溃。

但伊朗[重建基础设施并恢复进入导弹基地的能力](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/satellite-images-show-iran-is-rebuilding-fast-199978f7?mod=article_inline)的速度远超预期，这令熟悉进展的以色列官员感到担忧。伊朗还成功袭击了美军基地，并保留足够火力威胁船只和海湾能源基础设施。[阿拉伯官员正在准备](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/gulf-states-accept-a-new-normal-in-hormuz-iran-is-in-control-6db8908d?mod=article_inline)应对持续战斗和能源供应的长期中断。

对于该政权来说，“基线是战争，”前美国高级外交官、与伊朗进行核谈判的谈判代表艾伦·艾尔表示。“伊朗将保持战时状态，为下一次攻击做准备。”

6月17日，特朗普在法国凡尔赛宫与伊朗签署谅解备忘录时，最大的障碍似乎在于说服美国批评者，让他们相信这份备忘录对德黑兰并非过于慷慨。

该协议承诺给予制裁豁免，允许伊朗出售石油，获取数十亿美元被冻结的现金，并有望获得3000亿美元的重建基金。伊朗的主要义务是开放霍尔木兹海峡供航运通行，然后就其核计划进行真诚谈判。

三周后，即7月初，当伊朗开始向美国引导通过海峡的船只开火时，美国感到意外。

“来自伊朗的那些非常危险的人，他们有病，”特朗普说，他还称伊朗领导人是渣滓。“他们有问题。”

事实上，伊朗正在推行双轨政策。当他的外交官与美国寻求达成和平协议时，伊朗领导人穆杰塔巴·哈梅内伊正在采取措施，为可能由德黑兰自己发起的扩大对抗准备安全机构。

了解近期阿拉伯情报调查结果的官员表示，革命卫队利用谅解备忘录带来的平静，与该地区盟友民兵组织打好基础，以便在需要时升级攻击，并向伊拉克、也门和黎巴嫩派遣顾问讨论相关计划。

包括截获通信在内的情报显示，革命卫队向[也门](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/yemen)胡塞武装控制的地区派遣指挥官，计划与同样受伊朗支持的伊拉克民兵组织协调，加大对沙特的对抗力度。

官员们表示，革命卫队监督将导弹、海军武器和无人机发射器部署到俯瞰红海的位置，还向也门武装分子提供情报、包括沙特港口和能源设施在内的目标清单，以及如何躲避沙特报复的建议。

[结果是一场战斗](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/a-truce-in-yemen-unravels-threatening-to-widen-mideast-war-d0b2b678?mod=article_inline)，并持续升级到7月底。胡塞武装袭击了由沙特阿拉伯支持的也门政府军，并宣布连接红海与世界市场的曼德海峡对沙特阿拉伯关闭。该武装组织向沙特船只开火，使沙特王国的石油出口努力复杂化，并增加了全球市场的供应风险。

大约在同一时间，沙特阿拉伯表示，伊拉克境内受伊朗支持的民兵组织对其石油设施发动了[无人机袭击](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/saudi-arabia-blames-iran-backed-militias-for-attack-on-oil-facilities-eb6cf40c?mod=article_inline)。

革命卫队威胁海湾国家称，如果美国打击伊朗境内的类似设施，他们将摧毁这些国家的能源设施——官员们称，革命卫队甚至向其阿拉伯同行发送了详细的目标清单。

伊朗还加大了对美军的攻击。在冲突间歇期，德黑兰向约旦发射了五枚弹道导弹，[让美国措手不及](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-gambles-on-escalation-with-surprise-attack-on-u-s-forces-72cfd422?mod=article_inline)。这次袭击发生在另一场伊朗在约旦发动的袭击两週后，那场袭击[造成三名美国军人死亡](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-missile-struck-housing-for-u-s-troops-at-jordan-base-9f5b8120?mod=article_inline)。

海湾国家官员看到攻击范围扩大、伊朗导弹避开美国防御并更精确地击中约旦、科威特和巴林的目标，愈发感到警觉。战斗持续到8月，过去两周内，伊朗袭击了六艘试图穿越霍尔木兹海峡的阿联酋船只。

![A team carries the remains of a U.S. servicemember who was believed to be killed by an Iranian airstrike in Jordan.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14810a969ec7d6ede74db2/3bf81cc9-0b14-81fb-963b-d4a60600bd62-9515ba17091ede1e1539717f23c50b92.jpg)

“我们充分利用了停火期，”革命卫队发言人侯赛因·莫赫比准将（Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebbi）7月在接受卫队附属的塔斯尼姆通讯社采访时说。“我们增强了能力，加快了导弹生产速度，并提高了导弹精度。”

幕后，最高领袖哈梅内伊（Khamenei）正在部署其部队，准备进行更具攻击性的战斗。在一周前公布的重组中，他安排了七名关键安全领导人，负责掌管经过改组的军事与镇压机构。

这些任命伴随着激进的目标，包括收紧军事指挥链、将意识形态民兵转变为社区情报机构，并强调采取进攻方式。

穆赫森·雷扎伊（Mohsen Rezaei）曾在1980年代与伊拉克的战争中领导革命卫队，他被任命为伊朗最高决策机构——最高国家安全委员会（Supreme National Security Council）的秘书，并将在该委员会中担任哈梅内伊的代表。过去一周，该委员会阻止了逐步开放霍尔木兹海峡协议的进展。

为加强国内安全，哈梅内伊让资深亲信侯赛因·塔伊布（Hossein Taeb）重新执掌巴斯基（Basij）。塔伊布曾在2009年领导对抗议活动的致命镇压。在充分显示出以色列情报机构对伊朗渗透得多么彻底的两场战争之后，最高领袖交给他一项任务：把这支志愿力量转变为一个“人民情报网络”。

伊朗政府顾问认为，下一阶段的对抗将超越常规战争，包括外部势力试图煽动国内动荡的做法。政府曾在1月残酷镇压因国家经济危机引发的抗议活动，并担心在美国压力下局势持续恶化只是时间问题，届时示威者将再次走上街头。

哈梅内伊还通过将两支军事力量的控制权统一交由准将阿里·阿卜杜拉希（Ali Abdollahi）负责，巩固了革命卫队对正规军的影响力。阿卜杜拉希是革命卫队资深高级指挥官和军事领导人，曾积极主张对波斯湾周边设有美国军事基地的阿拉伯国家发动攻击。

新革命卫队总司令艾哈迈德·瓦希迪（Ahmad Vahidi）自3月以来一直非正式担任该职，周一任命他的法令正式授予他这一职务，并赋予他“对敌发动强大进攻行动”的任务。次日，瓦希迪的顾问穆罕默德·礼萨·纳格迪将军（Mohammad Reza Naghdi）表示，这支准军事力量必须做好准备，将行动推进到敌方领土。

“瓦希迪的晋升与领导层准备让安全机构进入持续对抗期、而非回归和平时期正常政治的做法相符，”田纳西大学查塔努加分校（University of Tennessee at Chattanooga）的伊朗安全事务专家赛义德·戈尔卡尔（Saeid Golkar）表示。

伊斯兰革命卫队（Revolutionary Guard）——[一支近20万人的武装力量](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/the-iranian-force-built-to-defend-the-regime-now-faces-the-ultimate-test-8a1a2cfe?mod=article_inline)——已制定进一步升级的新计划，包括先发制人的打击，据伊朗官员和公开声明称。其中选项包括：破坏波斯湾的互联网电缆，在科威特和巴林等有什叶派社群的国家制造政治动荡，甚至可能在科威特开展地面行动。

科威特认真对待这些风险。5月初，这个[海湾小酋长国](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/inside-the-tiny-oil-sheikhdom-cut-off-by-the-iran-war-f2b1f00a?mod=article_inline)称其安全部队拦截了六名革命卫队士兵，他们用租来的渔船登上一座科威特岛屿，该岛与伊朗之间仅隔着一条狭窄的伊拉克领土带。一些伊朗政治人物曾暗示，如果美国威胁到伊朗领土，例如[哈尔克岛](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/us-israel-iran-war-news-2026/card/map-of-kharg-island-YDCPXR2SiM6hkGNG3Z8c?mod=article_inline)，革命卫队可能通过伊拉克南部发动地面袭击。

一些海湾官员表示，伊朗最近的行为加剧了人们对战后与这个规模更大、新近更具攻击性的邻国关系的不确定性。他们说，仍难以评估美国和以色列对革命卫队能力造成的损害程度。尽管如此，该组织显然没有被击败或威慑住，而且它对霍尔木兹海峡的控制增强了其地区地位。

这一现实促使海湾国家支持甚至不利的协议，这些协议将承认伊朗对海峡的监督权，前提是伊朗停止进一步升级。但地区领导人也对德黑兰在回应外交接触时缺乏根本性改变感到越来越沮丧。

他们说，伊朗具有安全思维的领导层似乎对自己的要求越来越自信。

谈判中的调解人表示，伊朗外交官本月早些时候同意与阿曼达成协议，建立并监督通过霍尔木兹海峡的航线，这将使海峡逐步重新开放。但这些努力遭到革命卫队的阻挠，革命卫队又开始袭击船只。

## Iran’s Secret Plan to Escalate the War

An anti-US billboard in central Tehran. ABEDIN TAHERKENAREH/EPA/SHUTTERSTOCK

After President Trump signed a memorandum of understanding with [Iran](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran) in mid-June, administration officials fanned out to build support for an agreement they hoped would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and start winding down the war.

Iran’s hard-line leaders huddled in Tehran and came up with a different plan, according to Iranian and Arab officials. In their view the pact was likely just an attempt by the U.S. and Israel to take pressure off the global economy and buy time for a bigger attack down the road. Instead of putting faith in talks, they took the past two months to prepare for a bigger fight.

Their efforts include giving the powerful Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps more control of the country’s regular army, appointing hardened veterans of the war with [Iraq](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iraq) and past internal crackdowns to key posts, expanding domestic counterintelligence operations and ramping up production of missiles and drones. The leadership quickly seized the initiative, attacking ships to tighten Iran’s grip on Hormuz and expanding the battlefield to the Red Sea, which Saudi Arabia has used to get around Iran’s chokehold on the Persian Gulf.

Strikes by Iran-backed Houthi rebels on the city of Moktah, Yemen, on Aug. 9 killed at least 11 people and damaged the city’s port. Khaled Ziad/AFP/Getty Images

Arab intelligence officials have picked up evidence—including communications between Iran and militia allies in countries such as Yemen and Iraq—of a strategic shift inside the country’s hard-line leadership to get their forces ready to widen the war and raise the costs for the U.S., officials familiar with the findings say. Alarming weaker Gulf countries like Kuwait, Iran’s leaders are increasingly talking about offensive operations on enemy territory.

Their overriding goal is to inflict enough pain to ensure the kind of attacks Iran has endured with the [12-day war last year](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-iraq-war-lessons-israel-244acefe?mod=article_inline) and the continuing conflict aren’t repeated.

“There is also a widespread view in Iran that the main war has not yet begun,” said Mohammad Hassan Sangtarash, a Tehran-based defense analyst close to the Iranian government. “What we have seen so far is increasingly interpreted through the lens of ‘salami-slicing’ tactics—limited, incremental escalation designed to weaken capabilities before a larger confrontation.”

The Iranian preparations point to an enormous gulf between how Washington and Tehran are perceiving this stage of the war. The divergence has driven a hard-line negotiating stance and reluctance to close deals by Iran’s leaders that has persistently flummoxed Trump, who has [called them crazy](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/inside-trumps-oval-office-decision-to-ditch-the-iran-ceasefire-b70a8b8b?mod=article_inline) and accused them of lying.

Iran’s readiness to fight also highlights a deep lack of trust that is likely to keep the two sides from reaching a durable deal to end the conflict any time soon.

Iran’s proclamations include plenty of bravado. The country’s military and economic base have been battered by U.S. and Israeli airstrikes. More pragmatic members of the leadership including Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian have repeatedly warned Iran needs to negotiate a deal to end the war that brings sanctions relief or face the collapse of its economy.

But Iran is [rebuilding its infrastructure and restoring access to missile bases](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/satellite-images-show-iran-is-rebuilding-fast-199978f7?mod=article_inline) much more quickly than expected, a concern to Israeli officials who are familiar with the progress. It also has successfully targeted U.S. bases and retains enough firepower to threaten ships and Gulf energy infrastructure. [Arab officials are bracing](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/gulf-states-accept-a-new-normal-in-hormuz-iran-is-in-control-6db8908d?mod=article_inline) for continued fighting and an extended disruption of energy supplies.

For the regime, “the baseline is war,” said Alan Eyre, a former senior U.S. diplomat and nuclear negotiator with Iran. “Iran will remain on a wartime footing, preparing for a subsequent attack.”

When Trump signed the memorandum of understanding with Iran on June 17 at the French palace of Versailles, it looked like the biggest hurdle would be convincing American critics that it wasn’t overly generous to Tehran.

The deal promised sanctions waivers to let Iran sell its oil, access to billions of dollars in frozen cash and the prospect of a $300 billion fund to rebuild the country. Iran’s main obligation was to open the Strait of Hormuz to shipping traffic and then negotiate in good faith on its nuclear program.

President Trump signs a memorandum of understanding between the U.S. and Iran in Versailles, France, on June 17. White House/Planet Pix/ZUMA Press

When Iran started shooting at ships the U.S. was guiding through the strait three weeks later in early July, it caught the U.S. by surprise.

“The very dangerous people from Iran, they’re sick,” said Trump, who also called Iran’s leaders scum. “There’s something wrong with them.”

In fact Iran was pursuing a two-track policy. While his diplomats pursued a peace deal with the U.S., Iranian leader Mojtaba Khamenei was taking steps to prepare the security apparatus for an expanded confrontation that may be initiated by Tehran itself.

The Revolutionary Guard used the calm created by the memorandum of understanding to lay the groundwork with allied militias around the region to step up attacks if needed, sending advisers to Iraq, Yemen and Lebanon to discuss the plans, the officials familiar with recent Arab intelligence findings said.

The intelligence including intercepted communications showed the Revolutionary Guard sending commanders to areas controlled by [Yemen](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/yemen)’s Houthis to plan for a stepped-up confrontation with Saudi Arabia in coordination with Iraqi militias that also are backed by Iran.

The Revolutionary Guard supervised deployments of missiles, naval weapons and drone launchers to positions overlooking the Red Sea. It also provided the Yemeni militants with intelligence, target lists including Saudi ports and energy facilities, and advice on sheltering from Saudi retaliation, the officials said.

The [result was fighting](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/a-truce-in-yemen-unravels-threatening-to-widen-mideast-war-d0b2b678?mod=article_inline) that escalated through late July. The Houthis struck Yemeni government forces backed by Saudi Arabia and declared the Bab al-Mandeb Strait that links the Red Sea to world markets closed to Saudi Arabia. The militant group has fired on Saudi ships, complicating the kingdom’s efforts to export its oil and raising the supply risks for global markets.

Around the same time, Saudi Arabia said Iran-backed militias in Iraq had launched [drone attacks on its oil facilities](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/saudi-arabia-blames-iran-backed-militias-for-attack-on-oil-facilities-eb6cf40c?mod=article_inline).

The Revolutionary Guard threatened Gulf states that it would destroy their energy facilities if the U.S. were to hit similar sites in Iran—going as far as sending detailed lists of individual targets to its Arab counterparts, the officials said.

This satellite image shows smoke rising from the Saudi Aramco oil processing facility in Abqaiq, Saudi Arabia, in July. Planet Labs PBC/AP

Iran also escalated its attacks on American troops. Tehran [surprised the U.S.](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-gambles-on-escalation-with-surprise-attack-on-u-s-forces-72cfd422?mod=article_inline) by firing five ballistic missiles at Jordan during a lull in the conflict. The attack came two weeks after another Iranian strike in Jordan [killed three U.S. servicemembers](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-missile-struck-housing-for-u-s-troops-at-jordan-base-9f5b8120?mod=article_inline).

Officials in the Gulf grew more alarmed as they watched the scope of attacks grow and Iranian missiles evade U.S. defenses and hit targets more precisely in Jordan, Kuwait and Bahrain. The fighting spilled into August, with Iran striking six United Arab Emirates ships over the past two weeks as they tried to transit the Strait of Hormuz.

A team carries the remains of a U.S. servicemember who was believed to be killed by an Iranian airstrike in Jordan. Jim Lo Scalzo/EPA/Shutterstock

“We took full advantage of the ceasefire period,” Revolutionary Guard spokesman Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebbi said in an interview with the Guard-affiliated Tasnim news agency in July. “We enhanced our capabilities, accelerated our missile production rate and improved missile accuracy.”

Behind the scenes, Supreme Leader Khamenei was positioning his forces for a more aggressive fight, lining up seven key security leaders to run revamped military and repression institutions in a reorganization that was unveiled a week ago.

The appointments came with aggressive goals that included tightening the military chain of command, turning an ideological militia into a neighborhood intelligence agency and emphasizing an offensive approach.

Mohsen Rezaei, who led the Revolutionary Guard during the war with Iraq in the 1980s, was named secretary of Iran’s top decision-making body, the Supreme National Security Council, where he will be Khamenei’s representative. In the past week, the council squelched progress on a deal to gradually open Hormuz.

To step up internal security, Khamenei returned veteran Hossein Taeb, a close associate who led a deadly crackdown on protests in 2009, to the helm of the Basij. The supreme leader tasked him with pivoting the volunteer force into a “popular intelligence network” in the wake of two wars that showed how thoroughly Iran had been breached by Israeli intelligence.

Iranian government advisers see the next stage of the confrontation going beyond conventional warfare to include external attempts to foster domestic unrest. The government brutally suppressed protests over the country’s economic crisis in January and fears it’s only a matter of time before the continued deterioration under U.S. pressure brings demonstrators back to the street.

Khamenei also consolidated Revolutionary Guard influence over the regular army by unifying control of the two military arms under Brig. Gen. Ali Abdollahi, a veteran Revolutionary Guard senior commander and military leader who has aggressively advocated attacks on Arab states hosting U.S. bases around the Persian Gulf.

New Revolutionary Guard Commander in Chief Ahmad Vahidi—who has been unofficially on the job since March—was formally given the position and tasked with “powerful offensive operations against the enemy,” the decree appointing him said Monday. The next day, Gen. Mohammad Reza Naghdi, an adviser to Vahidi, said the paramilitary force must be prepared to take operations into enemy territory.

“Vahidi’s elevation is consistent with a leadership preparing the security establishment for a period of sustained confrontation rather than a return to normal peacetime politics,” said Saeid Golkar, an expert on Iran’s security services at the University of Tennessee at Chattanooga.

The Revolutionary Guard—[an armed force nearly 200,000 strong](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/the-iranian-force-built-to-defend-the-regime-now-faces-the-ultimate-test-8a1a2cfe?mod=article_inline)—has devised new plans for further escalation including pre-emptive strikes, according to Iranian officials and public statements. Among the options: sabotaging internet cables in the Persian Gulf, creating political unrest in countries with Shia communities such as Kuwait and Bahrain, and potentially even ground operations in Kuwait.

Kuwait takes those risks seriously. In early May, the [tiny Gulf sheikhdom](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/inside-the-tiny-oil-sheikhdom-cut-off-by-the-iran-war-f2b1f00a?mod=article_inline) said its security forces had intercepted six Revolutionary Guard troops that used a rented fishing boat to land on a Kuwaiti island separated from Iran only by a thin strip of Iraqi territory. Some Iranian political figures have suggested the Revolutionary Guard could launch ground raids via southern Iraq if the U.S. were to threaten Iranian territory [like Kharg Island](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/us-israel-iran-war-news-2026/card/map-of-kharg-island-YDCPXR2SiM6hkGNG3Z8c?mod=article_inline).

Some Gulf officials said Iran’s recent behavior is fueling uncertainty about relations with the much larger, newly more aggressive neighbor after the war. They say it remains difficult to assess the extent of the damage the U.S. and Israel did to the Revolutionary Guard’s capabilities. Still, it’s clear the organization hasn’t been defeated or deterred, and its hold on Hormuz has strengthened its regional position, they said.

That reality has helped push Gulf states to support even unfavorable deals that would acknowledge Iranian oversight of the strait if it stops further escalation. But regional leaders are also growing more frustrated by the lack of fundamental changes in Tehran’s behavior in response to diplomatic outreach.

Iran’s security-minded leadership, they said, appears to be growing more confident in its demands.

Commercial vessels are anchored near a beach in the Strait of Hormuz off Bandar Abbas, Iran. Amirhosein Khorgooi/ISNA/AP

Mediators in the talks said Iranian diplomats agreed early in the month on a deal with Oman to set up and oversee routes through Hormuz that would lead to the gradual reopening of the strait. Those efforts were blocked by the Revolutionary Guard, which went back to attacking ships.

With negotiations blocked and Iran not backing away from escalating the fight, [Trump has stepped back](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-shifts-to-economic-pressure-again-as-iran-wont-meet-his-demands-4445c840?mod=article_inline) to see if the economic pressure from sanctions and the Navy’s blockade will soften Iran’s position, U.S. officials said.

But Iran’s leaders are deeply skeptical of Trump’s motives and are slipping into a [survival economy](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-is-defying-u-s-pressure-by-becoming-a-survival-economy-5e8edf9f?mod=article_inline) aimed at holding out.

People close to Iran’s top negotiators have warned peace talks could be just an interlude before more conflict. Mehdi Mohammadi, a strategic adviser to chief Iranian negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, described the current stalemate Tuesday as the calm before the storm.

In an earlier social-media post, Mohammadi said the drivers of continued conflict include a “generational project for blood vengeance” for the killing of Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, Mojtaba’s father, in the opening strikes of the war.

“The Iranian nation still has much unfinished business with Trump,” said Mohammadi, who is also a member of Iran’s negotiating team. “Iran is prepared for the great confrontation.”
