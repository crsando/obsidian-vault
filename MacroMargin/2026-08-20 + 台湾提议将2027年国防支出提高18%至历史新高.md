### 中文译文

## 台湾提议将2027年国防支出提高18%至历史新高

![An M60A3 tank fires during a live-fire drill as part of the Han Kuang military exercise in Penghu](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b14816ea171d0e16d9bf9a1/3c281cc9-0b14-81ef-b59a-f658c5060992-df682212f54cf48cb352316cbe4674ca.jpg)

台湾澎湖，2026年8月10日，作为汉光军事演习的一部分，一辆M60A3坦克在实弹射击演练中开火。REUTERS/Ann Wang

**摘要**

- 2027年国防预算将达到T$1.1225万亿，超过GDP的3%
- 支出计划包括此前未获议会批准的无人机和导弹
- 预算还包括退伍军人和海岸警卫队，后者承担海军辅助角色
台北，8月20日（路透社） - 台湾政府周四提议明年国防支出增加18%，其中包括无人机和导弹，使国防支出首次超过新台币1万亿元（合314.1亿美元），台北正加强军力以防范中国。

预算细节公布之际，北京——视民主治理的台湾为其领土——在过去几年加大军事和政治压力以宣示主张，而台北强烈拒绝这一主张。

台湾还面临华盛顿要求其增加自身国防开支的呼声，这类似于美国对欧洲施压，台湾总统赖清德对此热情拥抱。

政府在批准2027年总预算的内阁会议后发表声明称，2027年国防开支将达到新台币1.1225万亿元，超过国内生产总值的3%。

国防部主计局局长谢继贤（Hsieh Chi-hsien）告诉记者，国防部的计划包括在无人机和导弹上的额外支出，此前政府一直未能获得议会批准。

### 军事现代化：关键政策纲领

赖清德面临来自台湾反对党占多数的议会的挑战，该议会在拖延数月后，直到上周五晚些时候才通过了今年的预算。

两个主要反对党表示，他们不会为政府开空白支票，尽管他们一再强调支持国防开支。

5月，议会批准了[额外国防开支](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/taiwan-parliament-approves-extra-defence-spending-less-than-government-wanted-2026-05-08/)250亿美元，仅约为政府所求金额的三分之二，且不包括无人机等国内项目的资金。

该预算包括用于退伍军人和海岸警卫队的资金，海岸警卫队在保护台湾海域方面对海军起辅助作用。

赖清德本周早些时候已经宣布了[国防开支数字](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-president-says-record-defence-spending-is-investment-peace-2026-08-17/)。

台湾政府已将军事现代化作为关键政策平台，并多次承诺增加国防开支，包括研发台湾自制潜艇，以应对来自中国大陆日益增长的威胁。

中国大陆军方几乎每天都在台湾周边海空域活动，北京方面还在磨练新战术，例如海警在台湾东海岸太平洋海域进行“[执法](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-coast-guard-patrols-east-taiwan-angering-taipei-2026-07-31/)”巡逻。

台湾上周完成了年度汉光军事演习，模拟了多种作战场景，包括如何打破中国大陆可能实施的[海上封锁](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-holds-anti-blockade-naval-drill-during-war-games-china-steps-up-maritime-2026-08-13/)。

中国国防部称此次演习是一场“[浪费的闹剧](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-calls-taiwans-annual-military-drills-wasteful-charade-2026-08-14/)”，并“煽动战争恐慌”。

## Taiwan proposes boosting 2027 defence spending 18% to a record high

An M60A3 tank fires during a live-fire drill as part of the Han Kuang military exercise in Penghu, Taiwan, August 10, 2026. REUTERS/Ann Wang

**Summary**

- Defence budget would reach T$1.1225 trillion in 2027, exceeding 3% of GDP
- Spending plan includes drones and missiles previously denied parliamentary approval
- Budget also covers veterans and Coast Guard, which has auxiliary navy role
TAIPEI, Aug 20 (Reuters) - Taiwan's government on Thursday proposed an 18% rise in defence spending for next year, including for drones and missiles, taking it above T$1 trillion ($31.41 billion) for the first time as Taipei bolsters its military to ward off China.

The budget details come as Beijing, which views democratically governed Taiwan as its own territory, has ramped up military and political pressure over the past few years to assert its claims, which Taipei strongly rejects.

Taiwan also faces calls from Washington to spend more on its own defence, mirroring U.S. pressure on Europe, something President Lai Ching-te has enthusiastically embraced.

The government said in a statement following a cabinet meeting to approve the 2027 general budget that defence spending in 2027 would reach T$1.1225 trillion, exceeding 3% of gross domestic product.

Hsieh Chi-hsien, who heads the defence ministry's comptroller bureau, told reporters that the ministry's plans include supplementary spending on drones and missiles, for which the government had previously been unable to get parliamentary approval.

### MILITARY MODERNISATION KEY POLICY PLATFORM

Lai faces a challenge from Taiwan's opposition-dominated parliament, which only passed this year's budget late last Friday after months of delays.

The two main opposition parties say they will not write blank cheques for the government, though they have repeatedly stressed they support defence spending.

In May, parliament approved [extra defence spending](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/taiwan-parliament-approves-extra-defence-spending-less-than-government-wanted-2026-05-08/) of $25 billion, or only around two-thirds of what the government had wanted and excluding money for domestic programmes like drones.

The budget includes funds for veterans and also the Coast Guard, which has an auxiliary role to the navy in protecting Taiwan's maritime areas.

Lai had already announced the [defence spending number](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-president-says-record-defence-spending-is-investment-peace-2026-08-17/) earlier this week.

Taiwan's government has made military modernisation a key policy platform and has repeatedly pledged to spend more on its defences, including developing made-in-Taiwan submarines, given the rising threat from China.

China's military operates almost daily in the waters and skies around Taiwan, and Beijing has also been honing new tactics, such as coastguard "[law enforcement](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-coast-guard-patrols-east-taiwan-angering-taipei-2026-07-31/)" patrols off the island's east coast in the Pacific Ocean.

Taiwan finished its annual Han Kuang military drills last week, simulating a range of battle scenarios including how to break a potential Chinese [maritime blockade](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-holds-anti-blockade-naval-drill-during-war-games-china-steps-up-maritime-2026-08-13/).

China's Defence Ministry called the drills a "[wasteful charade](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-calls-taiwans-annual-military-drills-wasteful-charade-2026-08-14/)" that "stoked war panic".

($1 = 31.8520 Taiwan dollars)
