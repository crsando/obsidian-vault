## 为什么中国与伊朗的关系并不像看上去那么牢固？

### **要点**

- 中国是伊朗最重要的经济伙伴，购买其部分石油出口，为这个饱受制裁的政权提供了一条金融生命线。
- 中国与伊朗的关系远比人们通常认为的更不对等，也更缺乏战略性：中国在伊朗的投资大幅低于协议宣传的水平，军事联系也有限。
- 中国面临的来自美国的潜在经济惩罚风险可能比看上去更有限，因为其与伊朗的关系基于务实主义而非深层战略一致，而且中国与伊朗的地区对手保持了富有成效的关系。
中国是伊朗最重要的经济伙伴，购买其约90%的石油出口，为这个饱受制裁的政权提供了金融生命线。** **但自美国和以色列六个月前开始与伊朗开战以来，北京对德黑兰提供的可见支持很少，除了[批评](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-01/trump-s-removal-of-another-xi-friend-complicates-planned-summit)这次攻击。

8月下旬，美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特（Scott Bessent）[威胁要对与伊朗做生意的任何国家实施经济惩罚](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/trump-s-iran-pivot-from-war-to-sanctions-will-hit-china-or-fail)，因为华盛顿试图进一步孤立这个伊斯兰共和国并结束战争。他的威胁使得中国与伊朗的关系受到新的审视——并提高了在9月特朗普与习近平峰会前华盛顿与北京再次冲突的可能性。

然而，中国面对贝森特威胁措施的风险可能比看上去更有限。中国与伊朗的关系远比人们通常认为的更不对等，也更缺乏战略性。北京在伊朗的投资大幅低于协议宣传的水平，其军事联系有限，而且其更广泛的中东战略依赖于与伊朗的地区对手保持平衡关系。

### **伊朗对中国在外交上有多重要？**

与德黑兰的关系对北京很重要，但远不如与俄罗斯的关系。中国与伊朗的关系似乎是基于务实主义而非深远的战略一致。

伊朗在中国主导的组织（如[金砖国家](https://www.bloomberg.com/explainers/what-is-brics-who-s-in-what-do-they-want-and-why-it-matters)和上海合作组织）中的成员身份，支持了北京稀释美国影响力并增强其作为全球南方主要声音的可信度的努力。北京在促成2023年伊朗与沙特[和解](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-03-13/what-role-did-china-play-in-brokering-iran-saudi-detente-q-a)中广受宣传的角色提升了其外交信誉，尽管一些西方外交官质疑其实际核心作用。北京希望赢得那些对特朗普在未先寻求国际共识的情况下就准备对他国进行军事干预感到震惊的小国的支持。

但长期以来，中国在中东奉行分析人士所说的双轨方针，在伊朗与美国及其地区盟友的关系之间取得平衡。清华大学国际关系教授佘纲正表示，对伊朗的军事支持“不是中国在该地区做事的方式”。

北京与莫斯科的关系完全是另一层次。俄罗斯是中国反抗美国主导地位的核心，是关键的能源供应国，也是多边论坛上的伙伴。中国国家主席习近平与俄罗斯的弗拉基米尔·普京之间的密切个人关系加强了这一纽带。

### **中国与伊朗的经济关系如何？**

中国是伊朗的主要贸易伙伴，但这种关系是不对称的。根据战略与国际研究中心的数据，中国吸收了伊朗约三分之一的贸易，而伊朗仅占世界第二大经济体贸易量的不到1%。

伊朗的折扣原油约占中国海运石油进口量的十分之一。这有助于北京从俄罗斯和沙特阿拉伯等国家多样化能源供应，但最终是[可替代的](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-02/oil-hoard-shields-china-s-refiners-from-iran-war-risks-for-now)。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b14817fbe4af69a12327ca6/3c781cc9-0b14-8126-97d7-c60e50e25993-6585745f7db3b1fbfee5d8ea75289302.png)

尽管头条交易显示出相反情况，但中国在伊朗的投资一直不多。2021年，中国外交部长王毅在访问德黑兰期间签署了一项25年战略合作协议，[据报道](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/03/27/world/middleeast/china-iran-deal.html)该协议设想了高达4000亿美元的中国投资。但实际上，据法国外贸银行亚太区首席经济学家艾丽西亚·加西亚·埃雷罗称，此后确认的投资仅有20亿至30亿美元，这一数字与中国在阿联酋或沙特的承诺相比相形见绌。

伊朗官员并未掩饰他们的失望。2023年，即协议签署两年后，时任伊朗总统易卜拉欣·莱希在前往北京前[表示](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-02-14/china-s-xi-vows-deeper-iran-ties-amid-us-push-to-curb-oil-trade)，双边关系出现“严重倒退”，经济关系也不能令人满意。几周前，一名伊朗贸易官员称，俄罗斯已取代中国成为伊朗最大的外国投资者。

中国官方数据显示，截至2024年底，中国在伊朗的外国直接投资存量为45亿美元，而在阿联酋为95亿美元。美国企业研究所的[中国全球投资追踪](https://www.aei.org/china-global-investment-tracker/?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=23485408307&gbraid=0AAAAABiFL_m16NpsZs7o7All46QwXUYtx&gclid=Cj0KCQiA5I_NBhDVARIsAOrqIsZABIxqxkjGl7HEzfqSf5s2joYKwJZMNjkB83D5npwfBMlsFxqVz_kaAlsHEALw_wcB)监测自2005年以来至少1亿美元的交易，其数据显示中国在伊朗的累计投资约为47亿美元，主要集中在能源和金属领域。相比之下，该追踪显示中国在沙特阿拉伯的投资约为160亿美元，涉及能源、技术、金属和娱乐领域。

“与该地区其他国家相比，中国企业在伊朗的业务非常有限，”伦敦智库Bourse & Bazaar基金会首席执行官埃斯凡迪亚尔·巴特曼赫利吉（Esfandyar Batmanghelidj）表示。“由于次级制裁风险，大型中国企业一直避开伊朗。”

伊朗正式参与中国的“一带一路”倡议。2016年，中国[开通](http://www.scio.gov.cn/news_0/202209/t20220921_411793.html)了首列货运列车，连接中国东部的[义乌](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-10-31/troubles-in-china-s-christmas-town-mean-higher-holiday-prices)与德黑兰，途经哈萨克斯坦和土库曼斯坦，但该服务在新冠疫情期间暂停，于2024年恢复。

### **中国与伊朗的军事关系如何？**

2005年，当国际原子能机构[宣布](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/documents/gov2005-77.pdf)德黑兰未遵守《不扩散核武器条约》时，中国正式停止向伊朗出售武器，促使联合国安理会（包括中国）[实施](https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/s/res/1737-%282006%29#:~:text=Security%20Council%20Resolution%201737%20(2006)%20established%20a,the%20supply%20of%20nuclear%2Drelated%20technology%20and%20materials)对伊朗核相关出口的事实禁运。据斯德哥尔摩国际和平研究所称，在此之前，中国提供了多种导弹、飞机和大炮。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b14817fbe4af69a12327ca6/3c781cc9-0b14-817f-ad81-fb2fff1c532d-426dd3bbba95128e533022c90df12aff.png)

官员们称，有报道说中国的[防空系统](https://x.com/IRIran_Military/status/1969959066975039790)已抵达伊朗，且有载有导弹推进剂成分的船只将从中国驶往伊朗，但中国和伊朗均未公开证实。在特朗普发动打击后，中国外交部驳斥了另一则[报道](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/iran-nears-deal-buy-supersonic-anti-ship-missiles-china-2026-02-24/)，称北京准备用超音速反舰导弹武装伊朗的说法“[不属实](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/xw/fyrbt/202603/t20260302_11867202.html)。”

更有可能的是供应“两用”物品，这些物品设计用于民用，但也可用于军事目的。这将为中国提供一定程度的可否认性，符合其一贯做法。据美中经济与安全审查委员会的一份[报告](https://www.uscc.gov/research/chinas-facilitation-sanctions-and-export-control-evasion#_Toc213758813)，在过去八年中，超过100家中国和香港实体因协助伊朗规避出口管制而被列入美国实体清单。

但中国支持的程度反映出这种关系比与俄罗斯的关系更为有限，中国曾向俄罗斯供应无人机及其他用于乌克兰战争的两用物品。

### **这段关系将走向何方？**

北京对伊朗的支持仅限于贸易和外交，没有提供像美国对某些伙伴那样的安全保障。中国还与伊朗的地区竞争对手保持着建设性关系，包括沙特阿拉伯和阿联酋，同时将伊朗的投资承诺保持在相对适度的水平。

这种平衡做法有时会引起与德黑兰的摩擦。2022年习近平访问沙特阿拉伯时，他没有像2016年中东之行那样接着访问伊朗。在习近平与沙特发表联合声明提到德黑兰的“破坏地区稳定的活动”及其“支持恐怖主义和教派组织”之后，几位伊朗议员和官员严厉批评了这次访问。

在保护与美国的经济利益方面，北京也很务实。虽然它不承认单边制裁，但其国有企业通常避免购买黑名单上的石油。中国最大的国有银行也有遵守美国对伊朗、朝鲜甚至香港高层官员制裁的历史，以避免失去进入美元清算系统的渠道。

尽管如此，中国似乎仍有意与德黑兰保持密切关系。根据在贝森特发表评论前不久发布的一份[声明](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/wjbxw_new/202608/t20260823_12008663.shtml)，8月17日，外交部副部长苗得雨在北京与伊朗副外长卡泽姆·加里巴巴迪举行了会谈。

“伊朗期待与中国在各领域深化合作，推进双边关系，”声明援引加里巴巴迪的话说。“伊朗赞赏中国秉持公正和公平，期待中国在维护地区和平与稳定方面发挥更大作用。”

## Why China’s Ties With Iran Aren’t as Strong as They Seem

### **Takeaways**

- China is Iran's most important economic partner, buying some of its oil exports and providing a financial lifeline to the heavily sanctioned regime.
- China's relationship with Iran is far more lopsided and less strategic than commonly assumed, with Beijing's investment in Iran falling dramatically short of headline agreements and its military ties limited.
- China's exposure to threatened economic punishment from the US may be more limited than it appears, as its relationship with Iran is grounded in pragmatism rather than deep strategic alignment and it has maintained productive relationships with Iran's regional rivals.
China is Iran’s most important economic partner, buying some 90% of its oil exports and providing a financial lifeline to the heavily sanctioned regime.** **But since the US and Israel began their war with Iran six months ago, Beijing has offered little visible** **support to Tehran beyond [criticizing](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-01/trump-s-removal-of-another-xi-friend-complicates-planned-summit) the attacks.

In late August, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent [threatened economic punishment](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/trump-s-iran-pivot-from-war-to-sanctions-will-hit-china-or-fail) against any country doing business with Iran as Washington sought to further isolate the Islamic Republic and end the war. His threats put China’s relationship with Iran under fresh scrutiny — and raised the prospect of another clash between Washington and Beijing ahead of a Trump-Xi summit in September.

However, China’s exposure to Bessent’s threatened measures may be more limited than it would appear. The relationship between China and Iran is far more lopsided and less strategic than commonly assumed. Beijing’s investment in Iran has fallen dramatically short of headline agreements, its military ties are limited and its broader Middle East strategy depends on balancing relations with Iran’s regional rivals.

### **How important is Iran to China diplomatically?**

Ties with Tehran matter to Beijing, but far less than those with Russia. China’s relationship with Iran appears grounded in pragmatism rather than deep strategic alignment.

Iran’s membership in China-led groupings such as [BRICS](https://www.bloomberg.com/explainers/what-is-brics-who-s-in-what-do-they-want-and-why-it-matters) and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization supports Beijing’s effort to dilute US influence and bolster its credentials as a leading voice of the Global South. Beijing’s widely touted role in facilitating a 2023 Iran-Saudi [rapprochement](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-03-13/what-role-did-china-play-in-brokering-iran-saudi-detente-q-a) enhanced its diplomatic credibility, even as some Western diplomats questioned how central it really was. Beijing hopes to win favor with smaller nations alarmed at Trump’s readiness to intervene militarily in other countries without first seeking an international consensus.

But China has long pursued what analysts describe as a dual-track approach in the Middle East, balancing ties with Iran against its relationships with the US and its regional allies. She Gangzheng, a professor of international relations at Tsinghua University in Beijing, said military support for Iran is “not the way that China does things in the region.”

Beijing’s relationship with Moscow is on a different level entirely. Russia is central to China’s pushback against US dominance, is a critical energy supplier and a partner across multilateral forums. Close personal ties between Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russia’s Vladimir Putin reinforce that bond.

### **What is China’s economic relationship with Iran?**

China is Iran’s dominant trade partner, but the relationship is asymmetric. China absorbs roughly a third of Iran’s trade, while the Islamic Republic accounts for less than 1% of commerce for the world’s second-largest economy, according to the Center for Strategic and International Studies.

Iran’s discounted crude** **makes up roughly a tenth of China’s seaborne oil intake. That helps Beijing to diversify its energy supply from countries such as Russia and Saudi Arabia, but is ultimately [replaceable](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-02/oil-hoard-shields-china-s-refiners-from-iran-war-risks-for-now).

Despite headline deals suggesting otherwise, Chinese investment in Iran has been modest. In 2021, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi signed a 25-year strategic cooperation agreement during a visit to Tehran that [reportedly](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/03/27/world/middleeast/china-iran-deal.html) envisioned up to $400 billion in Chinese investment. In practice, only $2 billion to $3 billion has been confirmed since then, a figure that pales next to China’s commitments in the United Arab Emirates or Saudi Arabia, according to Alicia Garcia Herrero, chief Asia Pacific economist at Natixis.

Iranian officials have not hidden their frustration. In 2023, two years after the deal was inked, Iran’s then-president, Ebrahim Raisi, [said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-02-14/china-s-xi-vows-deeper-iran-ties-amid-us-push-to-curb-oil-trade) before departing for Beijing that there had been a “serious regression” in the bilateral relationship and that economic ties had been unsatisfactory. Weeks earlier, an Iranian trade official said Russia had overtaken China as Iran’s biggest foreign investor.

China’s official data show its foreign direct investment stock in Iran totaled $4.5 billion by the end of 2024, compared with $9.5 billion in the UAE. The American Enterprise Institute’s [China Global Investment Tracker](https://www.aei.org/china-global-investment-tracker/?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=23485408307&gbraid=0AAAAABiFL_m16NpsZs7o7All46QwXUYtx&gclid=Cj0KCQiA5I_NBhDVARIsAOrqIsZABIxqxkjGl7HEzfqSf5s2joYKwJZMNjkB83D5npwfBMlsFxqVz_kaAlsHEALw_wcB), which monitors transactions of at least $100 million since 2005, puts cumulative Chinese investment in Iran at about $4.7 billion, concentrated primarily in energy and metals. By contrast, it places Chinese investment in Saudi Arabia at about $16 billion across energy,** **technology, metals and entertainment.

“Chinese companies have a very limited footprint in Iran relative to other countries in the region,” said Esfandyar Batmanghelidj, chief executive of the Bourse & Bazaar Foundation, a London think tank. “Major Chinese firms have steered clear of Iran due to secondary sanctions risks.”

Iran is formally part of China’s Belt and Road Initiative. In 2016, China [launched](http://www.scio.gov.cn/news_0/202209/t20220921_411793.html) its first cargo train linking [Yiwu](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-10-31/troubles-in-china-s-christmas-town-mean-higher-holiday-prices) in eastern China with Tehran via Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan, though the service was suspended during the Covid pandemic before resuming in 2024.

### **What about China’s military ties with Iran?**

China officially stopped selling weapons to Iran in 2005, when the International Atomic Energy Agency [declared](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/documents/gov2005-77.pdf) Tehran was not complying with the Non-Proliferation Treaty, prompting the UN Security Council — including China — to [impose](https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/s/res/1737-%282006%29#:~:text=Security%20Council%20Resolution%201737%20(2006)%20established%20a,the%20supply%20of%20nuclear%2Drelated%20technology%20and%20materials) a de facto embargo on nuclear-related exports to Iran. Before that, China supplied a range of missiles, aircraft and artillery, according to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute.

There have been reports of Chinese [air-defense systems](https://x.com/IRIran_Military/status/1969959066975039790) reaching Iran and shipments of Ships carrying missile propellant ingredients set to sail from China to Iran, say officials, but neither China nor Iran has publicly confirmed them. After Trump’s strike, China’s Foreign Ministry dismissed a separate [account](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/iran-nears-deal-buy-supersonic-anti-ship-missiles-china-2026-02-24/) that Beijing was poised to arm Iran with supersonic anti-ship missiles as “[not true](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/xw/fyrbt/202603/t20260302_11867202.html).”

More plausible would be the supply of “dual-use” items that are designed for civilian use but can also be employed for military purposes. These would offer China a degree of deniability consistent with its broader playbook. In the last eight years, over 100 Chinese and Hong Kong entities have been added to a US Entity List for assisting Iran’s export control evasion efforts, according to a [report ](https://www.uscc.gov/research/chinas-facilitation-sanctions-and-export-control-evasion#_Toc213758813)by the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission.

But the extent of Chinese support reflects a relationship that is more limited than with Russia, which China has [supplied with drones](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-19/ukraine-war-chinese-drones-are-now-entering-russia-via-thailand) and other dual-use items for the war in Ukraine.

### **Where does the relationship go from here?**

Beijing has limited its support for Iran to trade and diplomacy,** **stopping short of providing security guarantees like those the US offers some of its partners. China has also maintained productive relationships with Iran’s regional rivals, including Saudi Arabia and the UAE, while keeping its investment commitments in Iran** **relatively modest.

The balancing act has occasionally caused friction with Tehran. When** **Xi visited Saudi Arabia in 2022, he did not travel on to Iran, as he had during his 2016 Middle East tour. Several Iranian lawmakers and officials heavily criticized the trip after Xi released a joint statement with Saudi Arabia that referred to Tehran’s “destabilizing regional activities” and its “support for terrorist and sectarian groups.”

Beijing is also pragmatic when it comes to protecting its economic interests with the US.** **While it does not recognize unilateral sanctions, its state-owned entities generally avoid blacklisted oil. China’s biggest state banks also have a history of complying with US sanctions on Iran, North Korea and even top officials in Hong Kong to avoid losing access to the US dollar-clearing system.

Still, China appears intent on maintaining close ties with Tehran.** **On Aug. 17, Vice Foreign Minister Miao Deyu held talks in Beijing with Iran’s Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi, according to a [statement](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/wjbxw_new/202608/t20260823_12008663.shtml) published** **just before Bessent’s comments.

“Iran looks forward to deepening cooperation with China in various fields and advancing bilateral relations,” the statement cited Gharibabadi as saying. “Iran appreciates China’s upholding of justice and fairness and looks forward to China playing a greater role in maintaining regional peace and stability.”
