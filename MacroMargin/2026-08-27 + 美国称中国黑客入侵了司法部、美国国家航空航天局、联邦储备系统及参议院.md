### 中文译文

## 美国称中国黑客入侵了司法部、美国国家航空航天局、联邦储备系统及参议院

![Illustration shows U.S. and Chinese flags](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481e4be45fe76a65c875d/3c981cc9-0b14-8129-9693-ff5a71b5764e-f1d104f2685efb44466e59347855740d.jpg)

这张摄于2025年3月20日的插图中可以看到美国和中国国旗。路透社/达多·鲁维奇/插图/资料照片

- 摘要
- 公司
- 美国司法部表示，已查封QScan和QTRouter黑客平台所用的域名
- 宣誓书指认能源部、卫生与公众服务部、国立卫生研究院以及四家美国和韩国公司为受害者
- 宣誓书称，黑客至少自2018年以来一直以美国及全球敏感网络为目标
华盛顿8月26日（路透社）——美国周三表示，已挫败一个中国黑客行动，该行动针对司法部、美国国家航空航天局、联邦储备系统、参议院及其他敏感政府机构进行了入侵和未遂入侵。

[在一份声明中，打开新标签页](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-and-fbi-seize-platforms-operated-and-used-china-state-sponsored-hackers)，美国司法部表示，已查封两个黑客平台所用的域名，这两个平台被称为QScan和QTRouter，据称曾被用于该行动。

[一份宣誓书，打开新标签页](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1459096/dl?inline=)指出，美国能源部、卫生与公众服务部、国立卫生研究院以及美国和韩国的四家未具名公司均属于黑客的受害者。

中国驻华盛顿大使馆的一位发言人在电子邮件中表示，虽然他们不了解司法部声明中提到的具体细节，但“中国政府坚决反对并依法打击一切形式的网络攻击。”

该发言人表示，美国利用网络安全问题“抹黑或诋毁中国”，中国“反对美国过度延伸国家安全概念，以此为由对中国企业实施歧视性限制，并将坚决维护中国企业的合法权益。”

美国司法部表示，这些平台由一家中国公司——南京新久维网络科技有限公司运营，其客户据称包括中国的民用情报机构国家安全部和军方中国人民解放军。

南京新久维在正常工作时间之外未立即回应置评请求。

### 黑客攻击尝试始于2018年

宣誓书称，黑客使用他们开发的工具，自至少2018年以来，破坏了美国及全球的关键基础设施和其他敏感网络。

宣誓书称，并非所有进入网络的尝试都成功了。2019年8月，黑客曾通过针对虚拟专用网络漏洞尝试入侵NASA网络，但未能成功。

根据宣誓书，2024年9月，黑客入侵了三个未具名的能源部实验室、美国国家卫生研究院（NIH）、一个未具名的卫生与公众服务部（HHS）机构，以及一家美国安全设备制造商。

美国联邦调查局（FBI）、国家安全局（NSA）和美国网络司令部下属网络国家任务部队联合发布的一份网络安全[咨询报告](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Aug/26/2003986916/-1/-1/0/JCSA_CHINA_QTFY_MALICIOUS_SYSTEMS.PDF)详细描述了多年来的多次黑客行动。其中包括2024年5月从未具名的国防承包商、金融机构和大学成功窃取数据。

黑客还于2026年3月扫描漏洞，并试图入侵美国参议院和一家美国医院的网络，但未成功。

美国国家航空航天局（NASA）发言人表示，该机构不评论具体事件；卫生与公众服务部则将问题转交给司法部（DOJ）。司法部未回应提供更多细节的请求。

近年来，与中国有关的黑客活动已入侵了美国政府和私营部门的多个敏感网络。3月，联邦调查局（FBI）[通知](https://www.reuters.com/technology/fbi-identified-addressed-suspicious-cyber-activities-its-networks-agency-2026-03-05/)国会，黑客已渗透与FBI调查对象有关的某些机构网络，后来的公开报道将此次入侵归因于中国。与中国有关的黑客还与某些美国众议院委员会网络以及近年多家大型电信公司的入侵有关联。

关注中国网络活动的专家表示，私营承包商经常代表多个中国政府机构实施高调入侵。

网络安全公司SentinelOne的中国分析师Dakota Cary表示：“过去十年，提供小众网络攻击服务的公司数量激增。”

## US says Chinese hackers broke into Justice Department, NASA, Federal Reserve, Senate

U.S. and Chinese flags are seen in this illustration taken March 20, 2025. REUTERS/Dado Ruvic/Illustration/File Photo

- Summary
- Companies
- Justice Department says it seized domains for QScan and QTRouter hacking platforms
- Affidavit identifies Energy, HHS, NIH and four US and South Korean companies as victims
- Hackers targeted sensitive US and global networks since at least 2018, affidavit says
WASHINGTON, Aug 26 (Reuters) - The U.S. said on Wednesday it had disrupted a Chinese hacking operation responsible for break-ins and attempts on the Justice Department, NASA, the Federal Reserve, the Senate, and other sensitive government agencies.

[In a statement, opens new tab](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-and-fbi-seize-platforms-operated-and-used-china-state-sponsored-hackers), the Justice Department said it had seized domains used by two hacking platforms, dubbed QScan and QTRouter, which it said had been used as part of the campaign.

[An affidavit, opens new tab](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1459096/dl?inline=) identified the U.S. Department of Energy, Department of Health and Human Services, the National Institutes of Health, and four unnamed companies in the U.S. and South Korea as being among the hackers' victims.

A spokesperson for the Chinese Embassy in Washington said in an email that while they were not familiar with the specifics mentioned in the DOJ statement, the "Chinese government firmly opposes and combats all forms of cyberattacks in accordance with the law."

The U.S. uses cybersecurity issues to "smear or discredit China," the spokesperson said, and China "opposes the U.S. overstretching the concept of national security and using it as a pretext to impose discriminatory restrictions on Chinese companies and will firmly safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of Chinese companies."

The Justice Department said the platforms were run by a China-based firm, the Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology Company, whose clients it said included China's civilian intelligence agency, the Ministry of State Security, and its military, the People’s Liberation Army.

Nanjing Xinjiuwei did not immediately respond to a request for comment outside normal business hours.

### HACK ATTEMPTS STARTED IN 2018

The affidavit said the hackers used tools they developed to compromise critical infrastructure and other sensitive networks in the U.S. and worldwide since at least 2018.

The affidavit said that not all their attempts to gain access were successful. The hackers unsuccessfully attempted to gain access to NASA networks in August 2019 by targeting a virtual private network vulnerability.

In September 2024, the hackers carried out intrusions at three unnamed Energy Department laboratories, the NIH, an unnamed HHS agency, and a U.S. security device manufacturer, according to the affidavit.

A joint cybersecurity [advisory, opens new tab](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Aug/26/2003986916/-1/-1/0/JCSA_CHINA_QTFY_MALICIOUS_SYSTEMS.PDF) issued by the FBI, NSA and U.S. Cyber Command's Cyber National Mission Force detailed multiple hacking efforts over the years. These included successful data theft from unnamed defense contractors, financial institutions and universities in May 2024.

The hackers also scanned for vulnerabilities and made unsuccessful attempts to access networks of the U.S. Senate and a U.S. hospital in March 2026.

A NASA spokesperson said the agency does not comment on specific incidents, and the Department of Health and Human Services referred questions to the DOJ. The DOJ did not respond to a request for additional detail.

Chinese-linked hacking campaigns have compromised a string of sensitive U.S. government and private networks in recent years. In March, the FBI [notified](https://www.reuters.com/technology/fbi-identified-addressed-suspicious-cyber-activities-its-networks-agency-2026-03-05/) Congress that hackers had penetrated certain agency networks related to people under FBI investigation, with public reporting later attributing the compromise to China. Chinese-linked hackers have also been tied to a compromise of certain U.S. House of Representatives committee networks, as well as multiple major telecommunications companies in recent years.

Experts who follow Chinese cyber activity say private contractors routinely carry out high-profile intrusions on behalf of various Chinese government agencies.

"Over the last decade, the number of companies offering niche offensive services has exploded," said Dakota Cary, a China analyst with cybersecurity company SentinelOne.
