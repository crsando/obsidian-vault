### 中文译文

## 人工智能热潮推动中国科技股估值达到美国同行的数倍

![The upcoming listing of humanoid robotics group Unitree was more than 5,500 times oversubscribed © Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A21602c19-c9ce-4a01-9742-90c75d4e0dfb?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

人形机器人公司宇树科技(Unitree)即将上市，获得超过5500倍超额认购 © Qilai Shen/Bloomberg

来自北京的支持和投资者的狂热推动中国科技股估值远高于美国同行，随着中国人工智能行业崛起成为硅谷全球主导地位的有力挑战者。

上证科创板50指数（Star 50）追踪科创板50家规模最大、流动性最强的公司，今年回报率为29%。其表现轻松超过沪深300指数0.9%的涨幅，也远高于香港恒生指数，后者下跌1.5%。

该指数目前的交易价格也处于2020年推出以来的最高水平附近。即使经过近期抛售，科创50的整体市盈率仍超过150，远高于美国纳斯达克100指数的35。

这些天价估值反映出国内投资者的热情高涨，他们在痛苦的房地产崩盘后仍在寻找资金投放之处。

但基金经理表示，这也代表了一种信念，即中国蓬勃发展的[人工智能](https://www.ft.com/artificial-intelligence)产业能够与资金雄厚的美国竞争对手一较高下。

Allspring Global Investments的新兴市场投资组合经理Gary Tan表示，人们正在“押注中国生态系统”。

“要想投资于此，你只需相信中国能够迎头赶上，”他表示，并补充说，对中国人工智能的高预期是“昂贵”估值背后的原因。

[月之暗面（Moonshot）的Kimi K3的发布](https://www.ft.com/content/c6ecd8ce-c441-4d7c-aea6-fae3e28fb6ff)——这是中国迄今最大的AI模型，可与Anthropic的模型相媲美——以及本土芯片制造商长鑫存储（CXMT）的上市（周四其市值超越腾讯，成为中国市值最高的公司），都凸显了中国在AI领域的进步。

人形机器人集团宇树科技（Unitree）即将上市，其散户投资者认购量已超过可用配额的5500倍，显示了中国资本市场的狂热。

Ninety One的新兴市场投资组合经理Varun Laijawalla表示：“在AI方面，中国与世界其他地区之间的领先差距正在显著缩小。”

政府大力支持这一繁荣。在夏季全球芯片制造商抛售打击中国股市之后，中国由国有基金组成的“国家队”[纷纷入场购买股票](https://www.ft.com/content/e6e3acd7-81a2-471c-9be1-a7f7558d639b?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。

![Column chart of Star 50 earnings per share showing Chinese tech earnings are forecast to rise sharply](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A74e9c0a0-7dac-41db-b7f7-caf1655b31e5?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=700&dpr=2)

法国兴业银行亚洲股票策略主管Frank Benzimra表示，面对西方出口限制，北京日益迫切地推动半导体自给自足，这意味着高估值最集中在芯片制造领域。

长鑫存储最初得到[中国东部合肥市政府](https://www.ft.com/content/d6439cb3-5a9e-4f6e-b5d6-b0e5621567c4)关联基金的支持。

Benzimra表示：“我们看到越来越多的本地公司为其他本地公司供货。”他补充说，强劲的盈利增长支撑了高估值。“这一本地化进程是盈利的额外驱动力。”

在过去一年中，中国主要交易所放宽了对尚未盈利但从事战略重要技术研发的公司的上市要求。

今年在中国内地[中国内地](https://www.ft.com/china)的上市企业中，超过一半来自科技或工业领域，而香港的比例更高，达到77%。

上海科创板放宽未盈利公司的上市要求

#### **2026年4月**

深圳创业板推出针对未盈利科技企业的新上市标准

#### **2026年7月**

香港放宽小型公司的同股不同权规则

放宽的上市要求进一步推高了估值。今年在上海上市的公司平均市盈率为268倍，市销率为43倍，而去年上市的公司分别为67倍和22倍。

中国人工智能股票飙升之际，整体经济却明显疲软，呈现出技术领先与经济脆弱的矛盾景象。在2022年房地产市场崩盘后，北京方面试图将家庭储蓄从房地产转向股市。

“中国的房地产崩盘规模是我们见过的最大之一，”First Eagle Investments的投资组合经理兼高级研究分析师Idanna Appio表示，并补充说大多数房地产崩盘需要十年才能恢复。

国内需求疲软重创了与中国消费情绪相关的股票，其中包括互联网集团阿里巴巴和腾讯，这两家公司的股价今年分别下跌了17%和26%。

![Column chart of companies by year listed in Shanghai showing valuations for newer Chinese companies are much higher](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A9ded58dc-b770-4b2e-9156-cfedba3a0718?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=700&dpr=2)

当局似乎无法或不愿提供有意义的刺激措施来应对疲弱的消费需求。相反，北京方面希望“随着时间的推移，新产业将取代旧产业，并创造高价值就业岗位”，法国巴黎银行高级市场策略师Chi Lo表示。

估值过高还因为中国科技公司上市时间远早于美国同行，因为中国缺乏资金雄厚的风险投资。

“在美国，有一种越来越晚上市的趋势，”Benzimra表示。“你有这样一个拥有大量资金的私人基金生态系统。”

瑞银中国股票策略主管James Wang表示，中国股市的长期投资者数量不如美国多。

“他称：‘一些最大的参与者，如共同基金，往往相当注重短期。鉴于有更多的短线投资者，市场往往更加拥挤。’”

像Ninety One的Laijawalla这样的看多投资者正在押注，北京对科技的支持可能会持续，其基础是习近平主席对国家安全的关注。

“Laijawalla表示：‘如果你想到国家安全，今天中国的国家安全是什么？就是科技领先。’”

“他补充道，对于美中当前的投资环境，‘问题在于你认为什么是正常。我不认为现在还有正常。’”

## AI frenzy drives Chinese tech valuations to multiples of US peers

The upcoming listing of humanoid robotics group Unitree was more than 5,500 times oversubscribed © Qilai Shen/Bloomberg

Backing from Beijing and an investor frenzy have driven valuations of Chinese tech stocks far higher than US peers as the country’s AI sector emerges as a serious challenger to Silicon Valley’s global dominance.

Shanghai’s Star 50 index, which tracks the 50 largest and most liquid companies on the tech-focused Star board, has returned 29 per cent this year. It has handily outperformed the broader CSI 300’s 0.9 per cent gain and is well ahead of Hong Kong’s Hang Seng index, which is down 1.5 per cent.

The index is also trading around its most expensive levels since it launched in 2020. Even after a recent sell-off, the overall price-to-earnings ratio of the Star 50 is more than 150, well above the Nasdaq 100’s 35 in the US.

The sky-high valuations reflect a rush of enthusiasm from domestic investors who are still casting around for places to put their money following a painful housing bust.

But fund managers said it also represented a belief that China’s burgeoning [AI](https://www.ft.com/artificial-intelligence) industry could compete with deep-pocketed US rivals.

People are “making a bet on the Chinese ecosystem”, said Gary Tan, an emerging markets portfolio manager at Allspring Global Investments.

“To invest in this you just have to believe that China can catch up,” he said, adding that high expectations for Chinese AI were behind the “rich” valuations.

The [launch of Moonshot’s Kimi K3](https://www.ft.com/content/c6ecd8ce-c441-4d7c-aea6-fae3e28fb6ff) — China’s largest AI model to date, rivalling those of Anthropic — and the listing of homegrown chipmaker CXMT, which overtook Tencent as China’s most valuable company on Thursday, have underscored the country’s AI advances.

The upcoming listing of humanoid robotics group Unitree [attracted more than 5,500 times](https://www.ft.com/content/99824437-8173-4320-b84b-242c726ab437) the available allotment for retail investors, demonstrating the frenzy in China’s capital markets.

There is a “significant narrowing of leadership between China and the rest of the world as it relates to AI”, said Varun Laijawalla, an emerging markets portfolio manager at Ninety One.

The government has thrown itself behind the boom. China’s “national team” of state-backed funds [swooped in to purchase equities](https://www.ft.com/content/e6e3acd7-81a2-471c-9be1-a7f7558d639b?syn-25a6b1a6=1) after a global chipmaker sell-off this summer hit the country’s stocks.

Beijing’s increasingly urgent push for semiconductor self-sufficiency in the face of western export restrictions means the high valuations have been most concentrated in chipmaking, said Frank Benzimra, head of Asia equity strategy at Société Générale.

CXMT was initially backed by funds [affiliated with the municipal government of Hefei](https://www.ft.com/content/d6439cb3-5a9e-4f6e-b5d6-b0e5621567c4) in eastern China.

“We are seeing increasingly more local companies supplying other local companies,” said Benzimra, adding that strong earnings growth supported the high valuations. “This localisation process is an additional driver to the earnings.”

In the past year, China’s major exchanges have relaxed listing requirements for companies that are unprofitable but working on strategically important technologies.

More than half of listings in mainland [China](https://www.ft.com/china) this year were in technology or industrial sectors, while in Hong Kong the proportion was even higher at 77 per cent.

The looser listing requirements have driven valuations further. Companies that have listed in Shanghai this year have an average price-to-earnings ratio of 268 and a price-to-sales ratio of 43, compared with 67 and 22 respectively for companies listed last year.

China’s AI equity surge comes amid pronounced weakness in the overall economy, presenting a contradictory picture of technological supremacy and economic fragility. Beijing has sought to shift household savings from real estate to equities after the property market collapsed in 2022.

“The housing bust in China is as large as any we’ve seen,” said Idanna Appio, portfolio manager and senior research analyst at First Eagle Investments, adding that most property busts took a decade to recover from.

Weak domestic demand has hammered stocks linked to Chinese consumer sentiment, among them internet groups Alibaba and Tencent, whose shares have sunk 17 per cent and 26 per cent respectively this year.

Authorities appear unable or unwilling to provide meaningful stimulus to address lacklustre consumer demand. Instead, Beijing is hoping that “over time the new industries will replace the old industries and create high-value jobs”, said Chi Lo, a senior markets strategist at BNP Paribas.

The elevated valuations are also driven by the fact that Chinese tech companies are coming to market far earlier than their US counterparts because the country lacks deep-pocketed venture capital.

“There has been this tendency to list a company later and later” in the US, said Benzimra. “You had this ecosystem of private funds that had a lot of money.”

James Wang, head of China equity strategy at UBS, said Chinese equity markets did not have as many longer-term investors as the US.

“Some of the biggest players like the mutual funds are quite short-term focused,” he said. “Given you’ve got a lot more shorter-term investors, it tends to have more crowding.”

Bullish investors such as Ninety One’s Laijawalla are betting that Beijing’s commitment to technology is likely to endure, anchored by President Xi Jinping’s focus on national security.

“If you think about national security, what is national security in China today? It’s tech leadership,” said Laijawalla.

“It’s a question of what you think normal is,” he added of the current investment environment in the US and China. “I don’t think there is normal these days.”
