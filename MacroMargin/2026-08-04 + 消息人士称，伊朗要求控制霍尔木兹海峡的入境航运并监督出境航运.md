## 消息人士称，伊朗要求控制霍尔木兹海峡的入境航运并监督出境航运

![Vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, as seen from Musandam, Oman, August 3, 2026. REUTERS/Stringer/File Photo](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b281cc90b148167a968dbf0c5b4218c/3b281cc9-0b14-81a2-a6dd-c239fbada9d8-3aa4c394f2e506051d4d7cee253ebe74.jpg)

2026年8月3日，从阿曼穆桑代姆看到霍尔木兹海峡的船只。路透社/特约记者/资料图片

迪拜，8月4日（路透社）——一位伊朗高级消息人士周二对路透社表示，伊朗希望根据正在与阿曼讨论的一项临时计划，控制通过霍尔木兹海峡的入境航运，并对出境交通进行监督，且有能力在必要时进行干预，以[重新开放这条战略水道](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/how-iran-is-widening-its-pressure-campaign-force-us-concessions-2026-08-03/)。

参与谈判的这位消息人士称，这是“目前正在讨论的总体想法”，并具体说明，出境航道将沿伊朗和阿曼之间的路线行驶，在通知伊朗后，由阿曼批准离境许可。

消息人士补充说：“德黑兰不太可能改变其立场。”他表示，德黑兰已经表现出灵活性，从最初寻求完全控制海峡双向交通的立场上转变。

上个月，伊朗[拒绝了阿曼的提议](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-rules-out-regional-management-hormuz-strait-hitting-hopes-breakthrough-2026-07-29/)，该提议要求两国平均分配过境路线。德黑兰称，在实现长期地区稳定之前，该提议未能解决该国的安全关切。

霍尔木兹海峡是海湾与印度洋之间的狭窄水道，是全球约五分之一石油供应和其他重要物资的主要通道。对海峡的控制权一直是结束[美以对伊朗的战争](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/)努力的主要症结。

自2月下旬战争爆发以来，这条[水道基本被封锁](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/shipping-traffic-key-gulf-waterways-little-changed-uncertain-peace-talks-2026-08-04/)。关于霍尔木兹海峡的争端仍然是核心争议点。

华盛顿方面表示，6月与德黑兰达成的旨在停止战争的[谅解备忘录](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/14-point-draft-us-iran-deal-2026-06-17/)要求伊朗重新开放这条战略水道，而德黑兰则表示，文本明确保留了其对航运交通的管辖权。

战前，船只可以自由通过霍尔木兹海峡，该海峡位于伊朗和阿曼的领海内，但通常被视为国际水道。

1968年，联合国航运机构在该地区国家的同意下通过了一项所谓的双向交通分隔计划，建立了当前的船舶航线系统，将航行通道分隔为通过伊朗和阿曼水域的走廊。

霍尔木兹海峡是一条宽34公里（21英里）的水道，提供从海湾到印度洋的通道，是中东能源供应以及包括化肥在内的其他重要物资的主要运输路线。

## Iran demands inbound control of Hormuz and outbound oversight, source says

Vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, as seen from Musandam, Oman, August 3, 2026. REUTERS/Stringer/File Photo

DUBAI, Aug 4 (Reuters) - Iran wants control over inbound shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and visibility over outbound traffic, with the ability to intervene if necessary, under a temporary plan being discussed with Oman to [reopen the strategic waterway](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/how-iran-is-widening-its-pressure-campaign-force-us-concessions-2026-08-03/), a senior Iranian source told Reuters on Tuesday.

The source, who is involved in the talks, said this is "the general idea currently being discussed", and specified that the outbound lane would follow a route between Iran and Oman, with exit clearance granted through Oman after notifying Iran.

"Tehran is unlikely to change its position," the source added, saying that Tehran has already shown flexibility by moving from its initial position of seeking full control over traffic in both directions in the strait.

Last month, Iran [rejected an Omani proposal](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-rules-out-regional-management-hormuz-strait-hitting-hopes-breakthrough-2026-07-29/) for an equal division of transit routes between the two countries, which Tehran said failed to address the country's security concerns until long-term regional stability is achieved.

Control of the strait, the narrow waterway between the Gulf and the Indian Ocean that is the main route for about a fifth of world oil supplies and other vital goods, has been a main sticking point in efforts to end the [U.S.-Israeli war on Iran](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/).

The [waterway has been largely blocked](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/shipping-traffic-key-gulf-waterways-little-changed-uncertain-peace-talks-2026-08-04/) since the start of the war in late February. The dispute over Hormuz remains a central point of contention.

Washington says June's [memorandum of understanding](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/14-point-draft-us-iran-deal-2026-06-17/) with Tehran to halt the war required Iran to reopen the strategic waterway, while Tehran says the text explicitly preserved its authority over shipping traffic.

Before the war, shipping passed freely through the strait, which is in the territorial waters of both Iran and Oman but which is generally regarded as an international waterway.

A so-called two-way traffic separation scheme, which was adopted by the U.N. shipping agency in 1968 with agreement of countries in the region, created the current ship routing system that split sailing corridors through Iranian and Omani waters.

The strait, a strip of water 34 km (21 miles) wide, provides passage from the Gulf to the Indian Ocean and is a main route for energy supplies from the Middle East and other vital goods including fertilisers.
