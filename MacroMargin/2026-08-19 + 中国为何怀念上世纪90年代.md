### 中文译文

## 中国为何怀念上世纪90年代

一位深受爱戴的领导人的去世，常常给中国执政的共产党带来挑战。由于公开批评政权的途径很少，[公众哀悼](https://www.ft.com/content/6e426546-a570-4e0c-a8cf-5da9638fdc42?syn-25a6b1a6=1)可能成为更广泛不满的表达。

对上周去世的前总理朱镕基（享年97岁）的热烈悼念，应当结合这一背景来理解。朱镕基是一位富有魅力、直言不讳的改革者，他在20世纪90年代主持了一场经济转型，为随后二十年的飞速扩张铺平了道路。网上对他流露出的喜爱之情，不仅反映了人们的钦佩，也反映了对当前国家方向的不安。这是一种怀旧，也是[对过去](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-19/why-china-s-gen-z-want-to-go-back-in-time?srnd=undefined)的集体渴望。

如果领导层忽视[普遍存在的焦虑](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/16/business/china-young-people-boom.html)，将是一个错误。中国的工厂正在[大量生产](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-02/china-s-trade-defiance-will-come-at-a-cost?srnd=undefined)越来越多的汽车、机械和其他商品销往全球市场，但从长远来看，这一战略可能难以为继。长期的房地产低迷削弱了[家庭财富](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-13/the-luxury-chinese-shopper-looks-different-now?srnd=undefined)，[就业市场](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/china-at-risk-of-20-youth-joblessness-as-labor-distress-spreads)艰难，国内需求疲弱使价格[面临压力](https://www.economist.com/china/2026/05/21/what-china-can-learn-from-japan-about-escaping-deflation)。在政治上，随着权力在习近平主席领导下[日益集中](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/xi-jinping-much-more-just-one-man)，异议和公共辩论的空间已经缩小。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c181cc90b14818099acdcb784a4a32f/3c181cc9-0b14-8124-b3a6-ca062e0b41b7-4f27a09db4a5af21633dbf027a185b37.png)

在这种背景下，就容易理解为什么人们如此怀念20世纪90年代。1991年至2003年间，朱镕基先后担任副总理和总理，这一时期的特点是日益繁荣和开放。自那时以来，人均国内生产总值增长了十倍以上。他被誉为中国的“经济沙皇”，主导推动加入世界贸易组织、整顿银行体系并重组强大的国有部门，为加入世贸组织后带来的外国竞争做好准备。

正是这种可能性感让人们渴望那个时代。对20世纪90年代的怀旧正在经历一个[全球性时刻](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-real-reason-everyones-so-obsessed-with-90s-nostalgia-right-now/)，人们深情地记得那是一个更简单、更乐观的时代。在中国，著名经济学家赵健（Zhao Jian）最善于捕捉这种渴望，他在朱镕基去世后不久在社交媒体上发表了[一篇文章](https://archive.ph/WzLSx)。

尽管这篇文章是作为悼念而写的，[这篇文章](https://www.sinification.org/p/dreaming-of-zhu-rongjis-china-zhao)——很快被审查者删除——实际上是对当今的尖锐批评。赵写道，尽管三十年前物质生活更加艰难，当时一个月的工资只能买几磅肉，但人们被未来更好的日子的希望所鼓舞。他甚至说，他希望自己从20世纪90年代起就一直生活在一个可以醒来的梦里。

在这一叙事中，朱镕基成为了对未来信心的象征。赵描述了1998年在电视上观看这位言辞强硬的前总理的[首次记者招待会](https://www.nytimes.com/1998/03/20/world/new-china-leader-promises-reforms-for-every-sector.html)的情景；他给人的印象是“扛起这个国家的重担”和“为我们倾尽所有”。尽管作者小心地不指名道姓地指责任何人，但他的目标显而易见。

通过美化朱镕基的成就，赵的文章被广泛解读为对现任领导下中国经济方向的控诉，这一方向偏向高科技冠军企业，而非消费者。我以前写过，家庭支出[必须得到](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-20/china-is-solving-the-wrong-consumption-problem?srnd=undefined)与产业升级同等的战略优先地位。但将朱镕基的经济政绩理想化，可能会忽视其政策在多大程度上为当前问题奠定了基础。

他的一些决定经不起时间的考验。他的改革以牺牲市场力量为代价加强了北京方面的权力，[加州大学圣迭戈分校政治经济学教授石维（Victor Shih）指出](https://www.thewirechina.com/2026/08/15/zhu-rongji-a-reformer-who-centralized-more-power-in-the-chinese-state/)，并助长了更多坏账的产生。在他的任期内，农村收入增长滞后，加剧了日益扩大的不平等问题，朱镕基的继任者[不得不应对](https://yashenghuang.substack.com/p/zhu-rongji-a-complex-leader-with)这些问题。同时，这一经济模式在GDP中的占比越来越依赖投资和出口，为当前的经济失衡埋下了伏笔。

然而，怀旧很少基于对过去的精确记述。人们并非渴望大规模裁员或30年前微薄的生活水平。他们怀念的是这种信念：痛苦的变革正在引领某个方向。在朱镕基助力中国走上更加富裕、与世界更加接轨的道路三十年后，他的逝世揭示了一个事实：对未来的信心本身就是一种经济资产——这是北京领导层应该更加努力加以恢复的东西。

### English Original

## Why China Is Wistful for the 1990s

The death of a beloved leader has often presented challenges for China’s ruling Communist Party. With few avenues for overtly criticizing the regime, [public mourning](https://www.ft.com/content/6e426546-a570-4e0c-a8cf-5da9638fdc42?syn-25a6b1a6=1) can become an expression of broader discontent.

The glowing tributes for former Premier Zhu Rongji, who [died last week](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/zhu-rongji-chinese-premier-who-overhauled-economy-dies-at-97) at 97, should be understood in that context. The outpouring of affection online for a charismatic, plain-spoken reformer, who presided over an economic transformation in the 1990s that paved the way for two decades of breakneck expansion, reflects not only admiration, but unease with the country’s current direction. It’s nostalgia and collective longing [for the past](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-19/why-china-s-gen-z-want-to-go-back-in-time?srnd=undefined).

It would be a mistake for the leadership to ignore the [widespread anxiety](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/16/business/china-young-people-boom.html) on display. China’s factories are [churning out](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-02/china-s-trade-defiance-will-come-at-a-cost?srnd=undefined) ever larger volumes of cars, machinery and other goods for global markets, but this strategy may be unsustainable in the long term. A prolonged property slump has eroded [household wealth](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-13/the-luxury-chinese-shopper-looks-different-now?srnd=undefined), the [job market](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/china-at-risk-of-20-youth-joblessness-as-labor-distress-spreads) is difficult, and weak domestic demand has kept prices [under pressure](https://www.economist.com/china/2026/05/21/what-china-can-learn-from-japan-about-escaping-deflation). Politically, the space for dissent and public debate has narrowed as power has become [increasingly centralized](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/xi-jinping-much-more-just-one-man) under President Xi Jinping.

Against that backdrop, it’s easy to understand why the 1990s are being viewed so wistfully. The years between 1991 and 2003, when Zhu served as vice premier and then premier, were marked by rising prosperity and openness. Per-capita gross domestic product has risen more than tenfold since then. Lionized as China’s economic tsar, he led the push to join the World Trade Organization, clean up the banking system and restructure the powerful state-owned sector to prepare for the foreign competition that membership would bring.

It’s the sense of possibility that is making people hanker for that era. Nostalgia for the 1990s is having a [global moment](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-real-reason-everyones-so-obsessed-with-90s-nostalgia-right-now/), with the decade remembered fondly as a simpler, more optimistic time. In China, few captured this yearning better than Zhao Jian, a prominent economist, who posted [an essay](https://archive.ph/WzLSx) on social media shortly after Zhu's death.

Though written as a remembrance, [the piece](https://www.sinification.org/p/dreaming-of-zhu-rongjis-china-zhao) — which was quickly deleted by censors — amounted to a sharp critique of the modern day. Even though life was materially tougher** **three decades ago when a month’s salary could buy only a few pounds of meat, people were buoyed by the hope of better days ahead, Zhao wrote. He even said he wished he had been living in a dream since the 1990s from which he could wake up.

As part of that narrative, Zhu emerged as a symbol of confidence in the future. Zhao described watching on TV the tough-talking former premier’s [first press conference](https://www.nytimes.com/1998/03/20/world/new-china-leader-promises-reforms-for-every-sector.html) in 1998; he came across as someone “holding up the weight of this country” and “giving his all for us.” Though the writer was careful not to point the finger at anyone, his target was hard to miss.

By romanticizing Zhu’s achievements, Zhao’s essay was widely read as an indictment of the economic direction that China has taken under the present leadership, which favors high-tech champions over consumers. I’ve written before that household spending [must be given](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-20/china-is-solving-the-wrong-consumption-problem?srnd=undefined) the same strategic priority as industrial upgrading. But idealizing Zhu’s economic record risks overlooking the extent to which his policies helped lay the foundation for ongoing problems.

Some of his decisions haven’t aged well. His reforms strengthened Beijing’s hand at the expense of market forces, [noted Victor Shih](https://www.thewirechina.com/2026/08/15/zhu-rongji-a-reformer-who-centralized-more-power-in-the-chinese-state/), a professor of political economy at the University of California at San Diego, and helped create more bad debts. Rural incomes lagged during his tenure, contributing to problems of widening inequality, which Zhu’s successors [had to tackle](https://yashenghuang.substack.com/p/zhu-rongji-a-complex-leader-with). The economic model also became increasingly dependent on investment and exports as a proportion of GDP, setting the stage for current imbalances.

Yet nostalgia rarely turns on a precise account of the past. People aren’t yearning for mass layoffs or the meager living standards of 30 years ago. What they miss is the idea that painful change was leading somewhere. Three decades after Zhu helped set China on a path toward greater affluence and engagement with the world, his death has revealed that confidence about the future is itself an economic asset — something the leadership in Beijing should do much more to restore.
