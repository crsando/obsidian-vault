### 中文译文

## 中国太阳能企业争取美国税收抵免的努力因香港关联受到考验

### **要点** 由彭博AI提供

- 中国太阳能制造商晶科能源（Jinko Solar Co.）将其美国制造子公司的多数股权出售给FH Capital，以便可能符合美国税收抵免资格。
- 此举面临审查，因为FH Capital仍与中国保持联系，包括在香港的业务，这可能使其税收抵免资格复杂化。
- 美国财政部将最终决定资格，并预计将在未来几个月内开始就相关要求进行正式规则制定，而此时外界正在质疑中国公司能在多大程度上维持在美国太阳能市场的立足点。
美国业内人士认为，中国一家太阳能制造商此举是为了获得美国税收抵免资格，该举动正面临审查，因为其新合作伙伴仍与中国保持联系，包括在香港的业务。

总部位于上海的[晶科能源](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688223%20CH%20Equity)于5月[通过一家关联公司](https://www.fhcinv.com/insights/fh-capital-to-acquire-75-1-majority-stake-in-jinkosolars-u-s-manufacturing-operations-establishing-a-premier-domestic-solar-and-bess-platform?utm_source=chatgpt.com)将其美国制造子公司75.1%的多数股权出售给FH Capital——包括位于佛罗里达州杰克逊维尔的组件厂和电池储能业务。这使晶科能源在美国业务中保留了24.9%的少数股权，刚好低于自动取消税收抵免资格的中国所有权门槛。

然而，税务专家向彭博社表示，FH Capital与中国的其他联系仍可能使其税收抵免资格复杂化，尤其是如果特朗普政府在预计于今年晚些时候启动的政府规则制定过程中采取严格标准审查申请人的话。

关键问题在于该公司的领导层和香港业务——美国财政部在确定资格时实际上将香港视为中华人民共和国的一部分。

晶科能源未回应就这些问题（包括FH Capital总部所在地）寻求评论的提问。FH Capital表示，其当前结构符合美国限制中国参与的相关规定，并强调其在香港的存在是其更广泛国际布局的一部分，业务遍及三大洲。

在五月宣布该合作的[新闻稿](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507443974/en/FH-Capital-to-Acquire-75.1-Majority-Stake-in-JinkoSolars-U.S.-Manufacturing-Operations-Establishing-a-Premier-Domestic-Solar-and-BESS-Platform)中，两家公司表示，新架构符合“不断演变的美国清洁能源政策环境”，并为“这一新合资企业扩大产能提供了正确的所有权、管理和战略方向。”

晶科能源的出售交易以及其他制造商的类似重组，引发了人们对中国公司能够在多大程度上维持在美国市场的立足点——并从纳税人补贴中获益——的质疑，尽管国会努力使美国太阳能和电池供应链摆脱对北京的依赖。

特朗普周四又采取了一项措施鼓励美国太阳能制造业，下令对进口的多晶硅衍生物（包括硅片、光伏电池和太阳能组件）征收[15%的关税并设定价格下限](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-signed-order-on-tariffs-trade-actions-on-polysilicon)。

对于中国太阳能企业而言，美国市场仍是首要目标，因为它有望带来高利润率和强劲需求。而在可用的情况下，第45X条税收抵免（组件价值为每瓦0.07美元）有助于支撑盈利能力。

这一动态对特朗普政府构成了考验，因为其正在实施一项新禁令，禁止向由中国或所谓的外国关注实体拥有、控制或与之关联的先进能源设备制造商授予美国税收抵免。这些限制是去年作为唐纳德·特朗普总统的税法的一部分颁布的，当被禁止的外国实体向制造商提供实质性援助或对其运营拥有“有效控制”时，也排除了抵免资格。

美国太阳能制造商联盟执行董事迈克·卡尔表示，国会的目标是防止美国纳税人的钱流向中国企业，这些企业在美国的业务实际上已经使它们能够规避针对北京的关税。

晶科能源的交易**正值在美国运营的太阳能和电池制造商** [做出调整](https://wwww.invenergy.com/news/power-lines/continuing-to-advance-energy-leadership-through-domestic-manufacturing?utm_source=chatgpt.com) **以确保抵免资格**，涉及股权、治理和知识产权的重组。

尽管晶科能源（Jinko Solar）提交的监管文件为FH Capital提供了纽约市办公地址，但该地址对应的是一个虚拟办公室和邮箱服务商，许多其他公司也使用该地址，且没有证据表明该处有实质运营。与此同时，FH Capital的网站指向一个香港地址，并将其自身描述为“Fortune Harmony Investments Limited”，一家“在香港注册的公司”。FH Capital的首席信息官Sanjeev Chaurasia（桑吉夫·乔拉西亚）在领英（LinkedIn）上将其在FH Capital的工作地点描述为香港。

这家新成立的合资企业的领导层似乎也与中国太阳能行业有着长期联系。

晶科能源在上海证券交易所的[公告](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-05-09/688223_20260509_7WTF.pdf)中表示，FH JKV Holdings Limited为此次收购而新成立，并最终由张伟控制。张伟是FH Capital的董事，亚洲财经媒体报道称其与施正荣为夫妻，施正荣因在2000年代创立早期行业领军企业尚德电力控股公司（Suntech Power Holdings Co.）而被称为中国的“太阳王”。

FH Capital在一份电邮声明中表示，该公司的投资者或主要负责人中没有中国公民，并将继续“遵守所有适用的财政部指导意见，并在其不断演变时保持合规”。

“收购晶科能源的关联实体的所有权结构、运营及其他方面完全符合当前外国关注实体（FEOC）的要求，并且在中国香港或中国内地没有任何业务活动。”FH Capital在电邮声明中表示，这里指的是外国关注实体要求。“这一结论基于经验丰富的美国法律顾问的全面审查，反映了完整的事实和情况，而不是任何单一办公地址或个别高管的所称工作地点。”

最终资格的裁决者将是美国财政部，预计将在未来几个月内开始就这些要求进行正式规则制定，此前仅提供了初步的粗略指导。

与此同时，在财政部规则缺位的情况下，各种安排正被制定——而政府可能面临压力，要求将这些安排纳入“祖父条款”保护。贸易律师、Cassidy Levy Kent律师事务所华盛顿办公室合伙人汤姆·贝林（Tom Beline）表示，一些公司正在“采纳各种解释，并试图将其融入市场，这样一来财政部就无法更改这些解释。”

繁荣美国联盟（Coalition for a Prosperous America）执行副总裁尼克·亚科维拉（Nick Iacovella）表示，那些在切断与中国联系方面过于畏首畏尾的制造商将面临风险。

“除非你是一家真正的美国独资公司，试图真正以正确的方式做事，并在美国境内生产100%美国含量的产品，”他说，“否则你将很难绕过即将实施的政策。”

## China Solar Firm’s US Tax-Credit Bid Tested by Hong Kong Tie

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- A Chinese solar manufacturer, Jinko Solar Co., sold a majority stake in its US manufacturing subsidiary to FH Capital to potentially qualify for US tax credits.
- The move faces scrutiny because FH Capital still retains ties to China, including operations in Hong Kong, which could complicate its credit eligibility.
- The US Treasury Department will ultimately determine eligibility, and is expected to begin formal rulemaking on the requirements in the coming months, amid questions about the extent to which Chinese companies can maintain a foothold in the US solar market.
A move by a Chinese solar manufacturer seen by American industry insiders as a bid to qualify for US tax credits faces scrutiny because its new partner still retains ties to China, including operations in Hong Kong.

Shanghai-based [Jinko Solar Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688223%20CH%20Equity)in May sold a 75.1% majority stake in its US manufacturing subsidiary — including a module plant in Jacksonville, Florida, and a battery energy storage business — to FH Capital, [through an affiliate](https://www.fhcinv.com/insights/fh-capital-to-acquire-75-1-majority-stake-in-jinkosolars-u-s-manufacturing-operations-establishing-a-premier-domestic-solar-and-bess-platform?utm_source=chatgpt.com). That left Jinko with a minority 24.9% stake in the US operations, just below a Chinese-ownership threshold that’s automatically disqualifying for the tax credits.

Yet tax experts told Bloomberg that FH Capital’s other ties to China could still complicate its credit eligibility, especially if the Trump administration adopts strict standards for vetting claimants in a government rulemaking process expected to begin late this year.

At issue are the firm’s leadership and operations in Hong Kong — which the US Treasury Department is effectively treating as part of the People’s Republic of China when it comes to determining eligibility.

Jinko Solar didn’t respond to questions seeking comment on those matters, including the location of FH Capital’s headquarters. FH Capital said its current structure complies with US restrictions limiting Chinese involvement and stressed its presence in Hong Kong is part of a broader international footprint, with activities across three continents.

In a May [press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260507443974/en/FH-Capital-to-Acquire-75.1-Majority-Stake-in-JinkoSolars-U.S.-Manufacturing-Operations-Establishing-a-Premier-Domestic-Solar-and-BESS-Platform) announcing the tie-up, the companies said the new structure aligned “with the evolving US clean‑energy policy landscape” and provided “the right ownership, management and strategic direction for this new venture to grow capacity.”

The Jinko sale and similar shuffles by other manufacturers raise questions about the extent to which Chinese companies will be able to maintain a foothold in America — and benefit from taxpayer subsidies — despite Congress’ efforts to wean the US solar and battery supply chain away from Beijing.

Trump took another step to encourage US solar manufacturing on Thursday, ordering [15% tariffs and setting price floors](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-signed-order-on-tariffs-trade-actions-on-polysilicon) on imported polysilicon derivatives, including silicon wafers, photovoltaic cells and solar modules.

To Chinese solar companies, the US market remains a top prize because it offers the promise of high margins and robust demand. And when available, the Sec. 45X tax credits, valued for modules at $0.07 per watt, help buttress profitability.

The dynamic presents a test for the Trump administration as it implements a new ban on awarding US tax credits to advanced energy equipment manufacturers if they are owned by, controlled by or tied to China and other so-called foreign entities of concern. The restrictions, enacted last year as part of President Donald Trump’s tax law, also rule out the credits when prohibited foreign entities provide material assistance to the manufacturers or have “effective control” over their operations.

Congress’ goal was to prevent American taxpayer dollars from flowing to Chinese companies whose US operations already effectively allow them to avoid tariffs targeting Beijing, said Mike Carr, executive director of the Solar Energy Manufacturers for America coalition.

Jinko Solar’s transaction** **came as solar and battery manufacturers operating in the US** **[made changes](https://wwww.invenergy.com/news/power-lines/continuing-to-advance-energy-leadership-through-domestic-manufacturing?utm_source=chatgpt.com)** **to** **ensure eligibility for the credits, with shuffles in ownership, governance and intellectual property.

Although a regulatory filing by Jinko Solar provides a New York City office address for FH Capital, it corresponds to a virtual office and mailbox provider that’s used by a number of other companies without evidence of substantial operations at the site. Meanwhile, FH Capital’s website points to a Hong Kong address and describes itself as “Fortune Harmony Investments Limited,” a “Hong Kong registered company.” And FH Capital’s chief information officer, Sanjeev Chaurasia, describes his FH Capital work location as Hong Kong on LinkedIn.

The newly formed joint venture’s leadership also appears to have longstanding connections with China’s solar industry.

Jinko Solar said in a Shanghai Stock Exchange [filing](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-05-09/688223_20260509_7WTF.pdf) that FH JKV Holdings Limited was newly formed for the acquisition and ultimately controlled by Zhang Wei, a director of FH Capital identified in Asian financial press as married to Shi Zhengrong, the entrepreneur dubbed China’s “Sun King” for his role founding early industry leader Suntech Power Holdings Co. in the 2000s.

FH Capital said in an emailed statement that none of the firm’s investors or principals are Chinese nationals, and that it would continue to “comply with all applicable Treasury guidance as it evolves.”

“The ownership structure, operations and other aspects of the affiliated entities that acquired Jinko are fully compliant with current FEOC requirements and do not have any presence of activities in Hong Kong or China,” FH Capital said in the emailed statement, referring to foreign entity of concern requirements. “This conclusion is based on a comprehensive review by experienced US legal counsel and reflects the complete facts and circumstances, rather than any single office address or alleged location of an individual executive.”

The ultimate arbiter of eligibility will be the US Treasury Department, which is expected to begin formal rulemaking on the requirements in the coming months, after previously providing only initial, broad-brush guidance.

In the meantime, arrangements are being made in the absence of specific Treasury rules — and the administration could face pressure to grandfather them in. Some companies are “taking interpretations and trying to build them into the market, so that way Treasury can’t change those interpretations,” said Tom Beline, a trade lawyer and partner in Cassidy Levy Kent’s Washington, DC office.

Nick Iacovella, executive vice president of the Coalition for a Prosperous America, said there’s risk for manufacturers that move too timidly to jettison ties to China.

“Unless you are a true American-owned company trying to actually do things the right way and create a product that’s 100% US content in the United States,” he said, “you’re going to have a very difficult time getting around the policies that are going to be implemented.”
