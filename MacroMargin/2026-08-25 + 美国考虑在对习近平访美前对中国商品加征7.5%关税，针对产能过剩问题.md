### 中文译文

## 美国考虑在对习近平访美前对中国商品加征7.5%关税，针对产能过剩问题

加利福尼亚州长滩港的集装箱船。
*摄影师：Eric Thayer/Bloomberg*

据知情人士透露，美国计划在下月习近平与唐纳德·特朗普 planned 峰会前，以中国存在过剩产能为由，对其商品加征7.5%的关税。

此举将使特朗普第二任期内对华关税恢复至约20%的水平，北京方面此前表示这一水平与其与华盛顿的贸易休战一致。这些关税是在特朗普第一任期内征收、并在拜登政府期间延长的其他关税之外的额外关税。

这标志着特朗普在最高法院推翻其此前对来自中国及数十个其他经济体的产品征收的进口税后，再次重启其保护主义贸易议程的最新一步，但并未将中美贸易冲突升级至超出商定门槛的水平。

知情人士要求匿名，称具体税率尚未最终确定。特朗普也以在贸易公告前提出临时要求或更改而闻名。一位熟悉审议情况的人士表示，正在考虑的一个选项是宣布对中国征收更高的税率，但暂停其中一部分，以便将实际税率降至7.5%。

知情人士补充说，暂停哪些税率以及暂停多久的细节仍在谈判中。他们表示，北京和华盛顿也在寻求延长所谓的贸易协定，该协定确定了为期一年的休战，将于11月10日到期。

3月，特朗普政府根据《1974年贸易法》第301条，以产能过剩为由，对十几个主要贸易伙伴发起调查。这是总统团队用来以更持久的关税取代其先前关税的两项调查之一。

### **九月峰会**

政府官员希望在特朗普和习近平原定于9月24日在华盛顿会晤之前公布过剩产能调查的结果。据知情人士透露，过剩产能报告的细节在法律上已被证明具有挑战性。

美国贸易代表贾米森·格里尔7月对彭博电视台表示，由于复杂性，过剩产能调查将比另一项针对[强迫劳动](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/trump-extends-10-tariff-baseline-in-broad-use-of-trade-powers)的调查花费更长时间，并称这一延迟与为维持与北京的休战所做的努力无关。

当被问及关税计划时，一位白宫官员表示，任何公告都将直接来自政府，任何有关这些计划的报道或讨论都应被视为毫无根据的猜测。美国贸易代表办公室没有立即回应置评请求。中国商务部没有回复传真置评。

特朗普政府对其首要地缘政治对手中国的态度，与其对某些美国最大传统盟友的立场形成鲜明对比。虽然总统一直不愿破坏与中国的关税休战，但他的政府却对价值数十亿美元的加拿大产品征收50%的关税，并考虑撕毁他在第一任期重新谈判的北美贸易协定。

特朗普正在重建一道关税墙，这道关税墙在最高法院于2月裁定他根据《国际紧急经济权力法》征收的全球关税违反美国宪法后遭到推翻。总统的10%临时全球关税已于7月到期，也被贸易法院认定为非法。

政府正以其对数十个贸易伙伴经济体中强迫劳动和工业产能过剩的调查结果，为其新的全球关税进行辩护。

作为该行动的一部分，特朗普政府7月对中国商品[征收](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/us-sets-new-forced-labor-duties-as-trump-resurrects-tariff-wall)了12.5%的关税，理由是解决强迫劳动问题的努力不足。虽然北京批评此举，但并未宣布报复措施，反而表示华盛顿已同意将对中国出口商品征收的任何额外关税[限制](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-says-us-pledged-to-cap-replacement-tariffs-at-20)在20%以内。这澄清了早先提及去年贸易谈判中讨论的[关税上限](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/china-seeks-to-extend-us-trade-truce-sets-limit-on-301-tariffs)的言论。

### **马来西亚协议**

“我们希望美方信守承诺，确保未来无论以何种理由对华加征或取代关税，美对华关税都不会超过吉隆坡贸易磋商中规定的水平，”中国商务部在5月的一份声明中[表示](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/syxwfb/art/2026/art_3e89743a8d5d4a95bccdda685830afbd.html)。

301条款允许美国贸易代表在总统的指示下，针对其认为对美国企业具有歧视性或违反美国在国际贸易协定项下权利的其他国家贸易措施征收关税。然而，实施这些征税的努力已经遭遇法律挑战。

包括纽约、加利福尼亚和伊利诺伊在内的25个州组成的联盟本月早些时候在美国国际贸易法院提起[诉讼](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/trump-s-latest-tariffs-hit-with-new-lawsuit-by-group-of-states)，加入小企业提出的类似诉讼，这些企业声称这些征税是非法的。各州指控特朗普非法援引301条款，以强迫劳动理由为借口，取代被最高法院推翻的关税。

特朗普政府表示，根据301条款征收的关税在法律上站得住脚，而且该条款得到了此前法院裁决的支持。

其他争端可能给峰会带来新的不确定性。美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特周一威胁要对任何与伊朗有业务往来的国家实施[经济惩罚](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/bessent-pledges-to-isolate-iran-from-global-economy)，而美国近期的[技术限制](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs)已促使北京[收紧](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/china-hits-back-at-us-with-tighter-drone-export-curbs-sanctions)无人机出口管制、制裁美国实体，并警告如果华盛顿继续推进，将采取进一步反制措施。

## US Eyes China Overcapacity Tariffs of 7.5% Before Xi Visits

Container ships at the Port of Long Beach in Long Beach, California.  
*Photographer: Eric Thayer/Bloomberg*

The US is set to impose a 7.5% tariff on Chinese goods over allegations of excess manufacturing capacity before a planned summit between Xi Jinping and Donald Trump next month, according to people familiar with the matter.

The move would restore Trump’s second-term duties on China to around 20%, a level Beijing has previously said is consistent with its trade truce with Washington. Those come on top of other levies imposed during Trump’s first term and extended during the Biden administration.

It would mark the latest step by Trump to resurrect his protectionist trade agenda after the Supreme Court struck down his previous import taxes on products from China and dozens of other economies, while stopping short of escalating the trade conflict with Beijing beyond the agreed-upon threshold.

Exact rates have yet to be finalized, said the people, who spoke on condition of anonymity. Trump is also known to make last-minute demands or changes to trade announcements. One option under consideration is announcing a higher duty rate for China but suspending part of it in order to reduce the effective rate to 7.5%, a person familiar with the deliberations said.

The details of what rates would be suspended and for how long are still under negotiation, the people added. Beijing and Washington are also looking to extend their so-called trade pact, which established a one-year truce that’s set to expire on Nov. 10, they said.

In March, the Trump administration launched an investigation into more than a dozen major trading partners under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 over excess capacity concerns. It was one of two probes that the president’s team used to replace his prior tariffs with more durable ones.

### **September Summit**

Administration officials are hoping to publish the results of the excess capacity inquiry before Trump and Xi are due to meet in Washington on Sept. 24. The details of the overcapacity report have proven legally challenging, according to people familiar with the matter.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told Bloomberg Television in July that the excess capacity investigation would take longer than another on [forced labor](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/trump-extends-10-tariff-baseline-in-broad-use-of-trade-powers) due to its complexity and that the delay had nothing to do with efforts to maintain the truce with Beijing.

When asked about the tariff plans, a White House official said any announcements will come directly from the administration and that any reporting or discussion of them should be considered baseless speculation. The Office of the US Trade Representative didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment. China’s Ministry of Commerce did not respond to a fax for comment.

The Trump administration’s approach to China, its foremost geopolitical rival, stands in stark contrast to its stance toward some of the US’s biggest traditional allies. While the president has been keen not to upset his tariff truce with China, his administration applied a 50% levy on billions of dollars of Canadian products and has mused about tearing up the North American trade agreement he renegotiated during his first term.

Trump is rebuilding a tariff wall that was struck down when the Supreme Court in February ruled his global levies, issued under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, violated the US Constitution. The president’s temporary 10% global tariffs expired in July, and were also deemed illegal by a trade court.

The administration is justifying its new global duties under the findings of its investigations into forced labor and industrial overcapacity in the economies of dozens of trading partners.

As part of that campaign, the Trump administration in July [imposed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/us-sets-new-forced-labor-duties-as-trump-resurrects-tariff-wall) a 12.5% tariff on Chinese goods, citing inadequate efforts to address forced labor practices. While Beijing criticized the move, it stopped short of announcing retaliatory measures, instead saying Washington had agreed to [cap](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-says-us-pledged-to-cap-replacement-tariffs-at-20) any additional duties on Chinese exports at 20%. That clarified earlier comments that referenced [tariff ceilings](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-20/china-seeks-to-extend-us-trade-truce-sets-limit-on-301-tariffs) discussed during trade talks last year.

### **Malaysia Deal**

“We hope that the US will honor its commitments, ensuring that regardless of the reasons given for imposing or replacing tariffs on China in the future, US tariffs on China will not exceed the levels outlined in the Kuala Lumpur trade consultations,” China’s Ministry of Commerce [said](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/syxwfb/art/2026/art_3e89743a8d5d4a95bccdda685830afbd.html) in a May statement.

Section 301 allows the US Trade Representative, under the direction of the president, to impose tariffs in response to other nations’ trade measures it deems discriminatory to American businesses or in violation of US rights under international trade agreements. However, efforts to implement these levies have already come up against legal challenges.

A coalition of 25 states including New York, California and Illinois earlier this month filed a [lawsuit](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/trump-s-latest-tariffs-hit-with-new-lawsuit-by-group-of-states) at the US Court of International Trade, adding to similar proceedings by small businesses that claim the levies are illegal. The states allege Trump unlawfully invoked Section 301 to replace tariffs that were struck down by the Supreme Court by using the forced-labor rationale as a pretext.

The Trump administration has said the duties under Section 301 are legally sound and that the provision has been supported by previous court rulings.

Other disputes could inject fresh uncertainty into the summit. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Monday threatened [economic punishment](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/bessent-pledges-to-isolate-iran-from-global-economy) against any country doing business with Iran, and recent US [technology curbs](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs) have already prompted Beijing to [tighten](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/china-hits-back-at-us-with-tighter-drone-export-curbs-sanctions) drone export controls, sanction American entities and warn of further countermeasures if Washington presses ahead.
