### 中文译文

## 日元干预加大人民币压力，彭博经济研究称

自美国和日本采取措施提振日元以来，人民币面临更大的升值压力。彭博经济研究的报告《中国观察：日元干预加剧人民币与收益率脱节》指出，尽管债券收益率下降且经济疲软，人民币升值仍可能削弱对增长的支持。

“美日7月底的干预对日元的提振效应外溢至包括人民币在内的其他亚洲货币，”彭博经济学家曲大卫和张曙在周三发布的报告中写道。“这加剧了人民币的升值趋势，而这一趋势与对美元利差扩大以及除蓬勃发展的科技行业外大部分经济领域的疲软相悖。”

![Source: Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ba81cc90b14816d9755e30a2881c0c4/3ba81cc9-0b14-81db-add5-fe7bb728b37f-92cb83b5f20648dad2e091fd9faabece.png)

尽管日元现已回吐自7月31日（美日两国自1998年以来首次联手干预以支持日元的日子）以来的约半数涨幅，但韩元等亚洲货币仍处于更强势水平。

据花旗集团策略师称，过去一年中，韩元、新加坡元和台币与日元具有[最高相关性](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/yen-surge-will-drive-up-other-asian-currencies-strategists-say)，而印度卢比和印尼盾的相关性最低。

中国央行近期将人民币每日中间价设定在弱于预期的水平，这可能表明其试图抑制人民币的涨势。

对中国出口的旺盛需求今年推动人民币走强，使其成为亚洲兑美元表现最好的货币，涨幅约3.6%。人民币的走势在很大程度上与国内经济状况脱节，尤其是长期国债收益率下滑扩大了与美国国债的利差。

彭博经济研究认为，人民币走强“打开了一扇窗口”，使中国央行能够降息以支持经济。该机构预计中国央行将在年底前将政策基准利率下调10个基点至1.3%。

直到最近，由于油价上涨推高了工业品出厂价格通胀，许多经济学家一直在收回今年降息的呼吁。但7月消费者价格涨幅为六个月来最慢，而生产者价格通胀自2月底伊朗战争爆发以来首次放缓，再次引发通缩风险。

根据上个月分析师对彭博调查报告中2026年7月中国经济预测的中位数估计，中国人民银行可能会在今年和明年维持政策利率不变。

彭博经济编制的人民币压力指标显示，升值压力仍然强烈，出口商更愿意兑换其美元收入。相比之下，国内经济疲软正在导致中国债券收益率下降。

“强势人民币和中国不断扩大的收益率劣势是其双轨经济的症状，”彭博经济学家表示。“目前我们看不到多少政策催化剂能改变这一趋势。转折点将取决于最高领导层是否会在第三季度末重新评估宏观战略，并部署更广泛的资产负债表刺激措施。”

## Yen Intervention Put China’s Yuan Under More Pressure, BE Says

China’s currency has come under more pressure to appreciate since the US and Japan moved to strengthen the yen, according to CHINA INSIGHT: Yen Intervention Bolstered Yuan-Yield Disconnect, potentially draining support for growth as the yuan gains despite falling bond yields and a sluggish economy.

“The boost to the yen from US-Japan intervention in late July spilled over to other Asian currencies, including the Chinese yuan,” Bloomberg economists David Qu and Chang Shu said in a report published Wednesday. “This exacerbated an upward trend in the yuan that’s at odds with a widening yield disadvantage against the dollar and weakness across much of the economy outside a thriving tech sector.”

While the yen has now wiped out roughly half its gains from July 31, when Japan and the US conducted their first coordinated intervention to support the yen since 1998, Asian currencies like South Korea’s won are still trading at a stronger level.

The won, Singapore’s dollar and the Taiwanese dollar have had the [highest correlation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/yen-surge-will-drive-up-other-asian-currencies-strategists-say) to the yen over the past year, while the Indian rupee and Indonesian rupiah have had the lowest, according to strategists at Citigroup Inc.

China’s central bank has recently been setting the yuan’s daily fixing at a weaker-than-expected level, in what could indicate its effort to temper the currency’s rally.

Booming demand for Chinese exports has fed into a stronger yuan this year, making it Asia’s best-performing currency against the dollar with a gain of about 3.6%. It’s moved largely in disconnect from the state of the domestic economy, especially as slumping long-term sovereign yields widened the spread with comparable US Treasuries.

A stronger currency “opens a window” for the People’s Bank of China to reduce interest rates to support the economy, according to Bloomberg Economics, which expects it to lower its policy benchmark by 10 basis points to 1.3% before the end of the year.

Until recently, many economists have been retracting calls for a rate cut this year after higher oil prices pushed up factory-gate inflation. But in July, consumer prices grew at the slowest pace in six months while producer inflation eased for the first time since the Iran war broke out in late February, again reviving the risk of deflation.

According to the median estimate in a Bloomberg SURVEY REPORT: China Economic Forecasts in July 2026 of analysts last month, the PBOC will probably keep its policy rate unchanged this year and next.

Yuan stress indicators compiled by Bloomberg Economics show that appreciation pressures are still intense, with exporters more willing to convert their dollar revenues. By contrast, economic weakness at home is contributing to the decline in Chinese bond yields.

“The strong yuan and China’s widening yield disadvantage are symptoms of its dual-track economy,” Bloomberg’s economists aid. “We see little in the way of policy catalysts to change the trend now. A turning point will hinge on whether top leadership reassesses macro strategy toward the end of the third quarter and deploys broader balance-sheet stimulus.”
