### 中文译文

## 伊朗称让美国外交重回正轨并非不可能

伊朗最高外交官表示，在与卡塔尔（华盛顿与德黑兰之间战争的调解方）进行他所称的“创造性讨论”后，恢复与美国的外交“并非不可能”。

外交部长阿巴斯·阿拉格齐周五在X上表示，进展“取决于美国对一个简单事实的理解：压力不起作用。”他呼吁华盛顿建立信任并履行承诺，但未详细说明。

这些相对积极的言论是在一系列对德黑兰的外交访问以及伊朗和阿曼之间关于允许船只通过霍尔木兹海峡的框架的谈判之后发表的。巴基斯坦是这场为期六个月冲突的主要调解方，周一派代表团前往伊朗首都，而阿曼和卡塔尔的高级官员则在本周晚些时候跟进。

美国在公开评论中仍持鹰派立场。财政部长斯科特·贝森特周一发起了一场进一步施压伊朗经济的行动，威胁要惩罚任何与伊斯兰共和国做生意的国家。

据《华尔街日报》报道，总统唐纳德·特朗普已表示，他不会仅仅为了重启谈判而解除对伊朗港口的封锁——这是德黑兰的一项关键要求。他也没有兴趣恢复六月份达成的现已失效的临时和平协议的条款。

卡塔尔外交部周四表示，卡塔尔首相谢赫·穆罕默德·本·阿卜杜勒拉赫曼·阿勒萨尼在伊朗首都的会晤侧重于寻找为恢复与美国的对话创造“适当条件”的方法。卡塔尔首相与卡利巴夫讨论恢复外交谈判的方式(3)

他说，另一个优先事项是努力寻找重新开放霍尔木兹海峡的方法。自2月底美国和以色列发动战争以来，这条对石油和天然气供应至关重要的水道实际上已陷入瘫痪。

伊朗最高安全官员莫赫森·雷扎伊表示，如果美国满足一系列他未具体说明的条件，伊朗将采取行动重新开放霍尔木兹海峡。伊朗军方[表示](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/iran-oman-agree-to-share-strait-of-hormuz-revenue-irgc-says)，已于周三与阿曼就通过该海峡的过境达成收入分成协议，但伊朗官员一再表示，该协议并不等于立即重新开放这条水道。

尽管如此，随着伊朗对航运的威胁消退，通过该海峡的原油流量一直在上升。

据参与并监测货运活动的石油交易商估计，目前每天约有600万至800万桶原油通过这一关键咽喉要道，约为战前水平的一半。高盛集团分析师估计，来自波斯湾的石油出口总量（包括陆路）已恢复到2月前水平的约三分之二。

周五油价基本持稳，布伦特原油交易价约为每桶89美元，今年迄今已上涨约45%。

伊拉克[正提供](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/iraq-offers-oil-outside-hormuz-for-first-time-since-war-began)其原油买家自战争开始以来首次从波斯湾以外地点提货的选择，凸显出各国维持出口努力的韧性。但由于不确定性持续存在，卡塔尔将针对欧洲和亚洲买家的液化天然气供应的不可抗力延长了一个月。

阿拉格希是6月签署的现已破裂的停火协议的重要支持者，这一立场招致了普遍支持继续战斗的伊朗强硬派的批评。

## Iran Says Getting US Diplomacy Back on Track Not Impossible

Iran’s top diplomat said resuming diplomacy with the US “isn’t impossible,” after what he described as “creative discussions” with Qatar, a mediator in the war between Washington and Tehran.

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on X on Friday that progress “hinges on US understanding of one simple fact: pressure doesn’t work.” He called on Washington to build trust and uphold its commitments, without elaborating.

The relatively positive comments came after a series of diplomatic visits to Tehran alongside talks between Iran and Oman about a framework for allowing vessels through the Strait of Hormuz. Pakistan, the main mediator in the six-month conflict, sent a delegation to the Iranian capital on Monday, while senior officials from Oman and Qatar followed later in the week.

The US remains hawkish in its public comments. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent launched a campaign to further squeeze Iran’s economy on Monday, threatening punishment against any country doing business with the Islamic Republic.

President Donald Trump has signaled he will not lift a blockade of Iranian ports — a key demand of Tehran’s — just to restart talks. He’s also got no interest in returning to the terms of a now lapsed interim peace deal agreed to in June, the Wall Street Journal reported.

Another priority is to work on ways to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, he said. The critical waterway for oil and gas supplies has been effectively paralyzed since the US and Israel started the war at the end of February.

Iran’s top security official, Mohsen Rezaee, said the country would move to reopen the Strait of Hormuz if the US meets a set of conditions which he didn’t specify. Iran’s military [said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/iran-oman-agree-to-share-strait-of-hormuz-revenue-irgc-says) on Wednesday that a revenue-sharing agreement had been reached with Oman regarding transit through the strait, though Iranian officials have repeatedly said a deal would not equate to an immediate re-opening of the waterway.

Still, the flow of crude through the strait has been rising as Iran’s threat to shipping has dissipated.

About 6 million to 8 million barrels a day of crude are now being shipped through the the key chokepoint, according to estimates from oil traders involved in and monitoring cargo activity. That’s roughly half prewar levels. Total oil exports from the Persian Gulf, which includes land routes, have recovered to around two-thirds of volumes seen before February, analysts at Goldman Sachs Group Inc. estimated.

Oil prices were broadly steady on Friday, with Brent crude trading at about $89 a barrel, having gained about 45% this year.

Iraq [is offering](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-27/iraq-offers-oil-outside-hormuz-for-first-time-since-war-began) buyers of its crude the option to collect supplies from outside of the Persian Gulf for the first time since the war began, highlighting the resilience of nations’ efforts to maintain exports. But Qatar extended a force majeure on liquefied natural gas supplies to European and Asian buyers by another month as uncertainty persists.

Araghchi was a prominent supporter of the now-collapsed ceasefire deal signed in June, a stance that attracted criticism from Iranian hardliners who are broadly in favor of continued fighting.
