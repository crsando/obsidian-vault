### 中文译文

## 中国可再生能源巨头远景集团启动数据中心

### **彭博AI要点**

- 远景集团已在中国内蒙古启用了吉瓦级数据中心的一期项目，其中最初的120兆瓦容量已分配给两家当地科技巨头。
- 该公司计划最终将项目规模扩大到2吉瓦，这将是全中国最大的数据中心之一，这是中国企业在蓬勃发展的数据中心行业抢占一席之地的更广泛竞赛的一部分。
- 远景集团将受益于其广泛的可再生能源投资组合，项目80%的电力将来自附近的风电场，并配备电池储能作为备用电源，其目标是到2030年在全球开发5吉瓦的人工智能数据中心容量。
远景集团已在中国内蒙古启用了一个吉瓦级数据中心的一期项目，这是一家可再生能源公司向蓬勃发展的人工智能行业的一次不寻常进军。

该公司在一份声明中表示，已开始运营位于乌兰察布的银河园区项目——这座城市位于北京西北约350公里（217英里）处，拥有丰富的风力资源。公司表示，一期120兆瓦的容量已分配给两家当地科技巨头，并计划最终将项目规模扩大到2吉瓦，这将是全中国最大的数据中心之一。

远景集团计划在内蒙古乌兰察布建设一个大型数据中心（蓝色标记），这将成为中国最大的数据中心之一。

远景集团的行动是中国企业在蓬勃发展的数据中心行业抢占一席之地的更广泛竞赛的一部分。据彭博新能源财经（BloombergNEF）称，该领域迄今一直由电信运营商、专业开发商以及包括腾讯控股有限公司、阿里巴巴集团控股有限公司和中国联合网络通信有限公司在内的互联网巨头主导。

据知情人士透露，AI公司DeepSeek也[计划](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/deepseek-is-developing-massive-ai-data-center-in-inner-mongolia)在乌兰察布建设一个1吉瓦的数据中心。该市气候寒冷，年均气温4摄氏度（华氏39度），有助于降低为高耗电AI服务器降温所需的能耗。前身为智谱的Z.AI也正在[推进](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/z-ai-completes-giant-data-center-with-chinese-chips-to-train-ai)一个1吉瓦的项目，计划全部采用国产芯片。

远景（Envision）将受益于其广泛的可再生能源产品组合，涵盖风力发电机生产和电池制造等领域。这将赋予其独特优势，尤其是在政府要求数据中心五分之四的电力来自绿色电力之后。

银河项目是远景到2030年在全球开发5吉瓦AI数据中心产能雄心的基石。该公司表示，该项目80%的电力将通过专用输电电缆来自公司附近的风电场。电池储能将用作备用电源，摆脱传统数据中心依赖柴油发电机的模式。

远景将提供电力基础设施和配套系统，但不会处理实际的数据处理工作。这家能源公司表示，科技公司实际上将充当租户，租用设施并安装自己的芯片集群。

## Chinese Renewable Energy Giant Envision Launches Data Center

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- Envision Group has commissioned the initial phase of a gigawatt-scale data center in China's Inner Mongolia, with the initial 120-megawatt phase allocated to two local tech giants.
- The company plans to eventually scale the project to 2 gigawatts, which would make it one of the largest in China, as part of a broader race among Chinese firms to secure a foothold in the booming data center sector.
- Envision will benefit from its wide renewables portfolio and provide 80% of the project's power from nearby wind farms, with battery storage used as backup, as it aims to develop 5 gigawatts of AI data center capacity globally by 2030.
Envision Group has commissioned the initial phase of a gigawatt-scale data center in China’s Inner Mongolia, an unusual foray by a renewable energy company into the booming artificial intelligence industry.

The Chinese company has began operation of the Galaxy Campus project in Ulanqab — a city about 350 kilometers (217 miles) northwest of Beijing with abundant wind power resources, it said in a statement. The initial 120-megawatt phase has already been allocated to two local tech giants, with plans to eventually scale the project to 2 gigawatts, which would make it one of the largest in China, the company said.

Envision is planning a major data center in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia (blue marker), which would be among the biggest in China.

Envision’s push is part of a broader race among Chinese firms to secure a foothold in the booming data center sector. The segment has to date been dominated by telecom operators, specialized developers, and internet giants including Tencent Holdings Ltd., Alibaba Group Holding Ltd. and China United Network Communications Ltd., according to BloombergNEF.

AI company DeepSeek is also [planning](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/deepseek-is-developing-massive-ai-data-center-in-inner-mongolia) a 1 gigawatt data center in Ulanqab, according to people familiar with the matter. The city’s cold climate, with an average annual temperature of 4C (39F), helps reduce the energy required to cool power-hungry AI servers. Z.AI, formerly Zhipu, is also [working](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/z-ai-completes-giant-data-center-with-chinese-chips-to-train-ai) on a 1 gigawatt project that it plans to fill with only domestically made chips.

Envision will benefit from its wide renewables portfolio, which ranges from wind turbine manufacturing to battery production. That will give it a unique advantage, especially after the government required four-fifths of data center power to come from green electricity.

The Galaxy project is a cornerstone of Envision’s ambitions to develop 5 gigawatts of AI data center capacity globally by 2030. It will get 80% of its power from the company’s nearby wind farms through dedicated transmission cables, it said. Battery storage will be used as backup — stepping away from the traditional data center model of relying on diesel generators.

Envision will provide the power infrastructure and supporting system, but won’t handle the actual data processing. Tech companies will effectively act as tenants, leasing the facilities and installing their own chip clusters, the energy firm said.
