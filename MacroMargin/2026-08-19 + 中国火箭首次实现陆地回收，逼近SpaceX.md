## 中国火箭首次实现陆地回收，逼近SpaceX

*朱雀三号Y2火箭于8月19日从东风商业航天创新试验区发射。*

*来源：VCG/Getty Images*

作者：[Josh Xiao](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVxUV9dEiUc/josh-xiao) 和 [Danny Lee](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVmRKy8A2EI/danny-lee)

2026年8月19日 上午10:26 GMT+8
*更新于2026年8月19日 下午5:03 GMT+8*

中国首次在陆地上回收火箭助推器，这是追赶埃隆·马斯克旗下[SpaceX](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SPCX%20US%20Equity)等美国公司的重要一步。

据官方新华社[报道](https://www.news.cn/20260819/c985736a354d4e2495f61ec39c53a1de/c.html)，朱雀三号火箭于周三帮助将一颗卫星送入轨道后，成功在西北省份甘肃省着陆。火箭从中国主要航天发射中心之一酒泉起飞。

网上发布的视频片段显示，火箭逐渐减小推力，在特殊着陆腿上轻轻触地，这使其能够重复使用。

> 中国周三通过发射朱雀三号实现了首次陆地火箭回收。观看视频：[pic.twitter.com/IKvtGhHqAc](https://t.co/IKvtGhHqAc)

> — 中国《人民日报》（@PDChina）2026年8月19日

这一突破标志着中国太空计划以及该飞行器开发商[蓝箭航天（LandSpace Technology）](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1678414D:CH)的一个重要里程碑，因为可重复使用火箭能大幅降低将卫星送入太空的成本。中国上个月成功回收了一枚火箭，但那次是在[海上](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-10/china-successfully-launches-retrieves-reusable-rocket-booster)进行的。

“蓝箭航天凭借朱雀-3号成功回收助推器，是中国商业航天领域的分水岭，”彭博行业研究分析师Eric Zhu表示。

尽管如此，中国也遭遇了一些挫折，比如本月早些时候一枚火箭在[发射时爆炸](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/chinese-rocket-fails-while-launching-communications-satellite)。

朱雀-3号由商业发射供应商蓝箭航天（LandSpace）开发。这家中国火箭制造商去年曾寻求在上海IPO中募资75亿元人民币，以便在上海上市，与此同时，它正努力追赶SpaceX和杰夫·贝索斯支持的[蓝色起源（Blue Origin LLC）](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/5043517Z:US)。该公司过去的成就包括在发射甲烷燃料火箭方面[击败SpaceX](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-07-12/chinese-startup-beats-musk-with-launch-of-first-methane-rocket)。

自2017年以来，SpaceX一直回收和再利用其猎鹰9号火箭的助推器，这帮助它在重型运载火箭发射领域获得了近乎垄断的地位，并支撑了其数千颗星链（Starlink）卫星的增长。

这促使中国采取行动，该国正处于建设类似互联网卫星网络的早期阶段。这一努力一直依赖一次性火箭将卫星送入轨道。

蓝箭航天是中国政府向私营部门开放该行业十年来涌现的领先初创企业之一。2023年，它成为全球首家发射甲烷燃料火箭的公司，甲烷燃料有潜力比其他燃料更清洁、更安全。

朱强调，蓝箭航天“现在面临更艰巨的工程挑战，即证明其能够快速且经济高效地翻新和重新飞行这些硬件——这一过程 SpaceX 花了数年时间才掌握。”

中国并非唯一追赶 SpaceX 的国家。印度公司也在致力于建造可重复使用的火箭，初创公司 [AgniKul Cosmos](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1697522D:IN) 正准备执行一项 [2027年初](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/science/agnikul-cosmos-bets-on-reusable-rockets-to-make-launches-on-demand-and-affordable-eyes-early-2027-for-mission-2/article71297966.ece) 的任务，目标是在将有效载荷送入轨道后回收火箭的第一级。

## China Rocket Comes Home for First Time, Closing in on SpaceX

*The Zhuque-3 Y2 rocket sets off from the Dongfeng commercial space innovation pilot zone on Aug. 19.*

*Source: VCG/Getty Images*

By [Josh Xiao](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVxUV9dEiUc/josh-xiao) and [Danny Lee](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVmRKy8A2EI/danny-lee)

August 19, 2026 at 10:26 AM GMT+8  
*Updated on August 19, 2026 at 5:03 PM GMT+8*

China recovered a rocket booster on land for the first time, a major step toward catching up with US companies such as Elon Musk’s [SpaceX](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SPCX%20US%20Equity).

A Zhuque-3 rocket successfully landed on its legs in the northwestern province of Gansu after helping send a satellite into orbit on Wednesday, [according](https://www.news.cn/20260819/c985736a354d4e2495f61ec39c53a1de/c.html) to the official Xinhua News Agency. The rocket took off from Jiuquan, one of China’s main space launch centers.

A video clip posted online showed the rocket tapering back on thrust to touch down gently on special legs, which should allow it to be reused.

> China on Wednesday achieved its first rocket recovery on land with the launch of Zhuque-3. Watch video: [pic.twitter.com/IKvtGhHqAc](https://t.co/IKvtGhHqAc)

>

> — People's Daily, China (@PDChina) August 19, 2026

The breakthrough marks a significant milestone for China’s space program and the craft’s developer, [LandSpace Technology](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1678414D:CH), as reusable rockets can substantially lower the cost of launching satellites into space. China successfully recovered a rocket last month, but it did so [at sea](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-10/china-successfully-launches-retrieves-reusable-rocket-booster).

“LandSpace’s successful booster recovery with the Zhuque-3 is a watershed moment for China’s commercial space sector,” said Eric Zhu, an analyst at Bloomberg Intelligence.

Still, the nation’s had its share of setbacks, such as a rocket [exploding during launch](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/chinese-rocket-fails-while-launching-communications-satellite) earlier this month.

The Zhuque-3 is developed by commercial launch provider LandSpace, which Chinese Rocket Maker LandSpace Seeks 7.5b Yuan in Shanghai IPOlast year to go public in Shanghai, as it races to catch up to SpaceX and [Blue Origin LLC](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/5043517Z:US), which is backed by Jeff Bezos. The company’s past achievements include [beating SpaceX](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-07-12/chinese-startup-beats-musk-with-launch-of-first-methane-rocket) in launching a methane-fueled rocket.

SpaceX has been recovering and recycling the boosters of its Falcon 9 rockets since 2017, helping it grab a near-monopoly in heavy-lift rocket launches and supporting the growth of its thousands of Starlink satellites.

That’s spurred a push in China, which is in the early stages of building a similar internet satellite network. That effort has relied on single-use rockets to put satellites into orbit.

LandSpace is one of the leading startups to emerge in the decade since the Chinese government opened the industry to the private sector. In 2023, it became the first company in the world to launch a rocket fueled by methane, which has potential to be cleaner and safer than other fuels.

Zhu said LandSpace “now faces the tougher engineering challenge of demonstrating it can rapidly and cost-effectively refurbish and refly that hardware — a process that took SpaceX several years to master.”

China isn’t the only one chasing SpaceX. Indian firms are also working toward building reusable rockets, with startup [AgniKul Cosmos](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1697522D:IN) preparing for an [early-2027](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/science/agnikul-cosmos-bets-on-reusable-rockets-to-make-launches-on-demand-and-affordable-eyes-early-2027-for-mission-2/article71297966.ece) mission aimed at recovering a rocket’s first stage after delivering its payload into orbit.
