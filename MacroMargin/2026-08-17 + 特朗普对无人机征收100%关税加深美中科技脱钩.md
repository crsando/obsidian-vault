## 特朗普对无人机征收100%关税加深美中科技脱钩

作者：[Laura Curtis](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQx5ShZM0w/laura-curtis)、[Alicia Diaz](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVmRlguxhlo/alicia-diaz)和[Lucille Liu](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATya_uZWyL0/lucille-liu)
2026年8月14日上午5:35 GMT+8
*更新于2026年8月14日下午4:25 GMT+8*

### **要点**（Bloomberg AI）

- 特朗普政府正在对进口无人机及其零部件征收高达100%的关税，以减少美国对外国供应商的依赖。
- 最高税率将适用于较重的无人机或具备某些能力的无人机，而较小的无人机将被征收25%的关税，部分贸易伙伴适用较低的税率。
- 这些关税可能加剧与世界主要无人机制造商中国的分离，并增加美中之间一系列针锋相对的措施。
特朗普政府正在对进口无人机及其零部件征收高达100%的关税，加大力度减少美国对外国供应商的依赖，并可能加速与中国无人机供应链的脱钩。

白宫周四表示，最高税率将适用于较重的无人机（重量超过25公斤）或具备某些能力的无人机。较小的无人机将被征收25%的关税，而欧盟和日本等部分贸易伙伴适用较低的税率。

该声明没有提及中国，但这些关税可能加剧与世界主要无人机制造商之间已在进行中的双向分离。它们还增加了一系列针锋相对的措施，而此时距离中国领导人习近平预计将在美国与总统唐纳德·特朗普会晤仅数周。

“长期来看，我们可能正滑向至少在某些类型无人机上实现硬脱钩的结果，”龙洲经讯（Gavekal Dragonomics）中国研究部副主任克里斯托弗·贝多尔（Christopher Beddor）表示。“我认为这是更广泛模式的一部分，即美国和中国都维护彼此的基本贸易协议，但继续采取相对低级别的对抗行动。”

去年，美国对外国新机型的限制抑制了中国进入美国市场，而北京则加强了对向美国出口的无人机相关产品的审查。中国海关数据显示，今年上半年中国对美无人机出口降至约5000万美元，约为上年同期水平的一半。

![Drone export data chart](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b1481d7a87fc4c976c30fd9/3bc81cc9-0b14-81e4-9d03-f0b8f48849cf-5aa9d999ea12538b566f9e7f292dee25.png)

美国无人机制造商AeroVironment Inc.和Aevex Corp.以及零部件供应商Unusual Machines Inc.的股价在公告发布后的盘后交易中上涨。中国主要无人机制造商股价周五小幅收高，与硬件同行的涨幅一致。

据白宫称，周四，在关于调整美国无人机系统及无人机系统组件进口的公告中，商务部长霍华德·卢特尼克得出结论，外国无人机制造商的进口渗透“相当大”，美国“过于依赖”外国无人机系统及其组件。

俄罗斯在乌克兰的战争展示了无人机的军事效用，帮助双方守住领土，并开启了混合战争的新时代，这加剧了华盛顿保护国内产业的紧迫性。特朗普在公告中宣布，无人机系统对美国的“国家和经济安全”至关重要。

该声明发布之前，美国已针对中国在先进技术领域的主导地位采取了其他行动。美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）12月采取行动，[阻止](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-22/fcc-blocks-foreign-made-drones-from-future-us-use)进口部分外国制造的无人机，包括总部位于深圳的大疆（DJI Technologies）的新机型。大疆去年占美国商用无人机市场约70%的份额。

最近，美国联邦通信委员会还[禁止](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs)进口部分外国制造的机器人和电源逆变器。这些限制引发了中国商务部的报复，该部在一系列反制措施中，将可用于军事目的的无人机、关键组件及相关技术的出口置于“严格逐案审查”之下。

“这是为了减少对中国先进无人机及重要组件的依赖，因为此前FCC的壁垒并未涵盖原有型号和许多无人机组件的进口，”法国外贸银行（Natixis）亚太区首席经济学家艾丽西亚·加西亚-埃雷罗（Alicia Garcia-Herrero）表示。

![Trade data chart](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b1481d7a87fc4c976c30fd9/3bc81cc9-0b14-8178-bce1-cfbcf3c7d121-36391680cc4592f31d2419ec3ca0612a.png)

据中国[海关总署](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GACZ%20CH%20Equity)数据，今年前六个月中国向美国出口了近3亿美元的无人机和飞机零部件，而去年全年为5.8亿美元。海关总署没有提供无人机零部件的单独分类。

周四的决定标志着特朗普自最高法院2月裁定他援引联邦紧急权力征收全面全球关税超越权限以来，再次采取加征关税行动。

此次无人机关税依据的是1962年《贸易扩展法》第232条，该条款要求在允许以国家安全为由实施进口限制前进行调查。特朗普曾援引同样的权力对钢铁、铝、半成品铜、汽车和汽车零部件等商品征收关税。对机器人和风电涡轮机的调查正在232条款下进行。

能力不被视为对国家安全特别敏感的小型无人机将面临25%的关税，其他零部件也是如此。与特朗普达成贸易协议的多个经济体的无人机关税将为15%，前提是所有硬件和技术均来自这些地方和美国。

这些地方包括欧盟、日本、瑞士和台湾。英国对其无人机和零部件将被征收10%的关税。

大部分关税将在特朗普签署公告后21天生效。对不被视为特别敏感的零部件的关税将在180天后开始。

## Trump’s 100% Tariff on Drones Deepens US-China Tech Decoupling

By [Laura Curtis](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AQx5ShZM0Yw/laura-curtis), [Alicia Diaz](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVmRlguxhlo/alicia-diaz), and [Lucille Liu](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATya_uZWyL0/lucille-liu)
August 14, 2026 at 5:35 AM GMT+8
*Updated on August 14, 2026 at 4:25 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- The Trump administration is imposing tariffs of as much as 100% on imported drones and their components to reduce US reliance on foreign suppliers.
- The top rate will apply to heavier drones or those that possess certain capabilities, while smaller drones will be taxed at 25%, and lower rates apply to some trading partners.
- The tariffs risk reinforcing a separation with China, the world’s dominant drone manufacturer, and add to a series of tit-for-tat measures between the US and China.
The Trump administration is imposing tariffs of as much as 100% on imported drones and their components, escalating efforts to reduce US reliance on foreign suppliers and potentially accelerating a decoupling of drone supply chains with China.

The top rate will apply to heavier drones — weighing more than 25 kilograms — or those that possess certain capabilities, the White House said Thursday. Smaller drones will be taxed at 25%, while lower rates apply to some trading partners such as the European Union and Japan.

The statement didn’t mention China, but the tariffs risk reinforcing a two-way separation with the world’s dominant drone manufacturer already under way. They also add to a drumbeat of tit-for-tat measures just weeks before Chinese leader Xi Jinping is expected to meet with President Donald Trump in the US.

“In the long term, we’re probably slipping towards an outcome of a hard decoupling for at least certain types of drones,” said Christopher Beddor, deputy China research director at Gavekal Dragonomics. “I think this is part of a broader pattern in which both the US and China uphold their basic trade agreement but continue to take relatively low-grade actions against each other.”

US restrictions on new foreign-made models last year have curbed Chinese access to the American market, while Beijing has tightened scrutiny of drone-related exports to the US. Chinese drone exports to the US fell to about $50 million in the first half of this year, roughly half the level recorded a year earlier, according to Chinese customs data.

Shares of US drone makers AeroVironment Inc. and Aevex Corp., along with parts supplier Unusual Machines Inc., jumped in post-market trading after the announcement. Shares of major Chinese drone manufacturers closed with small gains on Friday, in line with increases seen across hardware peers.

According to the White House: Adjusting Imports of Unmanned Aircraft Systems and Unmanned Aircraft Systems Components into the United States Thursday, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick concluded that import penetration from foreign drone producers is “substantial,” and that the US is “too reliant” on foreign unmanned aircraft systems and their parts.

Adding to the Washington’s urgency to protect its domestic industry, Russia’s war in Ukraine has showcased the military effectiveness of drones, helping both sides hold territory and ushering a new era of hybrid warfare. Trump, in the proclamation, declared the unmanned aircraft systems “essential to the national and economic security” of the US.

The announcement follows other actions aimed at China’s dominance in advanced technology. The Federal Communications Commission moved in December to [block](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-22/fcc-blocks-foreign-made-drones-from-future-us-use) imports of some foreign-made drones including new models from Shenzhen-based DJI Technologies, which accounted for about 70% of the US commercial drone market last year.

More recently, the FCC also [banned](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs) some foreign-made robots and power inverters. These curbs provoked retaliation from China’s Ministry of Commerce, which placed exports of drones, their key components and related technologies that can be used for military purposes on “strict case-by-case review,” among a barrage of countermeasures.

“This is about reducing dependence on from China for advanced drones and importantly components, as previous FCC barriers did not cover imports of grandfathered models and many drone components,” said Alicia Garcia-Herrero, chief economist for Asia-Pacific at Natixis.

China exported nearly $300 million of drone and aircraft parts to the US in the first six months of this year, compared with $580 million for all of last year, according to China’s [General Administration of Customs](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GACZ%20CH%20Equity), which does not provide a separate breakdown for drone components.

Thursday’s decision marks another move by Trump to impose tariffs since the Supreme Court ruled in February that he exceeded his authority by invoking emergency federal powers to impose sweeping global levies.

The drone duties rely instead on Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, which requires an investigation before allowing import restrictions on national security grounds. Trump has invoked the same power to collect tariffs on goods including steel and aluminum, semi-finished copper, and cars and auto parts. Probes into robotics and wind turbines are ongoing under 232 authority.

Smaller drones lacking capabilities deemed especially sensitive for national security will face a 25% duty, as will other components. Tariffs on drones from several economies that struck trade deals with Trump will be 15%, so long as all hardware and technology originates from those places and the US.

Those locations include the European Union, Japan, Switzerland and Taiwan. The United Kingdom will be subject to a 10% tariff on its drones and components.

Most of the levies will take effect in 21 days after Trump signed the proclamation. Tariffs on components that aren’t considered particularly sensitive will begin after 180 days.
