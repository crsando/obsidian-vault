### 中文译文

## 台湾为军事演习降速互联网，当地人并不担忧。

关于攻击导致断网威胁的公共警报，民众耸耸肩不以为意。

台北——台湾政府本周向数百万部手机推送紧急警报，警告高速5G服务将受到干扰。这条消息伴随着通常用于自然灾害的紧急系统的响亮蜂鸣声发出，是演习的一部分，旨在唤醒民众对可能因中国攻击而断网的风险的认识。

从公众反应来看，政府可能需要更响亮的警报。

在目前台湾周边正在进行的[年度军事演习](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/taiwan-tests-war-plans-to-thwart-chinese-invasion-951a2932?mod=article_inline)中，针对“通信基础设施遭空袭模拟”，政府计划在下周两个下午将移动互联网速度放缓30分钟。

“哦，有演习吗？我没仔细看，”42岁的台北发型师陶陶在被问及警报时说。“我工作其实不太需要网络。”

台湾总统赖清德将说服岛上2300万居民为可能来自中国大陆的入侵做好准备视为自己的使命。他面临着相当多的公众不愿将危机应对视为生活方式的情况。

中国声称对该岛拥有主权，并使用军事攻击威胁以及更微妙的手段，试图引导台湾民众向北京屈服。近年来，与中国有关的船只切断了台湾[赖以保持联网](https://www.wsj.com/articles/remote-corner-of-taiwan-confronts-wartime-scenario-life-with-no-internet-bb7346c0?mod=article_inline)的海底光纤电缆至少五次。

台北承认网络连接是其弱点之一。

“台湾是全世界极少数每天都有人试图切断其海底电缆的地方之一，”一位台湾高级安全官员说，“这样的国家怎能不做好准备？”

![A Taiwanese military Patriot missile system deployed in a local park.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481e38c9edd7dde2c1eec/3b881cc9-0b14-8155-9631-df9c5bc0eddc-0af7e2580c9ce8117a39965d1094048a.jpg)

军事演习期间，新北市的一个爱国者导弹系统。Ann Wang/路透社

官员们表示，演习的目标是鼓励公众找出替代的沟通方式。

下周的网络放缓将与一年一度的防空演习同时进行——这意味着受影响地区的居民应待在室内，使用不受限的Wi-Fi。不依赖移动互联网的企业甚至可能不知道服务受到影响。

“政府极为谨慎，确保这次演习不会影响日常生活，”赖清德设立的改善台湾民防与灾害应对委员会成员Chiayo Kuo表示。

Kuo表示，政府希望提高人们对日常生活可能被打断的认识，但又不造成重大干扰。

总统府表示，这是台湾首次进行网络降速演习，旨在让公众熟悉“如何在紧急情况下确保自己和家人通信不中断”。相关工作还包括推广备用方法。

台湾官员表示，这一网速放缓将类似于在国外漫游时使用数据流量受限的 SIM 卡，对于习惯快速、普及的移动互联网的民众来说，这可能会是一种不寻常的体验。

台北手机用户周一收到紧急推送警报，警告 8 月 10 日当周将进行互联网减速演习。图片来源：Joyu Wang/WSJ

台湾还希望建立卫星互联网覆盖，以此规避该岛在海底电缆遭切断攻击时的脆弱性。

据台湾太空机构称，台湾正在研发自己的低地球轨道卫星，但首次发射计划要到 2028 年才进行。

台北也在寻求国际合作。立法机构最近放宽了一项外资持股规定，该规定 [曾阻碍](https://www.wsj.com/tech/taiwans-race-for-secure-internet-detours-around-musks-starlink-7c273912?mod=article_inline) 引入 [SpaceX 的](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/SPCX) Starlink，后者是全球最大的卫星互联网提供商。[Eutelsat 的](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/FR/XPAR/ETL) OneWeb 已经在与台湾合作，但服务有限，且不向个人用户开放。

在台北，台湾自行安排的网速放缓演习定于周四下午进行，许多人对这件事似乎并不在意，或者根本不知道。

“我不太明白为什么防空演习要关闭互联网，”31 岁的 Jack Chung 说，他在一家电子公司工作。他说这对他影响不大，因为他在办公室用 Wi-Fi 工作。

“半小时反正也不算长，”他说。

58岁的环保工程师杭格瑞·吴（Hangry Wu）表示，这半小时不会太难熬，尽管他不喜欢自己为最快的移动网络付费，政府却将其降速。

他补充说他会理解。

“如果政府这么做，我相信他们一定有理由，”吴说，不过他承认自己不知道理由是什么。

## Taiwan Is Slowing Its Internet for a War Drill. Locals Aren’t Worried.

## **Taiwan Is Slowing Its Internet for a War Drill. Locals Aren’t Worried.**

A public alert to the threat of being forced offline by an attack lands with a shrug

TAIPEI—[Taiwan](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/taiwan)’s government pushed urgent alerts to millions of cellphones this week to warn people their high-speed 5G service would be disrupted. The message, delivered with the loud buzz of an emergency system typically used for natural disasters, was part of a drill intended to awaken the population to the risk of being taken offline by a Chinese attack.

Judging by the public response, the government might need a louder alarm.

In “a simulation of air attacks on communications infrastructure” amid [annual military exercises](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/taiwan-tests-war-plans-to-thwart-chinese-invasion-951a2932?mod=article_inline) now under way around Taiwan, the government plans to slow mobile-internet speeds for 30 minutes on two afternoons next week.

“Oh, there will be a drill? I didn’t look that closely,” said Tao Tao, a 42-year-old Taipei hair stylist, when asked about the alert. “I don’t really need the internet for work.”

Taiwan President Lai Ching-te has made it his mission to convince the island’s 23 million residents to prepare for a potential invasion from China. Lai faces a fair amount of public reluctance to embrace crisis-readiness as a way of life.

China claims the island as its own and uses threats of military attack along with more subtle methods to try to steer the Taiwanese public toward giving in to Beijing. Ships with ties to China have severed underwater fiber-optic cables that Taiwan [relies on to remain connected](https://www.wsj.com/articles/remote-corner-of-taiwan-confronts-wartime-scenario-life-with-no-internet-bb7346c0?mod=article_inline) to the internet at least five times in recent years.

Taipei recognizes that connectivity is one of its weak spots.

“Taiwan is one of the very few places in the world where someone tries to sever its undersea cables on a daily basis,” said a senior Taiwan security official. “How can a country like that not prepare?”

A Patriot missile system in New Taipei City during the military exercises. Ann Wang/Reuters

The goal of the exercise, officials said, is to encourage the public to figure out alternative ways to communicate.

The slowdowns next week will coincide with annual air-raid drills—meaning residents in affected areas should be indoors, with unfettered Wi-Fi. Businesses that don’t rely on mobile internet may not even know that service is affected.

“The government is being extremely careful to make sure this exercise doesn’t affect daily routines,” said Chiayo Kuo, a member of a committee established by Lai to improve Taiwan’s civil defense and disaster response.

The government wants to raise awareness that daily life could be interrupted, but without causing significant disruption, said Kuo.

The slowdown drill is Taiwan’s first, the office of the president said, aimed at familiarizing the public with “how to ensure uninterrupted communication for themselves and their families in emergencies.” Related efforts include promoting backup methods.

The slowdown will be similar to roaming abroad with a data-limited SIM card, Taiwan officials said, describing what could be an unusual experience for a population accustomed to fast, pervasive mobile internet.

Taipei phone users received an emergency push alert on Monday warning of the coming internet slowdown drill during the week of Aug. 10. Joyu Wang/WSJ

Taiwan is also looking to establish satellite internet coverage as one way around the island’s vulnerability to a cable-cutting attack.

Taiwan is developing its own Low Earth Orbit satellites, but the first launch isn’t scheduled until 2028, according to the Taiwan Space Agency.

Taipei is also turning to foreign partnerships. The legislature recently relaxed a foreign-ownership rule that [has been an obstacle](https://www.wsj.com/tech/taiwans-race-for-secure-internet-detours-around-musks-starlink-7c273912?mod=article_inline) to the introduction of [SpaceX’s](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/SPCX) Starlink, the world’s largest provider of satellite internet. [Eutelsat’s](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/FR/XPAR/ETL) OneWeb is already working with Taiwan, but service is limited and not available to individual users.

In Taipei, where Taiwan’s self-imposed slowdown is scheduled for Thursday afternoon, many people appeared unfazed, or unaware.

“I don’t really get why they need to shut off the internet for an air-raid drill,” said Jack Chung, 31, who works at an electronics firm. He said it wouldn’t affect him much because he works in an office on Wi-Fi.

Half an hour “isn’t a long time anyway,” he said.

Hangry Wu, a 58-year-old environmental engineer, said the half-hour wouldn’t be a struggle, though he didn’t like that he had paid for the fastest mobile internet only for the government to slow it down.

He added that he would be understanding.

“If the government is doing this, I’m sure they must have a reason,” Wu said, though he admitted he didn’t know what it was.
