### 中文译文

## AIA及香港银行股因中国税收征管风险下跌

作者：彭博新闻
2026年8月6日 上午9:39 GMT+8
*更新于2026年8月6日 上午10:48 GMT+8*

[友邦保险集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1299%20HK%20Equity)、[汇丰控股](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HSBA%20LN%20Equity)和[渣打集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/STAN%20LN%20Equity)在香港股市交易中下跌，此前中国部分城市在最新增加税收收入行动中瞄准了海外保险单。

据知情人士透露，中国大陆居民购买的海外保险单已成为增加税收的最新目标。此前，中国已采取一系列[措施](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/china-targets-offshore-trusts-in-sweeping-tax-clampdown)加强跨境财富监管并提高税收透明度。

这些行动导致基准恒生指数周四上午下跌1.7%。友邦保险股价一度下跌9.2%，为2025年4月以来的最大跌幅。[保诚](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PRU%20LN%20Equity)一度下跌6.5%，而渣打和汇丰下跌超过2%。在财新传媒最先报道此事后，包括汇丰和保诚在内的公司周三在伦敦交易中已经重挫。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b148154987dc26d81d93850/3b481cc9-0b14-8128-8fd9-fd4fbc8bd75e-17c43e636d4a7022005d709ac79426ac.png)

知情人士称，根据现行规定，海外保险产品产生的投资回报须在内地纳税，但由于数据可得性差，大规模执法仍具挑战性。这些知情人士因信息属于私人信息而要求匿名。

知情人士称，自2025年以来，部分居住在北京并申报了其在香港购买的分红保险产品股息的保单持有人，已被要求就已实现收益缴纳20%的税。

高盛集团包括Thomas Wang在内的分析师表示，这种非正式执法可能导致面向中国大陆游客的新保单销售大幅下滑，并可能对现有保单造成干扰。

其中一位知情人士称，在一个案例中，一名个人从一份现金价值为50万美元的保单中提取了3万美元，被当地税务机关要求缴税。该保单持有人将这笔钱存入一家香港银行，该银行通过共同申报准则系统将收益报告为投资收入。

国家税务总局未立即回应置评请求。AIA（友邦保险）的一名发言人拒绝置评，而保诚、汇丰和渣打的代表也未立即回复。

中国加大力度加强对跨境财富的监管，并提高税收透明度，以填补巨大的预算赤字。这损害了那些从寻求海外投资的中国富人那里获得业务的金融公司的股价。

彭博情报分析师Steven Lam在周四早间发布的《中国对离岸保险收益征税或抑制香港销售：反应》报告中写道，对股息支付和预付离岸保险单所赚取的利息征收20%的税，可能会抑制一系列公司在香港销售的保险需求。

他计算出，内地访客的新销售额每下降10%，今年的新业务价值可能平均下降2%。

## AIA, Hong Kong Bank Shares Drop on China Tax Collection Risk

By Bloomberg News
August 6, 2026 at 9:39 AM GMT+8
*Updated on August 6, 2026 at 10:48 AM GMT+8*

[AIA Group Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1299%20HK%20Equity), [HSBC Holdings Plc](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HSBA%20LN%20Equity) and [Standard Chartered Plc](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/STAN%20LN%20Equity) dropped in Hong Kong stock trading after some Chinese cities targeted overseas insurance policies in their latest effort to boost tax revenues.

Offshore insurance policies purchased by mainland Chinese have become the latest targets for increasing tax collection, according to people familiar with the matter. That follows a series of earlier [efforts](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/china-targets-offshore-trusts-in-sweeping-tax-clampdown) by China to strengthen oversight of cross-border wealth and improve tax transparency.

The moves dragged the benchmark Hang Seng Index 1.7% lower on Thursday morning. AIA shares sank as much as 9.2%, the most since April 2025. [Prudential Plc](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PRU%20LN%20Equity) dropped as much as 6.5%, while Standard Chartered and HSBC fell more than 2%. Firms including HSBC and Prudential had tumbled in London trading on Wednesday after media outlet Caixin first reported on the matter.

Under current rules, investment returns generated by offshore insurance products are subject to taxation on the mainland, but broad-based enforcement remains challenging due to poor data availability, the people said, asking not to be identified because the information is private.

Since 2025, some policyholders who reside in Beijing and declared dividends from participating insurance products they purchased in Hong Kong have been asked to pay a 20% tax on the realized gain, the people said.

The informal enforcement could lead to a significant decline in new policy sales to mainland Chinese visitors and potential disruptions to existing policies, according to Goldman Sachs Group Inc. analysts including Thomas Wang.

In one case, an individual withdrew $30,000 from a policy with a cash value of $500,000 was required by local authorities to pay tax. The policyholder put the money in a Hong Kong bank that reported the gains as investment income through the common reporting standard system, one of the people said.

China’s central tax bureau didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment. A spokesperson for AIA declined to comment, while representatives for Prudential, HSBC and Standard Chartered didn’t immediately reply.

China has ramped up efforts to strengthen oversight of cross-border wealth and bolster tax transparency to close a yawning budget deficit. That has hurt shares of financial firms that get business from wealthy Chinese looking to invest abroad.

A 20% tax on dividend payouts and interest earned from prepayment of offshore policies could curb demand for insurance sold by a range of companies in Hong Kong, Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Steven Lam wrote in a China's Tax on Offshore Insurance Gains to Curb HK Sales: React early Thursday.

He calculated that every 10% drop in new sales from mainland Chinese visitors could drag down new business value by an average of 2% this year.
