> 📌 **核心摘要**
>
> 美国财长贝森特通过扩大国债回购计划干预长期利率，在收益率创近二十年新高后释放压低借贷成本的强烈信号。
> • 美国财政部宣布将单次操作中10至30年期国债回购上限从20亿美元提高至至少40亿美元，自9月9日起执行，至少持续至11月4日。
> • 30年期美债收益率在消息公布后数小时内下跌近0.1个百分点，标普500、道指、纳指均收涨0.2%。
> • 贝森特自称“全国头号债券推销员”，此前已两次干预外汇市场，买入阿根廷比索和日元，并以市场状况为由暂缓扩大中长期债务拍卖。
> • 市场对回购计划的效果存疑，部分投资者认为此举主要出于政治目的，旨在中期选举前压低利率，并质疑美国债券市场的有效性。

### 中文译文

## 贝森特倾力扮演美国首席债券交易员角色

在收益率触及近二十年高位后，财政部发出干预信号

![Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent walks past reporters after an interview outside the White House.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481b9b783d5e8f7982ce1/3c281cc9-0b14-8167-bda1-f367e6a9a1fd-569fcdf6685c894abc772ba77196c10e.jpg)

美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特 朱莉娅·德马里·尼金森/美联社

美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特已表明，他会采取非常规手段让市场按他的意愿运行。他最新的举措是迄今为止最为激进的。

面对[长期利率令人不安的上升](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/bonds-are-getting-hammered-and-wall-street-says-the-rout-wont-end-anytime-soon-895e0ad8?mod=article_inline)，贝森特周三早间采取行动，他所领导的机构宣布将大幅增加政府债券购买，作为现有回购计划的一部分。市场迅速作出了反应，这种方式是此前对贝森特举措未曾有过的——股市上涨，美国国债收益率大幅下跌。

“我一直说‘当美联储开始恐慌时，债券交易员可以停止恐慌了。’我想我应该说‘当斯科特·贝森特开始恐慌时，债券交易员可以停止恐慌了。’” 比亚诺研究公司总裁吉姆·比安科在公告发布后不久于X平台上发帖称。

贝森特曾是专注于分析地缘政治形势和经济数据以做出宏观市场押注的对冲基金经理，他塑造了一种格外精通市场的财政部长形象——不惧干预外汇市场，或在讨论[政府应如何管理](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/trump-and-bessent-bring-new-style-to-managing-americas-debt-5c2de0cc?mod=article_inline)借贷时援引市场状况。

贝森特自称“全国头号债券推销员”，公开表示希望压低债券收益率以降低抵押贷款利率和其他借贷成本。但这一[计划并未按预期推进](https://www.wsj.com/economy/housing/u-s-home-builder-sentiment-still-muted-by-affordability-concerns-2af68f49?mod=article_inline)，在今年的中期选举前给特朗普政府带来压力。

美国国债收益率作为整个经济利率的基准，近几个月来持续上升，30年期国债收益率本周突破5.3%，创下近二十年来的最高水平。平均抵押贷款利率已悄然回升至7%左右，而预算赤字占美国GDP的比重徘徊在6%附近，远高于贝森特设定的3%长期目标。

尽管该计划被表述为改善债券市场平稳运行的技术性操作，但华尔街许多人认为这项回购计划明显是为了实现贝森特先前所述的目标。而且它似乎奏效了，30年期收益率在消息公布后的最初几小时内下跌了近0.1个百分点——对于如此短的时间而言，这是一个相当大的变化。股市收高，标普500指数、道琼斯工业平均指数和纳斯达克综合指数均上涨0.2%。

法国外贸银行企业与投资银行美国利率策略主管约翰·布里格斯表示，公告的时机清楚地表明“他们不喜欢正在发生的事情”。

他补充说，即使财政部最终没有购买那么多债券，这一举措也发出了一个信号：政府总是可以采取更多行动来控制收益率。

美国财政部未回应置评请求。但贝森特的一些盟友表示，他们也认为此举旨在压低债券收益率。

“他在财务方面很精明，他认为这是缓解利率压力的一种方式，”特朗普的长期外部经济顾问史蒂夫·摩尔表示。

本周满64岁的贝森特在特朗普政府中并非唯一希望降低利率的人。因选民对生活成本感到愤怒而当选的特朗普总统多次表示，[美联储](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/federal-reserve)设定的短期利率应该低得多。

特朗普政府对前美联储主席杰罗姆·鲍威尔发起了[刑事调查](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/jerome-powell-justice-department-investigation-e9e3f84d?mod=article_inline)，鲍威尔称该调查与总统的利率目标有关。司法部后来放弃了这项调查，以便凯文·沃什能够被确认为鲍威尔的继任者。

去年年初贝森特关于压低美国国债收益率的言论，让许多观察人士认为这至少部分意在将特朗普的注意力从美联储转移开，而美联储的独立性在华尔街被广泛视为对金融稳定至关重要。

事实上，贝森特究竟能做些什么来对收益率产生重大影响，这一点从来都不清楚。收益率在很大程度上取决于经济前景以及投资者认为美联储将如何应对这些情况。

在一项举措中，贝森特[牵头推动](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/u-s-regulators-move-to-ease-financial-crisis-era-bank-capital-rules-fb526e9a?mod=article_inline)放宽一项金融危机时期的金融规定，一些人认为该规定使得银行无法持有更多美国国债。美国财政部还暂缓扩大中长期债务拍卖的规模——多数分析师认为这一步骤最终将是必要的，以资助政府巨额支出承诺。

然而，收益率持续攀升，在很大程度上是受顽固的高通胀和出人意料的强劲经济增长推动。这种组合导致投资者押注美联储的下一步行动将是加息。

![U.S. President Donald Trump and cabinet members at a meeting at Camp David.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481b9b783d5e8f7982ce1/3c281cc9-0b14-8179-adf6-d2a0457031ba-429764894654ff7df268dd10adfca399.jpg)

特朗普总统在内阁会议期间听取贝森特讲话。图片来源：Daniel Heuer/路透社

在与特朗普其他盟友发生争执后，贝森特已成为特朗普最信任的顾问之一。他在包括中美贸易谈判在内的众多政策决策中成为主要发声者。凭借其在华尔街的经验，他还两次干预货币市场，买入阿根廷比索和日元——这些举措即使并非前所未有，也被视为不同寻常，部分原因在于它们并非更广泛的国际努力的一部分。

一些投资者怀疑，财政部的回购计划对债券市场的影响能否超出短期。

美国政府如今已经回购较旧美国国债两年了。官方称，这些回购旨在提供“流动性支持”——并非压低新债券收益率，而是防止旧债券收益率仅因交易不活跃而上行。

美国财政部周三表示，将把单次操作中购买的10年期至30年期美国国债的最高金额从目前的20亿美元提高到至少40亿美元，从9月9日开始，至少持续到11月4日。

假设维持40亿美元的节奏，财政部现在一年将回购1280亿美元的10至30年期美国国债。据法国外贸银行（Natixis）称，这几乎占这些债券预期发行量的30%，但仅占这些期限未偿债务的2.4%。

“这不过是又一点噪音。我的意思是，他们在尽其所能，但考虑到所有其他因素，他们究竟能有多大的效果？”Badgley Phelps的固定收益交易员索伦·埃里克森（Soren Erickson）表示。

其他投资者则对贝森特（Bessent）更为不满，他曾在2024年辩称，拜登政府发行了过多的短期国库券，从而“扭曲了国债市场”。

Credent Wealth Management的首席投资官爱迪生·比齐卡（Edison Byzyka）表示，增加回购似乎是出于政治目的，旨在中期选举前压低利率。

他说道：“这令美国债券市场的有效性受到质疑。”他还表示，这可能会促使投资者转向派息股票等另类投资。

## Bessent Leans Into His Role as America’s Bond Trader in Chief

Treasury Department signals willingness to intervene after yields hit nearly two-decade highs

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent Julia Demaree Nikhinson/Associated Press

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has shown he’ll do unconventional things to get markets moving his way. His latest maneuver is his most radical yet.

Facing an [uncomfortable rise in longer-term interest rates](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/bonds-are-getting-hammered-and-wall-street-says-the-rout-wont-end-anytime-soon-895e0ad8?mod=article_inline), Bessent took action early Wednesday when the agency he leads announced that it would significantly step up purchases of government bonds as part of its existing buyback program. Markets quickly responded in a way they haven’t to Bessent’s previous moves, with stocks rising and Treasury yields falling sharply.

“I’ve been saying ‘bond traders can stop panicking when the Fed starts panicking.’ I guess I should have said, ‘bond traders can stop panicking when Scott Bessent starts panicking,’ ” Jim Bianco, president of Bianco Research, posted on X shortly after the announcement.

A former hedge-fund manager who once specialized in analyzing geopolitical situations and economic data to make big-picture market bets, Bessent has cultivated the image of an unusually market-savvy Treasury secretary—not afraid to intervene in currency markets or cite market conditions when discussing [how the government should conduct](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/trump-and-bessent-bring-new-style-to-managing-americas-debt-5c2de0cc?mod=article_inline) its borrowing.

Calling himself the “nation’s top bond salesman,” Bessent has talked openly about wanting to push down bond yields to lower mortgage rates and other borrowing costs. But that [hasn’t gone according to plan](https://www.wsj.com/economy/housing/u-s-home-builder-sentiment-still-muted-by-affordability-concerns-2af68f49?mod=article_inline), putting pressure on the Trump administration ahead of this year’s midterm elections.

Treasury yields, which set a floor on interest rates across the economy, have been steadily rising in recent months, with the yield on the 30-year bond topping 5.3% this week—its highest level in nearly two decades. Average mortgage rates have been creeping back up toward 7%, while the budget deficit has hovered near 6% of the country’s GDP—well above Bessent’s longer-term 3% target.

Though it was presented as a technical maneuver to improve the smooth functioning of the bond market, many on Wall Street saw the buyback plan as an obvious effort to achieve Bessent’s previously stated aims. And it appeared to work, with the 30-year yield dropping nearly a tenth of a percentage point in the initial hours after it was made—a sizable change for such a short period. Stocks ended higher, with the S&P 500, the Dow Jones Industrial Average and the Nasdaq composite all ticking up 0.2%.

The timing of the announcement clearly suggests “that they didn’t like what was happening,” said John Briggs, head of U.S. rates strategy at Natixis Corporate & Investment Banking.

Even if the Treasury doesn’t end up buying that many more bonds, the move sends a message that the government could always do more to keep yields in check, he added.

The Treasury Department didn’t respond to requests for comment. But some of Bessent’s allies said they also believed the move was intended to drive down bond yields.

“He’s financially savvy and he sees this as a way to alleviate the pressure on the rates,” said longtime external Trump economic adviser Steve Moore.

Bessent, who turns 64 years old this week, is hardly alone in the Trump administration in wanting lower interest rates. Elected by voters angry about the cost of living, President Trump has repeatedly argued that short-term rates set by the [Federal Reserve](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/federal-reserve) should be much lower.

Trump’s administration launched [a criminal investigation](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/jerome-powell-justice-department-investigation-e9e3f84d?mod=article_inline) into former Fed chair Jerome Powell, which Powell said was linked to the president’s rate goals. The Justice Department later dropped the probe so Kevin Warsh could be confirmed as Powell’s successor.

Bessent’s talk early last year about bringing down Treasury yields struck many observers as at least partially intended to redirect Trump’s attention away from the Fed, the independence of which is widely seen on Wall Street as critical for financial stability.

In fact, it was never clear what Bessent could do to have a big impact on yields, which are largely determined by the economic outlook and how investors think the Fed will respond to those conditions.

In one move, Bessent [spearheaded an effort](https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/u-s-regulators-move-to-ease-financial-crisis-era-bank-capital-rules-fb526e9a?mod=article_inline) to ease a crisis-era financial rule that some argued was keeping banks from holding more Treasurys. The Treasury Department has also held off on increasing the sizes of medium-to-long-term debt auctions—a step most analysts believe will eventually be necessary to fund the government’s hefty spending commitments.

Yields, though, have continued to climb, driven in large part by stubbornly high inflation and surprisingly strong economic growth. That combination has led investors to bet that the Fed’s next move will be to raise rates.

President Trump listens as Bessent speaks during a cabinet meeting. Daniel Heuer/Reuters

Bessent has become one of Trump’s most trusted advisers after previous tussles with other Trump allies. He became a lead voice on a host of policy decisions, including trade negotiations with China. Drawing on his Wall Street experience, he has also twice intervened in currency markets by buying Argentine pesos and Japanese yen—moves seen as unusual, if not unprecedented, in part because they weren’t part of a broader international effort.

Some investors were skeptical that Treasury’s buyback plan would have more than a short-term impact on the bond market.

The U.S. government has already been buying back older Treasurys for two years now. Officially, those buybacks are aimed at “liquidity support”—not driving down yields on new bonds but keeping yields on older bonds from drifting upward just because they are less actively traded.

Treasury on Wednesday said that it would increase the maximum amount of 10-year to 30-year Treasurys that it buys in a given operation from $2 billion currently to at least $4 billion, starting on Sept. 9 and running at least through Nov. 4.

Assuming a continuing $4 billion pace, Treasury would now buy back $128 billion of 10-year to 30-year Treasurys in a year. That is nearly 30% of expected issuance of those bonds, according to Natixis, but only 2.4% outstanding debt of those maturities.

“It’s just another piece of noise. I mean, they’re doing what they can, but how effective can they really be with all the other factors out there?” said Soren Erickson, a fixed-income trader at Badgley Phelps.

Other investors were more critical of Bessent, who argued in 2024 that the Biden administration had “distorted Treasury markets” by issuing too much in short-term Treasury bills.

Edison Byzyka, chief investment officer at Credent Wealth Management, said the increased buybacks appear political, meant to drive down rates ahead of the midterms.

“It puts into question the validity of the U.S. bond market,” he said, adding that it could push investors into alternative investments such as dividend stocks.
