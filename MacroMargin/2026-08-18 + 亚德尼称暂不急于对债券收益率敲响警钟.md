### 中文译文

## 亚德尼称暂不急于对债券收益率敲响警钟

作者：[Ruth Carson](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS35-mFs7Fw/ruth-carson) 和 [Masaki Kondo](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOcMqFOsbWI/masaki-kondo)

2026年8月18日 上午9:55 GMT+8

*更新于2026年8月18日 上午11:41 GMT+8*

Yardeni Research表示，尽管投资者对政府债务上升表现出越来越多的[不安迹象](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/us-bond-selloff-drives-30-year-yields-to-the-highest-since-2007)，但美国债券市场尚无需恐慌。

该公司表示，市场越来越担心超大规模企业借款激增，同时质疑如果油价再次攀升，美联储是否会对通胀保持足够的警惕。

由Ed Yardeni领导的策略师在周二的一份报告中写道：“我们并未按下恐慌按钮。然而，我们正在密切关注债券义警是否会这样做。”

Yardeni指出2023年夏季的情况，当时美国国债收益率在短短几个月内从4%飙升至5%。该水平最终被证明对买家具有吸引力，该公司表示未来可能还有类似的买入机会。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b1481838b59ccca5cc78957/3c081cc9-0b14-81f5-b61f-e27f136a2432-693c931901ac50072c93d52e0ba66d41.png)

策略师们写道：“我们坚持认为，美国国债收益率应继续在4%-5%的正常区间内交易，而不会对经济和公司盈利造成任何不利影响。然而，既然收益率正接近该区间的上端，我们正在更密切地监控债券义警的活动。”

美国10年期国债收益率现报4.73%，接近一年多来的最高水平，市场担忧政府支出庞大和通胀问题。伊朗战争持续导致油价上涨，可能加剧物价压力，并增强美联储加息的理由。与此同时，人工智能热潮推动企业借贷激增，使得华盛顿和硅谷在争夺同一资金池。

美国利率上升也会吸引全球资本流向美元，使日本阻止日元跌破160兑1美元的努力以及中国维持人民币稳定的尝试变得更加复杂。亚德尼表示，由于美国国债是全球债务定价的基准，美国利率上升会波及全球的主权债、企业债和抵押贷款成本。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b1481838b59ccca5cc78957/3c081cc9-0b14-81f6-b6e7-e850b973f3de-2d7430d5c92957dd73eee337f42d826c.png)

亚德尼是总裁兼首席投资策略师，他在20世纪80年代创造了“债券义警”一词，用以形容那些为抗议他们认为会导致通胀的政府政策而卖出债券的投资者。这导致债券价格下跌、收益率上升，迫使当局重新走上财政紧缩的道路。

该公司表示，近几个月来债券义警在世界各地活跃，表明对政府债务的担忧不仅限于美国。策略师们称，他们在英国和日本尤其活跃，这两个国家的债务负担相对于经济规模而言特别高。

## Yardeni Says Not Pushing Panic Button on Bond Yields Yet

By [Ruth Carson](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS35-mFs7Fw/ruth-carson) and [Masaki Kondo](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOcMqFOsbWI/masaki-kondo)

August 18, 2026 at 9:55 AM GMT+8

*Updated on August 18, 2026 at 11:41 AM GMT+8*

There’s no reason to panic on the US bond market just yet, even as investors are showing growing [signs of unease](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/us-bond-selloff-drives-30-year-yields-to-the-highest-since-2007) over rising government debt, according to Yardeni Research.

The firm said the market is becoming more concerned about the surge in borrowing by hyperscalers, while questioning whether the Federal Reserve will remain sufficiently vigilant on inflation if oil prices climb again.

“We aren’t pushing the panic button,” strategists led by Ed Yardeni wrote in a note Tuesday. “However, we are closely monitoring whether the bond vigilantes might do so.”

Yardeni pointed to the summer of 2023, when US yields surged from 4% to 5% in just a few months. That level ultimately proved attractive to buyers, and the firm says there may be a similar buying opportunity ahead.

“We are sticking with our view that the US bond yield should continue to trade in a normal range of 4%-5%, without causing any adverse consequences for the economy and corporate earnings,” the strategists wrote. “Nevertheless, now that the yield is approaching the top of this range, we are monitoring the activities of the bond vigilantes more closely.”

US 10-year yields are now at 4.73%, near their highest in more than a year on concerns over heavy government spending and inflation. Higher oil prices from a [prolonged](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/trump-threatens-to-bomb-oman-if-it-gets-in-way-of-us-fox-news-msx5rdoc) Iran war could add to price pressures and bolster the case for Fed rate hikes. Meanwhile, the AI boom has driven a surge in corporate borrowing, leaving Washington and Silicon Valley competing for the same pool of capital.

Higher US interest rates also draw global capital toward the dollar, [complicating](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/foreign-holdings-of-treasuries-fell-in-june-led-by-japan-drop) Japan’s efforts to prevent the yen from weakening beyond 160 per dollar and China’s attempts to hold the yuan steady. Because Treasuries are the benchmark for pricing debt worldwide, rising US rates ripple into sovereign, corporate and mortgage borrowing costs everywhere, Yardeni said.

Ed Yardeni, president and chief investment strategist, coined the term “bond vigilantes” in the 1980s to describe investors who sell bonds in protest against government policies they deem inflationary. This drives bond prices down and yields up, forcing authorities back on a course of fiscal restraint.

The firm said bond vigilantes have been stirring around the world in recent months, suggesting concerns over government debt extend beyond the US. They’ve been especially active in the UK and Japan, where debt burdens are especially high relative to the size of their economies, the strategists said.
