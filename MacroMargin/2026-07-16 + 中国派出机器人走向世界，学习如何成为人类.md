### 中文译文

## 中国派出机器人走向世界，学习如何成为人类

在北京郊区的一个工业园区，一只人形机械臂拿起一袋乐事薯片，将其整齐地放在货架上的一排零食旁。附近，一名工人拍摄自己从沙发上抓起靠垫、在床上折叠床单的视频，这些视频将用于开发机器人的大脑。

中国正在以前所未有的速度部署人形机器人，将数千台送往物流中心、电池工厂和其他工业场所，速度超过美国。在政府支持和大量投资的推动下，初创公司正将其最先进的机器推向世界，以收集海量数据，推动更智能、更类人机器的研发。

中国国家主席习近平希望借助机器人产业帮助中国在全球技术竞赛中击败美国。中国的制造业实力和强大的供应链管理使其在人形机器人领域领先于地缘政治对手。习近平计划本周首次在该国旗舰人工智能大会上发表讲话，届时将展示最新的人形机器人。

在宇树科技凭借其G1模型展示的功夫动作引发关注后，中国的人形机器人产业正经历从身体到大脑的重大转变。投资者正将资金投入开发机器人人工智能（即具身智能）的公司，而从阿里巴巴集团到小米集团等中国科技巨头也已加入战局，推出自己的模型。

![Unitree’s G1 humanoid robots during a martial arts demonstration. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b148199b9bfd9c150e88109/39f81cc9-0b14-8131-ac5c-ee4a8930835e-ebadd7b5cccfc050c8b8c07634d76b30.png)

在美国，从Alphabet Inc.旗下的谷歌等巨头到OpenAI支持的Physical Intelligence Inc.等初创公司，众多企业都在追逐相同的技术。Nvidia Corp.运营着其开源机器人基础AI模型GR00T，同时支持Figure AI Inc.等一批有前途的初创公司。这家全球市值最高的公司最近与宇树科技达成合作，罕见地打破了中美在敏感技术上的隔阂。

中美开发具身智能的公司之间出现分歧。在中国，模型越来越多地使用人形机器人在其运行环境中生成的数据进行训练。相比之下，美国公司则购买数据，并依赖实验室模拟以及印度、越南等低成本市场的人类工人进行训练。这场竞争关乎人形机器人市场的未来，摩根士丹利估计，到2050年该市场价值将达到每年5万亿美元。

“全世界在物理智能方面仍处于起跑线上，”在美国接受教育的计算机科学家苏浩说，他回到中国在上海复旦大学创办了一个研究所。“中国凭借其供应链优势，有机会设定方向并定义范式。”

![A Robotera mechanical hand. Photographer: Qin Zihang/VCG/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b148199b9bfd9c150e88109/39f81cc9-0b14-819b-8e5f-cdcaa2fa6d4f-58c5c2d79f8d14272e514d46d3adcb2a.png)

多年来，中国一直引领全球机器人技术。根据国际机器人联合会的最新估计，中国在2024年安装了约30万台机器人，远超美国去年安装的3.8万台。

如今，面对快速老龄化的人口，北京将未来经济增长的希望寄托于具身智能的突破，使类人机器人能够取代人类工人，缓解迫在眉睫的劳动力短缺。巴克莱银行分析师估计，政府希望这些机器能够弥补预计短缺的60%。工业和信息化部希望在今年年底前将1万台机器人部署到工厂。

从风险投资到汽车制造商和政府支持基金，投资者正在响应北京的号召。根据北京数据追踪机构IT桔子汇编的数据，今年迄今为止，该领域已投入至少1000亿元人民币（148亿美元），超过前五年的总和。数据显示，红杉中国（现更名为HSG）在今年上半年向13家初创公司投入了30亿元人民币，国有企业积极参与引导对该领域的投资。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b148199b9bfd9c150e88109/39f81cc9-0b14-81a9-bf89-c9c2f3f91331-704148c0886ca625e864ae070829e600.png)

中国机器人初创公司正在利用大部分资金来训练机器人的大脑。总部位于北京的机器人公司Robotera已将其人形机器人部署到十几个物流中心，而Galbot与宁德时代新能源科技股份有限公司签署协议，将其机器人派往该公司工厂执行重物搬运任务。AI² Robotics已将人形机器人安装到生产汽车、半导体和消费电子产品的工厂中。在硬件领域取得领先地位后，宇树科技现在希望在具身智能领域取得进展，承诺将即将上市的6.1亿美元募资中近一半用于开发AI模型。

但开发这些模型的初创公司面临一个根本性挑战：他们需要生成大量训练数据。与大型语言模型不同，目前明显缺乏可用于复杂课程（如学习握鸡蛋而不捏碎，或在杯子滑落桌子前接住一杯水）的相关可用数据。

公司一致认为，他们需要数千万小时的数据来训练模型，分析师表示，这关系到他们能否盈利。汇丰银行分析师Corey Chan在最近的一份研究报告中表示，人形机器人的商业化取决于强大的具身智能模型，而这又取决于可用于训练的数据量。

高盛驻香港分析师Jacqueline Du表示：“领先公司目前大约有50万小时。”她补充说，中国公司现在正在扩大部署到各种场景，以期在未来几年加速数据收集并达到临界规模。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b148199b9bfd9c150e88109/39f81cc9-0b14-81da-b6ef-c332e808ce5b-8f35b1e8c520b6da58679d02e337823e.png)

为了加快进度，中国地方政府已在全国开设了64个数据采集中心，例如北京郊外由X-Humanoid运营的那个中心，另有20个正在建设中，据研究公司Interact Analysis汇编的数据显示。这些设施允许公司模拟超市、装配线、办公室、商店和家庭等环境来训练机器人。

“在这方面，美国毫无优势可言，”深圳星动纪元算法负责人Gan Ruyi表示。据IT桔子数据，该公司今年迄今已获得63亿元人民币投资，是所有机器人公司中投资最多的。“这正是中国的优势所在：组织劳动力并大规模部署用于数据采集的机器。”摩根士丹利认为，这种规模“是模型玩家获胜的唯一决定性因素”。

美国初创公司与中国企业之间的竞争开始升温。

美国公司已在一些工厂部署了人形机器人，**[特斯拉公司 (Tesla Inc.)](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSLA%20US%20Equity)** 已将其部分 Optimus 模型送往自己的制造设施。包括 Figure AI、[Apptronik Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1412332D:US) 和 [Agility Robotics Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1585639D:US) 在内的初创公司也已向工业合作伙伴发送了少量机器。然而，这与中国公司的努力相比相形见绌，例如上海 Agibot 正迅速将工厂部署规模扩大到数千台。

![A Tesla Optimus robot hands out candy in front of the Nasdaq MarketSite in New York in 2025. Photographer: Michael Nagle/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b148199b9bfd9c150e88109/39f81cc9-0b14-81f4-8730-ce2362924149-8b42b50e3e406ce1ea4bdd5aefbf023d.png)

今年 5 月，总部位于加州森尼维尔的 Figure AI 庆祝了一个[里程碑](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-15/robotics-ceo-vows-no-intervention-in-humanoids-viral-trial-run)，此前该公司发布了 50 小时的直播，期间其人形机器人在其物流中心分拣包裹，零失误。首席执行官布雷特·阿德科克 (Brett Adcock) 告诉彭博新闻社，这些机器人在移动传送带上处理了近 6 万个包裹，速度与人类大致相同。

但中国的开发者对这次演示不以为然，[认为](https://m.eeo.com.cn/2026/0602/900202.shtml)它“太干净了”，条件与现实世界不符。

“Figure 的演示仍处于实验室阶段……我们的部署是在真实生产线上，以满足劳动力短缺并解放工人，”Agibot 的项目总监艾文 (Ai Wen) 表示。他说，该公司今年目标是在工厂部署超过 1000 台人形机器人，到 2027 年超过 10000 台。

X Square 的甘 (Gan) 表示，该演示实际上会削弱 Figure AI 在现实环境中的具身智能，因为模型会学习噪声等无关细节。“在真实的物流中心，你不可能一直用同一批包裹来调整机器人，”甘说。“递到你面前的包裹总是不同的。”

今年，X Square 启动了一项计划，将其机器送到真实家庭中做家务。虽然其他公司专注于在工厂部署人形机器人，但该公司表示，其在家庭内部收集的数据对复杂环境下的训练很有用。

![An X Square robot performing a house chore. Source: X Square Robot](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b148199b9bfd9c150e88109/39f81cc9-0b14-812d-a6fd-e5a226e55f89-ed3da9ca9bf7f305c0a1b133de29c981.png)

“家庭是模型的终极试验场，”甘说。“在真实的家庭中，没有脚本或标准操作程序。”

## China Sends Robots Out Into the World to Learn How to Be Human

In an industrial park on the outskirts of Beijing, a humanoid arm picks up a bag of Lay’s potato chips and places it neatly along a row of snacks on a shelf. Nearby, a worker films himself grabbing cushions off a sofa and folding sheets on a bed, recording videos that will be used to develop brains for robots.

China is deploying more humanoids than ever before, sending thousands to logistics hubs, battery factories and other industrial locations at a faster pace than the US. Buoyed by support from the government and a flood of investment, startups are getting their most advanced machines out into the world in a bid to gather torrents of data that can fuel the development of smarter, more humanlike machines.

President Xi Jinping is looking to the robotics industry for help beating the US in a global technology race. China’s manufacturing prowess and robust supply chain management position it well to reap the benefits of humanoids before its geopolitical rival. Xi is [due](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/xi-to-debut-at-china-s-flagship-ai-summit-as-us-rivalry-heats-up) to address the country’s flagship AI conference this week for the first time, where the latest humanoids will be on display.

After Unitree Robotics turned heads with the [popular](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-20/chinese-startup-s-kung-fu-robots-fire-up-tech-wary-investors) kung fu moves of its G1 model, China’s humanoid industry is now undergoing a seismic shift from body to mind. Investors are pouring cash into firms developing AI for robots, known as embodied intelligence, and the biggest names in Chinese tech, from [Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BABA%20US%20Equity) to [Xiaomi Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1810%20HK%20Equity), are joining the fray with their own models.

In the US, a host of firms from giants like [Alphabet Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GOOGL%20US%20Equity)’s Google to startups such as [OpenAI](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1554630D:US)-backed [Physical Intelligence Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2510836D:US) are chasing the same technology. [Nvidia Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity) operates its own open-source foundational AI model for robots, named GR00T, while backing an array of promising startups, including [Figure AI Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2265591D:US) The world’s most valuable company recently struck a [partnership](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-06-09/as-ai-moves-into-robots-nvidia-may-be-bridging-a-us-china-divide) with Unitree in a rare breach of the US-China divide over sensitive tech.

A divergence has emerged between Chinese and American firms developing embodied intelligence. In China, models are increasingly trained using data generated by humanoids in the environments in which they will be operated. In contrast, US firms are buying data and looking to lab simulations as well as human workers in India, Vietnam and other low-cost markets for their training needs. At stake is the future of the humanoid market, which Morgan Stanley [estimates](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/humanoid-robot-market-5-trillion-by-2050) will be worth $5 trillion a year by 2050.

“The whole world is still at the starting line in physical intelligence,” said Su Hao, a US-educated computer scientist who returned to China to set up an institute at Shanghai’s Fudan University. “China, with its supply chain edge, has the opportunity to set the direction and define the paradigm.”

For years, China has led the world in robotics. The country installed about 300,000 robots in 2024, [according](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/us-robot-industry-returns-to-double-digit-growth) to the latest estimates from the International Federation of Robotics. That eclipsed the 38,000 machines that were set up in the US last year, the group said.

Now, facing a rapidly aging population, Beijing is pinning hopes for future economic growth on a breakthrough in embodied intelligence that will allow humanoids to replace human workers and ease a looming labor shortage. The government is looking to the machines to make up as much as 60% of a projected shortfall, analysts at Barclays Plc [estimate](https://www.ib.barclays/our-insights/10-things-you-need-to-know-about-humanoid-robots.html). The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology [wants](https://www.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/tz/art/2026/art_f291ccd3da4c47ce95741de63cc088e6.html) to deploy 10,000 of the machines to factories by the end of the year.

Investors from venture capital to carmakers and state-backed funds are heeding Beijing’s call, pouring at least 100 billion yuan ($14.8 billion) into the sector so far this year, according to data compiled by Beijing-based tracker ITjuzi, more than the five previous years combined. HSG, formerly known as Sequoia China, sunk 3 billion yuan into 13 startups over the first half of the year, the data showed, with state firms heavily involved in directing investment to the sector.

Chinese robotics startups are using most of the funds to train robot brains. Beijing-based [Robotera](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2362422D:CH) has deployed its humanoids to a dozen logistical hubs, while [Galbot](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2455143D:CH) signed a deal with [Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300750%20CH%20Equity) to send its machines to perform heavy lifting at the company’s plant. AI² Robotics has installed its humanoids at factories producing cars, semiconductors and consumer electronics. After taking the lead in hardware, Unitree is now looking to gain ground in embodied intelligence, pledging to spend nearly half of the proceeds from its [upcoming](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-01/china-robotics-firms-line-up-ipos-to-pitch-next-phase-of-ai) $610 million listing on developing AI models.

But startups developing these models face a fundamental challenge: they need to generate vast amounts of training data. Unlike large language models, there is a distinct lack of relevant available data that can be used for complex lessons like learning to hold an egg without breaking it or catching a glass of water before it slips off a table.

Companies agree that they need tens of millions of hours of data to train their models and analysts say their ability to turn a profit is at stake. Humanoid commercialization depends on capable embodied intelligence models, which in turn hinges on the amount of data available for training, said HSBC analyst Corey Chan in a recent research note.

“The leading companies are now at around 500,000,” said Jacqueline Du, a Hong Kong-based analyst at Goldman Sachs. Chinese companies are now expanding deployment to a wide variety of scenarios in the hope of accelerating data collection and reaching a critical mass in the next few years, she added.

To speed things along, Chinese local authorities have opened 64 data collection centers across the country like the one outside Beijing operated by X-Humanoid, with 20 more under construction, [according](https://www.interactanalysis.cn/insight/%e7%9b%98%e7%82%b9%e5%9b%bd%e5%86%8590%e4%ba%ba%e5%bd%a2%e6%9c%ba%e5%99%a8%e4%ba%ba%e6%95%b0%e6%8d%ae%e9%87%87%e9%9b%86%e4%b8%ad%e5%bf%83/) to data compiled by research firm Interact Analysis. The facilities allow companies to train robots in settings that mimic supermarkets, assembly lines, offices, shops and homes.

“Here the US has no advantage at all,” said Gan Ruyi, head of algorithms at Shenzhen-based X Square Robot, which has drawn 6.3 billion yuan in investment so far this year, the most investment of any robotics firm, according to ITjuzi. “This is where China has an edge: organizing labor and deploying the machines used in data collection at scale.” That scale, according to Morgan Stanley, “is the single decisive factor for model players to win out.”

The competition between US startups and Chinese firms is starting to heat up.

American firms have deployed some humanoids to factories,** **with [Tesla Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSLA%20US%20Equity) sending some of its Optimus models to its own manufacturing facilities. Startups including Figure AI, [Apptronik Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1412332D:US) and [Agility Robotics Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1585639D:US) have all sent small numbers of their machines to industrial partners. Still, that pales in comparison to the efforts by Chinese firms like Shanghai-based Agibot, which is rapidly scaling up factory deployment by the thousands.

In May, Sunnyvale, California-based Figure AI celebrated a [milestone](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-15/robotics-ceo-vows-no-intervention-in-humanoids-viral-trial-run) after it released a 50-hour livestream during which its humanoid robots sorted packages at its logistics hub with no mistakes. The robots processed nearly 60,000 packages on a moving belt at about the same speed as a human, Chief Executive Officer Brett Adcock told Bloomberg News.

But developers in China were nonplussed by the demonstration, [dismissing](https://m.eeo.com.cn/2026/0602/900202.shtml) it as “too clean” with conditions that aren’t similar to the real world.

“Figure’s demo is still at a lab ... our deployment is in a real production line to meet labor shortages and liberate workers,” said Ai Wen, a project director at Agibot. The firm aims to have more than a thousand humanoids at factories this year and more than 10,000 in 2027, he said.

X Square’s Gan said the demo would actually hamper Figure AI’s embodied intelligence in a real-world setting because the model would learn unrelated details like noise. “There’s no way you can adjust your robot with the same group of packages all the time in a real logistics hub,” Gan said. “The packages that come your way are always different.”

This year, X Square launched a program to send its machines to do household chores at real homes. While other firms are focused on humanoid deployment at factories, the company says the data it gathers inside households is useful for training in complex environments.

“Households are the ultimate testing ground for models,” Gan said. “There is no script or standard operating procedure at real homes.”
