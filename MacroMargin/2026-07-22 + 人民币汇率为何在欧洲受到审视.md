## 人民币汇率为何在欧洲受到审视

### [政治](https://www.bloomberg.com/politics)解析

中国香港地区的100元人民币纸币。*摄影师：林亦/Bloomberg*

作者：彭博新闻社 2026年7月22日 上午8:09 GMT+8

### **要点**

- 人民币汇率已成为中国与欧盟贸易关系中的热点问题，许多欧盟政策制定者和经济学家认为人民币被低估，是欧盟对华贸易逆差不断加深的关键推动因素。
- 欧盟担忧人民币汇率，因为相对较弱的货币意味着该国商品对外国买家更便宜，这有助于推动贸易顺差，并使中国出口商获得竞争优势。
- 一些欧洲政策制定者认为，需要人民币升值来缩小中国的贸易优势，提议包括对从中国进口的商品征收统一关税，或让欧元对人民币贬值以重新平衡贸易流动。
[人民币](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNY%20CFEO%20Curncy)汇率已成为中国与欧盟贸易关系中的热点问题。许多欧盟政策制定者和经济学家认为人民币被低估，是欧盟对东亚国家贸易逆差不断加深的关键推动因素。6月份，这一[不平衡](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNTXEU%20%20%20Index)平均每天约为11亿美元。

过去12个月，人民币兑欧元汇率走强，截至7月下旬上涨约9%。但仍远未达到2015年高点。随着欧洲工业在与更廉价中国商品的竞争中苦苦挣扎，欧洲内部要求中国允许其货币升值约20%至30%以创造公平竞争环境的呼声日益高涨。

但对于人民币的公允价值并无共识。而在中国，几乎没有迹象表明政府愿意在经济疲弱之际策划大幅升值。这连同与中国的其他分歧，让欧盟面临两难：如何与这个既是战略对手又是关键贸易伙伴的国家重新平衡关系。双方已设定10月截止日期，以在贸易争端上取得进展。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3a581cc90b14814a9f8fcb336dad4843/3a581cc9-0b14-8142-80f8-f363cd3494c2-256a09edd6d7dc5fc21dcf6c1f2eb251.png)

### **人民币是否被低估？**

尽管以名义值衡量人民币走强——6月根据国际清算银行有效汇率指标触及历史新高——但该货币仍被普遍认为实际价值低估。其实际有效汇率（REER）是根据一篮子伙伴货币衡量并经通胀调整的，过去十年间已下降，而美元和欧元的实际有效汇率则有所上升。

国际货币基金组织（IMF）估计，2025年人民币实际有效汇率被低估12%至20%。6月，美国前财政官员布拉德·塞策（Brad Setser）认为人民币可能被低估超过20%，而大约同一时间，德意志银行、高盛和美国银行策略师分别使用不同估值模型得出10%至20%的贬低幅度。相比之下，澳大利亚和新西兰银行集团的一名策略师认为几乎没有名义低估的证据。

许多中国经济学家否认人民币估值不当的观点，认为估值模型夸大了中国贸易顺差的重要性，未能充分考虑影响汇率的其他市场力量，包括资本流动。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3a581cc90b14814a9f8fcb336dad4843/3a581cc9-0b14-81d9-9ad4-d3ae38a5252b-98db3c5093fa5c909a4f88b6836f4e0a.png)

### **中国在管理人民币方面扮演什么角色？**

中国维持人民币的“有管理浮动”，使当局能够对汇率施加重大影响。中国人民银行设定每日参考汇率（即定盘价），然后允许人民币在高于或低于该汇率2%的区间内交易。央行表示，其目标是保持人民币基本稳定，防止波动。

中国有多种手段来维持汇率稳定——有些手段比其他的更透明。一种方法是调整银行必须持有的外汇准备金规模，这可以影响对人民币的需求。国有银行也会介入买入美元以压低人民币，或卖出美元以支撑人民币。

近年来，中国人民银行采取了行动以限制双向波动。在2022年和2023年人民币大幅贬值期间，央行支撑了人民币；而在2026年初人民币走强后，央行又回撤了部分此前为支撑汇率而推出的措施。

然而，一些不那么透明的方法助长了** **指控，称北京人为压低汇率，使中国出口商相对于竞争对手更具优势。中国人民银行** **央行表示，中国并不寻求通过人民币贬值获得竞争优势，并称汇率最终由[市场决定](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/pboc-signals-comfort-with-yuan-levels-expects-two-way-moves)。

### **欧盟为何担忧人民币汇率？**

相对较弱的货币意味着一个国家的商品对外国买家而言更便宜。同时，进口商品更加昂贵。这可以[助长贸易顺差](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-10/imf-urges-bolder-stimulus-from-china-to-rebalance-economy-trade)，即一国出口价值超过进口价值。

一些经济学家和欧洲政策制定者认为，人民币相对较低的价值给中国出口商带来了额外的竞争优势。出口已成为中国经济增长日益重要的驱动力，根据中国海关数据，中国对欧盟的贸易顺差在6月达到[创纪录的329亿美元](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/china-s-trade-surplus-with-eu-hits-record-as-tensions-intensify)。

德国是欧盟中对华贸易逆差最大的国家，去年约为950亿欧元，其制造商，尤其是汽车制造商，正面临来自中国竞争日益加剧的压力。

中国的贸易顺差在新冠疫情期间起飞，因为欧洲人购买了更多商品以适应封锁和居家办公。最近，美国的关税促使中国出口商将货物转向其他市场，如欧洲和东南亚。与此同时，中国的进口长期停滞，使与欧盟的贸易关系更加失衡。

日益加剧的贸易失衡成为紧张源，欧洲企业在本土面临来自低成本中国商品的更大压力。与此同时，它们在中国市场销售困难，且随着中国企业向世界其他地区出口更多产品，它们在海外市场也面临更激烈的竞争。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3a581cc90b14814a9f8fcb336dad4843/3a581cc9-0b14-816c-a82a-c48a92604f5c-8d946abd9b465ec136d494689014e4fc.png)

### **欧洲提出了什么提议？**

一些欧洲政策制定者认为需要人民币升值来帮助缩小中国的贸易优势。这样的调整将使中国对欧洲的出口更加昂贵，而欧洲商品在中国变得更便宜。虽然这可能有助于减少部分失衡，但一些经济学家表示，仅凭这一点无法解决欧洲的竞争力问题。

尽管如此，这一想法在欧洲已获得一定支持。法国政府的一个咨询机构[今年早些时候表示](https://www.strategie-plan.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/2026/2026-02-09%20-%20Rouleau%20compresseur%20chinois/HCSP-2026-REPORT-THE%20CHINESE%20STEAMROLLER-18fevrier14h30-COUV.pdf)，欧盟应考虑对从中国进口的商品统一征收30%的关税，或寻求欧元兑人民币贬值20%至30%，以重新平衡贸易流动。

欧洲改革中心（Centre for European Reform）5月的一份[报告](https://www.cer.eu/publications/archive/policy-brief/2026/china-shock-20-cost-germanys-complacency)称，对中国货币进行“重估”将是贸易失衡的“即时解决方案”，报告援引声称人民币被低估幅度高达30%的说法。

最近，德国总理弗里德里希·默茨（Friedrich Merz）和法国总统埃马纽埃尔·马克龙（Emmanuel Macron）都呼吁就人民币问题与北京进行“对话”。默茨还[推动国际会谈](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-19/germany-s-merz-seeks-currency-talks-to-fix-undervalued-yuan)讨论汇率问题，甚至借鉴1985年《广场协议》作为解决货币失衡的可能模式。根据该协议，美国、日本、西德、法国和英国协调政策，使美元兑其货币贬值，以改善美国出口竞争力并缩小贸易逆差。

6月，欧洲央行行长克里斯蒂娜·拉加德（Christine Lagarde）淡化了用现代版《广场协议》推动人民币大幅升值的想法，认为当前形势“截然不同”。欧盟重新平衡对华贸易关系的更广泛战略包括为企业提供新的[资金支持](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-11/eu-warns-push-to-diversify-away-from-china-will-need-funding)，以分散供应链，同时更有效地利用现有贸易工具，如反补贴调查、关税和进口配额。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3a581cc90b14814a9f8fcb336dad4843/3a581cc9-0b14-81ab-a5f9-ff80a0287a63-e4269054e754da0b3527675c9d0afe72.png)

尽管有迹象表明欧盟愿意对中国采取[更强硬立场](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/left-with-few-choices-eu-braces-for-a-trade-fight-with-china)，但欧盟仍对引发与全球第二大经济体的贸易战保持警惕。北京方面一再[威胁报复](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-29/china-warns-of-retaliation-ahead-of-eu-meeting-on-trade-curbs)贸易壁垒，这将危及欧洲进入关键市场以及获得芯片和关键矿产等重要投入品。对抗的风险在去年变得明显，当时中国为回应美国关税而收紧了对稀土的控制。由此导致的供应中断迫使多家欧洲公司[停产](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-18/china-rare-earth-product-exports-hit-highest-since-at-least-2012)。

### **中国对人民币升值持何立场？**

一些中国经济学家和前央行官员[认为](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-15/xi-faces-calls-for-stronger-yuan-in-rare-public-currency-debate)，如果中国要重新平衡经济，减少对出口依赖型增长的依赖并缓解与贸易伙伴的紧张关系，就需要人民币升值。人民币升值将提高人们的购买力，使进口商品更便宜，从而可能提振低迷的消费支出。

中国人民银行已表现出对人民币适度升值的[容忍态度](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-28/world-s-appetite-for-ai-makes-china-less-afraid-of-stronger-yuan)。7月中旬，其将中间价设定为自2023年以来的[最高水平](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-10/pboc-sets-fixing-below-6-8-per-dollar-for-first-time-since-2023-mre95kva)。尽管如此，这并不意味着它对快速上涨或大幅升值持开放态度——中国政府极不可能同意签署类似《广场协议》的协议。中国人民银行未回复彭博社的传真置评请求。

其犹豫部分源于对日本在1985年协议后经历的解读。许多中国政策制定者和经济学家认为，日元快速升值导致了日本资产价格泡沫及随后数十年的经济疲软增长。一些人还认为，该协议是美国以牺牲他国利益为代价提升自身经济的[政治动机](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202606/1364067.shtml)行为，而非纯粹的经济协议。这种看法强化了中国捍卫“[自由贸易](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-11/china-vice-premier-to-join-macron-s-g7-call-on-trade-imbalances)”并保持自身经济政策自主权的决心。

相反，中国试图将贸易谈判重点放在如何增加从欧盟的进口和扩大中国市场准入上，而非采取会影响自身出口的措施。

### **欧盟对人民币的担忧是孤立现象吗？**

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普长期指责中国采取不公平的重商主义汇率政策。在他的第一个任期内，其政府曾短暂将中国[列为](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-08-05/u-s-treasury-department-labels-china-a-currency-manipulator)汇率操纵国，但在双边贸易协定谈判后取消了这一标签。

美国财政部1月发布的半年外汇[报告](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/January-2026-FX-Report.pdf)称，人民币“[被大幅低估](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-29/us-calls-on-china-to-strengthen-substantially-undervalued-yuan)”，并呼吁中国当局“顺应市场压力和宏观经济基本面，及时有序地”允许汇率走强。财政部未将人民币归类为操纵，但表示如果有证据表明中国通过正式或非正式渠道干预以抵制人民币升值，其准备使用这一标签。

## Why the Value of China’s Yuan Is Drawing Scrutiny in Europe

### [Politics](https://www.bloomberg.com/politics)Explainer

Chinese one-hundred yuan banknotes in Hong Kong, China.*Photographer: Lam Yik/Bloomberg*

By Bloomberg NewsJuly 22, 2026 at 8:09 AM GMT+8

### **Takeaways**

- The value of the yuan has become a flashpoint in trade relations between China and the European Union, with many EU policymakers and economists believing the currency is undervalued and a key driver of the bloc's deepening trade deficit with China.
- The EU is concerned about the value of the yuan because a relatively weaker currency means a country's goods are cheaper for foreign buyers, which can help fuel trade surpluses and give Chinese exporters a competitive advantage.
- Some European policymakers argue that a stronger yuan is needed to help narrow China's trade advantage, with proposals including a blanket duty on imports from China or a depreciation of the euro versus the yuan to rebalance trade flows.
The value of the [yuan ](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNY%20CFEO%20Curncy)has become a flashpoint in trade relations between China and the European Union. Many EU policymakers and economists believe the currency is undervalued and a key driver of the bloc’s deepening trade deficit with the East Asian nation. That [imbalance](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNTXEU%20%20%20Index) averaged about $1.1 billion a day in June.

The yuan has strengthened versus the [euro](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/EUR%20BGN%20Curncy) over the past 12 months, rising by around 9% as of late July. But it remains some way off its 2015 peak. As European industries struggle to compete with an influx of cheaper Chinese goods, there are increasing calls emerging from Europe for China to allow its currency to appreciate by about 20% to 30% to level the playing field.

But there’s no consensus on the yuan’s fair value. And in China, there’s [little indication](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/pboc-signals-comfort-with-yuan-levels-expects-two-way-moves) the government is willing to engineer a sharp appreciation while economic conditions are weak. This, along with other disagreements with China, leaves the EU with a dilemma of how to rebalance its relationship with a country that’s both a strategic rival and essential trading partner. The two sides have set an [October deadline](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-29/eu-and-china-agree-on-october-deadline-to-reset-trade-relations) to make progress on their trade disagreements.

### **Is the yuan undervalued?**

While the yuan has strengthened on a nominal basis —** **reaching a record high in June based on the [Bank for International Settlements’ effective exchange rate gauge](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BISBCNN%20%20Index) — the currency is still widely viewed** **as undervalued in real terms. Its [real effective exchange rate](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BISBCNR%20%20Index) (REER), which is measured against a basket of partners’** **currencies and adjusted for inflation,** **has declined over the past decade, while those of the** **dollar and euro have risen.

The [International Monetary Fund ](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/13347Z:US)estimated China’s REER was 12% to 20% undervalued in 2025. In June, former US Treasury official Brad Setser argued the yuan may be more than 20% undervalued, while around the same time, [strategists at Deutsche Bank](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/yuan-still-cheap-versus-euro-even-after-gain-deutsche-bank-says), Goldman Sachs and Bank of America each put the figure at somewhere between 10% and 20% using different valuation models. A strategist from Australia & New Zealand Banking Group, by contrast, sees little evidence of nominal undervaluation.

Many Chinese economists reject the view that the yuan is misvalued, arguing that valuation models overstate the importance of China’s trade surplus while failing to adequately account for other market forces on the exchange rate, [including capital flows.](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-10/china-s-4-5-trillion-flows-mark-tipping-point-in-market-opening)

### **What role does China play in managing the yuan?**

China maintains a “managed float” of the yuan, allowing authorities to exert significant influence over the exchange rate. The People’s Bank of China sets a daily reference rate, or [fixing](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNYMUSD%20%20Index), and then allows the currency to trade within 2% above or below it. The central bank says its aim is to keep the currency basically [stable](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-11/china-warns-of-imported-inflation-risk-as-oil-prices-increase) and prevent volatility.

China has a number of tools at its disposal to keep the currency stable — some more transparent than others. One method is adjusting the amount of foreign currency that banks are required to hold as reserves, which can influence demand for the yuan. State-owned banks also step in to buy dollars to weaken the yuan or sell dollars to support it.

The PBOC has acted to limit volatility in both directions in recent years. It supported the yuan during periods of heavy depreciation in 2022 and 2023, and in early 2026 it unwound some of the measures it had introduced to support the currency after it strengthened.

Some of the less transparent methods have, however, fueled** **accusations that Beijing keeps the currency artificially weak, to give Chinese exporters an edge over rivals. The central bank** **PBOC: China Doesn’t Seek Competitive Edge From Yuan Depreciation that characterization, saying it’s ultimately the [market that decides](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/pboc-signals-comfort-with-yuan-levels-expects-two-way-moves) the exchange rate.

### **Why is the EU concerned about the value of the yuan?**

A relatively weaker currency means a country’s goods are cheaper for foreign buyers. At the same time, imports are more expensive. This can [help fuel trade surpluses](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-10/imf-urges-bolder-stimulus-from-china-to-rebalance-economy-trade), whereby the value of a nation’s exports exceeds the value of its imports.

Some economists and European policymakers argue that the yuan’s relatively low value has given Chinese exporters an additional competitive advantage. Exports have become an increasingly important driver of China’s economic growth, and the country’s trade surplus with the EU hit a [record $32.9 billion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/china-s-trade-surplus-with-eu-hits-record-as-tensions-intensify) in June, according to Chinese customs data.

Germany has the EU’s biggest trade deficit with China, amounting to around €95 billion last year, and its manufacturers, in particular automakers, have come under increasing pressure from Chinese competition.

China’s trade surplus took off during the Covid-19 pandemic as Europeans bought more goods to adapt to lockdowns and working from home. More recently, US tariffs have prompted Chinese exporters to redirect their shipments to other markets, such as Europe and Southeast Asia. Meanwhile, China’s imports have flatlined over time, making the trade relationship with the EU ever more lopsided.

The growing imbalance is a source of tension as European firms face increased pressure at home from low-cost Chinese goods. At the same time, they’re struggling to sell to China and also face greater competition in other overseas markets as Chinese companies export more to the rest of the world.

### **What is being proposed in Europe?**

Some European policymakers argue that a stronger yuan is needed to help narrow China’s trade advantage. Such an adjustment would make Chinese exports more expensive in Europe and European goods cheaper in China. Although this could help to reduce some of the imbalance,** **some economists** **say that this alone would not solve Europe’s competitiveness problem.

Nevertheless the idea has gained some traction in Europe. An advisory body to the French government [earlier this year said](https://www.strategie-plan.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/2026/2026-02-09%20-%20Rouleau%20compresseur%20chinois/HCSP-2026-REPORT-THE%20CHINESE%20STEAMROLLER-18fevrier14h30-COUV.pdf) that the EU should consider a blanket 30% duty on imports from China or seek a 20% to 30% depreciation of the euro versus the yuan to rebalance trade flows.

A May [report](https://www.cer.eu/publications/archive/policy-brief/2026/china-shock-20-cost-germanys-complacency) by the Centre for European Reform said a “revaluation” of the Chinese currency would be an “immediate fix” for the trade imbalance, citing an alleged yuan undervaluation of as much as 30%.

More recently, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and French President Emmanuel Macron** **[have both called for “dialogue”](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-17/germany-and-france-to-draw-up-roadmap-on-china-trade-imbalances)** **with Beijing on the issue of the yuan.** **Merz has also [pushed for international talks](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-19/germany-s-merz-seeks-currency-talks-to-fix-undervalued-yuan) on exchange rates, even drawing on the 1985 Plaza Accord as a possible model for addressing currency imbalances.** **Under that agreement,** **the US, Japan, West Germany, France and the UK coordinated policies to weaken the US dollar against their currencies in an effort to improve US export competitiveness and narrow the country’s trade deficit.

In June, European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde played down the idea of a modern-day Plaza Accord to drive a sharp appreciation of the yuan, arguing that today’s situation is “vastly different.”** **The EU’s broader strategy to rebalance trade ties with China involves developing fresh [funding support](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-11/eu-warns-push-to-diversify-away-from-china-will-need-funding) for firms to diversify their supply chains while making better use of existing trade tools such as anti-subsidy investigations, tariffs and import quotas.

Despite signs the EU is willing to [take a tougher stance](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-03/left-with-few-choices-eu-braces-for-a-trade-fight-with-china) on China, the bloc remains wary of sparking a trade war with the world’s second-largest economy. Beijing has repeatedly [threatened retaliation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-29/china-warns-of-retaliation-ahead-of-eu-meeting-on-trade-curbs) against trade barriers, which would endanger European access to a pivotal market and to key inputs such as chips and critical minerals. The risks of a confrontation became clear last year, when China tightened controls over rare earths in response to US tariffs. The resulting supply disruptions forced a number of European companies to [halt production](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-18/china-rare-earth-product-exports-hit-highest-since-at-least-2012).

### **What is China’s position on strengthening the yuan?**

Some Chinese economists and former central bank officials have [argued](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-15/xi-faces-calls-for-stronger-yuan-in-rare-public-currency-debate) that a stronger yuan is needed if the country is to rebalance the economy away from export-dependent growth and reduce tensions with trade partners. An appreciation of the yuan would increase people’s purchasing power and make imported goods less expensive, which could boost tepid consumer spending.

The PBOC has signaled a [tolerance](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-28/world-s-appetite-for-ai-makes-china-less-afraid-of-stronger-yuan) for a modest appreciation in the value of the yuan. In mid-July, it set its fixing rate at the [strongest level](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-10/pboc-sets-fixing-below-6-8-per-dollar-for-first-time-since-2023-mre95kva) since 2023. Still, it doesn’t mean it’s open to a faster rally or sharp appreciation — and China’s government is highly unlikely to agree to a Plaza Accord-style deal. The PBOC didn’t reply to a Bloomberg fax seeking comment.

Part of its reluctance stems from how it views Japan’s experience after the 1985 accord. Many Chinese policymakers and economists believe the yen’s rapid appreciation contributed to Japan’s asset-price bubble and the decades of weak economic growth that followed. Some also believe the accord was a [politically motivated](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202606/1364067.shtml) effort by the US to boost its own economy at the expense of others,** **rather than a purely economic agreement.** **That perception has reinforced China’s determination to defend “[free trade](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-11/china-vice-premier-to-join-macron-s-g7-call-on-trade-imbalances)” and maintain control over its own economic policies.

China has instead tried to focus trade talks with the EU on how to increase imports from the bloc and expand access to the Chinese market, rather than measures that would affect its own exports.

### **Is the EU alone in its concerns about the yuan?**

US President Donald Trump has a long history of accusing China of having an unfair, mercantilist exchange-rate policy. During his first term in office, his administration briefly [designated](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-08-05/u-s-treasury-department-labels-china-a-currency-manipulator) the nation a currency manipulator, dropping the label after a bilateral trade deal was negotiated.

The US Treasury’s semiannual foreign exchange [report](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/January-2026-FX-Report.pdf) said in January that the yuan was “[substantially undervalued](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-29/us-calls-on-china-to-strengthen-substantially-undervalued-yuan)” and called on Chinese authorities to allow the exchange rate to strengthen “in a timely and orderly manner in line with market pressure and macroeconomic fundamentals.” The Treasury didn’t classify the currency as manipulated but said that it’s prepared to use this label if evidence suggests China is intervening through formal or informal channels to resist yuan appreciation.
