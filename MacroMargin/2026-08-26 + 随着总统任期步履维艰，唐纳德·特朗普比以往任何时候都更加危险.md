### 中文译文

## 随着总统任期步履维艰，唐纳德·特朗普比以往任何时候都更加危险

### 忘掉跛脚鸭吧。美国总统将像一只衰老的银背大猩猩一样捍卫自己的统治地位

![Illustration: Chloe Cushman](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b1481789de4d70eec6a8e59/3c881cc9-0b14-8160-a7b7-d897efdb4b46-16c970b04d3088664750f2195eab49d9.jpg)

插图：克洛伊·库什曼

唐纳德·特朗普正在变成一只跛脚鸭吗？随着他在国内外麻烦不断增多，与一位步履蹒跚的美国总统打交道的前景，足以让敌人和朋友都松一口气。

对于统治中国、伊朗和其他美国对手的强硬人物来说，挺过特朗普先生的各项极限施压运动将是一场载入史册的宣传胜利——即便这些威权统治者从未完全体会过困扰自由民主国家的“特朗普恐慌”。在内心深处，习近平和他的朋友们相信，所有白宫主人都是自私的、美国优先的霸凌者，但有些人比特朗普先生隐藏得更好。

对他们而言，许多盟友和伙伴希望看到特朗普先生蹒跚地走到总统任期结束。这并不意味着柏林、利雅得或东京的领导人希望特朗普先生输掉对伊朗的战争，或承认在对华贸易对抗中失败。美国变弱会吓坏欧洲、亚洲或中东的盟友，他们离在没有长期大国保护者的情况下自卫，至少还需要数年时间。但是，一个愤怒、无友的特朗普先生，因自己的失误和支持率暴跌而如此虚弱，以至于退回到他金碧辉煌的白宫堡垒，在那里琢磨舞厅和纪念拱门的计划？那将是许多政府欢呼的景象。如果11月中期选举中共和党惨败，松动了特朗普先生对国会中本党的铁腕控制，并对他的君主式统治施加一些约束，他们会尤其感到宽慰。

唉，任何希望看到特朗普先生因失败而收敛的人都看错了人。政治智慧可能很快会将特朗普先生宣布为最跛的鸭子，因为他的意愿受到国会挑战，或者金融市场对“让美国再次伟大经济学”作出严厉而代价高昂的评判。特朗普先生可能并不在意。让小人物去担心共和党、公共政策或美国的预算赤字吧。只要特朗普先生仍然是总司令，他就能通过重塑或破坏世界来确保自己在历史上的地位。

外国政府应停止幻想鸭子。相反，他们应该研究山地大猩猩，以及一只衰老的银背雄性在感觉到自己时日无多时能爆发的暴力。这尤其应该让盟友担忧。衰老的银背对它所领导的群体成员显得格外残忍。它们会毫不犹豫地对越界的下属动物释放犬齿和强壮的四肢。相比之下，当银背遇到来自敌对群体的强大雄性时——不知为何，让人想起赤膊上阵的弗拉基米尔·普京——它们的偏好往往是避免实际战斗，而选择喧嚣的支配性展示。由于没有霸道的Truth Social帖子作为发泄渠道，银背必须满足于捶胸、虚张声势的冲锋和折枝。

在特朗普总统的第一个任期内，怨恨感是其“美国优先”世界观的核心。特朗普抱怨说，几十年来，美国一直受到盟友和贸易伙伴的剥削。他通常把矛头指向美国1945年后的执政精英，他们太无能或太怯懦，无法利用国家无与伦比的财富和权力来推进其国家利益。更糟的是，他们让美国陷入无休止的战争，天真地相信他们能让世界变得更美好。他在整个第一任期内都保持着这种怨恨的语气。

第二个特朗普总统任期将怨恨与准帝国主义征服的言论结合在一起。2025年重返白宫后，特朗普称此前的总统“愚蠢”，因为他们二战后放弃了美国对格陵兰的控制权，或将巴拿马运河交给巴拿马，并承诺将这两者都夺回。这不仅仅是故作姿态。基调在1月份就已定下，当时美军从委内瑞拉领导人的住所将其抓走，使特朗普能够为这个石油资源丰富的国家挑选一个更顺从的统治者。“美国在西半球的主导地位将永远不会再受到质疑，”他得意地说。

现在，在他担任总统的第六个年头，第三个目标——复仇——与怨恨和主导地位一道，成为特朗普治国方略的指导原则。复仇的欲望有助于解释特朗普半年前与以色列联手打击伊朗的这一重大决定。《政权更迭》（Regime Change）一书由两位白宫记者玛吉·哈伯曼（Maggie Haberman）和乔纳森·斯旺（Jonathan Swan）撰写，内幕详尽得惊人，记述了特朗普在会议上享受杀死伊朗最高领导人和摧毁其武装部队的机会，而不论这是否会带来一个更好的伊朗。伊朗政权曾羞辱历届美国总统，还试图暗杀特朗普。现在，他得到了一个让他们付出代价的机会。这场战争的公开目标可能是推翻伊朗专制政府。但该书描述了特朗普在一次与亲密助手的闭门会议上，将伊朗政权更迭斥为“他们的问题”。他指的是伊朗人民的问题，还是以色列的问题，无人知晓。而惩罚无疑是他的意图。

### **犹豫不决时，便猛烈攻击**

现在，由于霍尔木兹海峡仍然关闭，特朗普的大部分怒火都指向欧洲、亚洲和阿拉伯世界的盟友与伙伴，他指责他们未能帮助他打开这条水道。如果胜利仍然难以实现，那么预计特朗普将继续处于复仇模式。可能的目标名单很长。北约盟国，尤其是加拿大和丹麦，以及乌克兰、韩国和古巴，多年来都曾激怒过特朗普。像习近平这样的大人物则没什么可害怕的。

特朗普自认为是阿尔法男性，骨子里如此，他绝不可能悄无声息地从白宫跛行而去。如今，既然他的主导地位受到威胁，世界将发现他比以往任何时候都更具破坏性。■

## As his presidency flounders, Donald Trump is more dangerous than ever

## As his presidency flounders, Donald Trump is more dangerous than ever

### Forget lame ducks. America’s president will defend his dominance like an ageing silverback gorilla

Illustration: Chloe Cushman

Is Donald Trump becoming a lame duck? As his troubles multiply at home and abroad, the prospect of dealing with a hobbled American president is enough to make foes and friends swoon with relief.

For the hard men who rule China, Iran and other American adversaries, to survive Mr Trump’s various maximum-pressure campaigns would be a propaganda win for the ages—even if those same autocrats never quite shared the Trump-horror that consumes liberal democracies. Deep down, Xi Jinping and his friends believe that all occupants of the White House are selfish, America First bullies, but some hide it better than Mr Trump does.

For their part, many allies and partners would like to see Mr Trump limp to the end of his presidency. That does not mean that leaders in Berlin, Riyadh or Tokyo are rooting for Mr Trump to lose his war in Iran, or for him to admit defeat in his trade confrontation with China. The enfeebling of America would frighten allies in Europe, Asia or the Middle East, who are years away, at best, from being able to defend themselves without their longtime great-power protector. But a fuming, friendless Mr Trump, so weakened by his own blunders and by plunging approval ratings that he retreats to the gilded fastness of his White House, there to tinker with plans for his ballroom and memorial arch? That is a vision that many governments would cheer. They would be especially relieved if a Republican wipeout in November’s midterms loosens Mr Trump’s iron grip on his own party in Congress, and imposes some constraints on his monarchical rule.

Alas, anyone hoping to see Mr Trump chastened by failure is misjudging the man. Political wisdom may soon declare Mr Trump the lamest of ducks, as his wishes are challenged by Congress, or as financial markets pass harsh and costly judgments on MAGA-nomics. Mr Trump may not care. Let little people worry about the Republican Party, public policy or America’s budget deficit. As long as Mr Trump is still commander-in-chief, he can secure a place in history by remaking, or breaking, the world.

Foreign governments should stop dreaming of ducks. Instead, they should study mountain gorillas, and the violence of which an ageing silverback male is capable, when it senses its time is up. This should worry allies especially. Ageing silverbacks are conspicuously vicious towards members of the band that they lead. They do not hesitate to unleash canine teeth and brawny limbs on subordinate animals who step out of line. In contrast, when silverbacks encounter powerful males from rival bands—a bare-chested Vladimir Putin springs to mind, for some reason—their preference is often to avoid actual combat in favour of noisy dominance displays. With blustering Truth Social posts unavailable as an outlet, silverbacks must content themselves with chest-beating, mock charges and the snapping of branches off trees.

In Mr Trump’s first term, a sense of grievance lay at the heart of his America First worldview. Mr Trump grumbled that for decades America had been ripped off by allies and trade partners alike. Typically, he blamed America’s post-1945 ruling elites, who were too incompetent or craven to use the country’s unrivalled wealth and power to advance their national interests. Worse, they embroiled America in forever wars in a naive belief that they could make the world a kindlier place. He maintained that tone of grievance throughout his first term.

The second Trump presidency has seen grievance combined with talk of quasi-imperialist conquest. Upon returning to office in 2025, Mr Trump declared earlier presidents “stupid” for giving up American control of Greenland after the second world war, or for handing the Panama Canal to Panama, and pledged to take both back. This was not mere posturing. The tone was set in January, after American troops snatched Venezuela’s leader from his home, allowing Mr Trump to hand-pick a more pliant ruler for that oil-rich country. “American dominance in the Western Hemisphere will never be questioned again,” he crowed.

Now, in his sixth year as president, a third goal has joined grievance and dominance as a guiding principle of Mr Trump’s statecraft. That goal is vengeance. A desire for revenge helps explain Mr Trump’s fateful decision to join Israel in attacking Iran, a half-year ago. “Regime Change”, a startlingly well-briefed book about the second Trump presidency by two White House correspondents, Maggie Haberman and Jonathan Swan, describes meetings in which Mr Trump savoured the chance to kill Iran’s top leaders and destroy their armed forces, whether or not this led to a better Iran. The Iranian regime had humiliated successive American presidents and tried to assassinate Mr Trump. Now he was being offered a chance to make them pay. A public aim of the war might have been the toppling of Iran’s despotic government. But the book describes Mr Trump, in a closed-door meeting with his close aides, dismissing Iranian regime change as “their problem”. Whether he meant the Iranian people’s problem or Israel’s, nobody quite knew. Punishment, though, was indisputably his intent.

### **When in doubt, lash out**

Now, as the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, much of Mr Trump’s wrath is aimed at allies and partners in Europe, Asia and the Arab world, whom he blames for failing to help him open that waterway. If victory remains elusive, expect Mr Trump to stay in revenge mode. The list of plausible targets is long. Members of the NATO alliance, notably Canada and Denmark, Ukraine, South Korea and Cuba have all angered Mr Trump over the years. Big beasts such as Mr Xi have less to fear.

Mr Trump, who sees himself as an alpha male to his core, was never likely to limp quietly from office. Now that his dominance is threatened, the world will find him more disruptive than ever. ■
