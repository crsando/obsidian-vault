### 中文译文

## 特朗普誓言将加拿大汽车关税提高一倍，加剧贸易争端

一辆汽车运输卡车正驶向大使桥，准备在安大略省温莎市进入美国底特律。*摄影师：Bill Pugliano/Bloomberg*

作者：[Josh Wingrove](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASLqWCO-Zh4/josh-wingrove) 2026年8月24日 晚上9:50 GMT+8*更新于2026年8月24日 晚上10:17 GMT+8*

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普承诺从明年起将对加拿大汽车和零部件的关税提高一倍，使这两个经济体之间不断升级的贸易争端进一步加剧。

特朗普周一发帖称，对“所有汽车、卡车（无论大小）、汽车零部件和钢铁”的关税将提高至50%，自1月1日起生效。目前的汽车关税税率为25%，但仅适用于非美国成分。进口钢铁已经面临50%的关税。

最新的关税威胁是在美国和加拿大之间的谈判上周五破裂后发出的，这导致对价值数十亿美元的加拿大商品征收[50%关税](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/us-canada-trade-talks-collapse-triggering-trump-s-50-tariffs)生效。加拿大总理马克·卡尼已经宣布了一揽子[报复性关税](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/canada-unveils-20-billion-counter-tariffs-to-mirror-trump-levy)措施。

实施汽车关税可能会严重扰乱美加之间过去几十年来深度融合的供应链。特朗普此前曾承诺对汽车零部件征收关税，但尚未推进这一物流复杂的任务。

目前尚不清楚特朗普将如何处理美国成分的豁免条款，这对关税税率有重大影响。实际上，对加拿大汽车征收的关税约为表面税率的一半，因为这些汽车通常有一半是美国制造的。

目前尚不清楚特朗普周一宣布的新关税是否会实施。1月1日的实施日期为潜在谈判留出了空间，尽管加拿大人认为在中期选举前恢复谈判的可能性不大。卡尼计划于9月8日对周末的关税作出回应，实施自己的关税措施，而美国已威胁称，如果他这样做，将进一步升级局势。

周一的威胁是特朗普自贸易谈判突然破裂（导致一项原本可以避免新关税并调整现有关税的协议流产）以来最全面的回应。双方相互指责对方导致谈判破裂。

美国贸易代表杰米森·格里尔表示，该协议为加拿大提供了关税减免，包括对钢铁、铝和汽车的减免，而卡尼则表示，他之所以退出，是因为美国在最后时刻提出了过于苛刻的要求。

特朗普周一宣布的新措施尤其针对加拿大安大略省。该省由省长道格·福特领导，他在谈判中的强硬态度激怒了特朗普政府。钢铁和汽车生产高度集中在安大略省，这是加拿大人口最多、选票最多的省份。

## Trump Vows to Double Canadian Auto Tariffs, Escalating Fight

A car hauler truck makes its way to the Ambassador Bridge to cross into the United States at Detroit in Windsor, Ontario. *Photographer: Bill Pugliano/Bloomberg*

By [Josh Wingrove](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASLqWCO-Zh4/josh-wingrove)August 24, 2026 at 9:50 PM GMT+8*Updated on August 24, 2026 at 10:17 PM GMT+8*

US President Donald Trump pledged to double the automobile tariff on Canadian vehicles and parts starting next year, escalating the spiraling trade fight between the two economies.

Trump posted on Monday that “Tariffs on all Cars, Trucks, both large and small, Automotive Parts, and Steel” would rise to 50%, effective Jan. 1. The current auto rate is 25% but is applied only to non-US content. Imported steel already faces a 50% charge.

The latest tariff threat comes after negotiations between the US and Canada broke down Friday, which led to a [50% levy](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/us-canada-trade-talks-collapse-triggering-trump-s-50-tariffs) on billions of dollars worth of Canadian goods to take effect. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has announced a package of [retaliatory duties](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-22/canada-unveils-20-billion-counter-tariffs-to-mirror-trump-levy).

Following through on the auto tariffs could significantly disrupt a supply chain between the US and Canada that has become deeply integrated over the past few decades. Trump has previously pledged to tariff auto parts but had not yet proceeded with the logistically complicated task.

Tractor trailers and vehicles cross the Peace Bridge at the Canada-United States border in Fort Erie, Ontario, Canada, on Thursday, Aug. 20, 2026. Photographer: Cole Burston/Bloomberg

It was not immediately clear how Trump would address the carve-out for US content in vehicles, which substantially affects the tariff rate. Levies on Canadian vehicles, in practice, are about half the headline rate because the vehicles are typically half US-made.

It is unclear if the new tariffs Trump announced Monday will be enacted. The Jan. 1 implementation sets up runway for potential negotiations, even as the Canadians see little chance of those resuming before the midterms. Carney is set to respond to the weekend tariffs with levies of his own on Sept. 8 and the US has threatened to escalate further if he does.

Monday’s threat was Trump’s most fulsome response yet since the sudden collapse of trade talks killed an emerging deal to head off the new levies and adjust existing ones. The two sides have blamed each other for the collapse of negotiations.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the deal offered Canada tariff reductions, including for steel, aluminum and autos, while Carney said he walked away because of 11th-hour US demands that were too great.

The new measures Trump announced Monday target the Canadian province of Ontario in particular. The province is led by Premier Doug Ford, whose pugnacious approach to talks has riled the Trump administration. Steel and auto production is heavily concentrated in Ontario, which is Canada’s most populous and vote-rich province.
