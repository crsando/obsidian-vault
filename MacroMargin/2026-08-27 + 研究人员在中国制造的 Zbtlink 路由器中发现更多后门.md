### 中文译文

## 研究人员在中国制造的 Zbtlink 路由器中发现更多后门

#### 摘要

- VulnCheck 表示，十几款 Zbtlink 路由器型号至少存在两个额外的后门
- VulnCheck 表示，Speakingstone 有可能重定向受影响路由器的网络流量
- Zbtlink 表示，远程访问功能用于授权的售后维护，不构成安全风险
8月27日（路透社）——根据网络安全公司 VulnCheck 的最新发现，超过十二款中国制造的 Zbtlink 路由器出厂时至少带有两个后门，这些后门可能允许类似于“监控”的侵入性远程访问。

这两个新后门——名为“Darklantern”和“Speakingstone”——早于先前在部分 Zbtlink 路由器型号上发现的名为“Endlessdoors”的后门。VulnCheck 在一篇详细介绍这些新发现的[博客文章](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/zbt-darklantern-speakingstone)中表示，这两个新后门使得轻易获取受影响路由器所安装网络的信息成为可能，而且就 Speakingstone 而言，还可能重定向网络流量，构成重大安全风险。

这些此前未报道的发现，是在同一家公司[发现](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-made-zbtlink-routers-have-backdoor-researchers-say-2026-08-05/)并披露“Endlessdoors”后数周公布的。该后门存在于20多款 Zbtlink 路由器中，任何能访问特定域名的人都可以借此从路由器收集数据，并有可能连接到同一网络上的其他设备。在 VulnCheck 披露一天后，Zbtlink [暂停](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-zbtlink-suspends-sales-routers-found-contain-backdoor-2026-08-06/)了这些路由器的销售，并下架了受影响的软件，同时表示该后门实际上是一项远程访问支持功能，从未被恶意利用。

发现这些后门的 VulnCheck 首席技术官 Jacob Baines 表示，所有这些植入后门的一个关键问题是提醒个人和组织，他们可能正在使用受感染的 Zbtlink 路由器——这些路由器以各种品牌名称在全球销售——无论他们是否意识到这一点。

“仅仅因为你从未听说过Zbtlink，并不意味着它没有在其他地方被转售，”贝恩斯在接受采访时说。

贝恩斯在博客文章中将Speakingstone称为“监控植入物”。

贝恩斯发现，包含Speakingstone的路由器曾试图连接一个未注册域名，于是他自行注册了该域名。来自受感染路由器的信息开始陆续涌入。数据显示，绝大多数此类路由器在中国活跃，这表明“这是中国的国内监控技术，被用于对付中国公民”，同时该技术仍在全球范围内以相同功能销售，包括在美国。

Zbtlink发言人迈克尔·夏在一封电子邮件中表示，公司产品的“合法远程支持和云访问功能仅用于授权的售后维护”。这些远程访问方法“不存在安全风险，我们极为重视产品安全”。

夏未回应有关涉嫌监控能力的问题，也未回应贝恩斯的论断，即公司关于其植入物是合法远程支持工具的解释讲不通。

报道：AJ Vicens（底特律）；编辑：Stephen Coates。

### English Original

## Researchers discover additional backdoors in Chinese-made Zbtlink routers

#### Summary

- VulnCheck says more than a dozen Zbtlink router models carry at least two additional backdoors
- Speakingstone could potentially redirect network traffic from affected routers, VulnCheck says
- Zbtlink says remote access functions are for authorized after-sales maintenance and pose no security risks
Aug 27 (Reuters) - More than a dozen models of Chinese-made Zbtlink routers ‌ship with at least two backdoors that could allow for invasive remote access akin to "surveillance," according to new findings from cybersecurity firm VulnCheck.

The two new backdoors — dubbed "Darklantern" and "Speakingstone" — preceded an earlier discovered backdoor called "Endlessdoors" on certain models of Zbtlink routers. The two new backdoors allow for the easy access to information about the network on which affected routers are installed as well as, in the case of Speakingstone, potentially for the redirection of ⁠network traffic, representing a significant security risk, VulnCheck said in a [blog post](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/zbt-darklantern-speakingstone) detailing the new findings.

The previously unreported findings come weeks after the same firm [discovered](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-made-zbtlink-routers-have-backdoor-researchers-say-2026-08-05/) and disclosed "Endlessdoors," a backdoor present in more than 20 Zbtlink routers that would have allowed anyone with access to certain domains to collect data from the routers and potentially connect to other devices on the same network. One day after VulnCheck's disclosure, Zbtlink [suspended](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-zbtlink-suspends-sales-routers-found-contain-backdoor-2026-08-06/) sales of the routers and pulled affected software offline, and said the backdoor was actually a remote access support function and was never abused for malicious purposes.

Jacob Baines, the chief technology officer at VulnCheck who found the backdoors, said a key issue across all the implants is to alert ‌people ⁠and organizations that they may be using infected Zbtlink routers – which are sold around the world under various brand names – whether they realize it or not.

"Just because you've never heard of Zbtlink doesn't mean it's not being resold in other places," Baines said in an interview.

Baines called Speakingstone a "surveillance implant" in the blog post.

Baines found that routers that included Speakingstone were attempting to connect to ⁠an unregistered domain, so he registered it himself. Information from infected routers began rolling in. The data revealed that the vast majority of the routers were active in China, suggesting that "this is domestic Chinese surveillance technology, deployed against Chinese citizens" while still being ⁠sold around the world, with the same functionality, including in the U.S.

Michael Xia, a spokesperson for Zbtlink, said in an email that the company's products' "legitimate remote support and cloud access functions are intended solely for authorized after-sales maintenance." The ⁠remote access methods "pose no security risks, and we place the utmost importance on product security."

Xia did not address questions about the alleged surveillance capabilities or respond to Baines' contention that the company's explanation that its implants were legitimate remote support tools did not make sense.

Reporting by AJ Vicens in Detroit; Editing by Stephen Coates
