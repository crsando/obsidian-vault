### 中文译文

## 中国指定德意志银行为欧洲首家外国人民币清算行

撰文：彭博新闻社 2026年8月12日 上午11:16 GMT+8

### **要点**（由彭博AI提供）

### **要点**

- 中国已授权德意志银行成为首家在欧洲结算人民币交易的非中资银行。
- 此举凸显了中国推动其货币国际化并减少对美元依赖的努力。
- 中国已批准更多外国银行进行人民币结算清算，包括阿联酋、新加坡和非洲的银行。
中国已授权[德意志银行](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/DBK%20GR%20Equity)成为首家在欧洲结算人民币交易的非中资银行，扩大了其清算网络以在海外推广人民币。

据中国人民银行（PBOC）发布的“央行指定德意志银行为法兰克福人民币清算行”消息，这家德国银行将从其总部所在地法兰克福处理该地区的人民币清算。此前，欧洲的此类清算业务由中国银行、中国工商银行和中国建设银行的分支机构在法兰克福、伦敦、巴黎、布达佩斯以及卢森堡等城市处理。

此举凸显了中国推动人民币国际化并减少对美元依赖的努力。在欧洲，人民币的使用得益于中国与[欧盟](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNTXEU%20%20%20Index)和[德国](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNTXDE%20%20%20Index)之间深厚的经济联系，这体现在创纪录的贸易顺差上，尽管欧盟对[失衡问题](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/germany-maps-china-s-weaknesses-in-preparation-for-a-trade-war)感到担忧。

德意志银行中国区总裁 Leo Yin 表示，近年来客户询价和人民币结算量有所上升，同时全球企业越来越多地直接以人民币开具发票，以降低成本并提高与中国供应商的定价透明度。他补充说，对人民币计价资产的更大兴趣也将进一步提振对人民币的需求。

“在中国有大量商业活动的欧洲跨国公司，尤其是制造业、汽车和绿色科技领域，可能成为主要驱动力，”尹说。

自去年以来，中国已授权更多外国银行开展人民币结算清算，包括中国人民银行任命阿布扎比第一银行为阿联酋人民币清算行、中国人民银行授权星展集团为新加坡人民币清算行，以及[标准银行](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-26/standard-bank-wins-yuan-clearing-role-eyes-china-africa-trade)在非洲获得人民币清算资格。

另一个显示全球人民币需求上升的迹象是，中国自己的人民币支付系统——跨境银行间支付系统（CIPS）——吸收了更多外资银行作为直接参与者，还有更多银行[希望加入](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-18/absa-weighs-joining-yuan-platform-on-thriving-china-africa-trade)。

中银国际全球首席经济学家管涛表示，外资银行可以通过自己的网络提供独特优势，而中国也渴望扩大其海外清算基础设施。

## China Taps Deutsche Bank as First Foreign Yuan Clearer in Europe

By Bloomberg NewsAugust 12, 2026 at 11:16 AM GMT+8

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

### **Takeaways**

- China has authorized Deutsche Bank AG as the first non-Chinese lender that can settle yuan transactions in Europe.
- The move underscores China’s push to internationalize its currency and lessen reliance on the dollar.
- China has been authorizing more foreign lenders to clear yuan settlements, including banks in the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, and Africa.
China has authorized [Deutsche Bank AG](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/DBK%20GR%20Equity) as the first non-Chinese lender that can settle yuan transactions in Europe, expanding its clearing network to promote the currency overseas.

The German bank will handle yuan clearing in the region from Frankfurt, where it’s headquartered, according to a PBOC Names Deutsche Bank as Frankfurt Yuan Clearing Bank from the People’s Bank of China. Previously, such processing in Europe was handled by units of Bank of China, Industrial & Commercial Bank of China and China Construction Bank from cities including Frankfurt, London, Paris and Budapest, as well as Luxembourg.

The move underscores China’s push to internationalize its currency and lessen reliance on the dollar. In Europe, yuan usage is supported by China’s deep economic ties, reflected in record trade surpluses with the [bloc ](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNTXEU%20%20%20Index)and [Germany](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNTXDE%20%20%20Index), even amid EU concerns over [imbalances](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/germany-maps-china-s-weaknesses-in-preparation-for-a-trade-war).

Client inquiries and yuan‑settlement volumes have risen in recent years, while global firms increasingly invoice directly in yuan to cut costs and improve pricing transparency with Chinese suppliers, Leo Yin, president of Deutsche Bank China said. Greater interest in yuan-denominated assets will also further boost demand for the yuan, he added.

“European multinationals with significant commercial activity in China, particularly in manufacturing, automotive and green technology, are likely to be key drivers,” Yin said.

Since last year, China has been authorizing more foreign lenders to clear yuan settlements, including PBOC Appoints First Abu Dhabi Bank as Yuan Clearing Bank in the United Arab Emirates, PBOC Authorizes DBS Group as Yuan Clearing Bank in Singapore in Singapore, and [Standard Bank](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-26/standard-bank-wins-yuan-clearing-role-eyes-china-africa-trade) in Africa.

In another sign of rising global demand for the yuan, China’s own yuan payment network, Cross-Border Interbank Payment System, or CIPS, has absorbed more foreign banks as direct participants, with more [looking to join](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-18/absa-weighs-joining-yuan-platform-on-thriving-china-africa-trade).

Foreign banks could offer distinct advantages through their own networks, while China is also eager to expand its overseas clearing infrastructure, said Guan Tao, global chief economist of Bank of China International.
