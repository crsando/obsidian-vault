### 中文译文

## 独家 | 消息人士称，诺基亚将在年底前关闭在中国大陆的几乎所有办事处

#### 从中国——曾是诺基亚全球最大单一国家市场——撤退，将意味着向国内竞争低头

![The Nokia campus in Espoo, Finland. Photo: dpa](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b14810e8955c58da0a2c236/3c081cc9-0b14-81d2-b280-eb350efa6a41-996a4dff8b23393e1a5f6af124d967af.jpg)

[**Minxiao Chang**](https://www.scmp.com/author/minxiao-chang)**在深圳和**[**Wency Chen**](https://www.scmp.com/author/wency-chen)**在上海**

发布时间：2026年8月18日晚上9:00更新：2026年8月18日晚上9:05

据知情人士透露，芬兰电信设备巨头诺基亚计划在中国大陆裁减大部分员工，并在年底前分阶段关闭办事处，这标志着在四十多年后，随着其落后于国内竞争对手，诺基亚正全面撤退。

一位驻上海的消息人士表示，诺基亚在中国大陆的移动网络和网络基础设施业务团队将分批裁员，预计大部分裁员将在今年年底前完成。

尽管受影响员工总数仍不清楚，但诺基亚的年报显示，截至2025年底，该公司在中国大陆、香港和台湾共有约7200名员工，并在北京、上海、杭州、成都和青岛等城市设有办事处。

消息人士称，诺基亚本月早些时候在赫尔辛基总部召开的一次内部在线视频会议上宣布了缩减中国大陆业务的计划。

一位人士表示，中国将仅保留售后服务，这将构成“基本上是从市场逐步退出”。

一位曾在诺基亚软件测试部门工作的驻上海前员工表示，他在过去几周内离开了公司，获得了“N+3”的遣散费——即基于工作年限“N”的补偿，外加三个月的工资。

该消息人士称，今年早些时候，该国部分员工已转至诺基亚在芬兰的总部工作。

### 美国将阿里巴巴、比亚迪、百度及其他中国科技巨头列入军事黑名单

诺基亚杭州研发中心的一名员工也受到了最新一轮裁员的影响，他证实该公司正在缩减其在中国的大部分业务。

诺基亚从中国的撤退原本是渐进式的，但近期加速了。上周，电信行业刊物《Light Reading》报道称，诺基亚将关闭其在杭州的研发设施，导致约1600个工作岗位流失。

诺基亚向南华早报证实了关闭杭州基地的消息，并发表声明称：“诺基亚一直在采取措施，使其在中国的运营更好地与诺基亚的全球运营模式保持一致。”

当被问及诺基亚是否计划在年底前逐步淘汰并关闭其在中国的所有站点，以及解雇所有在华员工并仅保留有限的售后团队时，该公司没有直接证实或否认这一说法。

诺基亚发言人向南华早报回应称：“此外，诺基亚在中国的业务在过去几年中持续下滑。因此，我们正在调整在中国的运营布局，以应对这一现实。”

其中一名上海员工表示，近年来政府机构和国有企业越来越青睐国产技术和设备，给诺基亚的电信产品留下了更少空间。该人士还补充说，来自私营企业客户的业务也在缩减。

财报显示，诺基亚在中国的营收从2019年的18.4亿欧元（21.2亿美元）降至去年的9.13亿欧元，同期该地区占集团营收的比例从7.9%下降至4.6%。

![The front of a Nokia booth during the WAIC conference 2026 in Shanghai, China. Photo: Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b14810e8955c58da0a2c236/3c081cc9-0b14-81fa-9153-c2d40d47d98a-50ef167ec131bb63ab75508ea02cbbe3.jpg)

2026年世界人工智能大会（WAIC）期间，中国上海诺基亚展台前。图片来源：Getty Images

诺基亚于1985年进入中国，作为电信设备供应商在北京开设了首个办事处。20世纪90年代，它深度参与了中国网络建设，并后来成为中国最受欢迎的手机品牌之一。

2010年，诺基亚在中国营收达到76.2亿欧元的峰值，当时中国是诺基亚全球最大的单一国家市场。随着手机市场竞争加剧，诺基亚于2014年将其设备与服务业务的大部分出售给微软，电信网络设备成为其在华业务的核心。

然而，在中国5G基础设施建设过程中，诺基亚的业务日益被边缘化，并输给了华为技术和中兴通讯等国内企业。

此次最新撤退是在诺基亚对中国业务进行更广泛重组之后进行的，此前该公司于去年底全面收购了其与前国有背景合作伙伴中国华信合资的上海诺基亚贝尔（Nokia Shanghai Bell）的全部股权。

诺基亚在最新财报中表示，随着将中国业务并入全球运营结构，预计到2026年底将产生3.5亿欧元的整合费用，目标是节省约2亿欧元的成本。根据同一份财报，该公司现在的目标是在两年内完成整合，比最初计划的两到三年更快。

诺基亚加入了越来越多的外国科技公司行列，这些公司在中国缩减了曾经庞大的业务，原因是当地竞争加剧、商业回报减弱以及地缘政治障碍日益增加。

IBM于2024年[关闭了其在中国的主要研发部门](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3300579/ibm-shuts-china-research-arm-after-lay-offs-and-revenue-decline)，影响超过1000名员工；而微软在过去五年中关闭了分支机构和合资企业，并将一些云和人工智能研究人员转移到海外。亚马逊在早些时候退出电子商务和电子书之后，[去年关闭了其上海人工智能实验室](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-war/article/3319310/amazon-web-services-close-shanghai-ai-lab-amid-us-china-tech-tensions)。

## Exclusive | Nokia to close almost all sites in mainland China by year end, sources say

## ***Exclusive*****Nokia to close almost all sites in mainland China by year end, sources say**

#### Retreating from China, once Nokia’s largest single-country market globally, would mark an acquiescence to domestic competition

[**Minxiao Chang**](https://www.scmp.com/author/minxiao-chang)**in Shenzhenand**[**Wency Chen**](https://www.scmp.com/author/wency-chen)**in Shanghai**

Published: 9:00pm, 18 Aug 2026Updated: 9:05pm, 18 Aug 2026

Finnish telecoms equipment giant Nokia plans to cut most of its workforce in mainland China and close sites in stages by year end, marking a sweeping retreat after more than four decades as it loses ground to domestic rivals, according to sources familiar with the situation.

One Shanghai-based source said teams in both Nokia’s mobile networks and network infrastructure businesses in mainland China were set to undergo lay-offs in batches, with most of the cuts expected to be completed by the end of this year.

While the total number of employees who would be affected remains unclear, Nokia had about 7,200 staff in mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan at the end of 2025, its annual report showed, with sites in cities such as Beijing, Shanghai, Hangzhou, Chengdu and Qingdao.

The sources said Nokia announced its plan to scale back mainland China operations earlier this month during an internal online video conference from its Helsinki headquarters.

Only after-sales services would remain in China, which would constitute “basically a gradual exit from the market”, one person said.

A Shanghai-based former Nokia employee who had worked in its software testing division said he left the company in the past few weeks, receiving an “N+3” severance package – compensation based on years of service, “N”, plus three months’ pay.

The source said that some employees in the country had already transferred to Nokia’s headquarters in Finland earlier this year.

An employee at Nokia’s Hangzhou research and development centre, who was also affected by the latest round of cuts, confirmed the company was winding down much of its China operations.

Nokia’s retreat from China, which had been gradual, accelerated recently. Last week, telecoms industry publication Light Reading reported that Nokia would close its R&D facility in Hangzhou, resulting in the loss of about 1,600 jobs.

Nokia confirmed the closure of the Hangzhou site to the South China Morning Post, with a statement saying: “Nokia has been taking steps to better align its operations in China with Nokia’s global mode of operation.”

When asked whether Nokia planned to phase out and close all of its sites in China by the end of the year, as well as lay off all China-based employees and retain only a limited after-sales team, the company did not directly confirm or deny the claim.

“Further, Nokia’s business in China has steadily declined over the last several years. Thus, we are adjusting our operational footprint in China to address this reality,” a Nokia spokesperson said in the same response to the SCMP.

One of the Shanghai employees said government agencies and state-owned companies had increasingly favoured domestic technology and equipment in recent years, leaving less room for Nokia’s telecoms products. Business from private-sector enterprise customers had also shrunk, the person added.

Nokia’s revenue in China fell from €1.84 billion (US$2.12 billion) in 2019 to €913 million last year, while the region’s share of group revenue dropped from 7.9 per cent to 4.6 per cent over the same period, earnings showed.

The front of a Nokia booth during the WAIC conference 2026 in Shanghai, China. Photo: Getty Images

Nokia entered China in 1985, opening its first office in Beijing as a telecoms equipment supplier. It became deeply involved in the country’s network buildout during the 1990s and later emerged as one of the country’s most popular mobile phone brands.

Revenue in China peaked at €7.62 billion in 2010, when China was Nokia’s largest single-country market globally. As competition in mobile phones intensified, Nokia sold most of its Devices & Services subsidiary to Microsoft in 2014, leaving telecoms network equipment as the core of its operations in China.

However, its business was increasingly sidelined during the buildout of 5G infrastructure in China, and Nokia lost ground to domestic players such as Huawei Technologies and ZTE.

The latest retreat follows a broader restructuring of Nokia’s China operations after the company took full ownership late last year of Nokia Shanghai Bell, its former joint venture with state-backed China Huaxin.

Nokia said in its latest earnings that it expected to see €350 million in integration charges by the end of 2026 as it folds the China business into its global operating structure, targeting about €200 million in cost savings. It now aims to complete the integration within two years, faster than the two to three years initially planned, according to the same earnings report.

Nokia joins a growing list of foreign technology companies that have scaled back once-substantial operations in China amid intensifying local competition, weaker commercial returns and growing geopolitical barriers.

IBM [closed its main China research and development units](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3300579/ibm-shuts-china-research-arm-after-lay-offs-and-revenue-decline) in 2024, affecting more than 1,000 employees, while Microsoft has closed branches and joint ventures over the past five years and moved some cloud and artificial-intelligence researchers overseas. Amazon [closed its Shanghai AI lab last year](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-war/article/3319310/amazon-web-services-close-shanghai-ai-lab-amid-us-china-tech-tensions) after earlier retreats from e-commerce and e-books.
