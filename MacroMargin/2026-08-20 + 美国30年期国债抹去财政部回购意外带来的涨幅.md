### 中文译文

## 美国30年期国债抹去财政部回购意外带来的涨幅

作者：[Michael MacKenzie](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVgVy7_JF34/michael-mackenzie) 和 [Alice Gledhill](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUFfTWYF4c4/alice-gledhill)
2026年8月20日下午5:23 GMT+8 | *更新于2026年8月20日下午10:19 GMT+8*

美国国债几乎抹去了特朗普政府意外决定增加较长期债券回购后录得的全部涨幅，表明此举对缓解市场对政府债务激增的担忧几乎没有帮助，这种担忧已将部分收益率推高至近二十年来的高点。

周四，[30年期收益率](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG30YR%20Index)上涨超过7个基点，最高达到5.27%，接近周三早些时候美国财政部发布公告前的水平，随后涨幅收窄。[10年期收益率](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG10YR%20Index)一度触及4.71%，略低于2025年初以来的最高水平。

这些跳涨凸显出市场对财政部长斯科特·贝森特（Scott Bessent）的措施能否对债券市场产生持久影响持怀疑态度。债券市场收益率一直在上涨，原因是市场担心近2万亿美元的预算赤字、通胀上升以及投资人工智能的科技公司大量发债。

贝森特试图通过更依赖短期借款来弥补政府资金缺口，从而抵消这些影响，使其得以保持较长期债券销售规模稳定。周三，他所在的部门表示，将“至少”把10至30年期到期债券的回购规模增加一倍，这为国债市场提供了短暂的提振，外界猜测其可能会缩减这些证券的新发行规模。

道明证券（TD Securities）美国利率策略师莫莉·布鲁克斯（Molly Brooks）表示：“财政部需要更明确地传达降低长期债券拍卖规模的信息，才能使利率下行走势得以持续，在昨日看到财政部介入的意愿后，这种情况更有可能发生。”

周四早些时候债券抛售达到顶峰，同时油价攀升超过3%。布鲁克斯表示，收益率上升的很大一部分“实际上是由油价推动的”，再加上其他基本面因素，“确实表明回购政策的转变更像是财政部为长期债券市场打的一个补丁。”

贝森特最新的[干预措施](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades)暴露了华盛顿方面对政府借贷成本上升日益感到不安，这正在11月国会选举前推高抵押贷款和其他贷款的利息支出。特朗普政府曾声称，一旦其财政计划遏制了联邦支出和通胀，利率就会下降，但这两点均未实现，收益率已升至唐纳德·特朗普总统重返白宫前的水平之上。

伊朗战争引发的石油冲击几乎没有结束的迹象，就在市场对美联储主席凯文·沃什（Kevin Warsh）遏制自2021年以来一直高于央行目标的通胀的承诺表示怀疑之际，这场冲击正波及整个经济。实际收益率（即经通胀调整后的收益率）的上升表明，投资者要求更高的溢价来购买政府债务，以补偿不断积累的风险。

“鉴于赤字规模以及人工智能带来的部分挤出效应，这相当于向海啸中投掷纸巾，”DWS美洲固定收益主管乔治·卡特兰博内（George Catrambone）表示。“这不会阻止财政状况并压低收益率。”

三菱日联金融集团（MUFG）美国宏观策略主管乔治·贡萨尔维斯（George Goncalves）也表达了同样的观点：“这种干预是渐进的和战术性的，只是想减缓长期债券的抛售，并试图争取时间，然后观察经济和美联储的反应。预算赤字是无限期的，而且不会停止。”

[富兰克林坦伯顿](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BEN%20US%20Equity)和[野村控股公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/8604%20JP%20Equity)等公司也表示，这一宣布的积极影响（已波及全球市场）预计将有限。英国和大多数欧洲国家的收益率周四小幅走高，维持在近日触及的多年高位附近。

富兰克林坦伯顿固定收益董事安德鲁·卡诺比（Andrew Canobi）表示，存在一种“推动收益率上升和收益率曲线趋陡的力量的同步性”，最大的发达市场都面临财政压力和顽固的通胀。“只要这些力量占主导地位，我看不到长期债券会有太多买盘。”

## US 30-Year Bonds Erase Gains From Treasury’s Buyback Surprise

By [Michael MacKenzie](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVgVy7_JF34/michael-mackenzie) and [Alice Gledhill](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUFfTWYF4c4/alice-gledhill)
August 20, 2026 at 5:23 PM GMT+8 | *Updated on August 20, 2026 at 10:19 PM GMT+8*

US Treasuries erased nearly all of the gains that followed the Trump administration’s surprise decision to increase buybacks of longer-dated bonds, signaling the move has done little to alleviate the angst about the surging government debt that has pushed some yields to a nearly two-decade high.

The [30-year yield](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG30YR%20Index) on Thursday rose over seven basis points to as much as 5.27%, where it was just ahead of the US Treasury Department’s announcement early Wednesday, before paring the gain. The [10-year yield](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG10YR%20Index) touched 4.71%, just shy of its highest level since early 2025.

The jumps highlight the skepticism that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s moves will have a lasting impact on the bond market, where yields have been rising on concerns about the nearly $2 trillion budget deficit, elevated inflation and a flood of debt from tech companies investing in artificial intelligence.

Bessent has sought to offset the impacts by leaning more heavily on short-term borrowing to cover the government’s shortfall, allowing him to keep the size of longer-term bond sales steady. On Wednesday, his department said it would “at least” double buybacks of bonds maturing from 10 to 30 years, which provided a fleeing boost to the Treasury market, and there has been speculation it could scale-back new sales of the securities.

“Treasury will need to more firmly communicate long-end auction size decreases to have the move lower in rates to be sustained, which is more likely after seeing Treasury’s willingness to step in yesterday,” said Molly Brooks, US rates strategist at [TD Securities](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/7606355Z:US).

The peak of the bond selloff early Thursday was accompanied by oil prices climbing more than 3%. Brooks said the bulk of the rise in yields “is actually driven by oil,” and that together with other fundamental drivers “does point to the idea that the shift in buybacks is more of a Band-Aid for Treasury’s long-end.”

Bessent’s latest [intervention](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades) betrayed Washington’s growing unease with the government’s elevated borrowing costs, which is pushing up interest bills on mortgages and other loans ahead of the November Congressional elections. The administration once claimed that rates would come down once its fiscal plans tamed federal spending and inflation, but it has done neither and yields have risen above where they were before President Donald Trump returned to the White House.

The oil shock from the Iran war, which shows few signs of ending, is rippling through the economy just as the markets are showing doubts about Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh’s commitment to reining in inflation that’s held above the central bank’s target since 2021. The rise in real, or inflation-adjusted, yields suggests that investors are demanding a higher premium to buy the government’s debt and compensate for the building risks.

“Given the size of the deficit and some crowding out effect that AI has had, this is the equivalent of tossing paper towel into a tsunami,” said George Catrambone, head of fixed income at DWS Americas. “It’s not going to stop the fiscal situation and bring lower yields.”

George Goncalves, head of US macro strategy at MUFG echoed that sentiment: “This intervention is incremental and tactical, just trying to slow down the long-end selling and an attempt to buy time and then see how the economy and the Fed reacts. There is an open-ended budget deficit and that doesn’t stop. ”

Firms like [Franklin Templeton](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BEN%20US%20Equity) and [Nomura Holdings Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/8604%20JP%20Equity) also said the positive effect of the announcement — which spilled into global markets — was expected to be limited. Rates in the UK and most European nations edged higher on Thursday, hovering around the multi-year peaks reached in recent days.

There’s a “synchronicity of forces arguing for higher yields, steeper yield curves,” with the largest developed markets all facing fiscal pressures and stubborn inflation, said Andrew Canobi, a director of fixed income at Franklin Templeton. “I can’t see the longer end finding too much of a bid as long as those forces are prevailing.”
