### 中文译文

## 特朗普威胁削减美韩同盟核心军演，首尔不安

到黎明时分，他们已准备就绪。从庞大的汉弗莱营指挥中心，到戒备森严的朝鲜边境附近的空军基地，数千名美韩人员已准备好与一个拥有核武器的对手进行模拟冲突。

但在周一的演习原定开始前数小时，唐纳德·特朗普向美韩同盟发出了冲击波，宣布他已命令五角大楼“[大幅减少](https://www.ft.com/content/8889375a-bcaa-4539-a000-c89b73dbcf4c?syn-25a6b1a6=1)”这些演习，并援引他与朝鲜领导人金正恩的“非常好的关系”。

美国总统通过Truth Social帖子发出的意外抨击，震动了[韩国](https://www.ft.com/south-korea)的安全机构，加深了首尔对其最重要安全伙伴可靠性的担忧。

分析人士表示，美国和韩国自1976年以来一直举行的“乙支自由之盾（Ulchi Freedom Shield）”等大型演习，是同盟为保持对朝军事准备所做努力的基石。今年的演习原定在11天内进行14项训练，涵盖无人机攻击、GPS干扰以及电子和网络战。

虽然首尔和华盛顿会公开否认这对备战有任何影响，但韩国风险集团的约翰·李表示，“在私下里，人们将开始提出令人不安的问题。”

与此同时，首尔韩国外国语大学国际政治学教授梅森·里奇表示，特朗普的言论“听起来像是在照搬平壤的论调”。

美国总统表示，这些演习对朝鲜是“不恰当且充满敌意的”，而朝鲜被视为一个“不具威胁且尊重他人”的国家。特朗普还表示，他的决定与首尔拒绝支持美国对伊朗的军事行动“有些关系”——这进一步向首尔施压。

“这只是又一个例子，表明美国作为盟友目前是多么不可靠，无论是对亚洲还是欧洲的盟友。”里奇补充道。

![. . . and are seen as a crucial part of military readiness against North Korea © Ahn Young-joon/AP](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c181cc90b1481919405f235dc748701/3c181cc9-0b14-81f1-9d20-e04f94083201-1de9a520ca1c80db1f720ca027406233.png)

演习于周一按计划开始，华盛顿和首尔均未说明哪些部分可能被削减。但特朗普的干预只是自朝鲜战争以来一直存在的同盟所面临的一系列考验中的最新一次。

今年3月，五角大楼将萨德导弹防御系统（韩国防空的重要一环）的[部分设施](https://www.ft.com/content/84349890-7a46-43b2-9987-a27e9e1f5d4e?syn-25a6b1a6=1)从韩国迁出，因为美国将资源转向中东。

几周前，首尔曾抱怨称，美国举行的一次涉及乌山空军基地F-16战机飞近中国防空识别区的演习，韩方未得到充分通报。

韩国风险集团的Lee指出，削减后的演习将与美国“乔治·华盛顿”号航母打击群重新部署至中东的时间重合，这将使“在可预见的未来，西太平洋没有美国航母”。

经济关系也趋于紧张。据多名知情人士透露，特朗普政府官员对首尔在为换取较低关税而承诺对美投资的[3500亿美元投资](https://www.ft.com/content/435698ba-ef81-4722-ab52-50b886e557bd?syn-25a6b1a6=1)确定项目方面的拖延越来越感到失望。

![. . . with South Korea’s President Lee Jae Myung, left, or US President Donald Trump © Evelyn Hockstein/Reuters](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c181cc90b1481919405f235dc748701/3c181cc9-0b14-81a7-b460-e24316126269-013d357724123f81c9ccd0ee42ba6a6d.png)

分析人士称，特朗普此举可能是重启与平壤外交的试探性举动，这是他第一任期一个未实现的重大优先事项。朝鲜长期以来一直认为联合演习尤其具有威胁性，上周更将“乙支自由护盾”演习描述为“侵略战争预演”。

上周六，特朗普发布了一张他与金正恩的合影，并宣称两人“相处得非常好”。今年6月在法国举行的七国集团(G7)峰会上，特朗普告诉韩国总统李在明，他希望重新与平壤接触。

特朗普周一表示，金正恩已回应了进行对话的接触努力，但没有提供任何细节。

“通过缩减这些[演习]的规模，特朗普似乎是在伸出橄榄枝，以吸引金正恩，”战略与国际研究中心韩国事务主席维克托·查表示。特朗普在2018年与金正恩举行峰会时同样暂停了联合演习，那是两人三次会晤中的第一次。

李一直寻求与平壤重新开启对话，这个周末利用纪念日本殖民统治结束的年度光复节讲话，重申与朝鲜“和平共处”的呼吁。

韩国总统办公室青瓦台周一表示，希望特朗普的言论能促成美朝重新对话。

金正恩与唐纳德·特朗普在特朗普首个总统任期内曾会面三次，但未取得外交突破。© Kim Hong-Ji/路透社

![Trump has also put pressure on South Korea to support the US war against Iran © Kim Dohyeon/Zuma/Reuters](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c181cc90b1481919405f235dc748701/3c181cc9-0b14-814a-b109-fe819a8fd0d2-013d357724123f81c9ccd0ee42ba6a6d.png)

在特朗普与金正恩频繁峰会以来的七年里，朝鲜扩充了核武器和常规武器库，深化了与俄罗斯的军事和经济[合作](https://www.ft.com/content/294eed45-9e16-434e-81dd-5903e2a5657e?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，并在乌克兰获得了宝贵的现代战场经验。

查表示：“鉴于俄罗斯和中国的全力支持，金正恩现在没有迫切需要会面。”他还补充说，这位朝鲜领导人可能认为在11月美国中期选举后将拥有更好的筹码。

即使金正恩重返谈判桌，首尔也可能被排除在外。平壤一直拒绝李重启朝韩对话的努力。平壤还[修订了宪法](https://www.ft.com/content/7d795bbc-7c8e-46c9-8705-2f06c58eb961?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，正式放弃与韩国统一的目标，现在将韩国视为一个独立的敌对国。

首尔世宗研究所研究员崔恩珠（Choi Eun-joo）表示：“韩国需要做好准备，应对与其安全直接相关的问题，如联合军事演习或战略资产部署，可能在未来美朝对话中被用作讨价还价的筹码。”

最新事件似乎已促使韩国寻求在自身防务中发挥更积极的作用。

李周二呼吁加快取得[核动力潜艇](https://www.ft.com/content/4e00ded7-109a-45fe-963c-f55a1caec08f)和移交韩国军队战时作战指挥权的进展，他承诺在2030年总统任期结束前实现这些目标。

“随着我们增强自身能力，我们作为盟友的价值和重要性也会增长，’他说。”

“重要的不仅仅是联合演习的缩减，而是作出决定的背景和方式，’崔说。‘我们需要拭目以待，看这一决定是预先计划政策的结果，还是一时冲动的判断。”

## Seoul rattled by Trump’s threat to military drills at heart of US alliance

By dawn, they were ready. From command centres at the sprawling Camp Humphreys to air bases near the heavily fortified North Korean border, thousands of US and South Korean personnel were poised for simulated conflict with a nuclear-armed adversary.

But hours before the drills were set to begin on Monday, Donald Trump sent a shockwave through the US-South Korean alliance, announcing that he had ordered the Pentagon to “[substantially reduce](https://www.ft.com/content/8889375a-bcaa-4539-a000-c89b73dbcf4c?syn-25a6b1a6=1)” them, and citing his “very good relationship” with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un.

The US president’s unexpected salvo via Truth Social post rattled [South Korea](https://www.ft.com/south-korea)’s security establishment, deepening concerns in Seoul about the reliability of its most important security partner.

Analysts said major exercises such as Ulchi Freedom Shield, which the US and South Korea have been holding since 1976, are a cornerstone of the allies’ efforts to maintain military readiness against North Korea. This year’s drills were set to include 14 exercises over 11 days, ranging from drone attacks and GPS jamming to electronic and cyber warfare.

While Seoul and Washington would publicly deny any impact on that readiness, “in private”, said John Lee of Korea Risk Group, “people will begin to ask uncomfortable questions”.

Meanwhile, Trump’s language “sounds like he is parroting Pyongyang’s line”, said Mason Richey, professor of international politics at Hankuk University of Foreign Studies in Seoul.

The US president said the drills were “inappropriate and hostile” to North Korea, a country deemed “unthreatening and respectful”. Trump also said his decision was “somewhat related” to Seoul’s refusal to support US military action against Iran — piling further pressure on Seoul.

“This is just another example of how unreliable the US is as an ally right now, whether that be to allies in Asia or Europe,” Richey added.

The exercises began as scheduled on Monday and neither Washington nor Seoul specified which parts could be cut. But Trump’s intervention was just the latest in a series of tests to the alliance that has lasted since the Korean war.

In March, the Pentagon [relocated parts](https://www.ft.com/content/84349890-7a46-43b2-9987-a27e9e1f5d4e?syn-25a6b1a6=1) of the Thaad missile defence system — an important layer of South Korea’s air defences — from the country as it shifted resources towards the Middle East.

Weeks earlier, Seoul had complained it had not been fully briefed about a US exercise involving F-16s from Osan Air Base flying close to China’s air-defence identification zone.

Lee of Korea Risk Group noted the reduced drills would coincide with the redeployment of the USS George Washington strike group to the Middle East, which would leave “the western Pacific without an American aircraft carrier for the foreseeable future”.

Economic ties have also been strained. Trump officials are increasingly frustrated by what they see as Seoul’s foot-dragging in identifying projects for the [$350bn of investments](https://www.ft.com/content/435698ba-ef81-4722-ab52-50b886e557bd?syn-25a6b1a6=1) it pledged to make in the US in exchange for lower tariffs, according to several people familiar with the situation.

Analysts said Trump’s move could be an overture to revive diplomacy with Pyongyang, a major unfulfilled priority of his first term. North Korea has long seen the joint exercises as particularly threatening, and last week described Ulchi Freedom Shield as “a rehearsal for a war of aggression”.

On Saturday, Trump posted a photo of himself with Kim, declaring that the two “get along GREAT”. At the G7 summit in France in June, Trump told South Korean President Lee Jae Myung that he wanted to renew engagement with Pyongyang.

Trump on Monday said Kim had responded to outreach to have a conversation without providing any details.

“By scaling these [exercises] down, Trump appears to be extending an olive branch to draw Kim in,” said Victor Cha, Korea chair at the Center for Strategic and International Studies. Trump similarly suspended joint drills that coincided with his summit with Kim in 2018, the first of three meetings between the pair.

Lee has sought to reopen dialogue with Pyongyang and this weekend used an annual liberation day address marking the end of Japanese colonial rule to reiterate calls for “peaceful coexistence” with North Korea.

South Korea’s president’s office, the Blue House, on Monday said it hoped Trump’s comments would lead to renewed US-North Korean talks.

Kim Jong Un and Donald Trump met three times during the US president’s first term but failed to yield a diplomatic breakthrough © Kim Hong-Ji/Reuters

In the seven years since the flurry of Trump-Kim summitry, North Korea has expanded its arsenal of nuclear and conventional weapons, deepened military and economic [co-operation with Russia](https://www.ft.com/content/294eed45-9e16-434e-81dd-5903e2a5657e?syn-25a6b1a6=1) and gained valuable modern battlefield experience in Ukraine.

“Kim has no compelling need to meet now given all the support from Russia and China,” said Cha, who added that the North Korean leader may believe he will have better leverage after US midterm elections in November.

Even if Kim returns to the negotiating table, Seoul could be frozen out. Pyongyang has consistently rebuffed Lee’s efforts to restart inter-Korean dialogue. It has also [revised its constitution](https://www.ft.com/content/7d795bbc-7c8e-46c9-8705-2f06c58eb961?syn-25a6b1a6=1) to formally drop the aim of reunification with South Korea, which it now treats as a separate, hostile state.

Choi Eun-joo, researcher at the Sejong Institute in Seoul, said: “South Korea needs to prepare for the possibility that issues directly related to its security, such as joint military exercises or the deployment of strategic assets, could be used as bargaining chips during future US-North Korea dialogue.”

The latest episode already appeared to be driving South Korea to pursue a more active role in its own defence.

Lee on Tuesday called for faster progress towards acquiring [nuclear-powered submarines](https://www.ft.com/content/4e00ded7-109a-45fe-963c-f55a1caec08f) and transferring wartime operational control of South Korean forces, which he has pledged to achieve before his presidency ends in 2030.

“Our value and importance as an ally will grow as we strengthen our own capabilities,” he said.

“What matters is not just the reduction of the joint exercises, but the background and manner in which the decision was made,” said Choi. “We need to wait and see whether this decision is the result of a pre-planned policy or an impulsive judgment.”

*Additional reporting by Demetri Sevastopulo in Washington*
