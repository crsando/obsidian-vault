### 中文译文

## 中国生物技术公司基因编辑试验中一名儿童死亡

一名患有[杜氏肌营养不良症](https://www.mda.org/disease/duchenne-muscular-dystrophy)的儿童在中国接受基因编辑治疗后死亡，这引发了对监管监督和透明度的新担忧，因为中国正寻求巩固其作为生物技术领导者的地位。

周三，该疗法的开发商、总部位于上海的HuidaGene Therapeutics在一份[声明](https://www.huidagene.com/new/news/82)中，将2025年8月的死亡事件归因于急性呼吸窘迫综合征，这是该疗法引发的严重免疫反应的一部分。尽管公司没有确认或提供患者细节，但临床试验[记录](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06594094)显示，该研究仅招募了4至8岁的男孩。

HuidaGene的披露是在STAT News进行了数月之久的[调查](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/05/china-clinical-trial-fatality-safety-questions-investigator-led-studies/)之后。该公司未立即回应有关该疗法后续步骤的问题，电话也无法联系到。

该公告发布数周前，Science[披露](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/patient-death-in-china-gene-editing-trial-draws-fire-science)了一项先前未报告的年轻女孩在另一项研究者发起的试验（IIT）中死亡的事件，这类研究由研究人员而非制药公司主导。中国有关部门和与该研究相关的一所上海大学已展开调查。

这些案例凸显了中国IIT体系的不透明性，该体系允许大医院的研究人员在没有国家药品监管机构批准的情况下试验新型疗法。近年来此类研究激增，推动了尖端细胞和基因疗法的发展，进而助长了中国的[生物技术热潮](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-07-13/china-drugmakers-catching-up-to-us-big-pharma-with-new-medicine-innovation)。但这些死亡事件可能会加剧对其监管、安全报告和公众披露是否跟得上行业快速发展的审视。

该患者是接受HuidaGene的HG302疗法的第四位也是最后一位参与者，该疗法使用被称为腺相关病毒（AAV）载体的改良病毒将DNA编辑组件递送到细胞中。该患者还接受了高剂量治疗。高剂量AAV给药已在世界各地的其他临床试验中引起致命免疫反应，包括患有杜氏肌营养不良症的男孩。

HuidaGene表示，这一致命事件已通过医院的伦理和研究监督流程报告，“在研究中心知晓后适用的报告时限内，并遵循《药物临床试验质量管理规范》。”公司表示，分析已于1月提交同行评审，结果将在发表后公布。

### English Original

## Child Died in Chinese Biotech Firm’s Gene Editing Trial

A child with [Duchenne muscular dystrophy](https://www.mda.org/disease/duchenne-muscular-dystrophy) died after receiving a gene-editing therapy in China, sparking new concerns about regulatory oversight and transparency as China seeks to cement its position as a biotechnology leader.

In a [statement](https://www.huidagene.com/new/news/82) Wednesday, the treatment’s developer, Shanghai-based HuidaGene Therapeutics, attributed the August 2025 death to acute respiratory distress syndrome as part of a severe immune reaction to the therapy. Though the company didn’t identify or provide details about the patient, clinical trial [records](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06594094) show the study only enrolled 4- to 8-year-old boys.

HuidaGene’s disclosure follows a months long [investigation](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/05/china-clinical-trial-fatality-safety-questions-investigator-led-studies/) by STAT News. The firm did not immediately respond to questions on next steps for the therapy, and could not be reached by phone.

The announcement also comes weeks after Science [revealed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/patient-death-in-china-gene-editing-trial-draws-fire-science) the previously unreported death of a young girl in a separate investigator-initiated trial (IIT), a type of study led by researchers rather than pharmaceutical companies. Chinese authorities and the Shanghai university affiliated with the study have since launched probes.

The cases highlight the opaque nature of China’s IIT system, which permits researchers at major hospitals to test novel therapies without approval from the national drug regulator. Such studies have surged in recent years, helping accelerate the development of cutting-edge cell and gene therapies that are fueling China’s [biotech boom](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2025-07-13/china-drugmakers-catching-up-to-us-big-pharma-with-new-medicine-innovation). But the deaths are likely to intensify scrutiny of whether oversight, safety reporting and public disclosure have kept pace with the sector’s rapid growth.

The patient was the fourth and last participant to receive HuidaGene’s HG302, which uses modified viruses known as adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors to deliver DNA-editing components to cells. The patient also received a high dose. High-dose AAV administration has caused fatal immune reactions in other clinical trials around the world, including in young boys with Duchenne.

HuidaGene said the fatal event was reported through the hospital’s ethics and study-oversight processes “within the applicable reporting time frame after the site became aware of it, in accordance with Good Clinical Practice.” The analysis was submitted for peer review in January and findings will be shared upon publication, the company said.
