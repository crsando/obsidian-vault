### 中文译文

## 中国的特朗普红利能持续多久？

![Illustration: Carmen Casado for Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b1481a787b8f7e683977265/3ca81cc9-0b14-81b1-8fb5-f2e7ad3cc694-cb91011df7d9336e689acca0d2505bb3.png)

*插图：Carmen Casado for Bloomberg*

从印度到欧洲，各国都在寻求从华盛顿获得更大的自主权。但对北京的深度不信任使它们仍锚定在西方主导的秩序中。

### **要点**

- 皮尤研究中心的一项研究显示，在接受调查的36个国家中，中国现在在25个国家的形象比美国更正面，中国国家主席习近平在多个国家比特朗普更受信任。
- 尽管舆论发生这种转变，但许多国家出于对中国政治和法律制度的担忧，正在对冲风险并分散与美国和中国的关系，而非转而效忠北京。
- 美国仍是许多国家至关重要的长期伙伴，包括印度。印度正在追求战略自主，并加强与美国其他盟友的关系，同时与中国和其他国家接触，以减少对任何单一国家的依赖。
在整个北京，很难找到比唐纳德·特朗普更快提升中国形象的人。

皮尤研究中心上月[发布](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/07/15/people-in-many-countries-now-view-china-more-positively-than-the-u-s/)的一项研究显示，在接受调查的36个国家中，中国现在在25个国家的形象比美国更正面——与2023年相比出现[巨大转变](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2023/11/06/comparing-views-of-the-us-and-china-in-24-countries/#favorable-views-of-the-us-and-china)，当时美国在除少数几个国家外的所有国家都遥遥领先。即便在英国、加拿大、法国和澳大利亚等美国长期盟友中，中国国家主席习近平也比特朗普更受信任。

虽然这是一个惊人的里程碑，但并不令人意外。如果美国设法避免了因特朗普不分青红皂白的关税、夺取领土的威胁、新的移民限制以及带有“美国优先”虚张声势的日常侮辱而遭受声誉损失，那才会更令人侧目。加拿大总理马克·卡尼1月[警告](https://www.weforum.org/stories/forum-institutional/davos-2026-special-address-by-mark-carney-prime-minister-of-canada/)称，中等强国必须团结起来，以避免被掠夺性霸权国家吞并。

然而，皮尤调查也显示了世界转向中国的限度。在法国和德国等公众情绪转向北京的国家，中国的支持率在30%出头到30%中段——勉强足以盖过特朗普领导下的美国。在仍对美国持更积极看法的六个国家中，有四个——日本、韩国、印度和菲律宾——位于中国的家门口。与北京存在摩擦的另外两个地方——越南和台湾——未被纳入调查。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b1481a787b8f7e683977265/3ca81cc9-0b14-81f6-a3cd-d927b56825d7-6fc08bc46f1ffd68351ea5f27e513912.png)

中国周边的国家需要保护，以抵御领土掠夺、经济压力以及北京用来宣示控制权的其他灰色地带策略。尽管日本和韩国等国家正在增加自身防务投入，但除非进行破坏稳定的核威慑冲刺，美国仍然是唯一有效的制衡力量。

经济因素也发挥作用。即使关税上调，许多亚洲国家仍依赖美国作为重要的出口市场。中国目前占全球制成品的近三分之一——这是自二战以来美国之后未曾见过的工业实力[集中](https://english.mofcom.gov.cn/cms_files/filemanager/675731329/attach/202511/e6c9f4dc7f83439898999f8c3f4f52e8.pdf?fileName=China%26apos%3Bs%20Position%20on%20the%20So-called%20Excess%20Capacity%20Issue.pdf)——并在电动汽车、机器人和其他前沿行业占主导地位。这使得获取关键矿产和先进技术的非中国供应链不仅是战略对冲，也是经济上的必需。

还有一点就是透明度。美国的传统盟友和安全伙伴或许不再信任美国，但至少任何人都能实时看到其内部辩论，一条接一条的Truth Social帖子摆在那里。美国民主尽管承受压力，但维持着基本的公民自由和定期选举，国会和最高法院仍在某种程度上制约行政权力。相比之下，中国共产党仍然是一个黑箱。

即使美国盟友减少对华盛顿的整体依赖，他们也不会投入北京的怀抱。相反，他们通过与志同道合的伙伴进行贸易和防务协议来进行对冲，这一策略被称为“联盟多元化”。在涉及国家安全的核心问题上——包括先进技术、出口管制和防务伙伴关系——许多中等强国仍锚定在更广泛的美国主导体系中。

尽管习近平善于利用美国的过度扩张，避免采用特朗普首个任期内那种咄咄逼人的“战狼”外交，但中国政治和法律体系的不透明限制了北京能获取的地缘政治优势。公众舆论或许已转向中国，但战略上的靠拢却被证明难以撼动。

### **对冲而非转向**

去年在特朗普的毁灭性打击下正面受创的印度，为任何更广泛转向中国的举措的局限性提供了一个很有说服力的例证。

自冷战结束以来，美国历届领导人都积极拉拢印度以制衡中国，无视其与俄罗斯的密切军事关系以深化防务合作。进展往往异常缓慢，但华盛顿始终坚持这条路。

2025年5月印度与巴基斯坦军队发生冲突后，这种不稳定的平衡被打破。特朗普一再声称自己促成了停火，但印度总理纳伦德拉·莫迪强烈予以否认；如果莫迪承认屈服于外部压力，他将在国内面临政治风暴。几个月后，特朗普以印度购买俄罗斯石油为由，对印度征收了一些美国有史以来最高的关税，并收紧了技术工人的签证政策，对印度人造成了尤为严重的影响。

尽管如此，皮尤调查显示，在印度受访者中，美国仍比中国保持22个百分点的优势——这是在以色列和日本之后的第三大优势。或许更令人意外的是，印度官员仍然在很大程度上将美国视为关键的长期伙伴。

上个月访问德里时，随着季风降雨逐渐退去，迎来难得几天的晴朗蓝天，印度官员以清醒的现实主义态度与美国进行了讨论。多年来，官僚们一直在辩论印度能否信任美国。特朗普给了他们答案：不能。

然而，印度的回应并不是抛弃美国另寻伙伴，而是加强了印度长期以来对战略自主的追求。莫迪前往中国，并与习近平和俄罗斯的弗拉基米尔·普京进行了亲密会谈，还确保摄影师拍到三人手拉手、开怀大笑的照片。这位印度领导人将于下月在德里举行的金砖国家峰会上与习近平和普京再次会面。

然而，这两种关系都没有为印度提供替代华盛顿的简单选择。与中国的合作尤其存在明显局限：双方争议的喜马拉雅边界仍是爆发点，经济紧张持续存在。尽管印度需要从中国获取某些先进技术以促进制造业发展，并借助苹果公司加大在南亚生产iPhone的势头，但新德里的领导人仍对在经济上更加依赖中国心存警惕。而北京方面则几乎无意帮助印度成为可行的制造业替代选择。

印度与俄罗斯的密切关系可追溯至冷战时期，普京仍是廉价石油和武器的可靠供应者。尽管莫迪政府试图减少对莫斯科的依赖，但德里官员表示，印度永远不会像美国希望的那样大幅缩减这种关系。

与此同时，印度领导人意识到，俄罗斯的经济并非真正的增长引擎，印度的经济未来更取决于与西方的牢固关系。在莫迪政府内部，当前与特朗普政府的摩擦更多被视为两国关系在十年走向更紧密趋势中的一次异常，而两国的立场天然一致。

一位官员这样描述该战略：在加强与美国伙伴关系的同时，悄悄修复与美国的关系，而印度此前在能直接与“山姆大叔本人”对话时忽视了这些伙伴。过去一年左右，印度争相签署长期搁置的贸易协议，并与美国更广泛安全轨道上的多个国家加强防务合作。

自2025年4月特朗普首次以“解放日”关税震惊世界以来，印度已结束与欧盟的贸易谈判，并完成了与英国和新西兰的协议。印度还加强了与欧盟以及美国在亚洲的三个最坚定盟友——日本、澳大利亚和菲律宾——的防务关系。

“他是一个伟大的颠覆者，可能帮了我们大忙，”这位印度官员这样评价特朗普。“有些事情需要大规模洗牌，除非有这种混乱，否则很难做到。”

### **战略自主的艺术**

欧洲长期以来一直是对冲和多元化结盟的范例。最著名的可能是法国领导人夏尔·戴高乐对战略自主的[追求](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP79T00826A000400010062-3.pdf)，最终他在1966年[决定](https://www.nytimes.com/1966/02/22/archives/de-gaulle-insists-on-rule-of-bases-of-nato-in-france-says-allied.html)将法国从北约一体化军事指挥部中撤出，并迫使盟军离开法国。

身着双排扣西装，在涌入巴黎爱丽舍宫的1000多名记者面前，这位前将军表示，法国掌控自身命运的能力“与一个她发现自己处于从属地位的防务组织不相容。”他警告称，美国可能将北约国家拖入战争，并说欧洲将“自动卷入斗争，即使它并不希望如此。”

然而，戴高乐并未走到放弃西方联盟的地步——只是退出了各国汇集资源并作为一个整体运作的一体化指挥体系。他说：“这绝不是分裂，而是必要的调整。”一年后，北约将其总部从巴黎迁至布鲁塞尔。

法国一直置身于一体化指挥体系之外，直到总统尼古拉·萨科齐于2009年重新加入。在以第一次世界大战中指挥盟军的费迪南德·福煦元帅命名的巴黎礼堂发表讲话时，萨科齐表示，法国“宣称但未实现的与北约的疏远限制了我们的国家独立和回旋余地。”他说，有可能在成为美国盟友的同时，仍然保持“自己做决定的朋友、独立的盟友和自由的伙伴。”

转变的一个原因是认识到国家安全本身的性质已经改变。萨科齐说：“法国的防务现在既取决于我们的领土，也取决于数千公里之外、太空或IT网络。”

现任领导人埃马纽埃尔·马克龙更倾向于戴高乐阵营，主张法国战略自主，呼吁欧洲在防务上自力更生。今年1月在瑞士达沃斯世界经济论坛上，他批评了美国和中国，同时将法国标榜为抵御以“强权法则”和“附庸化”为特征的“新殖民做法”的堡垒。

尽管马克龙对特朗普批评不断，从关税到格陵兰岛，但他并未考虑退出北约。今年6月，他与另外四位欧洲领导人共同宣布，他们“致力于在北约内部发挥更强大的欧洲作用，让欧洲为我们共同的跨大西洋安全承担更大责任，并与美国密切协调。”

更大程度地摆脱华盛顿的独立也不意味着更多地迁就北京。尽管马克龙宣称自己并不“反华”，但他一直试图团结欧盟，在对华经济事务上采取更强硬的立场。在上个月与德国总理弗里德里希·默茨的联合新闻发布会上，马克龙警告称，欧洲正面临一场“[咄咄逼人的](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-17/germany-and-france-to-draw-up-roadmap-on-china-trade-imbalances)”贸易战，并指出法德两国在对华问题上“从未如此一致”。

### **旧联盟仍然重要**

尽管有许多关于中等强国开辟新道路的谈论，但传统的西方机构，如北约、七国集团和五眼联盟——一个由美国、英国、加拿大、澳大利亚和新西兰组成的情报共享组织——尽管受到特朗普的猛烈抨击，仍被证明是持久的。并且，新的集团仍在类似的地缘政治断层线上形成。

美国加大力度打破中国对关键矿产的主导地位，今年早些时候主办了一个有55个国家参加的会议，其中包括印度和法国，旨在“重塑”这些元素的全球市场。法国还利用其七国集团主席国身份，与加拿大、德国、意大利、日本、英国和美国等其他成员一道，[推进](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/en/presse-et-ressources/decouvrir-et-informer/actualites/declaration-des-chefs-d-etat-et-de-gouvernement-du-g7-sur-la-securisation-des-chaines-d)一个重叠的“关键矿产韧性与生产联盟”。

新兴的人工智能治理结构也反映了类似的裂痕。美国支持的[Pax Silica](https://www.state.gov/pax-silica)包括欧盟，以及大约二十多个其他成员；而中国倡导的[世界人工智能合作组织](https://eng.sectsco.org/20260719/2462053.html)有近40个签署国，其中包括俄罗斯和缅甸。只有哈萨克斯坦同时加入了这两个组织，这促使美国[据报道](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-tell-partners-they-must-pick-sides-ai-race-with-china-2026-08-14/)考虑要求Pax Silica成员在中美人工智能竞赛中选边站。

世界最终是否会分裂成两条独立的人工智能供应链仍是一个悬而未决的问题。目前硬件是最明显的分界线，美国施压盟友和伙伴，阻止中国获得最先进的芯片。北京通过发布更便宜、更易获取的开权重模型来反击美国对计算能力的限制，这帮助阿里巴巴集团控股有限公司的通义千问（Qwen）和月之暗面（Moonshot AI）的Kimi K3在全球（包括美国、欧洲和印度）赢得用户。

到目前为止，美国尚未直接禁止中国的模型，这一举动在技术上难以执行，而且可能适得其反。作为欧洲对标Anthropic和OpenAI等全球参与者的挑战者，Mistral AI已在其平台上接纳了中国的开权重模型，并[淡化](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2088000058910199921)了潜在的安全风险。

但是，尽管西方国家在争论开源模型所构成的威胁，对中国硬件的担忧却在与日俱增。由于担心中国可能窃取数据，美国联邦通信委员会最近加强了对一系列产品的限制，包括机器人、Wi-Fi路由器、数据中心组件和无人机。英国本月披露，其[海军无人机](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/uk-steps-up-scrutiny-of-defense-supply-chain-after-china-scare)的部分组件曾秘密向中国传输信号。

巨大且长期存在的信任赤字意味着，即使与美国结盟的伙伴寻求从中国的低成本、高科技产品中获益，它们仍对受制于中国保持警惕。也许有一天，中国的创新将提供如此决定性的优势，以至于与北京保持距离将变得代价过高。

在那之前，许多美国的伙伴很可能会继续锚定在西方主导的秩序中，即使它们正进行对冲并分散对华盛顿的依赖。一位印度官员将其比作一群投资者，他们把大量现金投入一只股票，却眼睁睁看着糟糕的管理层让股价下跌。没有人准备亏本抛售全部股份，而且他们仍然希望它能反弹。

## How Long Will China’s Trump Dividend Last?

*Illustration: Carmen Casado for Bloomberg*

From India to Europe, countries are seeking greater autonomy from Washington. But deep distrust of Beijing is keeping them anchored in the Western-led order.

### **Takeaways**

- A Pew Research Center study showed China is now viewed more positively than the US in 25 of the 36 nations surveyed, with Chinese President Xi Jinping more trusted than Trump in several countries.
- Despite this shift in public opinion, many countries are hedging and diversifying their relationships with the US and China, rather than switching allegiance to Beijing, due to concerns about China's political and legal systems.
- The US remains a crucial long-term partner for many countries, including India, which is pursuing strategic autonomy and strengthening ties with other US allies, while also engaging with China and other nations to reduce its dependence on any one country.
In all of Beijing, it would be hard to find anyone who’s improved China’s image faster than Donald Trump.

A Pew Research Center study [released](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/07/15/people-in-many-countries-now-view-china-more-positively-than-the-u-s/) last month showed China is now viewed more positively than the US in 25 of the 36 nations surveyed — a [dramatic swing from 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2023/11/06/comparing-views-of-the-us-and-china-in-24-countries/#favorable-views-of-the-us-and-china), when America was well ahead in all but a handful of countries. Chinese President Xi Jinping is more trusted than Trump even in the UK, Canada, France and Australia — all longtime US allies.

While a stunning milestone, it’s hardly a surprise. It would’ve been more head-turning had the US somehow managed to avoid a reputational hit from Trump’s indiscriminate tariffs, threats to seize territory, new immigration curbs and regular insults tinged with “America First” bravado. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney [warned](https://www.weforum.org/stories/forum-institutional/davos-2026-special-address-by-mark-carney-prime-minister-of-canada/) in January that middle powers must band together to avoid getting swallowed up by predatory hegemons.

Yet the Pew survey also showed the limits of how far the world will swing toward China. In nations such as France and Germany where public sentiment flipped to Beijing, China’s favorability ratings were in the low to mid-30s — barely enough to eclipse a Trump-led America. Of the six countries that still viewed the US more favorably, four — Japan, South Korea, India and the Philippines — sit on China’s doorstep. Vietnam and Taiwan, two other places sparring with Beijing, weren’t included.

Countries in China’s neighborhood need protection from territorial grabs, economic pressure and other gray-zone tactics Beijing uses to assert control. While nations including Japan and South Korea are investing more in their own defense, the US remains the only effective counterbalance short of a destabilizing sprint for a nuclear deterrent.

Economics also come into play. Even with higher tariffs, many Asian nations rely on the US as a vital export market. China now accounts for almost a third of the world’s manufactured goods — a concentration of industrial [power](https://english.mofcom.gov.cn/cms_files/filemanager/675731329/attach/202511/e6c9f4dc7f83439898999f8c3f4f52e8.pdf?fileName=China%26apos%3Bs%20Position%20on%20the%20So-called%20Excess%20Capacity%20Issue.pdf) unseen since the US after World War II — and dominates electric vehicles, robotics and other leading-edge industries. That makes access to non-Chinese supply chains for critical minerals and advanced technology not just a strategic hedge but also an economic necessity.

Then there’s simple transparency. Traditional American allies and security partners may no longer trust the US, but at least anyone can see its internal debates play out in real time, one Truth Social post after another. American democracy, while under stress, maintains basic civil liberties and regular elections, with Congress and the Supreme Court still restraining executive power, at least to some degree. The Chinese Communist Party, by contrast, remains a black box.

Even as US allies reduce overall reliance on Washington, they aren’t jumping into Beijing’s arms. Instead they’re hedging through trade and defense deals with like-minded partners, a strategy known as aligned diversification. On issues central to national security — including advanced technology, export controls and defense partnerships — many middle powers remain anchored in the broader US-led system.

Although Xi has done well to capitalize on US overreach, refraining from the aggressive “wolf warrior” diplomacy deployed during Trump’s first term, the opacity of China’s political and legal systems limits how much geopolitical advantage Beijing can extract. Public opinion may have shifted toward China, but strategic alignment is proving much harder to budge.

### **Hedging, Not Switching**

India, which took Trump’s wrecking ball on the chin last year, offers a telling example of the limits of any broader pivot toward China.

Since the end of the Cold War, successive US leaders have courted India as a counterweight to China, overlooking its close military ties with Russia to deepen defense cooperation. Progress was often painfully slow, but Washington stayed the course.

That uneasy balance collapsed after India’s military clashed with Pakistan in May 2025. Trump’s repeated claims that he brokered a ceasefire were strongly denied by Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who would’ve faced a political uproar at home if he acknowledged bowing to foreign pressure. A few months later, Trump hit India with some of the highest US tariffs over its purchases of Russian oil and made it harder for skilled workers to get visas, disproportionately affecting Indians.

Nevertheless, the Pew survey showed the US retained a 22-point edge over China among Indian respondents — the third-biggest after Israel and Japan. Perhaps even more surprising is the extent to which officials in India still see the US as a crucial long-term partner.

Pakistani Rangers (L) and Indian Border Security Force (BSF) personnel take part in the beating retreat ceremony at the Attari-Wagah border on May 20, 2025. Photographer: Narinder Nanu/Getty Images

On a visit to Delhi last month, as the monsoon rains gave way to a rare few days of clear blue skies, Indian officials discussed the US with a sober realism. For years bureaucrats had debated whether India could trust the US. Trump gave them their answer: No.

The response, however, was not to abandon the US for another partner but to reinforce India’s long-standing pursuit of strategic autonomy. Modi headed to China and held chummy talks with Xi and Russia’s Vladimir Putin, making sure photographers captured the trio holding hands and laughing. The Indian leader is set to meet both Xi and Putin again next month at the BRICS summit in Delhi.

Yet neither relationship offers India an easy alternative to Washington. Cooperation with China in particular has clear limits: Their disputed Himalayan border remains a flash point, and economic tensions persist. Although India needs certain advanced technology from China to boost its manufacturing sector, building on Apple Inc.’s push to make more iPhones in South Asia, leaders in New Delhi remain wary of becoming more economically dependent on China. Beijing, for its part, has little interest in helping India become a viable manufacturing alternative.

Modi talks with Russia’s Vladimir Putin (L) and Chinese President Xi ahead of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization Summit in Tianjin on Sept. 1, 2025. Photographer: Suo Takekuma/Getty Images AsiaPac

India’s close ties with Russia date back to the Cold War, and Putin remains a reliable supplier of cheap oil and weapons. While Modi’s government has sought to reduce its dependence on Moscow, officials in Delhi say India would never scale back the relationship as much as the US would like.

At the same time, Indian leaders realize that Russia’s economy isn’t exactly a growth engine and that India’s economic future hinges more on strong ties with the West. Within Modi’s government, the current friction with the Trump administration is seen more as an aberration in a decade-long trend toward closer ties between two countries that are naturally aligned.

One official describes the strategy this way: Work quietly to repair ties with the US while strengthening relations with its partners, which India had previously neglected when it could speak directly with “Uncle Sam himself.” Over the past year or so, India has raced to sign long-stalled trade agreements and improve defense cooperation with a range of nations in the broader US security orbit.

Since Trump first shocked the world with his “Liberation Day” tariffs in April 2025, India has concluded trade negotiations with the European Union and completed deals with the UK and New Zealand. It’s also strengthened defense ties with the EU and with three of America’s staunchest allies in Asia — Japan, Australia and the Philippines.

“He’s a great disrupter who has probably done us a great favor,” the Indian official says of Trump. “Some things needed a massive shake-up, which is hard to do unless you have this kind of chaos.”

### **The Art of Strategic Autonomy**

Europe has long offered examples of hedging and diversified alignment. Perhaps the most famous was French leader Charles de Gaulle’s [pursuit](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP79T00826A000400010062-3.pdf) of strategic autonomy, culminating in his 1966 [decision](https://www.nytimes.com/1966/02/22/archives/de-gaulle-insists-on-rule-of-bases-of-nato-in-france-says-allied.html) to remove France from NATO’s integrated military command and force allied troops to leave the country.

Dressed in a double-breasted suit before more than 1,000 journalists [crammed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PtSADAkjZDY) into the Élysée Palace in Paris, the former general [said](https://www.elysee.fr/front/pdf/elysee-module-16476-fr.pdf) France’s ability to control its own destiny “is incompatible with a defense organization in which she finds herself subordinated.” Warning that the US could drag NATO nations into war, he said Europe would “be automatically involved in the struggle even though it would not have wanted it.”

French President Charles de Gaulle addresses the media on Feb. 21, 1966 in Paris. Photographer: Patrice Picot/Gamma-Rapho/Getty Images

Yet de Gaulle stopped short of abandoning the Western alliance — leaving just the integrated command through which nations pool resources and operate as a single entity. “This is not at all a breakup, but a necessary adaptation,” he said. A year later, NATO shifted its headquarters from Paris to Brussels.

France would remain outside the integrated command until President Nicolas Sarkozy [rejoined](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/03/08/world/europe/08nato.html) in 2009. In an [address](https://www.acronym.org.uk/old/archive/docs/0903/doc03.htm) at a Paris amphitheater named after Marshal Ferdinand Foch, who commanded Allied forces in World War I, Sarkozy said France’s “proclaimed but unrealized distancing from NATO limits our national independence and our room for maneuver.” It was possible, he said, to be allies with the US while remaining “friends making their own decisions, independent allies and free partners.”

One reason for the shift was a recognition that the nature of national security itself had changed. “France’s defense is decided now as much on our territory as thousands of kilometers away, in space or on IT networks,” Sarkozy said.

Current leader Emmanuel Macron has aligned himself more with the de Gaulle camp, asserting France’s strategic autonomy and calling for European self-reliance on defense. At the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland in January, he [criticized both the US and China](https://www.weforum.org/stories/forum-institutional/davos-2026-special-address-by-emmanuel-macron-president-of-france/) while touting France as a bulwark against a “new colonial approach” characterized by “the law of the strongest” and “vassalization.”

Macron delivers a speech at the World Economic Forum in Davos on Jan. 20, 2026. Photographer: Ludovic Marin/Getty Images

Yet for all Macron’s criticism of Trump, on everything from tariffs to Greenland, he’s not contemplating pulling out of NATO. In June he joined four other European leaders [in declaring](https://uk.diplomatie.gouv.fr/en/european-leaders-commit-euro-atlantic-security-and-transatlantic-bond) they were “committed to a stronger European role within NATO, with Europe taking greater responsibility for our shared transatlantic security, coordinating closely with the US.”

Nor has greater independence from Washington meant more accommodation of Beijing. While proclaiming he’s not “anti-China,” Macron has sought to rally the EU to take a stronger stance against China on economic matters. In a joint press briefing with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz last month, Macron warned that Europe faced an “[aggressive](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-17/germany-and-france-to-draw-up-roadmap-on-china-trade-imbalances)” trade war, noting that France and Germany had “never been so convergent” on China.

### **Old Alliances Still Matter**

For all the talk of middle powers charting a new course, traditional Western bodies such as NATO, the Group of Seven and Five Eyes — an intelligence-sharing group comprising the US, the UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand — have proved durable despite Trump’s broadsides. And newer groupings are still forming along similar geopolitical fault lines.

The US has stepped up efforts to break China’s dominance of critical minerals, hosting 55 countries earlier this year — including India and France — in a bid to “reshape the global market” for the elements. France also used its G-7 leadership to [advance](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/en/presse-et-ressources/decouvrir-et-informer/actualites/declaration-des-chefs-d-etat-et-de-gouvernement-du-g7-sur-la-securisation-des-chaines-d) an overlapping “Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance” with fellow members Canada, Germany, Italy, Japan, the UK and the US.

The emerging governance structure for AI reflects similar divisions. The US-backed [Pax Silica](https://www.state.gov/pax-silica) includes the EU among roughly two dozen other members, while the China-initiated [World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization](https://eng.sectsco.org/20260719/2462053.html) counts Russia and Myanmar among almost 40 signatories. Only Kazakhstan has joined both, prompting the US to [reportedly](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-tell-partners-they-must-pick-sides-ai-race-with-china-2026-08-14/) consider demanding that Pax Silica members pick sides in the AI race with China.

Whether the world ultimately bifurcates into two separate AI supply chains remains an open question. Hardware is the clearest dividing line so far, with the US pressuring allies and partners to block China from obtaining the most advanced chips. Beijing has countered American restrictions on computing power by releasing cheaper, more accessible open-weight models, helping Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.’s Qwen and Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3 gain users around the world, including in the US, Europe and India.

So far the US hasn’t directly banned Chinese models, a move that would be technically difficult to enforce and could backfire. Mistral AI, Europe’s challenger to global participants such as Anthropic and OpenAI, has embraced Chinese open-weight models on its platforms, [downplaying](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2088000058910199921) potential security risks.

But while Western nations debate the threat posed by open-source models, concerns about Chinese hardware are only growing. Amid fears that China could siphon data, the US Federal Communications Commission recently tightened curbs on a range of products including robotics, Wi-Fi routers, data center components and drones. The UK disclosed this month that some components of its [naval drones](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/uk-steps-up-scrutiny-of-defense-supply-chain-after-china-scare) were secretly transmitting signals to China.

That vast and lingering trust deficit means US-aligned partners remain wary of becoming beholden to China even as they seek to benefit from its low-cost, high-tech products. Perhaps one day Chinese innovations will offer such a decisive advantage that keeping Beijing at arm’s length becomes too costly.

Until then, many US partners are likely to remain anchored in the Western-led order, even as they hedge and diversify away from Washington. One Indian official likens it to a group of investors who put much of their cash into a single stock only to see poor management send the shares lower. Nobody is ready to sell their entire stake at a loss, and they still hope it can recover.
