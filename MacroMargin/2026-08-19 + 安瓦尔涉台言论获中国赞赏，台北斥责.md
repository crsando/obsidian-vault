## 安瓦尔涉台言论获中国赞赏，台北斥责

作者：Netty Ismail、Colum Murphy、Miaojung Lin\n2026年8月19日下午6:00 GMT+8

### **要点**

- 马来西亚总理安瓦尔·易卜拉欣暗示不会谴责中国对台湾使用武力，此举赢得北京赞扬，同时遭到台北批评。
- 中国外交部发言人林剑表示，“世界上只有一个中国”，“台湾是中国领土不可分割的一部分”。
- 台湾外交部谴责了安瓦尔的言论，称其“单方面支持中国可能对台湾企业在马来西亚投资的信心产生负面影响”。
马来西亚总理安瓦尔·易卜拉欣暗示不会谴责中国对台使用武力，此举赢得北京赞赏，同时遭到台北批评，这增强了这个东南亚国家深化与其最大贸易伙伴关系的努力。

“中国赞赏安瓦尔总理的言论，”中国外交部发言人林剑在周三的例行记者会上表示。“我想重申，世界上只有一个中国。台湾是中国领土不可分割的一部分，中国必须也必将实现统一。”

安瓦尔在周日发布的半岛电视台[采访](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/the-india-report/2026/8/16/anwar-ibrahim-malaysia-between-the-superpowers)中表示，他认为“台湾是中国的一部分，就是这样”，重申马来西亚坚持一个中国政策。他也支持中国为统一国家而使用武力的权利。

中国将台湾视为其领土的一部分，台北政府拒绝这一主张，并宣称该岛屿已经是一个事实上的独立国家。

台湾外交部谴责安瓦尔的言论，在声明中表示，他“单方面支持中国可能会对台湾企业投资马来西亚的信心产生负面影响。”

台北警告说，支持以武力威胁改变台海现状的单方面努力的言论“严重损害了台湾与马来西亚之间的政治信任。”

该部还质疑安瓦尔的立场，评论称马来西亚领导人“长期以来将自己描绘成民主和人权的捍卫者”，其中包括尊重“台湾人民的意愿”。

这一来一往突显了马来西亚微妙的平衡之举，该国寻求与中国建立更紧密的经济联系，同时与台湾及其他亚洲主要经济体保持广泛的贸易和投资关系。

在中美竞争日益激烈之际，马来西亚试图将自己定位为一个中立、务实的参与者。与此同时，包括[鸿海集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2317%20TT%20Equity)和[日月光科技控股公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3711%20TT%20Equity)在内的台湾企业已投资于马来西亚的电子和半导体行业。

在接受半岛电视台采访时，当被问及是否谴责中国保留在无法和平统一时使用武力的权利这一声明时，安瓦尔以马来西亚为例，表示如果其一个州决定分离，他也会试图阻止。

“如果一个省决定脱离，我们会使用武力吗？”安瓦尔被引述说。“我想我会保护国家的神圣和统一。”

## Anwar’s Taiwan Remarks Draw Praise From China, Taipei Rebuke

By Netty Ismail, Colum Murphy and Miaojung Lin
August 19, 2026 at 6:00 PM GMT+8

### **Takeaways**

- Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's implication that he wouldn’t condemn China’s use of force against Taiwan has drawn praise from Beijing and criticism from Taipei.
- China's Foreign Ministry spokesman Lin Jian said "there's only one China in the world" and that "Taiwan is an inalienable part of China's territory".
- Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs condemned Anwar's comments, saying his "one-sided support for China could negatively impact Taiwanese businesses' confidence in investing in Malaysia".
Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s implication that he wouldn’t condemn China’s use of force against Taiwan has drawn praise from Beijing and criticism from Taipei, bolstering the Southeast Asian nation’s effort to deepen ties with its largest trading partner.

“China appreciates the remarks by Prime Minister Anwar,” China’s Foreign Ministry spokesman Lin Jian said on Wednesday at a daily briefing. “I would like to reiterate that there’s only one China in the world. Taiwan is an inalienable part of China’s territory, and China must and will be reunited.”

Anwar, in an [interview](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/the-india-report/2026/8/16/anwar-ibrahim-malaysia-between-the-superpowers) with Al Jazeera released on Sunday, said that he saw “Taiwan as part of China, period,” reiterating Malaysia’s adherence to the one-China policy. He also supported China’s right to use force in order to unify the country.

China views Taiwan as part of its territory, a claim the government in Taipei rejects, asserting the island is already a de facto independent nation.

Taiwan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs condemned Anwar’s comments, saying in a statement that his “one-sided support for China could negatively impact Taiwanese businesses’ confidence in investing in Malaysia.”

Taipei warned that rhetoric supporting unilateral efforts to change the status quo in the Taiwan Strait via the threat of force “severely damages political trust between Taiwan and Malaysia.”

The ministry also questioned Anwar’s position, commenting that Malaysia’s leader has “long portrayed himself as a defender of democracy and human rights,” which includes respecting “the will of Taiwan’s people.”

The back and forth underscores the delicate balancing act for Malaysia, which has sought closer economic ties with China while maintaining extensive trade and investment relationships with Taiwan and other major Asian economies.

Malaysia has tried to position itself as a neutral and pragmatic player amid intensifying rivalry between China and the US. Taiwanese companies including [Foxconn Group](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2317%20TT%20Equity) and [ASE Technology Holding Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3711%20TT%20Equity) have meanwhile invested in Malaysia’s electronics and semiconductor industries.

In his interview with Al Jazeera, Anwar, when asked whether he would condemn China’s statement that it reserves the right to use force if unification cannot be achieved peacefully, cited Malaysia as an example, saying that if one of its states were to decide to separate, he’d also try to stop it.

“If one province decides to break away, do we use force?,” Anwar was quoted as saying. “I think I will protect the sanctity and unity of the nation.”
