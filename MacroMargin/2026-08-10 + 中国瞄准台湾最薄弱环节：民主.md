### 中文译文

## 中国瞄准台湾最薄弱环节：民主

*对民主的防御够了吗？* *摄影师：程昱辰/法新社*

这是彭博观点今日，一份彭博观点的自由配额。

### **为台湾民主喝彩两声**

自由是中华民国引以为傲的美德，中华民国位于台湾，被海峡对岸的中华人民共和国视为叛乱省份。但自由民主是一团糟，北京一直在挑该岛执政的民进党的种种问题。事实上，在追求统一的过程中，影响台湾的公众情绪可能比中国人民解放军在该地区空中和水上的粗暴展示更有效。正如**卡里什玛·瓦斯瓦尼** [写道](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-06/taiwan-s-biggest-threat-may-not-be-china-s-military)：“北京正瞄准该岛的政治韧性，让公民相信抵抗统一是徒劳的——早在导弹发射之前。”

“北京正发动一场信息战，以影响选民并削弱对政府的信任，”卡里什玛说。“这些宣传活动似乎正在奏效，年轻人尤其容易受影响。2025年的一项研究发现，花大量时间在TikTok上的台湾人更可能认同亲中叙事，例如统一不可避免或为了和平应牺牲民主。”

这——加上军事胁迫——已经让台北的赖清德总统感到不安。他已经背负着一个严重分裂的立法机构（民进党比国民党少一个席位，而国民党更倾向于与大陆接触）。卡里什玛指出：“国防预算被推迟，政治内斗意味着选民优先事项，如生活成本，被放在了次要位置。”

与此同时，民进党以安全为由，试图取缔规模很小、在议会中没有一个席位的中国统一促进党。该党可能是一个令人讨厌的麻烦，但试图解散一个政治组织对任何民主国家来说都不好看。事实上，这可能会让人联想到大陆，我上次查看时，那里没有反对派。

### **法拉奇距击败宾法斯还有一周**

宾菲斯伯爵（Count Binface）的奇遇逗乐了全世界的人，也让英国大部分地区感到有趣。然而，对于这个真正要投票补选的狭小选区来说，这并不好笑。**罗莎·普林斯（Rosa Prince）**在访问海滨城镇克拉克顿后[写道](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-06/farage-v-binface-the-clacton-joke-is-losing-its-comedy-value)：“人们不喜欢被大都会喜剧演员高高在上地对待，也不喜欢自己的家乡被当作笑柄。”8月13日，那里的居民极有可能再次选举深受争议的奈杰尔·法拉奇（Nigel Farage）为他们的国会议员——同时送别那位自称外星人、头上套着垃圾桶的竞选者。然而，罗莎指出，选举中的胜利并不意味着法拉奇——曾一度势如破竹的英国改革党（Reform UK）领袖——是赢家。作为议员，他仍有可能因收到捐助者的各类资金而受到议会调查。虽然罗莎表示，经济萎靡的克拉克顿“把法拉奇视为救世主”，但英国其他地区已不再这样看他。就在不久前，他还被吹捧为一旦大选举行，将成为唐宁街10号未来的主人。罗莎说：“他看起来越来越不可能成为首相。笑话依然在他身上。”

### **趋势图表**

“从布鲁塞尔和柏林的政策辩论基调来看，目前只有两个值得考虑的汽车市场：欧洲和中国本身。……他们需要面对现实。中国作为汽车增长市场的黄金时期已经过去——但它在出口方面仍有光明前景。中国本土汽车公司没有在国内市场参与已经白热化的竞争，而是过去几年里争相向地球上其他每个地方销售电动汽车。欧洲汽车制造商担心掏空自己的本土出口机器，追赶的速度太慢了。”—— **大卫·菲克林**（David Fickling）在“[中国正在超越欧洲，争夺下一个十亿司机](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-05/china-is-beating-europe-to-the-next-billion-drivers)”中写道。

“在华尔街，金钱比言辞更有说服力。目前大型石油公司分配资金的方式表明，这些超级巨头正在悄悄地为更艰难的时期做准备。这是正确的做法：石油是周期性业务，低价总在拐角处。这次，它们可能更近。……[它]终于在做一件过去不太擅长的事情：逆周期运作，在繁荣期存钱以备萧条期支出。……通过今天把现金留起来，石油巨头们正在积累战争基金，以便在能源价格不可避免地下跌时，保持股息增长，甚至可能进行回购。”—— **哈维尔·布拉斯**（Javier Blas）在“[大型石油公司正在为油价下跌做准备——而且理应如此](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-06/shell-bp-exxon-big-oil-is-bracing-for-lower-prices-and-rightly-so)”中写道。

### **城市漫步：桥上与桥下**

作为曼哈顿的公寓业主，我常常忘记纽约市远不止这座岛屿的22.63平方英里。它是五个行政区中最小的一个，仅占全市土地面积的7.6%。曼哈顿是驻扎在纽约的庞大媒体群体的主要活动场所，**贾斯汀·福克斯**（Justin Fox）本周[注意到](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-03/the-new-york-ification-of-media-keeps-growing-warts-and-all)了这一点。这支记者和网红大军如此强大，以至于不成比例地放大了曼哈顿在全球想象中的面积。

上周我从布鲁克林的红钩区（Red Hook）打车前往皇后区的约翰·F·肯尼迪国际机场，在韦拉扎诺-纳罗斯桥（Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge）跨入史坦顿岛（Staten Island）的地方经过时，我重新记起了纽约的规模。

桥下或许有7000多人（摄影：Howard Chua-Eoan/Bloomberg）

那一刻的视角令人印象深刻：*海洋绿洲号*，皇家加勒比邮轮运营的一艘巨型游轮，正从桥下驶过。这艘船满员时可容纳超过7000人（其中包括约2000名船员）。到了11月1日，大约6万人将从这座桥的史坦顿岛一侧出发，参加纽约市马拉松比赛。

人们对布鲁克林大桥、曼哈顿大桥和乔治·华盛顿大桥更为熟悉，但韦拉扎诺大桥在近20年里曾是世界上最长的悬索桥——1964年从加利福尼亚的金门大桥手中夺得这一纪录。无论如何，马拉松是唯一能步行穿越这座桥的方式。与我提到的其他城市桥梁不同，它没有常规的人行道。

## China Goes After Taiwan’s Weakest Link: Democracy

*Enough of a defense for democracy?* *Photographer: CHENG YU-CHEN/AFP*
*民主的防御够了吗？摄影师：程昱辰/法新社*

This is Bloomberg Opinion Today, a liberal allotment of Bloomberg Opinion’s opinions.

### **Two Cheers for Taiwan’s Democracy**

Freedom is the vaunted virtue of the Republic of China, which is based in Taiwan and considered a rebel province by the People’s Republic of China across the strait. But liberal democracy is a shambolic thing, and Beijing has been poking at the many messes of the island’s ruling Democratic Progressive Party. Indeed, influencing public sentiment in Taiwan may prove more effective in the quest for reunification than the brutish displays of the People’s Liberation Army in the area’s air and waters. As **Karishma Vaswani** [writes](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-06/taiwan-s-biggest-threat-may-not-be-china-s-military): “Beijing is targeting the island’s political resilience to convince citizens that resistance to unification is futile — long before a missile is ever fired.”

“Beijing is waging an information war to influence voters and erode trust in the government,” says Karishma. “The campaigns appear to be working, and youth are particularly vulnerable. A 2025 study found that Taiwanese people who spend large amounts of time on TikTok are more likely to agree with pro-China narratives, such as the idea that unification is inevitable or that democracy should be sacrificed for peace.”

That — in addition to the military coercion — has flustered President Lai Ching-te in Taipei. He’s already saddled with a deeply divided legislature (where the DPP has one fewer seat than the Kuomintang, which is more amenable to the mainland). Karishma notes: “Defense budgets have been delayed, and political infighting has meant voter priorities such as the cost of living are taking a backseat.”

In the meantime, the DPP is citing security reasons as it attempts to outlaw the tiny Chinese Unification Promotion Party, which doesn’t have a single seat in parliament. The CUPP may be a disagreeable nuisance, but trying to disband a political organization isn’t a good look for any democracy. Indeed, it may draw parallels with the mainland, where, the last time I checked, there is no opposition.

### **One Week to Go for Farage to Beat Binface**

The adventures of Count Binface have entertained people around the world, and much of the UK. It’s not funny, however, to the tiny constituency that will actually cast its votes in the by-election. “People don’t like being patronized by metropolitan comedians or having their hometown used as a punchline,” **Rosa Prince** [wrote](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-06/farage-v-binface-the-clacton-joke-is-losing-its-comedy-value) after a visit to seaside Clacton. On August 13,** **residents there will more than likely re-elect the controversy-tainted Nigel Farage as their Member of Parliament — and send off the self-described alien with a garbage can for a head. Rosa notes, however, that a victory at the polls does not mean Farage — the leader of the once surging Reform UK party — is a winner. As an MP, he will still be the likely subject of a parliamentary investigation into the various funds he’s received from benefactors. While Rosa says economically despondent Clacton “sees Farage as a savior,” the rest of Britain no longer does. He was not too long ago touted as a future occupant of No. 10 Downing Street once a general election is held. Says Rosa: “He looks less and less likely to ever become PM. The joke is still on him.”

### **Telltale Charts**

“To judge by the tone of the policy debate in Brussels and Berlin, there are only two car markets worth thinking about at the moment: Europe, and China itself. ... They need to face up to reality. China’s best years as a growth market for autos are behind it — but it still has a bright future in exports. Rather than fight over domestic drivers for whom competition has become cutthroat, China’s own car companies have spent the past few years rushing to sell electric vehicles to every other spot on the planet. European carmakers, wary of hollowing out their own home-grown export machines, have been far too slow in catching up.” — **David Fickling** in “[China Is Beating Europe to the Next Billion Drivers](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-05/china-is-beating-europe-to-the-next-billion-drivers).”

“On Wall Street, money speaks louder than words. The way Big Oil is currently allocating dollars suggests the supermajors are, quietly, preparing for leaner times. It’s the right approach: Oil is a cyclical business, and lower prices are always around the corner. This time, they may be even closer. ... [It] is finally doing something it hasn’t being very good at in the past: acting counter-cyclically, saving money during the boom to spend it during a bust. ... By putting cash aside today, the oil majors are building war chests to keep dividends growing, and perhaps even buybacks, when energy prices inevitably fall.” **— Javier Blas** in “[Big Oil Is Bracing for Lower Prices — and Rightly So](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-06/shell-bp-exxon-big-oil-is-bracing-for-lower-prices-and-rightly-so).”

### **Walk of the Town: Over and Under**

As an apartment owner in Manhattan, I often forget that New York City is much more than the island’s 22.63 square miles. It is the smallest of the five boroughs, just 7.6% of the city’s land area. Manhattan is the main stomping ground of the enormous contingent of media based in New York, which **Justin Fox** took [note](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-08-03/the-new-york-ification-of-media-keeps-growing-warts-and-all) of this week. The army of reporters and influencers is so overpowering it disproportionately magnifies Manhattan’s size in the global imagination.

I was reminded of New York’s dimensions when I took a car last week from Red Hook in Brooklyn to John F. Kennedy International Airport in Queens and passed the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge at the point where it crosses into Staten Island.

Perhaps 7,000+ people under the bridge Photograph by Howard Chua-Eoan/Bloomberg

The perspective was impressively on display in that moment: *Oasis of the Seas*, a mammoth cruise ship operated by the Royal Caribbean line, was sailing beneath the span. At maximum capacity, the ship can hold more than 7,000 people (that includes about 2,000 crew members). Come Nov. 1, about 60,000 people will take off from the Staten Island side of the bridge in the New York City Marathon.

People are more familiar with the Brooklyn, Manhattan and George Washington Bridges, but the Verrazzano was, for almost 20 years, the longest suspension bridge in the world — a record it took from the Golden Gate in California in 1964. In any case, the marathon is the only way you can cross it on foot. Unlike the city’s other bridges I mentioned, it has no regular pedestrian walkway.
