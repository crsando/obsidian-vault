### 中文译文

## 伊朗与阿曼同意分享霍尔木兹海峡收入

伊朗军方表示，已与阿曼就霍尔木兹海峡达成收入共享协议，双方正推动就这一关键水道的管理达成更广泛的协议。

据官方通讯社塞帕通讯社报道，伊斯兰革命卫队发言人侯赛因·莫赫比周三表示：“已就各国在海峡水域的份额以及伊朗和阿曼在海峡收入中的份额达成协议。”

他说：“美国正在阻挠这一进程，导致进展延迟。”但没有详细说明。

革命卫队的表态比两国外交部周二发表的联合声明更进一步，该声明称双方讨论了恢复船舶通行的“临时框架”，但未宣布最终协议，也未提及费用。

两国外交部均未立即回应置评请求。

伊朗官员一再警告，与阿曼就海峡航行达成的协议并不等于立即重新开放这条航道，战前该航道承载着全球五分之一的石油和液化天然气运输。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c881cc90b1481a2b4cbeee521bfc526/3c881cc9-0b14-81d8-bdfc-c294049e89cd-96ccfae2c72ee12275cb7fef9a45920d.png)

德黑兰表示，任何交通正常化都将取决于美国是否遵守6月签署的谅解备忘录条款，包括解除制裁和对伊朗港口的海上封锁，以及解冻伊朗海外资产——美国尚未表示准备采取其中任何一项行动。

本月初到期的60天临时协议从一开始就屡遭违反，因为美国和伊朗在霍尔木兹海峡的控制权上发生冲突。一项缓解该咽喉要道交通的协议可能有助于为恢复谈判铺平道路，以更持久地结束冲突。

据知情人士透露，伊朗在冲突最初几周[开始](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-24/iran-charges-some-ships-hormuz-transit-fees-for-safe-passage)对部分船只征收安全通行费，但后来转而将这些费用称为服务费。

阿曼7月[告知](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/oman-tells-un-it-s-against-imposing-transit-fees-in-hormuz)联合国航运机构，反对在海峡征收过境费，但据彭博社6月[报道](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-26/oman-tells-allies-ships-going-through-hormuz-may-have-to-pay)，阿曼私下警告一些欧洲官员，船只可能必须为相关服务付费。

自美国和以色列于2月28日对伊朗发动战争以来，霍尔木兹海峡基本上一直处于关闭状态，尽管仍有一些船只通过。每日有数百万桶原油正在离开这条水道，尽管对船只的袭击仍在继续。

## Iran, Oman Agree to Share Strait of Hormuz Revenue

Iran’s military said it reached a revenue-sharing agreement with Oman on the Strait of Hormuz, as the two sides push for a wider deal on the management of the crucial waterway.

“Agreements have been reached regarding each country’s share of the strait’s waters as well as Iran and Oman’s share of its revenues,” the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps’ spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said on Wednesday, according to the state-run Sepah News agency.

“The US is obstructing this process, causing progress to be delayed,” he said, without elaborating.

The comments by the IRGC go further than a joint statement issued by the two countries’ foreign ministries on Tuesday, which said they discussed an “interim framework” for resuming ship transits but stopped short of announcing a final deal and didn’t mention fees.

Neither country’s foreign ministry immediately responded to a request for comment.

Iranian officials have repeatedly warned that a deal with Oman on the strait’s navigation would not equate to an immediate re-opening of the waterway that served as a conduit for a fifth of the world’s oil and liquefied natural gas before the war.

Tehran has said any normalization of traffic would hinge on the US complying with the terms of a memorandum of understanding signed in June, including lifting sanctions and a naval blockade on its ports, as well as unfreezing Iranian assets abroad — none of which the US has signaled it’s ready to do.

The 60-day interim deal that lapsed earlier this month suffered repeated breaches from early on as the US and Iran clashed over control of Hormuz. An agreement to ease traffic through the chokepoint would likely help pave the way for a resumption of talks on a more permanent end to the conflict.

Iran [started](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-24/iran-charges-some-ships-hormuz-transit-fees-for-safe-passage) charging some vessels transit fees in the early weeks of the conflict, according to people familiar with the matter, but later pivoted to framing charges as service fees.

Oman [told](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/oman-tells-un-it-s-against-imposing-transit-fees-in-hormuz) the United Nations’ shipping agency in July that it opposes the imposition of transit fees in the strait, but has privately warned some European officials that vessels may have to pay for related services, Bloomberg [reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-26/oman-tells-allies-ships-going-through-hormuz-may-have-to-pay) in June.

The Strait of Hormuz has remained largely closed since the US and Israel launched the war against Iran on February 28, although some vessels are still making the crossing. Millions of barrels a day of crude are escaping the waterway, although attacks on vessels have continued.
