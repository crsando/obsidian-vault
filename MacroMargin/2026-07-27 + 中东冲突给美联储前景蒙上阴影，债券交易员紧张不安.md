### 中文译文

## 中东冲突给美联储前景蒙上阴影，债券交易员紧张不安

*摄影：Pete Kiehart/彭博*

作者：[谢晔](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOaIfVMEEws/ye-xie) 和 [近藤正树](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOcMqFOsbWI/masaki-kondo)
2026年7月27日上午3:00 GMT+8
*更新于2026年7月27日下午5:11 GMT+8*

债券交易员即将迎来新的一周，市场定价显示美联储周三加息的可能性约为三分之一。此前，中东紧张局势加剧，导致油价和美国国债收益率波动，加剧了通胀担忧。

为什么美联储说得更少了
凯文·沃什希望成为一位更安静的美联储主席。他领导利率制定委员会后的第一份声明，篇幅只有前任杰罗姆·鲍威尔上一份声明的一半。他不再在利率预测图表上标注点，并希望成立一个工作组来改革央行的沟通方式。美联储少说话对市场和货币政策意味着什么？我们的美联储记者恩达·柯伦将与斯蒂芬·卡罗尔一起讨论。

基准10年期美国国债收益率周一下跌4个基点，此前美国[暂停](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/us-pauses-iran-strikes-for-second-night-as-red-sea-tensions-rise)了其对伊朗近两周的空袭行动，导致布伦特原油价格跌破每桶90美元。即便如此，收益率仍保持在周五峰值10个基点以内，为2025年1月以来的最高水平。彭博美元现货指数在上一周上涨0.5%后，下跌0.2%。

全球债券市场也出现反弹。德国和英国10年期国债收益率分别下跌4个基点和5个基点，触及一周低点，而日本10年期国债收益率下跌3个基点。

中东局势和油市的快速演变给美国货币政策前景蒙上了阴影，投资者正在权衡自2023年以来首次加息的可能性。更增加了不确定性的是，主席凯文·沃什放弃了美联储长期以来预先暗示利率可能路径的做法，使得这次会议成为[多年来最难以预测](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-22/warsh-leaves-bond-traders-in-the-dark-on-next-week-s-rate-move)的一次。

“中东危机局势仍存在很大不确定性，可以说需要美联储更多的考虑，”新加坡瑞穗证券亚太宏观策略主管维什努·瓦拉森在研究报告中写道。不过，“由于未来鹰派预期仍在定价中，美元和美国国债收益率可能仍将得到支撑。”

隔夜指数互换显示，本周加息25个基点的概率约为33%。相比之下，彭博调查的经济学家几乎全都预计美联储将按兵不动。交易员则完全押注10月前会加息。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3aa81cc90b14817cb2ebffe533d4329a/3aa81cc9-0b14-811e-b13a-cbb8de97f8db-e4d71525084fc994edcdd9359767dd35.png)

美联储官员在加息的紧迫性上仍存在分歧。一些决策者认为，[稳定的劳动力市场](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/us-initial-jobless-claims-fall-to-lowest-level-since-1969)让央行有余力专注于将通胀拉回2%的目标。另一些人则倾向于等待更多数据，再决定是否有必要提高借贷成本。

令通胀担忧雪上加霜的是，总统唐纳德·特朗普上周对来自多数主要贸易伙伴的进口商品加征了新关税，此举旨在重建被最高法院推翻的关税壁垒。

在当前背景下，交易员得出的结论是“通胀故事尚未终结，所以我们正在推高债券收益率，”美国世纪投资全球固定收益首席投资官Charles Tan在彭博电视上表示。“当沃什沉默时，债券市场就会尖叫。”

## Bond Traders on Edge as Middle East Conflict Clouds Fed Outlook

*Photographer: Pete Kiehart/Bloomberg*

By [Ye Xie](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOaIfVMEEws/ye-xie) and [Masaki Kondo](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AOcMqFOsbWI/masaki-kondo)July 27, 2026 at 3:00 AM GMT+8*Updated on July 27, 2026 at 5:11 PM GMT+8*

Bond traders head into the week pricing in roughly a one-in-three chance of a Federal Reserve interest rate hike on Wednesday, after flaring Middle East tensions fueled volatility in oil prices and Treasury yields, heightening inflation worries.

Here's Why The Fed Is Saying Less
Kevin Warsh wants to be a quieter kind of Federal Reserve Chair. His first statement leading the rate-setting committee was half the length of the previous one from his Jerome Powell. He's no longer placing a dot on the chart of interest rate projections, and wants a task force to overhaul the central bank's communication. What does the Fed saying less mean for markets and monetary policy? Our Federal Reserve reporter Enda Curran joins Stephen Carroll to discuss.

Benchmark 10-year US Treasury yields fell 4 basis points on Monday after the US [paused](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/us-pauses-iran-strikes-for-second-night-as-red-sea-tensions-rise) its nearly two-week campaign of strikes against Iran, driving Brent crude prices below $90 a barrel. Even so, yields remained within 10 basis points of Friday’s peak, their highest level since January 2025. The Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index eased 0.2% after gaining 0.5% last week.

Global bond peers also rallied. German and UK 10-year yields fell to a one-week low, dropping four basis points and five basis points, respectively, while Japanese counterparts fell three basis points.

A rapidly evolving situation in the Middle East and the oil market has clouded the outlook for US monetary policy, as investors weigh the possibility of the first hike since 2023. Adding to the uncertainty, Chairman Kevin Warsh has abandoned the Fed’s long-standing practice of signaling the likely path of rates in advance, making this meeting one of [the most unpredictable](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-22/warsh-leaves-bond-traders-in-the-dark-on-next-week-s-rate-move) in years.

“A great deal of uncertainty about the Middle East crisis situation remains, arguably requiring greater consideration” for the Fed, Vishnu Varathan, head of macro strategy for Asia Pacific at Mizuho Securities in Singapore, wrote in a research note. Still, “the greenback and US Treasury yields may remain underpinned as future hawkish expectations remain priced in.”

Overnight-indexed swaps implied a roughly 33% chance of a quarter-percentage-point rate increase this week. In comparison, almost all economists surveyed by Bloomberg see the Fed keeping rates unchanged. Traders are fully pricing in a hike by October.

Fed officials remain divided over the urgency to raise rates. Some policymakers argue that a [steady labor market](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-23/us-initial-jobless-claims-fall-to-lowest-level-since-1969) gives the central bank room to focus on bringing inflation back to its 2% target. Others favor waiting for more data before deciding whether higher borrowing costs are warranted.

Adding to inflation concerns, President Donald Trump imposed fresh levies on imports from most major trading partners last week, in a move to reconstruct the tariff wall that was struck down by the Supreme Court.

Against the current backdrop, traders are concluding “the inflation story is not dead, so we’re driving bond yields up,” Charles Tan, chief investment officer of global fixed income at American Century Investments, said on Bloomberg Television. “When Warsh goes silent, the bond market goes screaming.”
