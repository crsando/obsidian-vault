## 中国罕见招标购入沙特原油，进口依然低迷

一艘油轮在中国卸载进口原油。

*来源：法新社/盖蒂图片社*

作者：[Yongchang Chin](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJztO3yV14/yongchang-chin)、[Rong Wei Neo](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXhJVqT3jL4/rong-wei-neo)和[Bingyan Wang](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWkIRS9XufA/bingyan-wang)

2026年8月20日下午5:43（GMT+8）

中国已购买数百万桶沙特原油，这些原油通过罕见招标及长期合同提供，尽管总采购量仍远低于战前水平。

知情交易商称，国有炼油企业中国石油和中化集团，以及中石化旗下贸易子公司联合石化，还有私营加工企业荣盛石化，在一次招标中合计购买了1000万桶阿拉伯中质和重质原油。这些原油用于即期装运。

这些交易商还补充说，另外，包括振华石油在内的炼油企业已根据年度合同获得由国有沙特阿美分配的至少1400万桶下月装运的原油。由于未获授权对媒体发言，这些交易商要求匿名。

市场正密切关注中国购买力是否持续回升，以及储备是否恢复，此前这个世界最大进口国削减了原油交付并减少炼油活动，以缓冲伊朗战争的影响。这种回落有助于防止油价飙升。

沙特阿美拒绝置评。荣盛、中国石油、联合石化母公司中国石化、中化和振华未回应置评请求。

在此次招标中，沙特阿美[提供现货供应](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/saudis-offer-oil-from-near-oman-in-possible-sign-of-shuttling)的高硫原油，来自霍尔木兹海峡以外地点，最早可于本月装船。这些品级原油产自波斯湾内，表明该国可能正在寻找通过该水道运输更多原油的方法。

这正值沙特阿拉伯及其亚洲客户应对战争带来的物流挑战之际。在霍尔木兹海峡中断后，沙特阿美位于红海的延布港成为关键出口点，但伊朗支持的胡塞武装分子的威胁增加了船东的风险。一些油轮为避开潜在袭击，绕行非洲，航程更长、成本更高。

沙特阿美对中国的9月配额是在与亚洲客户就交付条款进行谈判后确定的——一些客户在被告知从延布装载原油后[表示反对](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/asian-refiners-ask-to-pick-up-saudi-oil-outside-risky-red-sea)。中国买家的交付和定价条款尚未立即明确。战前，这个亚洲国家通常每月从沙特阿拉伯进口约4000万至5000万桶原油。

尽管如此，中国炼油商对中东原油的需求日益增长。荣盛和其他买家近期购买了[伊拉克](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/chinese-refiners-snap-up-iraqi-oil-as-more-supplies-exit-hormuz)原油，同时阿布扎比国家石油公司（Adnoc）发布了第九次石油招标，提供10月至11月装船的原油。在这场冲突中，Adnoc一直是成功维持[波斯湾](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/uae-s-adnoc-defies-hormuz-risks-to-keep-crude-flowing-to-global-market)原油外运的生产商之一。

## China Buys Saudi Crude in Rare Tender as Imports Stay Muted

A tanker unloading imported crude oil in China.

*Source: AFP/Getty Images*

By [Yongchang Chin](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUJztO3yV14/yongchang-chin), [Rong Wei Neo](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXhJVqT3jL4/rong-wei-neo), and [Bingyan Wang](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWkIRS9XufA/bingyan-wang)

August 20, 2026 at 5:43 PM GMT+8

China has bought millions of barrels of Saudi crude offered via a rare tender and under long-term contracts, although overall purchases still remain well below pre-war levels.

State-run refiners PetroChina Co. and Sinochem Group, along with Unipec — the trading arm of Sinopec Group — and private processor [Rongsheng Petrochemical Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/002493%20CH%20Equity), bought a combined 10 million barrels of Arab Medium and Heavy grades in a tender, said traders familiar with the matter. The oil is for prompt loading.

Separately, refiners including [Zhenhua Oil Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/OPAHVZ%20CH%20Equity) have been allocated at least 14 million barrels of crude for loading next month by state-run Saudi Aramco under annual contracts, the traders added, asking not to be identified as they’re not authorized to speak to the media.

The market is keenly watching for any sustained rebound in buying from China, along with a resumption of [stockpiling](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/china-s-oil-imports-may-be-set-to-recover-as-stockpiling-returns), after the world’s biggest importer cut its crude deliveries and reduced refining to cushion the impact of the Iran war. The pullback has helped to prevent a spike in oil prices.

Aramco declined to comment. Rongsheng, PetroChina, Unipec’s parent Sinopec, Sinochem and Zhenhua, didn’t respond to a request for comment.

In the tender, Aramco [offered spot supplies](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/saudis-offer-oil-from-near-oman-in-possible-sign-of-shuttling) of sulfur-rich crude from locations outside of the Strait of Hormuz for loading as soon as this month. The grades are produced within the Persian Gulf, suggesting the kingdom may be finding ways to move more barrels through the waterway.

That comes as Saudi Arabia and its Asian customers grapple with the logistical challenges created by the war. Aramco’s Yanbu port on the Red Sea became a crucial export point after the disruptions at Hormuz, but threats by Iran-backed Houthi militants have raised risks for shipowners. Some tankers are taking longer, costlier voyages around Africa to avoid potential attacks.

Aramco’s September allocations to China follow talks with Asian customers on the delivery terms — with some [pushing back](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/asian-refiners-ask-to-pick-up-saudi-oil-outside-risky-red-sea) after being told to load oil from Yanbu. The delivery and pricing terms for Chinese buyers wasn’t immediately clear. Pre-war, the Asian nation typically took around 40 million to 50 million barrels a month from Saudi Arabia.

Still, Chinese refiners have shown a growing appetite for Middle Eastern crude. Rongsheng and other buyers have recently purchased [Iraqi](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/chinese-refiners-snap-up-iraqi-oil-as-more-supplies-exit-hormuz) barrels, while Abu Dhabi National Oil Co. has issued a OIL TENDER: Adnoc Offers Crude for Oct.-Nov. in Ninth Tender for supplies. Adnoc has been among the most successful producers at keeping oil flowing out of the [Persian Gulf](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/uae-s-adnoc-defies-hormuz-risks-to-keep-crude-flowing-to-global-market) during the conflict.
