### 中文译文

## 中国警告：热浪席卷农业产区，农作物面临风险

未来几天，热浪席卷中国北部和东部的主要农业产区，中国玉米、水稻和棉花田地面临更高的受损风险。

商业气象预报机构维萨拉（Vaisala）称，本周高压天气系统将导致气温高于正常水平，东北辽宁省省会沈阳截至周四最高气温将达35至38摄氏度（95至100华氏度）。北京气温至周末将徘徊在30至35摄氏度之间。

占中国玉米产量10%的山东省气候中心上周[警告](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/pPl5bPOyPAORwV2sZucHlQ)称，至少持续到8月5日的35至38摄氏度高温可能影响夏玉米收成。该中心补充说，湿润季风带来的高湿度也会增加作物病害风险。

再往北，占全国玉米产量约7%的辽宁省官员也表示担忧。锦州市政府上周五[表示](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/tkHcfKJJ1r2drOg0Z9TgMg)，玉米和水稻等主要作物正处于关键生长期，坡地和沙地土壤失墒正在加速。

另外，维萨拉气象学家凯尔·塔普利（Kyle Tapley）表示，辽宁和内蒙古的玉米和大豆作物面临高温胁迫的潜在风险。

新疆也将迎来酷热天气，这一中国西北自治区种植着全国几乎所有的棉花。在上周发布的预警通知中，该自治区气候中心预测，截至8月11日将出现大范围至少35摄氏度的高温，其中首府乌鲁木齐东南方的吐鲁番最高气温可达50摄氏度。

新疆维吾尔自治区气候中心在[公报](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/BzodkymP_pOGTDcOAQ91Aw)中表示，持续高温可能对玉米灌浆、棉铃生长和果实膨大产生不利影响，导致发育不良、日灼和提前成熟。

商业气象预报机构[Marcus Weather Inc](https://www.marcusweather.com/)表示，持续的高温和干旱可能导致新疆本月棉花产量减少多达5%。

受到最新热浪影响的不仅是中国。韩国东南部城市梁山上周末报告了创纪录的[最高气温](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/south-korea-issues-heat-wave-alert-as-temperature-hits-record)——42.5摄氏度。据韩国农村振兴厅的声明，在蔚山的一些梨园，果实膨大速度有所放缓，而一些排水不良的葡萄园出现了果实枯萎。

在中国北方酷热难耐之际，中国南方省份——由于数周以来降雨偏多，土壤已经饱和——未来几天将持续降雨。中国气象局在上周末发布的一份[公报](https://www.nmc.cn/publish/crop/index.htm)中表示，特别是广东和广西本周将出现强降雨，加剧中国南方稻瘟病的风险。

### English Original

## China Warns of Crop Risks as Heat Sweeps Agricultural Regions

China’s corn, rice and cotton fields are at heightened risk of crop damage in the coming days as a heat wave sweeps through key agricultural regions in the country’s north and east.

High-pressure weather systems will lead to hotter-than-normal conditions this week, according to commercial forecaster Vaisala, with Shenyang in the northeastern province of Liaoning seeing highs of 35C to 38C (95F to 100F) through Thursday. Beijing will hit the low- to mid-30C range into the weekend.

The provincial climate center for Shandong, which accounts for 10% of China’s corn production, [warned](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/pPl5bPOyPAORwV2sZucHlQ) last week that sustained temperatures in the mid- to high-30C range through at least Aug. 5 may crimp yields of the summer corn crop. High humidity caused by moist monsoonal flows could also raise the risk of crop diseases, it added.

Further north, officials in Liaoning – which contributes some 7% of national corn output – have also expressed concern. “Major crops such as corn and rice are currently in their critical growth stages, and soil moisture loss is accelerating on sloping hills and sandy areas,” the city government of Jinzhou, a city in the province, [said](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/tkHcfKJJ1r2drOg0Z9TgMg) Friday.

Separately, Vaisala meteorologist Kyle Tapley also said there was potential for heat stress on corn and soybean crops in Liaoning and Inner Mongolia.

Scorching heat is set to take hold in Xinjiang too, the autonomous region of northwest China where almost all of the country’s cotton is grown. In an early-warning notice last week, the regional climate center forecast widespread temperatures of at least 35C through Aug. 11, with Turpan, southeast of the regional capital Urumqi, hitting as high as 50C.

“Sustained high temperatures could negatively impact corn grain filling, cotton boll growth and fruit enlargement, leading to stunted development, sunburn and premature ripening,” the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region Climate Center said in a [bulletin](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/BzodkymP_pOGTDcOAQ91Aw).

The ongoing heat and drought may cut Xinjiang’s cotton production by as much as 5% this month, according to commercial forecaster [Marcus Weather Inc](https://www.marcusweather.com/).

Not only China has been affected by the latest heat wave. In South Korea, the [highest temperature](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/south-korea-issues-heat-wave-alert-as-temperature-hits-record) on record — 42.5C — was reported over the weekend in the southeastern city of Yangsan. Fruit enlargement has slowed somewhat at some pear orchards in Ulsan, while fruit shriveling occurred at some grape orchards with poor drainage, according to a statement by the Rural Development Administration of South Korea.

While the north of China swelters, the country’s southern provinces — where soils are already saturated from weeks of wetter-than-normal weather — are set to see rains drag on in the coming days. In particular, Guangdong and Guangxi are set to see heavy rain this week, heightening the risk of rice blast disease in the south of the country, the China Meteorological Administration said in a [bulletin](https://www.nmc.cn/publish/crop/index.htm) published over the weekend.
