### 中文译文

## 中国加大信贷支持力度，推进房地产市场改革

中国推出了一系列措施，加大对购房者和开发商的融资支持，标志着其为长达五年的房地产危机托底的最新尝试。

购房者的抵押贷款期限将从目前的30年延长至最长40年，[根据](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/goutongjiaoliu/113456/113469/2026082816463825521/index.html)央行的规定。中国证监会周五在一份单独的[声明](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7655027/content.shtml)中表示，中国还将支持开发商通过股权和债券销售进行融资。

这些措施单独来看幅度不大，未达到一些经济学家认为结束房地产低迷所需的大规模刺激。但总体而言，它们是北京多年来为结束这一低迷所采取的最大胆尝试。这场低迷已引发约1300亿美元的违约，并给经济带来沉重压力。

“今天公布的政策强于市场预期，”Pinpoint资产管理公司首席经济学家张志伟表示。“它们朝着正确方向迈出了有意义的一步，表明政策制定者理解稳定房地产行业的紧迫性。”

中国经济在7月表现出普遍疲软，消费放缓程度超过预期，新房价格持续下跌。许多经济学家估计，经济增长进一步低于政府年度目标，这促使国务院总理李强呼吁加大支持措施。

信贷支持是全国住房销售方式[改革](https://www.mohurd.gov.cn/gongkai/zc/wjk/art/2026/art_45644612a7064289bdc0d5b1427a25d8.html)的一部分。北京周五宣布，将推动开发商提供现房，而不是使用加剧住房危机的预售模式。

在新模式下，开发商只能向购房者收取少量定金。如果未能按时交付房屋，购房者有权退出购买。

为缓解资金压力，开发商可以分期支付土地购置费，为资金紧张的开发商提供喘息之机。土地购置费曾是地方政府收入的主要来源。

根据多个政府部门发布的声明，地方当局应规范新建住房的预售条件，并有效防范交付风险，以保障购房者权益。

### **根本性转变**

随着房价下跌，且购房者担心新建房产的工期，中国房地产销售已转向二手房。尽管在政府支持下，部分住房领域略有回暖，但回升主要出现在二手房市场——中国二手房销售首次超过新房（见图表）。

“中国二手房销售已经超过新房，因此以往由预售和快速周转定义的住房销售机制已不再适应当前市场，”官方新华社在与起草这些措施的三个中央政府部门的问答中写道。“预售模式亟需改革。”

官方数据显示，截至去年，中国[新建住宅](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CHRESORE%20Index)销售面积较2021年峰值水平下降了一半以上。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ca81cc90b14810ea9dfda6a89fd3141/3ca81cc9-0b14-811d-ad4a-f715e65cf639-e30ef902b99bde16195b438dfd9495a8.png)

目前的预售制度允许开发商在完工前出售住宅，被广泛指责为加剧了该行业的供应过剩，并导致开发商债务堆积。几年前，未完工的住宅建筑项目在全国引发了广泛的断供抗议和不满。

上周，中国判处开发商债务狂潮的代表人物——[中国恒大集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3333%20HK%20Equity)创始人许家印——终身监禁，终结了其一度成为全球负债最严重的开发商、而后又从巅峰跌落的戏剧性历程。

据研究公司Proptech Innovations称，2021年房地产行业开始下行时，约90%的新房在完工前就已售出。到2025年底，这一比例降至约68%。

### English Original

## China Ramps Up Credit Support in Overhaul of Property Market

China unveiled a slew of measures to boost financing support for homebuyers and developers, marking its latest attempt to put a floor under the nation’s five-year property crisis.

Homebuyers will be able to borrow for a term of up to 40 years for their mortgages, up from the current 30 years, [according to](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/goutongjiaoliu/113456/113469/2026082816463825521/index.html) the central bank. China will also support fundraising by developers through equity and bond sales, the China Securities Regulatory Commission said in a separate [statement](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7655027/content.shtml) Friday.

The changes were small in isolation, stopping short of the big bang stimulus that some economists think is necessary to bring an end to the real estate slump. But together they add up to Beijing’s boldest attempt in years to end the downturn, which has fueled around $130 billion of defaults and weighed heavily on the economy.

“The policies announced today are stronger than the market expected,” said Zhang Zhiwei, chief economist at Pinpoint Asset Management. “They are a meaningful step in the right direction, suggesting policy makers understand the urgency to stabilize the property sector.”

China’s economy showed widespread weakness in July, with consumption softening more than expected and new-home prices continuing to fall. Many economists estimate that growth slipped further below the government’s annual target, sparking a call from Premier Li Qiang to ramp up supportive measures.

The credit support is part of an [overhaul](https://www.mohurd.gov.cn/gongkai/zc/wjk/art/2026/art_45644612a7064289bdc0d5b1427a25d8.html) of the way homes are sold nationwide. Beijing announced on Friday it will push developers to offer completed properties instead of using the pre-sales model that exacerbated the housing crisis.

Under the new model, developers will only collect a small deposit from homebuyers. If they fail to deliver homes on schedule, buyers have right to walk away from the purchase.

To ease funding pressure, builders can pay land purchase fees in installment, offering respite to cash-strapped developers whose land purchases were once a major source of local government revenues.

Local authorities should standardize pre-sale conditions for newly built housing and effectively prevent delivery risks to safeguard homebuyers, according to a statement issued by several government departments.

### **Fundamental Shift**

China’s real estate sales have shifted toward used homes as prices decline and buyers worry about how long it will take to build new properties. While some parts of the housing sector have picked up modestly on the back of government support, upticks are mostly in the China Used Home Sales Top New Ones for First Time Ever: Chart.

“China’s used-home sales has surpassed that of new homes, so the previous sales mechanism for housing — defined by pre-sales and rapid turnover — no longer fits the current market,” the official Xinhua news agency wrote in [Q&A](https://www.news.cn/20260828/b2228e17249b48e3b21a1459430dac87/c.html) with the three central government authorities drafting the measures. “The pre-sales model needs urgent reform.”

As of last year, China’s sales of [newly built residences](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CHRESORE%20Index) have plunged more than half by area from the 2021 peak level, official data showed.

The current pre-sales system, which allows developers to sell residences before completion, has been widely blamed for fueling excess supply in the sector and contributing to a debt pile-up by developers. Unfinished home construction projects sparked widespread mortgage boycotts and dissent in the country a few years ago.

China last week sentenced the poster child of developer debt binge, [China Evergrande Group](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/3333%20HK%20Equity)’s founder Hui Ka Yan, to life in prison, bringing an end to its dramatic rise to once the world’s most indebted developer and fall from that.

When the property sector began its downward spiral in 2021, about 90% of new homes were sold before they were finished, according to research firm Proptech Innovations. That rate declined to about 68% in late 2025.
