### 中文译文

## 香港计划清拆乡村以打造通往中国的新门户

[**威廉·兰利**](https://www.ft.com/william-langley) 于香港

发布于 10 小时前

更新于 15:57

在香港偏远的北部地区，科技枢纽深圳的高楼大厦在地平线上浮现，标示着与中国内地的边境。

不久，一项雄心勃勃的项目将使双方更加紧密地联系在一起。

一项大型基础设施计划旨在将香港新界的大片区域——一片由鱼塘、城镇和棕地组成的杂乱地带——转变为工业和科技带，为重振这个亚洲金融中心注入活力。

当局希望这项名为“北部都会区”的计划能够为[香港经济](https://www.ft.com/hong-kong-economy)注入新活力。香港经济曾因2019年的反政府抗议活动和新冠疫情限制而受挫，这些限制导致人口和外国投资外流。

他们还设想此举能将香港[与中国内地更紧密地联系在一起](https://www.ft.com/content/ae2ccf6e-45e6-40d8-a0d6-b9085e5788c8?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，将商业重心向北移至边境，并深化与内地城市的融合。

然而，对于66岁的郭先生等长期居民来说，该计划意味着更直接的事情：搬迁。他的村庄是未来几周内首批被清拆的村庄之一。

“很难割舍。租金每月只需几千港元，”他在1998年（即英国领土回归中国一年后）以来一直居住的铁皮和木板搭建的临时棚屋外说道。“但现在没办法——他们要建楼了。”

![Residents being evicted from their homes to make way for the Northern Metropolis mega-project © Elson Li/SCMP/Reuters](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A59298f58-0fae-486e-a4b5-cbb4f9a30f53?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=700&dpr=2)

居民因北部都会区大型项目而被迫迁离家园 © Elson Li/南华早报/路透社

该计划于2021年首次公布，将把新界未充分利用的地区改造成四个经济枢纽，聚焦商业、专业服务、科技和生态旅游，占地3万公顷，相当于香港陆地面积的三分之一。

政府表示，随着大量新住宅开发项目，这些项目最终还将容纳250万人，占全市人口的三分之一，有助于缓解长达数十年的住房危机。

这包括目前居住在该项目区的110万人，以及通过就业机会和进入内地市场的渠道，吸引来自香港密集市区、中国内地和海外的新的外来人口。

对于当局来说，北部都会区是北京所称粤港澳大湾区的重要组成部分。大湾区是珠三角11个城市组成的网络，被设想为可与硅谷或东京相媲美的巨型都市区。

“香港政府非常重视北部都会区的发展，”香港大学教授卢兆兴（Sonny Lo）表示。

“这个想法是让香港北部在经济、物流和社会方面更快地融入大湾区，尤其是深圳。”

![图片](https://www.ft.com/content/undefined)

分析人士表示，这一发展反映出近年来香港的政策制定更加大胆。自2019年抗议活动以来，当局瓦解了公民社会，压制了异议，并彻底改革了立法体系，使政治反对派失去了资格。

“过去……强大的反对政府的公民社会以及利益相关者的法律行动可能会拖延进程，”罗说。“但现在我们可以看到……障碍……已经被清除。”

这还效仿了中国以基础设施大型项目刺激增长的模式，重点关注科技等优先领域。

10月，香港特首李家超将北部都会区列为该市首个五年计划的支柱，[效仿中国标志性的](https://www.ft.com/content/75bfa7d3-d604-48cb-a7f9-2ac2474d1cfc?syn-25a6b1a6=1)经济发展路线图。

据当地一位官员透露，北京负责香港事务的最高官员夏宝龙也敦促该市加快该项目，他在6月对该地区进行为期两天的访问时告诉官员们，要充分利用“宝贵的机遇窗口”来加快发展。

香港和中国高级官员，包括香港特首李家超（后排中），出席2024年北部都会区项目签约仪式 © Hou Yu/China News Service/VCG/Reuters

对于支持者来说，北京的支持是一大福音。北部都会区有助于香港经济多元化，超越其传统的金融和贸易支柱，并利用推动[邻近广东省](https://www.ft.com/content/488ee3c4-7545-4c2f-a42b-ae32dcc421a0?syn-25a6b1a6=1)发展的科技热潮。

“以前，有些人就是不想接触内地，”前立法会议员、新任负责开发洪水桥（该项目首批工业园之一）的公司主席林健锋表示。

“我们是中华人民共和国的一部分，”他补充道，“香港有需要时，他们一直在伸出援手。”

林健锋表示，他希望该园区最终能与深圳湾对岸的前海经济特区实现无边界安排。

标普全球评级分析师估计，该项目未来五六年将耗资超过3600亿港元（合460亿美元），高于政府估计的2240亿港元。标普表示，其中约1400亿港元成本将由政府相关实体承担。

这也标志着香港向内地式发展模式迈出的新一步，即政府结合债务和股权融资以及私营合作伙伴关系来为开发项目提供资金。该评级机构表示，过去香港通过庞大的财政盈余为大型基础设施项目买单。

上个月，与洪水桥园区相关的“北部都会区”首块土地招标仅吸引两份标书，一份来自香港开发商恒基兆业地产，另一份来自中国开发商与电商集团京东组成的财团。

香港发展局在回应置评请求时表示，“北部都会区”将“提供通往大湾区的战略连接点”，“整合科技、教育、人才和产业，深化我们与大湾区城市的合作”。

该局补充道：“社会明确支持我们加快[北部都会区]发展，以加强香港在该地区的关联度，为下一代提供更多优质就业机会，并创造更宜居的环境。”

![Much of Hong Kong’s New Territories is taken up by wetlands and fish farms, which will be transformed into tech hubs © Edmond So/SCMP/Reuters](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A7ee52884-ab3f-4e7f-86e3-32079718be29?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=700&dpr=2)

香港新界的大部分地区被湿地和鱼塘占据，这些将被改造为科技中心 © Edmond So/SCMP/Reuters

村庄将被拆除，为新的开发项目让路 © Edmond So/SCMP/Reuters

法国投资银行Natixis的高级经济学家Gary Ng表示，香港可以利用其在生物技术等领域的[研发优势](https://www.ft.com/content/26c6c672-dd46-4e38-946a-5b2d3c22d3a2)，但可能难以与深圳和广东的低成本枢纽竞争。

“香港政府在过去30年里一直试图推动创新，”他说，“并非所有项目都真正达到了预期。”

此前的一个大型项目是“明日大屿”（Lantau Tomorrow Vision），这是一项耗资5800亿港元的填海计划，计划在一系列人工岛上建设一个新的商业区和可容纳多达110万人的住房。该项目于2024年被无限期搁置。

在新界攸壆村（Yick Yuen Tsuen），这样的雄心似乎同样遥不可及。在那里，茂盛的榕树根缠绕在已拆除房屋的轮廓上。但要求政府提高补偿的大幅红色横幅预示着即将到来的变化。

“我们村民其实不想离开，”自2010年以来一直住在该村的建筑工人黄超（Wong Chiu）说，“但如果政府坚持这样做，我们别无选择。”

## Hong Kong’s plan to clear rural villages for a new gateway to China

[**William Langley**](https://www.ft.com/william-langley) in Hong Kong

Published10 hours ago

Updated15:57

From Hong Kong’s rural northern reaches, the towers of tech hub Shenzhen loom on the horizon, marking the border with mainland China.

Soon, an ambitious project will bring the two sides much closer together.

A huge infrastructure initiative aims to transform a swath of Hong Kong’s New Territories — a ramble of fish farms, towns and brownfield sites — into an industrial and technology belt that will revive the Asian financial hub.

Authorities hope the project, termed the Northern Metropolis, will breathe new life into [Hong Kong’s economy](https://www.ft.com/hong-kong-economy), which was dented by anti-government protests in 2019 and Covid restrictions that led to an outflow of people and foreign investment.

They also envision it as a way to bind the territory [closer to China](https://www.ft.com/content/ae2ccf6e-45e6-40d8-a0d6-b9085e5788c8?syn-25a6b1a6=1), shifting the centre of commercial gravity northwards towards the border and deepening integration with mainland cities.

For longtime residents such as Kwok, 66, however, the plan means something more immediate: eviction. His village is among the first set to be cleared in the coming weeks.

“It will be hard to part with it. The rent is just several thousand dollars a month,” he said outside the makeshift shelter of corrugated iron and wooden boards where he has lived since 1998, a year after the British territory was handed back to China. “But now there’s nothing for it — they want to build.”

Residents being evicted from their homes to make way for the Northern Metropolis mega-project © Elson Li/SCMP/Reuters

The scheme, first unveiled in 2021, will transform underutilised parts of the New Territories into four economic hubs focused on commerce, professional services, technology and eco-tourism spanning 30,000 hectares, one-third of Hong Kong’s landmass.

With a slew of new residential developments, the projects will also eventually house 2.5mn people, the government says, a third of the city’s population, helping to ease a decades-long housing crisis.

That includes the 1.1mn currently residing in the project area as well as luring new arrivals from Hong Kong’s dense urban districts, China and overseas with job opportunities and access to mainland markets.

For authorities, the Northern Metropolis forms a critical part of what Beijing calls the Greater Bay Area, a network of 11 cities of the Pearl River Delta envisioned as a mega-metropolis to rival Silicon Valley or Tokyo.

“The Hong Kong government is attaching great importance to the development of the Northern Metropolis,” said Sonny Lo, a professor at the University of Hong Kong.

“The idea is to integrate the northern part of Hong Kong economically, logistically and socially with the Greater Bay Area, especially Shenzhen, at a much faster pace.”

Analysts said the development reflected more emboldened policymaking in Hong Kong in recent years. Since the 2019 protests, authorities have dismantled civil society, stifled dissent and overhauled the legislative system to disqualify the political opposition.

“In the past . . . a strong civil society opposing the government [and] legal action by stakeholders could delay the process,” said Lo. “But now we can see that . . . the roadblocks . . . have already been cleared.”

It also emulates China’s model of spurring growth with infrastructure mega-projects focused on priority sectors such as technology.

In October, Hong Kong’s leader John Lee highlighted the Northern Metropolis as a pillar of the city’s five-year plan, its first ever, [mirroring China’s trademark](https://www.ft.com/content/75bfa7d3-d604-48cb-a7f9-2ac2474d1cfc?syn-25a6b1a6=1) economic development road maps.

Xia Baolong, Beijing’s top official for Hong Kong, has also urged the city to accelerate the project, telling officials on a two-day tour of the territory in June to make the most of a “precious window of opportunity” to speed up development, according to a local official.

Top Hong Kong and Chinese officials, including the city’s chief executive John Lee, back row centre, attend the signing for the Northern Metropolis project in 2024 © Hou Yu/China News Service/VCG/Reuters

For proponents, Beijing’s backing is a blessing. The Northern Metropolis could help diversify Hong Kong’s economy beyond its traditional pillars of finance and trade and tap into the tech boom that has powered [neighbouring Guangdong province](https://www.ft.com/content/488ee3c4-7545-4c2f-a42b-ae32dcc421a0?syn-25a6b1a6=1).

“Before, some people just didn’t want to touch the mainland,” said Jeffrey Lam, a former legislator and newly appointed chair of the company responsible for developing Hung Shui Kiu, one of the project’s first industrial parks.

“We are part of the People’s Republic of China,” he added. “They have been showing helping hands when Hong Kong is in need.”

Lam said he hoped the park could eventually have a borderless arrangement with Qianhai, a special economic zone across Shenzhen Bay.

Analysts at S&P Global Ratings have estimated the project will cost more than HK$360bn (US$46bn) over the next five to six years — higher than the government’s estimate of HK$224bn. About HK$140bn of that cost is set to be borne by government-related entities, S&P said.

It also marks a new precedent for Hong Kong towards mainland-style development, where the government uses a combination of debt and equity funding and private partnerships to fund developments. In the past, Hong Kong paid for large-scale infrastructure projects via its large fiscal surpluses, the rating agency said.

The first land tender for Northern Metropolis, related to the Hung Shui Kiu park, last month drew just two bids, one from Hong Kong developer Henderson Land and another from a consortium of Chinese developers and ecommerce group JD.com.

In response to a request for comment, Hong Kong’s Development Bureau said the Northern Metropolis “provides a strategic connection point to the Greater Bay Area” that would “integrate technology, education, talent and industry, deepening our collaboration with GBA cities”.

It added: “There has been clear support within society that we should speed up [Northern Metropolis] development to strengthen Hong Kong’s relevance in the area, provide more quality jobs for our next generations and create a more liveable environment.”

Much of Hong Kong’s New Territories is taken up by wetlands and fish farms, which will be transformed into tech hubs  © Edmond So/SCMP/Reuters

Villages stand to be torn down to make way for new developments © Edmond So/SCMP/Reuters

Gary Ng, senior economist at French investment bank Natixis, said that Hong Kong could leverage its [strengths in research and development](https://www.ft.com/content/26c6c672-dd46-4e38-946a-5b2d3c22d3a2) in fields such as biotechnology but could struggle to compete with lower-cost hubs in Shenzhen and Guangdong.

“The Hong Kong government has been trying to boost innovation over the past 30 years,” he said. “Not all projects have really lived up to expectations.”

One previous mega-project was Lantau Tomorrow Vision, a HK$580bn land reclamation plan that would host a new business district and housing for as many as 1.1mn people on a series of artificial islands. It was shelved indefinitely in 2024.

Such ambitions seem similarly far-fetched in the New Territories village of Yick Yuen Tsuen, where overgrown banyan tree roots twist around the shape of an already demolished home. But large red-ink banners demanding greater compensation from the government point to changes to come.

“We villagers actually don’t want to leave,” said Wong Chiu, a construction worker who has lived in the village since 2010. “But if the government insists on doing this, we have no choice.”
