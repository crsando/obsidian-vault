## **贝森特为何倚重美联储来支撑日元**

### 财政部长希望央行通过提高一项鲜为人知的贷款计划600亿美元的借款上限来提供帮助。

Nick Timiraos

2026年8月3日 美国东部时间晚上8:00

7月31日戴维营内阁会议上，财政部长斯科特·贝森特面前的一个记事本。Daniel Heuer/路透社

- 日本计划利用美联储的一个贷款项目筹集美元以捍卫日元，同时不出售其持有的美国国债。
- 美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特公开敦促美联储提高该贷款机制的600亿美元借款上限。
- 这一请求考验美联储主席凯文·沃什将如何在央行与政府之间划清界限。

- 日本计划利用美联储的一项贷款安排筹集美元以捍卫日元，同时不出售其持有的美国国债。
一个为应对2020年美元短缺而设立、鲜少使用的[美联储](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/federal-reserve)支持工具，正被用于其设计初衷之外的用途：[为日本捍卫日元提供资金](https://www.wsj.com/finance/currencies/the-worries-that-drove-uncle-sam-to-buy-yen-4ff554f1?mod=article_inline)。财政部长斯科特·贝森特希望扩大这一支持工具的规模。

[日本](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/japan)周一表示，计划动用该安排，而就在前一天，贝森特公开鼓励美联储扩大该安排。这一安排将使东京方面能够筹集美元以阻止日元下跌，同时无需出售原本用于干预的美国国债。

问题在于[一项贷款安排](https://www.wsj.com/finance/currencies/fed-federal-reserve-jerome-powell-covid-coronavirus-dollar-lending-economy-foreign-currency-11596228151?mod=article_inline)，该安排是在新冠疫情暴发初期美国国债市场面临严重压力时创建的。美国以外大量借入美元的银行和公司发现资金枯竭，转而向本国央行求助。那些无法利用美联储为亲密盟友提供的美元贷款网络的机构，只有一个明显的选择：向一个本已难以消化美国国债的市场抛售国债。而美联储的这一安排让它们可以用所持国债作抵押借入美元，而不必抛售。

日本官员已表示，他们将利用该计划筹集用于兑换日元的美元，而无需清算原本用于干预的美债投资组合。

贝森特表示，美联储可以通过提高单一交易对手借款上限（目前为600亿美元）来提供帮助。贝森特在[社交媒体帖子中写道](https://x.com/SecScottBessent/status/2084051676801933622?s=20)：“我们鼓励在未来几个月内扩大这一规模。”

![U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent attends a media interview.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b281cc90b1481d48260cb3c9af3108e/3b281cc9-0b14-81b4-b704-d08ac54688fc-73a33cb3ee85b61fc7f9673016ad2a1e.jpg)

贝森特在X平台上呼吁扩大美联储的外国和国际货币当局回购便利。 凯莉·库珀/路透社

截至5月底，日本持有约1.1万亿美元的美国国债，因此，目前限制其通过这种方式筹集资金规模的，不是其投资组合的规模，而是这一上限。打破该上限将让东京方面能够迅速将其更多持仓转换为美元，而无需出售这些持仓。

贝森特的请求是对5月上任的美联储主席凯文·沃什（Kevin Warsh）如何在央行与任命他的政府之间划清界线的一次早期考验。提高上限至少需要联邦公开市场委员会（一个由12人组成、负责制定利率和监督货币政策的机构）部分成员的批准。美联储发言人拒绝置评。

委员会通常只有在明确的金融市场运转或金融稳定风险可能损害货币政策时才会动用此类工具，而当前状况似乎并未达到这一门槛。

这一请求可以从两方面解读。作为向市场发出的信号，表明[东京](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/tokyo)有限的工具并不像看上去那么有限，这是一种无需成本的精明姿态。作为对央行的施压，它引发了人们对财政部权力止于何处、美联储权力始于何处的审视——而美联储领导层更迭使这一界限变得更加微妙。

贝森特与沃什定期交谈。上周日，他在X上公开提出了这一请求。“有几件事很奇怪，”华盛顿咨询公司Monetary Policy Analytics的经济学家德里克·唐（Derek Tang）表示，“一是，这件事是在公开场合进行的，”这可能是“一种向联邦公开市场委员会其他成员施压的方式”。

贝森特可以自行筹集美元，方式是发行美国国库券，并将所得资金通过约2000亿美元的外汇稳定基金（Exchange Stabilization Fund）进行调度。但以这种方式进行的防御行动比美联储的兜底支持更为显眼，也更有限。

唐表示：“如果贝森特没有得到美联储的支持，那么他的话语权——这一威胁的可信度——就会减弱。”

此事发生在周五联合行动数日后，[这是自1998年以来美国首次与东京方面联手买入日元](https://www.wsj.com/finance/currencies/japan-intervened-to-support-yen-together-with-u-s-treasury-4627741f?mod=article_inline)以提振日元汇率。特朗普总统称此次行动是友谊的信号。近期日元汇率已跌至40年低点。

贝森特的日本对应官员片山五月（Satsuki Katayama）周一表示，东京计划利用美联储的外国和国际货币当局回购便利（FIMA）为未来的干预行动提供资金。

这一操作旨在避免华盛顿想要阻止的一件事——日本为干预行动而抛售美国国债。没有央行政策或财政政策转变支撑的货币干预，从历史上看只能争取数周时间，之后就可能失效。美联储这一工具提供更大火力的前景，可能会增加做空日元的成本。

华盛顿在日元问题上的利益贯穿美国国债市场。当一个国家捍卫本币时，会买入本币并卖出外汇储备。日本的外汇储备绝大部分是美国国债。如果以传统方式为持续干预融资，就意味着东京要在30年期美国国债收益率升至2007年以来最高水平之际抛售这些债券。

这背后是一个缓慢演变的问题。日元疲软加剧了投资者对日本通胀上升的预期，促使他们要求更高的回报来持有日本国债。今年10年期国债收益率已上升0.75个百分点。

由于日本机构是全球最大的跨境债权人，国内收益率上升削弱了持有外国债券的理由，并让配置在美国国债、德国国债和英国国债中的资金有了回流国内的动力。

美联储不受白宫影响，这一惯例通常被理解为涵盖利率决策，因为这些决策最容易受到政治压力影响，而且一旦政治化后果最严重。但围绕美联储的其他工作，包括向外国央行提供的贷款，这一界线从未如此明确。

“美联储在做那些看起来涉及外交政策或财政政策的事情时非常谨慎，”唐（Tang）说。与日本的协调干预“似乎是政府的一个非常政治性的项目，问题是：美联储是否有责任促成这件事？”

在他获得确认之前写给参议员的书面答复中，沃什（Warsh）表示，美联储在设定利率时独立性最强，而在其他方面则较弱。他写道，在涉及国际金融的问题上，美联储官员不会得到特别尊重，美联储将与政府和国会合作。

上个月在国会山听证会上被问及同一问题的不同版本时，他给出了不同的回答。他说，美联储与各国央行之间的常设美元借贷额度是其货币政策的一部分。没有人问沃什，这一最新操作将属于哪一边。

## **Why Bessent Is Leaning on the Fed to Help Prop Up Japan’s Currency**

### Treasury secretary wants the central bank to help by raising its $60 billion borrowing limit on an obscure lending program.

By

Nick Timiraos

Aug. 3, 2026 8:00 pm ET

A notepad in front of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent from a July 31 cabinet meeting at Camp David. Daniel Heuer/Reuters

- U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly urged the Federal Reserve to raise the $60 billion borrowing limit on the lending facility.
- The request tests how Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh will draw the line between the central bank and the administration.

A little-used [Federal Reserve](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/federal-reserve) backstop built for the 2020 dollar shortage is being put to a use it was never designed for: [bankrolling Japan’s defense of the yen.](https://www.wsj.com/finance/currencies/the-worries-that-drove-uncle-sam-to-buy-yen-4ff554f1?mod=article_inline) Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants the backstop made bigger.

[Japan](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/japan) said Monday it planned to draw on the facility, a day after Bessent publicly encouraged the Fed to expand it. The arrangement would let Tokyo raise dollars to halt the yen’s slide without selling the U.S. government debt that would otherwise pay for it.

At issue is [a lending program created when](https://www.wsj.com/finance/currencies/fed-federal-reserve-jerome-powell-covid-coronavirus-dollar-lending-economy-foreign-currency-11596228151?mod=article_inline) the Treasury market faced severe strains at the onset of the Covid pandemic. Banks and companies outside the U.S. that had borrowed heavily in dollars found that funding drying up and turned to their own central banks for help. Those without access to the Fed’s dollar-lending network for close allies had one obvious source: selling Treasurys into a market that was already struggling to absorb them. The Fed facility let them borrow dollars against those holdings instead.

Japanese officials have indicated they will use the program to raise the dollars they sell for yen without liquidating the Treasury portfolio that would otherwise pay for the intervention.

Bessent said the Fed could help by raising the $60 billion limit on how much any one counterparty can borrow. “We would encourage it to be upsized in the coming months,” [Bessent wrote in a social-media post.](https://x.com/SecScottBessent/status/2084051676801933622?s=20)

Bessent went on X to push for an expansion of the Fed’s Foreign and International Monetary Authorities Repo Facility. Kylie Cooper/Reuters

Japan held roughly $1.1 trillion in Treasurys at the end of May, so the cap, not the size of its portfolio, is what currently limits how much it can raise this way. Lifting it would let Tokyo turn more of its holdings into dollars quickly, and without selling them.

Bessent’s request represents an early test of how Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, who took office in May, intends to draw the line between the central bank and the administration that installed him. Raising the cap is subject to approval by at least a subset of the Federal Open Market Committee, the 12-person body that sets interest rates and oversees monetary policy. A Fed spokesman declined to comment.

The committee typically engages such tools if a clear market-functioning or financial-stability risk threatens to impair monetary policy, a bar that current conditions don’t appear to meet.

The ask can be read two ways. As a signal to markets that [Tokyo](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/tokyo)’s limited tools are less limited than they look, it is shrewd posturing at no cost. As pressure on the central bank, it invites scrutiny of where Treasury’s authority ends and the Fed’s begins—a boundary made more delicate by the change in Fed leadership.

Bessent, who speaks regularly with Warsh, made the request publicly Sunday on X. “There are a few things that are odd about it,” said Derek Tang, an economist at Monetary Policy Analytics, a Washington-based consulting firm. “One, that it’s being done in public,” which could be “a way to exert pressure on the rest of the FOMC.”

Bessent can raise dollars on his own, by issuing Treasury bills and routing the proceeds through the roughly $200 billion Exchange Stabilization Fund. But a defense funded that way is more visible and finite in a way that a Fed backstop isn’t.

“If Bessent doesn’t have the Fed on board, then his rhetorical power—the credibility of this threat—is lessened,” Tang said.

It comes days after Friday’s joint action,[ the first time since 1998 that the U.S. bought yen alongside Tokyo ](https://www.wsj.com/finance/currencies/japan-intervened-to-support-yen-together-with-u-s-treasury-4627741f?mod=article_inline)to strengthen the currency. President Trump described the operation as a signal of friendship. The value of the yen had recently fallen to a 40-year low.

Bessent’s Japanese counterpart, Satsuki Katayama, said Monday that Tokyo planned to use the Fed’s Foreign and International Monetary Authorities Repo Facility, or FIMA, to finance future intervention.

The operation is designed to avoid the one thing Washington wants to prevent—Japan selling U.S. government debt to pay for the intervention. Currency interventions that aren’t backed by a shift in central bank policy or fiscal policy historically buy weeks but can fizzle out after that. The prospect of greater firepower from the Fed facility could make it more costly to short the yen.

Washington’s stake in the yen runs through the Treasury market. When a country defends its currency, it buys its own currency and sells foreign reserves. Japan’s reserves are overwhelmingly U.S. Treasurys. A sustained defense financed the conventional way would mean Tokyo unloading them at a moment when the yield on the 30-year U.S. bond has risen to its highest level since 2007.

Behind that is a slower-moving problem. A weak yen feeds expectations by investors of higher Japanese inflation, leading them to demand more compensation to hold Japanese government bonds. The 10-year yield is up 0.75-percentage point this year.

Because Japanese institutions are the world’s largest cross-border creditors, rising yields at home weaken the case for owning foreign debt and give capital parked in U.S. Treasurys, German bunds and British gilts a reason to come home.

The Fed’s insulation from the White House is conventionally understood to cover interest rates because those decisions are most vulnerable to political pressure and most consequential if politicized. It has never been as clearly drawn around the Fed’s other work, including loans offered to foreign central banks.

“The Fed is very cautious when it comes to doing things that look like they’re veering into foreign policy or fiscal policy,” Tang said. The coordinated intervention with Japan “does seem to be a very political project by the administration, and the question is: Is it the Fed’s role to enable that?”

In written answers to senators before he was confirmed, Warsh said the Fed is most independent when it is setting interest rates, and less so everywhere else. On questions touching international finance, he wrote, Fed officials get no special deference, and the central bank would work with the administration and Congress.

Asked a version of the same question at a Capitol Hill hearing last month, he gave a different answer. He said the Fed’s standing dollar-borrowing lines with central banks were part of its monetary policy. No one asked Warsh which side of the line this latest operation would fall on.
