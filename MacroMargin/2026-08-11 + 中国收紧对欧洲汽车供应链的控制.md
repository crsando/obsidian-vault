### 中文译文

## 中国收紧对欧洲汽车供应链的控制

法兰克福的[**Sebastien Ash**](https://www.ft.com/sebastien-ash)、伦敦的[**Kana Inagaki**](https://www.ft.com/kana-inagaki)、东京的[**Harry Dempsey**](https://www.ft.com/harry-dempsey)和布鲁塞尔的[**Ian Johnston**](https://www.ft.com/ian-johnston)

发布于2小时前

中国汽车零部件制造商已悄悄积聚了对欧洲汽车供应链的更大控制权，在北京方面寻求扩大其在欧洲大陆足迹之际，它们纷纷收购当地供应商，而欧洲对其出口的抵制日益强烈。

根据咨询公司Rhodium的数据，自2000年代中期以来，中国企业已投资了130多家欧洲汽车零部件制造商，主要位于德国和法国的关键汽车制造中心。

欧洲零部件供应商被收购的事件已令欧盟政策制定者和汽车业高管感到震惊。双方都担心，在汽车出口爆炸式增长的推动下，[中国](https://www.ft.com/china)可能很快重新绘制汽车零部件行业版图。

巴黎咨询公司Do Well Do Good的首席执行官Sébastien Frendo表示：“如果不久的将来，十大供应商中有两三家是中国企业，那也不足为奇，尽管目前情况并非如此。”

![A Volkswagen EV assembly line in Zwickau, Germany © Moritz Richter/FT](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3Ac0fdec83-c19f-40d5-a191-d1ce63f64827?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=700&dpr=2)

德国茨维考的大众电动汽车装配线 © Moritz Richter/FT

早期的收购得到了北京“走出去”战略的支持，该战略鼓励中国企业通过投资实现业务全球化并获取外国技术——这些投资越来越多地投向欧洲制造商。

Rhodium的数据显示，中国在欧洲的汽车交易量在2010年代中期达到顶峰，当时吉利以18亿美元收购了瑞典汽车制造商[沃尔沃汽车](https://www.ft.com/content/e6874a70-3a93-11df-b6d5-00144feabdc0)。

但北京的对外投资限制和贸易壁垒的上升意味着过去十年中的许多交易都低于1亿欧元——规模太小，不足以引发欧洲监管机构的干预。

2024年，欧盟在调查国家补贴对相关企业的影响后，在对汽车进口征收的现有10%关税基础上，对比亚迪等中国电动汽车制造商征收额外关税。

企业情报公司Sayari的首席执行官法利·梅斯科告诉英国《金融时报》，中国所有权的真实规模可能比人们所认识到的还要大。

他说，在该公司迄今为止在德国测绘出的中资控制的汽车资产中，“大约五分之四至少通过一个离岸中介机构持有，或涉及一家以当地名称注册的德国控股公司”。

政策制定者一直担心中国在欧洲大陆汽车供应链中的持续投资，一位欧盟官员将中国对该地区汽车行业的威胁描述为“欧洲十年来的挑战”。

![图片](https://www.ft.com/content/undefined)

这位官员指出，中国企业在欧洲扩张采取了四管齐下的方式。它们增加了出口，入股当地公司，建立合资企业，并自行建厂——既在欧盟内部，也在塞尔维亚、土耳其和摩洛哥等邻近国家。

Rhodium的高级研究分析师阿曼德·迈耶表示，中国交易可能大多不为人所知，但“我们仍将看到小额收购的连续性——这让许多（欧洲）政策制定者感到担忧”。

布鲁塞尔最新计划实施严格的本地成分规则，要求使用欧洲制造的汽车零部件和劳动力，这也给了中国企业额外的动力去投资欧盟。

一家领先的日本汽车零部件供应商的高管表示：“对于中国供应商来说，收购[欧洲]公司是快速获得生产基地的高度有效方式”，并且无需从头建设就能获得“欧盟制造”的地位。

只有少数中国汽车零部件供应商规模足够大，能在全球舞台上竞争，例如电池制造商[宁德时代（CATL）](https://www.ft.com/stream/15eee97a-7b2b-4ff7-bd7b-6cf890e7cd86)和名爵母公司上汽集团旗下的延锋。然而，欧洲零部件制造商持续的财务困境使它们成为其他中国企业的有吸引力的收购目标。

Sayari对德国供应链中62家中国拥有的实体样本的分析显示，它们如何在德国关键的汽车制造中心形成了产业集群。

欧盟官员表示，欧洲零部件制造商并非家喻户晓的名字，但“在地方层面提供了大量就业机会”，并补充说，需要采取措施限制中国企业的市场准入和控制战略投资，以补充更严格的本地成分规则。

根据欧洲贸易机构Clepa的数据，该行业在欧洲直接雇佣约170万人，包括博世、法雷奥和佛瑞亚在内的零部件制造商在过去两年中已裁减超过10万个工作岗位。

与此同时，保时捷咨询合伙人本杰明·布兰德表示，该地区的汽车制造商正在密切关注中国企业的收购，因为如果被收购的制造商是重要零部件的单一供应商，所有权的变更可能带来供应链风险。

Sayari确认了23家中国集团在德国境内进行的收购，这些集团收购了供应链中的高科技零部件制造商。其中包括高端汽车发动机垫片、自动驾驶系统和无线连接天线的制造商。

一位欧洲行业高管表示，现在每家汽车公司都有一份欧洲中资零部件制造商名单，以确保拥有替代供应商。

美国对汽车中使用的中国支持的硬件和软件的限制进一步使供应商所有权问题复杂化。

倍耐力就是一个例子，意大利政府连同这家轮胎制造商的股东正在寻找办法终止[中化集团的参与](https://www.ft.com/content/c81c84de-f746-4d22-bda2-8c43ccd8c8d7?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。由于这家中国化工集团的持股，它也可能被美国禁止。

尽管对欧洲中国供应商存在的担忧日益加剧，一些分析师认为他们的成功率参差不齐。许多供应商利润率微薄，同时输出本地工程人才和服务能力一直困难重重。

“我们确实看到一些供应商向海外扩张，但真正成功的案例仍然非常罕见，”Omdia驻上海电动汽车分析师Chris Liu表示。

在该国著名的零部件制造商中，一些最初是在西方汽车制造商的敦促下向海外扩张的，随着更多制造商希望通过从更便宜的中国供应商那里采购来降低成本，这一趋势有所加速。

其中，延锋最初于1994年与福特在中国成立合资企业，从其主要客户大众汽车那里学到了经验。随后，它与通用汽车在中国的业务关系为其在美国的生产铺平了道路，并在那里为特斯拉供货。

![The Italian government and Pirelli’s shareholders are looking for ways to end Sinochem’s involvement in the tyremaker © Beata Zawrzel/NurPhoto via Getty Images](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3Aef536391-c560-46cf-be16-25ff7e6e22ab?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=700&dpr=2)

延锋一位前高管表示：“包括梅赛德斯-奔驰和宝马在内的所有主要全球公司都在鼓励延锋向海外扩张。”

这家总部位于上海的集团与美国集团江森自控组建了价值75亿美元的汽车内饰合资企业后，已深入欧洲供应链，从座椅到方向盘无所不包。延锋现在拥有该合资企业。

随着西方汽车品牌在中国的销量下滑，延锋赢得了比亚迪和新兴本土企业的订单。这位前高管表示，延锋“处于任何供应商中最有利的位置，可以在欧洲支持中国汽车制造商”。

虽然一些中国供应商花费了数十年时间逐步进入全球供应链，但其他供应商最近却从渴望获得中国最新技术专长的欧洲汽车制造商那里得到了现成的支持，从而搁置了收购风险的担忧。

去年，中国电子集团立讯精密同意以5.25亿欧元收购德国莱尼，该交易在完成前得到了这家陷入困境的德国汽车电缆集团的欧洲客户的“积极支持”。

莱奥尼首席执行官克劳斯·里纳伯格告诉英国《金融时报》，汽车高管们远未反对收购这家正在进行大规模重组的供应商，反而热衷于从中国的发展周期中学习。

![Leoni chief Klaus Rinnerberger: ‘If you are not willing to work with the Chinese . . . you will not be able to keep up with the pace of change that is coming’ © Rodrigo Capote/FT](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3Afc4ff1ac-ffb6-4c99-a1b6-8b902184fbb6?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=700&dpr=2)

莱奥尼首席执行官克劳斯·里纳伯格：“如果你不愿意与中国合作……你将无法跟上即将到来的变革步伐” © Rodrigo Capote/FT

他表示，对于中方而言，莱奥尼通过其关系网络和产品组合，提供了“与欧洲原始设备制造商建立立足点”的机会。

与此同时，其他西方集团则希望，中国供应商在出海时，与成熟的欧洲合作伙伴进行合作而非收购，将是一条可行的路径。

“我想说，我们几乎就是一家中国公司，在很长一段时间里真正融入了[中国]生态系统，”博世汽车部件（Bosch Mobility）董事会成员克里斯托夫·哈通表示，“我们现在正与这里的所有合作伙伴讨论如何帮助他们成长并走向全球。”

里纳伯格警告称，最终，那些无法适应比亚迪和吉利等企业所需速度的欧洲零部件制造商，将在竞争力上被中国竞争对手超越。

“我坚信，许多[欧洲]供应商将日益陷入困境，因为如果你不愿意与中国合作，而是与他们对抗，你将无法跟上即将到来的变革步伐，”他说。

## China tightens grip on Europe’s car supply chain

[**Sebastien Ash**](https://www.ft.com/sebastien-ash) in Frankfurt, [**Kana Inagaki**](https://www.ft.com/kana-inagaki) in London, [**Harry Dempsey**](https://www.ft.com/harry-dempsey) in Tokyo and [**Ian Johnston**](https://www.ft.com/ian-johnston) in Brussels

Published2 hours ago

China’s car parts makers have quietly amassed greater control of Europe’s automotive supply chains, snapping up local suppliers as Beijing seeks to expand its footprint on the continent amid growing resistance to its exports.

Since the mid-2000s, Chinese companies have invested in more than 130 European automotive parts makers, mainly in key car manufacturing hubs in Germany and France, according to consultancy Rhodium.

The acquisitions of European parts suppliers have alarmed EU policymakers and auto executives. Both parties fear that [China](https://www.ft.com/china) could soon redraw the industry map for car components on the back of explosive growth in vehicle exports.

“It will not be surprising if in the near future, two or three of the top 10 suppliers are Chinese, which is not the case yet,” said Sébastien Frendo, chief executive of Paris-based consultancy Do Well Do Good.

A Volkswagen EV assembly line in Zwickau, Germany © Moritz Richter/FT

The early acquisitions were backed by Beijing’s “go global” strategy, which encouraged Chinese companies to globalise their operations and acquire foreign technology through investments — increasingly in European manufacturers.

Rhodium’s data shows the volume of those Chinese automotive deals in Europe peaked in the mid-2010s with Geely’s $1.8bn acquisition of Swedish carmaker [Volvo Cars](https://www.ft.com/content/e6874a70-3a93-11df-b6d5-00144feabdc0).

But Beijing’s outbound investment restrictions and rise in trade barriers meant many of the deals over the past decade have been below €100mn — too small to trigger intervention from Europe’s regulators.

In 2024, the EU imposed additional duties on Chinese EV manufacturers including BYD on top of its existing 10 per cent tariff on car imports after probing the impact of state subsidies on the companies.

Farley Mesko, chief executive of corporate intelligence firm Sayari, told the FT that the true scale of Chinese ownership may go further than has been recognised.

Of the Chinese-controlled automotive assets the firm has mapped across Germany to date, “roughly four in five are held through at least one offshore intermediary or involve a German-registered holding company carrying a local name”, he said.

Policymakers have been concerned about ongoing Chinese investments in the continent’s automotive supply chain, with one EU official describing the China threat to the region’s car sector as “the challenge of the decade for Europe”.

Chinese companies had taken a four-pronged approach to expanding in Europe, the official noted. They boosted exports, took stakes in local companies, entered joint ventures and built factories themselves — both in the EU and nearby countries such as Serbia, Turkey and Morocco.

The Chinese deals may have largely been below the radar, but “we will still see a continuity of small-value acquisitions — and that worries a lot of [European] policymakers”, said Armand Meyer, senior research analyst at Rhodium.

The latest plans by Brussels to impose stringent local-content rules that require the use of Europe-made car parts and labour have also given the Chinese an added impetus for investing in the bloc.

“For Chinese suppliers, acquiring [European] companies is a highly effective way to rapidly obtain production bases” and gain “made in EU” status without building it all from scratch, said a senior executive at a leading Japanese car parts supplier.

There are only a few Chinese car parts suppliers big enough to compete on the global stage, such as battery maker [CATL](https://www.ft.com/stream/15eee97a-7b2b-4ff7-bd7b-6cf890e7cd86) and Yanfeng, part of MG owner SAIC. However, the ongoing financial struggles of European parts makers have made them attractive targets for other Chinese players.

Analysis by Sayari of a sample of 62 Chinese-owned entities in the German supply chain showed how they had built up clusters in the country’s key carmaking hubs.

European parts makers were not household names, but “provide a lot of jobs at the local level”, the EU official said, adding that measures restricting Chinese companies’ market access and controls on strategic investments were needed to complement tighter local-content rules.

The sector directly employs about 1.7mn people in Europe, and parts makers including Bosch, Valeo and Forvia have already shed more than 100,000 jobs in the past two years, according to European trade body Clepa.

The region’s carmakers, meanwhile, were scrutinising the Chinese acquisitions closely since the change in ownership could pose supply-chain risks if the purchased manufacturer was a single supplier for important parts, said Benjamin Bulander, partner at Porsche Consulting.

Sayari identified acquisitions by 23 Chinese groups inside Germany, which have bought up high-tech parts makers in the supply chain. These include manufacturers of gaskets for high-end car engines, self-driving systems and antennas for wireless connectivity.

Each car company now had a list of Chinese-owned parts makers in Europe to ensure they had alternative suppliers, said a European industry executive.

This is the third in a three-part series on Chinese carmakers in Europe

US restrictions on Chinese-backed hardware and software used in cars further complicate supplier ownership issues.

Pirelli is a case in point, with the Italian government, along with the tyremaker’s shareholders, looking for ways to end [Sinochem’s involvement](https://www.ft.com/content/c81c84de-f746-4d22-bda2-8c43ccd8c8d7?syn-25a6b1a6=1). It could also be banned from the US due to the Chinese chemical group’s stake.

Even as concerns over the presence of Chinese suppliers in Europe mount, some analysts see their success rate as mixed. Many operate on thin margins, while exporting local engineering talent and service capabilities has been a struggle.

“We do see some suppliers expanding overseas, but genuinely successful cases are still very rare,” said Chris Liu, a Shanghai-based EV analyst with Omdia.

Among the country’s prominent parts makers, some first expanded overseas at the urging of western carmakers, a trend that has accelerated as more manufacturers look to cut costs by buying from cheaper Chinese suppliers.

Among them, Yanfeng, which originally formed a joint venture in China with Ford in 1994, learnt the ropes from its key client Volkswagen. Its business ties with GM in China then paved the way for production in the US, where it also supplied Tesla.

The Italian government and Pirelli’s shareholders are looking for ways to end Sinochem’s involvement in the tyremaker © Beata Zawrzel/NurPhoto via Getty Images

“All the major global companies including Mercedes-Benz and BMW were encouraging Yanfeng to expand overseas,” said a former Yanfeng executive.

The Shanghai-based group became embedded in European supply chains for everything from seats to steering wheels after forming a $7.5bn auto interiors joint venture with US group Johnson Controls, which Yanfeng now owns.

As western car brands’ sales in China waned, it won orders from BYD and emerging local players. Yanfeng was in “the best position of any supplier to support Chinese carmakers in Europe”, the former executive said.

While some Chinese suppliers have spent decades building their way into global supply chains, others have more recently found ready backing from European carmakers eager for the latest Chinese technological expertise, casting aside takeover-risk concerns.

When Chinese electronics group Luxshare last year agreed to buy Germany’s Leoni for €525mn, the deal received “active support” from the struggling German car cable group’s European clients before the transaction closed.

Far from opposing the acquisition of the supplier undergoing heavy restructuring, car executives were keen to learn from China’s development cycle, Leoni chief executive Klaus Rinnerberger told the FT.

Leoni chief Klaus Rinnerberger: ‘If you are not willing to work with the Chinese . . . you will not be able to keep up with the pace of change that is coming’ © Rodrigo Capote/FT

For the Chinese, Leoni offered the opportunity “to establish a foothold with European original equipment manufacturers” through its relationships and product portfolio, he said.

Meanwhile, other western groups are hopeful that, rather than an acquisition, collaboration with established European partners will offer a path for Chinese suppliers as they venture overseas.

“I would say we are half a Chinese company really integrated into the [Chinese] ecosystem for a very long time,” said Christoph Hartung, a board member at Bosch Mobility. “We are now discussing with all our partners here on how to help them grow and go global.”

Ultimately, European parts makers that fail to adapt to the speed required by the likes of BYD and Geely will be overtaken by Chinese rivals in competitiveness, Rinnerberger warned.

“What I firmly believe is that many [European] suppliers will increasingly run into difficulties, because if you are not willing to work with the Chinese instead of against them, you will not be able to keep up with the pace of change that is coming,” he said.
