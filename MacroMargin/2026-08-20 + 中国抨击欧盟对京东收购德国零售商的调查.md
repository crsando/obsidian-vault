### 中文译文

## 中国抨击欧盟对京东收购德国零售商的调查

![Ceconomy operates more than 1,000 stores in Europe under the Saturn and MediaMarkt brands © Krisztian Bocsi/Bloomberg](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A65a87c94-84cd-4256-8147-90f8d5dee3de?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

Ceconomy 以 Saturn 和 MediaMarkt 品牌在欧洲经营着超过 1,000 家门店 © Krisztian Bocsi/Bloomberg

中国抨击欧盟对京东收购德国电子产品零售商 Ceconomy 的调查，称其构成“不当的域外管辖权”。

中国司法部已禁止组织和个人协助调查这家中国电商公司，这表明两个大国之间的贸易紧张局势持续存在。

一位司法部发言人表示，欧盟正在“滥用”其外国补贴调查机制，并警告称，如果欧盟坚持这样做，北京可能会“依法坚决反制”。

该声明是北京对与布鲁塞尔关系日益紧张感到不满的最新迹象。欧洲监管机构和当局已根据欧盟的《数字服务法》调查了一系列中国电商平台，并通过反倾销行动针对从钢铁到北京烤鸭等各种商品。

欧盟执行机构欧盟委员会还在制定一项新法律，要求企业将供应链从中国多元化转移。

京东是中国销售额最大的电商零售商，去年7月发起了[对Ceconomy的22亿欧元收购要约](https://www.ft.com/content/f773c342-1044-43c5-a106-54274e466fe7?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，这笔交易被称为中国近年来在欧洲大陆最大的投资之一。Ceconomy 以 Saturn 和 MediaMarkt 品牌在欧洲经营着超过 1,000 家门店。

这笔交易最初预计将在今年上半年完成，但欧盟委员会于5月开始了[深入调查](https://www.ft.com/content/662aac0e-54c8-45be-9ed3-7fdbffb1d4f0?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。该调查正在审查交易中是否涉及不公平补贴，并可能令交易复杂化。欧盟委员会须在10月2日前做出决定。

京东拒绝置评。欧盟委员会表示，其外国补贴条例“不因企业的国籍或所有权而有所区别，且符合欧盟的国际义务”。

北京方面的最新禁令是根据4月出台的新规实施的，该新规针对外国的“非法”域外管辖权。

司法部表示：“欧盟利用其《外国补贴条例》对京东展开调查，任意向这家中国企业索取大量不必要的有关中国的信息。”

分析人士表示，北京方面希望通过抨击布鲁塞尔的贸易政策制定者、同时单独拉拢较脆弱的成员国，来[反制欧盟对其不断上升的贸易顺差的抵制](https://www.ft.com/content/ccf8b73f-c798-46d8-a00b-47df2a6c6859?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。

## China hits out at EU probe into JD.com bid for German retailer

Ceconomy operates more than 1,000 stores in Europe under the Saturn and MediaMarkt brands © Krisztian Bocsi/Bloomberg

China has hit out at the EU’s investigation into JD.com’s bid for German electronics retailer Ceconomy, saying it constituted “undue extraterritorial jurisdiction”.

China’s justice ministry has barred organisations and individuals from assisting with the investigation of the Chinese ecommerce company, in a sign of persistent trade tension between the two powers.

A ministry spokesperson said the EU was “abusing” its foreign subsidies investigation mechanism and warned that Beijing could “resolutely retaliate in accordance with the law” if the EU persisted.

The statement is the latest sign of Beijing’s aggravation over an increasingly strained relationship with Brussels. European regulators and authorities have investigated a series of Chinese ecommerce platforms under the bloc’s Digital Services Act and targeted everything from steel to Peking duck with anti-dumping campaigns.

The European Commission, the bloc’s executive branch, is also working on a new law to oblige companies to diversify supply chains away from China.

JD.com, China’s largest ecommerce retailer by sales, launched [a €2.2bn bid for Ceconomy](https://www.ft.com/content/f773c342-1044-43c5-a106-54274e466fe7?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in July last year in a deal billed as one of the country’s largest investments in the continent in recent years. Ceconomy operates more than 1,000 stores in Europe under the Saturn and MediaMarkt brands.

The transaction was initially expected to complete in the first half of this year before the European Commission began an [in-depth probe in May](https://www.ft.com/content/662aac0e-54c8-45be-9ed3-7fdbffb1d4f0?syn-25a6b1a6=1). The investigation is examining whether unfair subsidies were involved in the deal and threatens to complicate the transaction. The European Commission has until October 2 to reach a decision.

JD.com declined to comment. The European Commission said its foreign subsidies regulation did not “distinguish between companies based on their nationality or ownership and is consistent with the EU’s international obligations”.

Beijing’s latest ban was invoked under new regulations introduced in April that target “unlawful” extraterritorial jurisdiction by foreign countries.

“The EU used its Foreign Subsidies Regulation to investigate JD.com, arbitrarily demanding extensive and unnecessary information about China from the Chinese entity,” the justice ministry said.

Analysts have said Beijing is hoping to [counter EU pushback](https://www.ft.com/content/ccf8b73f-c798-46d8-a00b-47df2a6c6859?syn-25a6b1a6=1) against its rising trade surplus by attacking trade policymakers in Brussels but courting more vulnerable member states individually.
