- 针对公司和高管的暴力威胁和安全事件在公众对人工智能日益反对的背景下不断上升。
- 根据Liferaft的数据，针对AI高管和数据中心的数字威胁在2月底至5月间增长了七倍。
- 科技公司正在增加安全预算，雇佣武装警卫，并建议员工隐藏公司标志。
旧金山——Anthropic的一名保安冲过去阻止一名男子潜入这家全球最有价值AI初创公司的大厅。

该男子是紧跟在一名刷卡员工身后进入的。他向保安出示了一个信封，上面写着Anthropic一位高管的姓名。

根据《华尔街日报》查看的4月15日事件记录，该男子告诉保安，这位高管“将被杀害”，他需要警告某人。

这次接触发生在[OpenAI](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/openai)首席执行官山姆·奥特曼住宅遭[未遂燃烧弹袭击](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/sam-altman-attack-suspect-had-anti-ai-document-with-ceo-names-authorities-say-74ddfe88?mod=article_inline)的五天后，最终没有发生暴力事件，也无人被捕。但对于Anthropic以及整个人工智能行业的高管而言，威胁远未结束。

最近几个月，[对AI日益增长的反对](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/the-american-rebellion-against-ai-is-gaining-steam-94b72529?mod=article_inline)引发了暴力言论的激增，对人和财产的威胁，以及一次严重的伤害未遂。这一现象已促使大大小小的科技公司的高管们重新考虑他们的个人安全安排，以及如何向日益警惕该技术及其带来的社会变革的公众谈论他们的产品。

![Demonstrators march in a protest against artificial intelligence.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b14810097b7ccaff21d47e1/39f81cc9-0b14-8184-9826-ddf2a3acc0bd-712dc5ef4fa4e3d4454c460d6f26c75c.jpg)

本月初在旧金山的一场反AI游行。Jason Henry/Bloomberg News

据《华尔街日报》查阅的记录，旧金山警方已对针对Anthropic和OpenAI员工的几起威胁事件做出响应。

这名德克萨斯州男子涉嫌向Altman的住宅投掷燃烧装置，被指控谋杀未遂和纵火未遂。警方发现了一份鼓吹杀害AI首席执行官和投资者的宣言。他拒不认罪。

同年同月，一名使用假名申请Anthropic职位的男子据称在网上发布威胁，要剥光公司员工的孩子的皮，作为对他所称的“窃取其工作成果”的“惩罚”。警方将此事归类为恐怖威胁，但未逮捕任何人。该男子表示自己“并没有实际伤害他人的意图”。

据警方记录，今年6月，Anthropic安全官员向警方举报了一名俄克拉荷马州男子，该男子在要求退款时威胁实施暴力。他想找真人对话。

该男子写道：“既然你们拒绝让真人联系我并退款，我将带着手枪前往你们办公室，然后我们他妈的谈谈我的钱。”

据为《财富》100强企业扫描社交媒体和暗网的Liferaft公司称，针对AI高管和数据中心的数字威胁数量在2月底至5月间增长了七倍。

“让我惊讶的是，情况在这么短的时间内恶化到如此地步，”Liferaft首席执行官Jonathan Graff说。6月份威胁数量有所下降。

意识到这种反弹，一些科技领袖已开始携带武装保镖出行。有些人选择对AI话题保持沉默以避免关注。此前一直对AI给劳动力带来的风险发出严厉警告的行业领袖，已转而谈论其潜在好处。尽管如此，他们仍在推进开发更先进的模型，而美国人一边越来越多地使用这项技术，一边表达对其影响就业、孩子福祉和能源价格的担忧。

奥尔特曼对其住宅遇袭事件的回应是发布了一张他的丈夫和婴儿的照片，“希望这能阻止下一个人向我们的房子扔燃烧瓶，无论他们对我有何看法”。OpenAI未回应置评请求。

#### **‘拿起干草叉’**

人工智能保险公司Corgi在旧金山金融区经营一家咖啡馆。Corgi约有200名员工，不如Anthropic或OpenAI知名。然而，Corgi首席执行官尼科·拉夸表示，每天都有路人在咖啡馆外驻足，大喊或咒骂。拉夸说，有时他们会抨击人工智能“抬高租金、偷走水源”。

Corgi咖啡馆和公司位于旧金山金融区的班车。Corgi保险

“我们此刻已经相当厚脸皮了，”他说。

今年早些时候，在有人破坏公司的免费班车后，拉夸为咖啡馆增加了安保人员。

根据Equilar对申报文件的分析，2025年，标普500指数中的科技公司有38.1%披露了高管安保支出，高于2021年的26.8%。

有三家报告安保支出大幅增长的公司位于人工智能热潮的中心附近。Palantir Technologies的高管安保支出在一年内增长了150%，到2025年达到近300万美元。Oracle的支出从上一年的300万美元增长了85.5%，达到560万美元。披露文件显示，大部分资金用于拉里·埃里森在“特定威胁和安全担忧”环境下的住宅安保。Salesforce的支出增长至约400万美元，比2024年增加了约100万美元。

Salesforce拒绝置评。Oracle和Palantir未回应置评请求。在今年由American Compass主办的关于人工智能与劳动力的一次会议上，Palantir首席执行官Alex Karp表示，对失业的担忧正在助长反弹。当被告知“你的工作将会消失”时，他说，“人们会拿起干草叉。”

![Palantir Technologies CEO Alex Karp speaks at the World Economic Forum.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39f81cc90b14810097b7ccaff21d47e1/39f81cc9-0b14-81a3-a9a9-e748b2b038a9-25891358c04655808a8a23bfcf16547f.jpg)

Palantir Technologies首席执行官Alex Karp。Denis Balibouse/Reuters

“几年前，科技CEO们绝对没有配备安保，”位于硅谷的JPT Security客户关系副总裁Dakota Dominguez说。“现在很多科技公司正在将安保纳入预算。”

Dominguez表示，由于针对该行业的反弹，科技公司越来越多地要求配备武装警卫。他说，与通常喜欢魁梧保镖的音乐明星或政客不同，科技高管通常要求不那么显眼的安保。

“在科技环境中，我看到的更多是苗条的身材，”他说。

硅谷资深安保专业人士Nabih Numair表示，由于针对性攻击的风险，特别是在不熟悉的区域，人工智能公司不鼓励普通员工穿着带有公司标识的服装。

一名现任和一名前任Anthropic安保员工在《华尔街日报》看到的在线帖子中表示，过去几年里，公司的安保工作大幅加强。其中一人表示，2025年他的职责是保护CEO Dario Amodei，但很快范围扩大到支持创始人、其他高管及其全球家庭成员。这些安保员工未回应置评请求。

Anthropic自2024年起实施24小时安保，并定期与员工沟通新兴威胁，一位公司发言人表示。

“我们通过人员关注流程长期追踪可疑行为，以便尽早发现升级模式，”发言人表示。

发言人表示，涉及向警方报案事件的数人已被Anthropic安保部门追踪。对于潜入大堂的男子，发言人表示公司安保团队被指示寻求降级处理，而非拘留人员。

#### **‘你无法回到农奴制’**

民意调查显示，公众对人工智能的热情正在急剧下降，美国人普遍对该技术对就业和可负担性的影响表示担忧。随着公司将裁员归因于其带来的效率提升，员工对AI的愤怒也在增加。

密苏里州堪萨斯城的顾问丹尼尔·格林（Daniel Green）从事AI培训和企业技术采纳工作，他表示，他所遇到的人已经吸收了高管们的言论，即该技术是就业杀手，使用它就像是在培养替代者。

“人们将AI与工业革命相提并论，而卢德分子当时实际上非常暴力，”他说。

5月，马克·扎克伯格的游艇在西雅图被目击。[Meta Platforms](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/META) 刚刚宣布在华盛顿州裁员约1400人，正值AI转型之际。在线评论者表示希望有人能放火烧掉它、炸毁它或使其沉没。Meta拒绝置评。

在AI与劳动力会议上，Palantir的卡普称政治动荡是该行业最大的挑战。卡普表示，他会建议同行们，“当国家崩溃时，我们没人能赚到钱。”

根据昆尼皮亚克大学3月份对约1400名美国成年人进行的调查，对AI感到担忧的美国人是非担忧者的4倍多。该调查中越来越多人——55%——认为AI弊大于利。

34岁的邦妮·凯特·沃尔夫，[Pinterest](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/PINS)的设计师，在公司运营中采用AI后被解雇。在她的账户被停用之前，她在公司Slack频道上发帖：“请不要忘记我们这些被AI抛在身后并取代的人。抵抗。”数百人用爱心或举拳的表情符号回应。

西雅图的沃尔夫表示，高管们似乎认为失业是合理的，因为用AI赚钱的潜力如此巨大。“这就是为什么人们放火烧仓库，”她说。“你无法回到农奴制。感觉掌权者真的想成为国王。从历史上看，这对国王来说行不通。”

## The AI Backlash Has Tech Executives Fearing for Their Lives

## **The AI Backlash Has Tech Executives Fearing for Their Lives**

### Violent threats against AI companies are rising and spilling over into real-world security incidents

By

Lindsay Ellis

Zusha Elinson

and

Updated July 15, 2026 9:03 pm ET

A surveillance image showing the attempted firebombing in April of OpenAI CEO Sam Altman’s home. Justice Department

- Violent threats and security incidents targeting companies and executives are rising in the midst of growing public opposition to AI.
- Digital threats targeting AI chiefs and data centers grew sevenfold between late February and May, according to data from Liferaft.
- Tech companies are increasing security budgets, hiring armed guards and advising employees to hide corporate logos.
SAN FRANCISCO—A security guard at Anthropic rushed to stop the man sneaking into the lobby of the world’s most valuable AI startup.

The man had entered by following closely behind a badge-swiping employee. He showed the guard an envelope marked with the name of a top Anthropic executive.

The executive was “going to be killed,” he told the guard, and he needed to warn someone, according to records of the April 15 incident viewed by The Wall Street Journal.

The encounter, which took place five days after an [attempted firebombing](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/sam-altman-attack-suspect-had-anti-ai-document-with-ceo-names-authorities-say-74ddfe88?mod=article_inline) of [OpenAI](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/openai) Chief Executive Officer Sam Altman’s house, ended without violence or an arrest. But for executives at Anthropic—and across the artificial-intelligence industry—the threat was far from over.

In recent months, [mounting opposition to AI](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/the-american-rebellion-against-ai-is-gaining-steam-94b72529?mod=article_inline) has given rise to a surge of violent rhetoric, threats against people and property, and a serious attempt at harm. The phenomenon has executives at tech companies large and small reconsidering their personal-security arrangements and how they talk about their products to a public that is increasingly wary of the technology and the societal changes it is ushering in.

A march in San Francisco against AI earlier this month. Jason Henry/Bloomberg News

Police in [San Francisco](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/san-francisco) have responded to several threats against employees of Anthropic and OpenAI, according to records viewed by the Journal.

The Texas man who allegedly threw an incendiary device at Altman’s house was charged with attempted murder and attempted arson. Officers [found a manifesto](https://www.wsj.com/us-news/altman-attack-suspect-called-for-luigi-ing-tech-ceos-in-online-messages-2f1702da?mod=article_inline) advocating for the killing of AI CEOs and investors. He pleaded not guilty.

That same month, a man who had applied for a job at Anthropic using a fake name allegedly posted a threat to skin the children of company employees as “punishment” for what he alleged was the theft of his work, according to police records. Police categorized the incident as a terroristic threat but made no arrest. The man said he had “no actual desire to physically harm anyone.”

In June, Anthropic security officials reported an Oklahoma man to police after he threatened violence while seeking a refund, according to police records. He wanted to talk to a human.

“Since yall refuse to have a real person to contact me and refund my money ill be coming to your office with my pistol and then we will have a f—ing talk about my money,” the man wrote.

The volume of digital threats targeting AI chiefs and data centers grew sevenfold between late February and May, according to Liferaft, which scans social media and the dark web for Fortune 100 companies.

“What has surprised me is how bad it’s gotten over such a short period of time,” said Jonathan Graff, Liferaft’s CEO. The number of threats declined somewhat in June.

Aware of the backlash, some tech leaders have begun traveling with armed guards. Some stay quiet on the topic of AI to avoid attention. Industry leaders who had been issuing dire warnings about the risks AI poses to the workforce have pivoted to [talking up its potential benefits](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/ai-workers-tech-ceos-job-losses-afc71e15?mod=article_inline). Still, they are pushing ahead on developing more-sophisticated models, just as Americans increasingly use the technology while expressing misgivings about its impact on jobs, their [children’s well-being](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/ftc-probes-big-tech-about-child-safety-concerns-with-ai-chatbots-e8817dc6?mod=article_inline) and [energy prices](https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/phoenix-is-a-data-center-meccaand-test-case-for-how-to-pay-for-ais-power-needs-c671056f?mod=article_inline).

Altman responded to the attack on his home by [posting a photo](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/sam-altmans-home-attacked-with-molotov-cocktail-suspect-taken-into-custody-ad3d7d6e?mod=article_inline) of his husband and baby “in the hopes that it might dissuade the next person from throwing a Molotov cocktail at our house, no matter what they think about me.” OpenAI didn’t respond to requests for comment.

#### **‘Go for the pitchfork’**

The AI insurance company Corgi runs a cafe in San Francisco’s Financial District. With about 200 employees, Corgi isn’t as well-known as Anthropic or OpenAI. Yet passersby stop outside the cafe daily, shouting or cursing, Corgi CEO Nico Laqua said. At times, they rail against AI “raising their rents and stealing their water,” Laqua said.

The Corgi Cafe and the company’s shuttle bus in San Francisco’s Financial District. Corgi Insurance

“We have pretty thick skin at this point,” he said.

Earlier this year, Laqua hired extra security for the cafe after someone vandalized the company’s free shuttle bus.

In 2025, 38.1% of S&P 500 technology companies disclosed spending on executive protection, according to an analysis of filings by Equilar, up from 26.8% in 2021.

Three companies reporting major jumps in security spending operate near the center of the AI boom. The spending by [Palantir Technologies](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/PLTR) on executive protection rose 150% in a year to nearly $3 million in 2025. At [Oracle](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/ORCL), spending rose 85.5% to $5.6 million from $3 million in the prior year. Disclosures show that most of that money funded Larry Ellison’s residential security in an environment with “specific threats and safety concerns.” [Salesforce’s](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/CRM) spending grew to about $4 million, about $1 million more than in 2024.

Salesforce declined to comment. Oracle and Palantir didn’t respond to requests for comment. At a conference this year on AI and labor hosted by American Compass, Alex Karp, Palantir’s CEO, said fear of unemployment is feeding a backlash. When told “your job is going to disappear,” he said, “people go for the pitchfork.”

Palantir Technologies CEO Alex Karp. Denis Balibouse/Reuters

“Tech CEOs, a few years ago, definitely did not have security,” said Dakota Dominguez, vice president of client relations at JPT Security, which is based in Silicon Valley. “A lot of tech companies now are incorporating that into their budgets.”

Dominguez said tech companies are increasingly requesting armed guards because of the backlash against the industry. Unlike music stars or politicians who often prefer hulking bodyguards, tech execs usually ask for less-conspicuous security, he said.

“In tech environments, what I see is a lot more of a slender profile,” he said.

AI companies discourage their rank-and-file workers from wearing corporate logos because of the risk of targeted attacks, particularly in unfamiliar areas, said Nabih Numair, a longtime security professional in Silicon Valley.

A current Anthropic security employee and a former one said in online posts viewed by the Journal that security has grown considerably at the company in the past few years. One said that in 2025, his role was to protect CEO Dario Amodei, but that the remit soon expanded to support founders, other C-suite executives and their families globally. The security employees didn’t respond to requests for comment.

Anthropic has run round-the-clock security since 2024 and communicates regularly with employees about emerging threats, a company spokesman said.

“We track concerning behavior over time through a person-of-interest process, allowing us to catch escalation patterns early,” the spokesman said.

The spokesman said several individuals involved in incidents reported to police were already being tracked by Anthropic security. In the case of the man who sneaked into the lobby, the spokesman said the company’s security team is instructed to seek de-escalation and not to detain people.

#### **‘You can’t go back to serfdom’**

In polls, which show public enthusiasm for AI has been plummeting, Americans regularly express concern about the technology’s effects on jobs and affordability. Employee anger with AI has mounted as companies attribute layoffs to the efficiencies it creates.

Daniel Green, a Kansas City, Mo., consultant who works on AI training and corporate-tech adoption, said people he encounters have absorbed executives’ rhetoric that the technology is a job killer and that using it is akin to training a replacement.

“People talk about AI in the context of the Industrial Revolution, and the Luddites were actually very violent,” he said.

In May, Mark Zuckerberg’s yacht was spotted in Seattle. [Meta Platforms](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/META) had just announced around 1,400 layoffs, in the midst of an AI pivot, in Washington state. Online commenters said they wished someone would light it ablaze, blow it up or sink it. Meta declined to comment.

At the conference on AI and labor, Palantir’s Karp called political unrest the industry’s No. 1 challenge. Karp said he would advise his peers that “none of us are going to make any money when the country blows up.”

Americans concerned about AI outnumber those who aren’t by more than a 4 to 1 margin, according to a March survey of about 1,400 U.S. adults by Quinnipiac University. A growing share of respondents to that survey—55%—said they believed AI was doing more harm than good.

Bonnie Kate Wolf, 34 years old, a [Pinterest](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/PINS) designer, was laid off as the company embraced AI in operations. Before her accounts were deactivated, she posted to an office Slack channel: “PLS DO NOT FORGET ALL OF US WHO ARE BEING LEFT BEHIND AND REPLACED BY THE AI. RESIST.” Hundreds responded with emojis of hearts or raised fists.
