### 中文译文

## 中国税收整顿担忧引发保险和银行股下跌

友邦保险股价下跌近9%，而保诚在香港上市的股票下跌约6% © Lam Yik/Bloomberg

周四，由于担心对出售给中国内地储户的保险产品进行税收整顿，香港保险和银行股大幅下跌。

友邦保险领跌，下跌近6%，而保诚在香港上市的股票下跌4.6%，此前一日在伦敦收跌6.4%。本地保险公司富卫和加拿大宏利分别下跌5.6%和2.1%。汇丰收跌1.2%。

此前，中国财经新闻媒体财新周三报道称，北京和杭州的地方税务机关已对离岸[保险](https://www.ft.com/insurance)保单的股息和利息适用20%的所得税率。

这些保单是该地区公司最大的摇钱树之一，在香港销售，许多情况下类似于长期投资产品。

它们是将财富转移出中国内地的流行方法，中国内地实行严格的[资本管制](https://www.ft.com/chinese-capital-controls)。此前，尚不清楚中国当局是否会对此类产品的回报征税。

一位香港保险业内人士表示：“推动这一举措的可能是税收征管更加明确，而不是阻止通过正规渠道的资本流动。”

不过，税务机关的这一举措已在保险公司内部引起震动，这些公司通过向中国内地储户销售保单获得可观收入。香港一家跨国保险公司的一名人士对英国《金融时报》表示，该公司周四正在召开“紧急会议”。

中国已采取更广泛的措施来监管对外投资流动，并堵住富人利用离岸资产避税的漏洞。

据中国财政部和国家税务总局的联合声明，上个月，中国对转入离岸信托的资产推出了全面的税收规则，在多个环节适用20%的税率。

这位跨国保险公司人士表示，财新网的报道举例说明了当局可能如何对保险回报征税。

英国《金融时报》周三报道，北京已发起一场全球追查行动，追查数十年来数千亿美元的未缴税款，重点关注中国富人的海外投资。

香港保险公司去年报告的人寿保险新业务达到创纪录的420亿美元。汇丰及其子公司恒生是市场领导者。

并非所有保单都支付股息，许多投资者选择将收益再投资以获得额外利息，而不是定期领取现金。

花旗分析师将新的税收处理描述为当局解决中国个人所得税法中关于“股息/红利”一词的“长期模糊性”。

他们表示，对香港保险单的监管将“持续构成压力”，但称此次股票抛售是“恐慌驱动且过度的”，并补充称：“对香港保险的结构性需求……从根本上依然完好。”

## Insurance and bank stocks slide amid China tax crackdown fears

AIA’s stock price shed almost 9%, while Prudential’s Hong Kong-listed shares dropped about 6% © Lam Yik/Bloomberg

Shares of insurers and banks tumbled in Hong Kong on Thursday amid fears of a tax crackdown on insurance products sold to mainland Chinese savers.

AIA led the declines, sliding almost 6 per cent, while Prudential’s Hong Kong-listed shares dropped 4.6 per cent after ending 6.4 per cent lower in London a day earlier. FWD, a local insurer, and Canada’s Manulife fell 5.6 per cent and 2.1 per cent, respectively. HSBC closed down 1.2 per cent.

The declines followed a report by Chinese financial news outlet Caixin on Wednesday that local tax authorities in Beijing and Hangzhou had applied a 20 per cent income tax rate on dividends and interest from offshore [insurance](https://www.ft.com/insurance) policies.

The policies, some of the biggest cash cows for companies in the territory, are sold in Hong Kong and in many cases resemble long-term investment products.

They are a popular method of moving wealth outside mainland China, which maintains strict [capital controls](https://www.ft.com/chinese-capital-controls). Previously, it had been unclear whether Chinese authorities would tax returns from such products.

“More clarity around tax collection is probably what’s driving this, rather than preventing capital movement that follows the formal channels,” said a Hong Kong insurance industry insider.

Still, the move by tax authorities has caused consternation within insurers, which derive considerable revenues from selling policies to mainland Chinese savers. One person at a multinational insurer in Hong Kong told the FT it was holding an “emergency meeting” on Thursday.

China has moved more broadly to regulate outbound investment flows and close loopholes used by wealthy individuals to shelter overseas assets from tax liabilities.

Last month, it introduced sweeping tax rules for assets transferred into offshore trusts, applying a 20 per cent rate at multiple stages, according to a joint statement from China’s finance ministry and national tax bureau.

The Caixin report provided an example of how authorities might tax insurance returns, said the person at the multinational insurer.

The FT reported on Wednesday that Beijing had launched a global hunt for hundreds of billions of dollars in unpaid taxes going back decades, focusing on overseas investment by wealthy Chinese.

Hong Kong insurers reported a record $42bn in new business from life insurance policies last year. HSBC, including its subsidiary Hang Seng, is the market leader.

Not all insurance policies pay dividends, and many investors choose to reinvest proceeds for additional interest rather than take regular cash payments.

Analysts at Citi characterised the new tax treatment as authorities resolving a “longstanding ambiguity” in China’s individual income tax law regarding the term “dividends/bonuses”.

They said the regulation of Hong Kong insurance policies would “remain a persistent overhang” but called the share sell-off “panic-driven and overdone”, adding: “The structural demand for Hong Kong insurance . . . remains fundamentally intact.”
