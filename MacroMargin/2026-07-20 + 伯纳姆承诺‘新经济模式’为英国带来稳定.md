### 中文译文

## 伯纳姆承诺‘新经济模式’为英国带来稳定

**要点**

安迪·伯纳姆（Andy Burnham）承诺为英国推行“新经济模式”，试图安抚民众，他的上任——作为十多年来第七位首相——将终结国家的政治动荡。

伯纳姆在周一作为首相的首次讲话中承诺立即采取行动降低生活成本，包括解释如何为这些措施买单。他称此举是“十年计划”的第一步，旨在重组政府、向地方当局下放更多权力，并将部分基本公共服务“置于更强的公共控制之下”。

![Andy Burnham, UK prime minister, and Marie-France van Heel, his wife, outside 10 Downing Street on Monday, July 20, 2026. Burnham is the UK’s seventh prime minister in just over a decade. Photographer: Chris J. Ratcliffe/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3a381cc90b1481318e4fd422a895368e/3a381cc9-0b14-8130-84de-c9ed2bc05749-9d92740bb6a12a589e05f412fff76016.png)

“我知道国内民众已经厌倦了政治——我听到了你们的声音，我想坦诚相告：我们做得不够好，”伯纳姆在唐宁街10号门前由支持者簇拥着说道，“我们将让这一时刻成为英国的断路器，推动40年来最大的变革：新政治模式和新经济模式。”

为强调其更亲民的形象，伯纳姆演讲时没有使用讲台，也未带讲稿，历时约五分钟。其更新的个人简介页面称呼他为“安迪”，而非姓氏。

伯纳姆的讲话中包括旨在安抚投资者和英国海外盟友的语句，承诺“遵守财政规则”并履行国防承诺。这位前大曼彻斯特市长在谋求首相职位前几乎毫无外交政策经验，他私下向广播公司表示，将在周一晚些时候与乌克兰总统泽连斯基通话。

入住唐宁街的仪式对这位曾两次竞选工党领袖均告失败的人来说，是一次惊人的崛起。在2015年输给杰里米·科尔宾后，他完全退出国家政治，转而担任曼彻斯特市长——这一局外人身份帮助他重返威斯敏斯特。

周一，伯纳姆乘坐车队抵达白金汉宫，在那里由国王查尔斯三世正式邀请组建政府，这一仪式被称为“吻手礼”，尽管如今已不真正亲吻。预计他将在周一晚些时候宣布内阁，据广泛报道，内政大臣沙巴纳·马哈茂德有望出任财政大臣。

早些时候，即将离任的首相基尔·斯塔默在正式辞职前，在唐宁街10号黑色大门外发表了最后讲话。斯塔默此前已宣布辞职，原因是他领导的政府民调持续低迷，5月地方选举结果不佳加剧了这一局面。他在最后一次以首相身份从唐宁街发表讲话时，全力支持继任者。

“我们面临的世界，敌人会不择手段地分裂我们，”他补充道，“我们必须牢记我们身边英国的伟大。”

伯纳姆接下来将着手任命政府高级成员这一关键任务。这对任何领导人来说都是一个充满政治风险的时刻，但对这位首相而言或许更为严峻——他必须在一个分裂的政党中构建支持联盟，党内议员们担心自己会被选民赶下台，而左右两翼新兴的边缘政党正吸引着选民。

任命马哈茂德为财政大臣将被视为对能源大臣埃德·米利班德的冷落，后者曾是这一职位的早期热门人选，并在伯纳姆上台过程中发挥了关键作用。伯纳姆的亲密盟友露易丝·海格、安妮莉丝·米奇利以及工党副领袖露西·鲍威尔都可能被授予重要职务。

财政大臣雷切尔·里夫斯、副首相戴维·拉米、外交大臣伊薇特·库珀、前卫生大臣韦斯·斯特里廷和前副首相安吉拉·雷纳的命运都将受到密切关注，因为伯纳姆试图避免在工党后座议员中出现强大的对手。仍是议员的斯塔默预计不会想要或获得内阁职位。

伯纳姆成功夺权，但其执政计划鲜为人知，他面临压力，需要阐明政策重点，特别是税收和公共支出方面的方针。

在他唯一一次重大政策[演讲](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-29/burnham-vows-economic-boost-by-taking-fiscal-power-out-of-london)中，他重点阐述了加强权力下放的计划，包括在曼彻斯特设立唐宁街10号分部、监督战后最大规模的房屋建设计划，以及在税收和水、能源、交通等服务方面给予各地区更多控制权。

伯纳姆还暗示了为低收入者减税的计划。在接受《泰晤士报》采访时，他表示将他描述为“增税者”的说法“过于简单”。他承认人们对冻结免税收入上限（12,570英镑，约17,000美元）的决定感到沮丧，暗示他可能允许所谓的个人免税额上调。

他已承诺将遵守工党 2024 年大选宣言中的承诺，不提高所得税、国民保险和增值税。他还承诺遵守所谓的“三重锁定”，保证国家养老金在下次大选前每年上涨。伯纳姆还表示，他将遵守里夫斯的预算规则。

他将接手一个充满棘手问题首相的待办事项，包括如何继续降低移民水平——这曾助长了奈杰尔·法拉奇领导的改革英国党的崛起；如何控制不断增长的福利支出；如何重振英国停滞的生产率；以及如何改革社会保障以适应老龄化人口。

在全球层面，他需要应对不可预测的美国总统唐纳德·特朗普，同时寻求保持英国在乌克兰防御方面的领导地位。一个关键挑战是增加核心国防开支，到 2035 年达到承诺的国内生产总值 3.5% 的水平。

“无论是安迪·伯纳姆政府还是基尔·斯塔默政府，至少从言论上都诊断出了问题，”Panmure Liberum 首席经济学家西蒙·弗伦奇在彭博电视上说。“两年多前，基尔·斯塔默在唐宁街的台阶上谈到要更轻地干预人们的生活——这涉及供给侧摩擦。而安迪·伯纳姆今早在评论中谈到‘喘息空间’——这反映了生活成本问题。”

“他们是否有政治骨气和耐心来实施这些长期改革？”弗伦奇问道。

## Burnham Promises ‘New Economic Model’ to Bring Stability to UK

**Takeaways**

Andy Burnham pledged a “new economic model” for the UK as he sought to reassure Britons that his ascension as their seventh prime minister in little over a decade would bring an end to the country’s political instability.

Burnham promised in his first remarks as prime minister on Monday to deliver immediate action to reduce the cost of living, including explaining how to pay for the measures. He described to move as the first step toward a “10-year plan” to restructure government, hand more power to local authorities and bring some essential public service “under stronger public control.”

“I know people at home are fed up with politics — I hear you, and I want to be honest with you: We have not been good enough,” Burnham said, flanked by supporters in front of No. 10 Downing St. “We will make this moment a circuit breaker for Britain, bringing forward the biggest changes in the last 40 years: a new political model and a new economic model.”

As if to underscore his efforts to appear more accessible, Burnham spoke without the usual lectern and used no notes in remarks lasting about five minutes. His updated biography page referred to him as “Andy,” rather than using his surname.

Burnham’s speech included lines meant to reassure investors and Britain’s allies abroad, vowing to “meet our fiscal rules” and honor the country’s commitments on defense. The former mayor of Greater Manchester, who had little foreign policy experience before seeking the prime minister’s job, told broadcasters separately that he would speak with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy later on Monday.

The ceremonial arrival at Downing Street represents a breathtaking rise for a man who had twice before sought and failed to win leadership of the Labour Party. After coming second to Jeremy Corbyn in 2015 he retreated from national politics altogether and went be mayor of Manchester — an outsider status that helped propel him back to Westminster.

On Monday, Burnham was driven by motorcade to Buckingham Palace, where he formally invited by King Charles III to form a government in a ritual known as “kissing hands,” although no kissing is done these days. He’s expected to announce his Cabinet later on Monday, with Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood widely reported to be in line to be Chancellor of the Exchequer.

Earlier, outgoing Prime Minister Keir Starmer gave his final remarks from outside the black door of No. 10 before formally stepping down. Starmer had announced he was resigning in the face of consistently bad polling for his government which intensified over poor local election results in May. He gave his successor his full backing as he spoke from Downing Street for the final time as PM.

“We face a world now where our enemies will stop at nothing to divide us,” he added. “It is vital that we remember the greatness of the Britain all around us.”

Burnham will now turn to the crucial task of appointing senior members of his government. It is a moment of political jeopardy for any leader but perhaps even more acutely so for this PM who must now try to build a coalition of support in a fractured party filled with MPs fearful of being dumped from office by an electorate tempted by newly prominent fringe parties on the left and right.

Naming Mahmood as chancellor would be seen as a snub to Energy Secretary Ed Miliband who was the early favorite for the role and instrumental in Burnham’s ascent to power. Close Burnham allies Louise Haigh, Anneliese Midgley and Deputy Labour leader Lucy Powell are all likely to be given significant roles.

The fates of Chancellor of the Exchequer Rachel Reeves, Deputy Prime Minister David Lammy, Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper, former Health Secretary Wes Streeting and former Deputy Prime Minister Angela Rayner will all be closely watched as Burnham seeks to avoid having powerful rivals on Labour’s back benches. Starmer, who remains an MP, is not expected to want or be given a cabinet role.

Burnham successfully pulled off his power-grab with very little known about his plans for office and is under pressure to offer clarity as to his policy priorities and, in particular, his approach to taxation and public spending.

In his only major policy [speech](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-29/burnham-vows-economic-boost-by-taking-fiscal-power-out-of-london) he majored on plans to ramp up devolution, including establishing an outpost of No. 10 in Manchester, overseeing the biggest house-building program since the postwar period and giving more control over taxation as well as services such as water, energy and transport to the regions.

Burnham has also hinted at plans for tax cuts for lower earners. In an interview with [The Times](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/785333Q:LN) he said efforts to characterize him as a “tax-raiser” were “simplistic.” He acknowledged people’s frustration over the decision freeze the cap on tax-free income at £12,570 ($17,000), suggesting he might allow the so-called personnel allowance to rise.

He has promised to will stick to Labour’s 2024 election manifesto promises not to raise income tax, national insurance and value added tax. It also commits him to the so-called triple lock guaranteeing annual rises in the state pension until after the next election. Burnham has also said he’ll stick to Reeves’ budgetary rules.

He will inherit a prime ministerial inbox bursting with tricky issues, including how to keep lowering immigration levels that helped spur the rise of Nigel Farage’s Reform UK party, containing the nation’s ever-increasing welfare bill, reinvigorating Britain’s stagnant productivity and overhauling social care to cater for an ageing population.

On the global front, he’ll have to contend with an unpredictable [US](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/3352Z:US) president in Donald Trump, while seeking to maintain British leadership in defense of Ukraine. A key challenge will be ramping up core defense spending to get to a pledged level of 3.5% of national output by 2035.

“Both the Andy Burnham administration and the Keir Starmer administration have both diagnosed the problems, at least rhetorically,” Panmure Liberum Chief Economist Simon French, chief economist said on [Bloomberg Television](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/5350Z:US). “On the steps of Downing Street, Keir Starmer, just over two years ago, talking about treading more lightly on people’s lives — that talks to supply-side frictions. Then, Andy Burnham in his comments this morning talking about ‘breathing space’ — that channels the cost of living.”

“Have they got the political backbone, the patience for some of these long-dated reforms?” French asked.
