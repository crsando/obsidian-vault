### 中文译文

## 中国高科技热潮料难阻止经济放缓

### **彭博AI要点**

- 中国官方7月数据预计将显示工业增加值增长放缓，固定资产投资降幅加深。
- 中国的科技行业，尤其是人工智能硬件生产，正在蓬勃发展，但这对于就业和收入的推动力有限，也无法解决根本性的通缩压力。
- 零售额预计将上升，但增速缓慢，商品和大件商品的购买可能仍然低迷，其中汽车销售暴跌。
中国经济很可能以与上半年基本相同的方式开启2026年下半年，即科技行业蓬勃发展，却未能弥补历史性的投资低迷和消费疲软。

原定于周一发布的官方7月数据预计还将反映极端天气的干扰，多场台风带来强降雨和洪水，扰乱了航运。

彭博调查的经济学家表示，上月工业增加值增长放缓，固定资产投资降幅加深。零售额可能有所回升，但仍徘徊在除新冠疫情封锁外近几十年来最差的水平附近。官方失业率预计将小幅上升，尽管该指标的价值近年来因未能反映劳动力市场的显著恶化而受到质疑。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bc81cc90b148181a322ffeeb79fe83d/3bc81cc9-0b14-81d0-947c-d11585292f7d-ca8f6d39c82a9b80406cc49de8289995.png)

中国近年来持续存在的失衡——强劲的出口导向型制造业与疲弱的国内需求并存——如今正因人工智能硬件生产的激增而加剧。在政府寻求通过发展人工智能和其他前沿技术来提高生产率之际，风险在于这无法为全球第二大经济体提供更广泛的提振。

AI的高度自动化和资本密集性意味着对就业和收入的推动有限，而且对解决中国潜在的[通缩压力](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/china-s-inflation-cools-as-oil-shock-of-iran-war-starts-to-ease)毫无帮助。在一些从AI硬件繁荣中获益最多的城市，零售销售表现甚至比其它地方[更差](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-s-ai-boomtowns-race-ahead-as-rest-of-economy-falls-behind)。

“中国可能正是在朝着让再平衡更难的方向成功升级，”牛津经济研究院亚洲经济主管路易斯·卢（Louise Loo）周四在一份报告中写道。

根据卢的计算，平均而言，人工智能、机器人和创新制药业务创造的就业岗位仅为房地产、家具、服装和家电等传统行业过去创造的一半。

目前，政策制定者已暗示将采取[克制的应对措施](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-s-top-officials-to-consider-new-stimulus-after-slowdown)。在收紧上半年财政立场后，他们加快了政府债券的销售，这可能导致更强劲的支出。

他们还表示愿意在必要时加大刺激力度，尽管中国出口的强劲表现目前可能缓解了压力。在6月出口[激增27%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/china-s-exports-imports-soar-faster-than-forecast-amid-ai-rush)之后，7月海外发货量同比增长24%。

“未来政策将取决于数据，潜在的新刺激措施可能于9月落地，”澳新银行集团有限公司（Australia & New Zealand Banking Group Ltd.）由雷蒙德·杨（Raymond Yeung）领导的中国经济学家团队在其数据预览中写道。

*“仅靠科技还不足以支撑更广泛的经济。在传统行业疲软的情况下，二季度GDP增长跌破4.5%，持续的政策支持将对遏制科技领域之外的疲软至关重要。”——埃里克·朱（Eric Zhu）。如需完整分析，请点击《中国洞察：芯片、机器人、铜——追踪新经济（一）》*

### **工业生产**

彭博调查经济学家的预估中值显示，周一公布的数据预计将显示工业产出同比（与去年7月相比）增长4.9%，较6月5.3%的增速有所放缓。

至少部分放缓可能源于近年来西北太平洋最强烈的台风季节之一。由于强降雨，中国一些最繁忙的港口暂停运营，而“海豚”台风导致上海等地本月出现大规模航班取消。

尽管如此，对人工智能和其他技术硬件日益高涨的需求仍在支撑产出。高科技制造业目前约占工业总产出的25%至30%，彭博经济研究《中国洞察：芯片、机器人、铜——追踪新经济》。

### **投资支出**

预计7月中国固定资产投资将出现除新冠疫情冲击外有记录以来的最大跌幅，且跌幅可能进一步加深。经济学家预计，2026年前七个月的固定资产投资将同比下降6.2%，而上半年降幅为5.7%。政府支出缩减拖累了该指标，同时随着投资回报率下降，民营企业越来越不愿扩大大规模资本支出。

预计政府将加快“六网”重大项目的建设，该项目包括电网、数据中心、电信网络和物流的建设和升级。加快这一项目可能缓解投资下滑，但融资和部门间协调可能带来一些挑战。

### **零售销售**

7月零售额预计同比增长1.5%，较6月1%的增幅略有改善。但这部分归功于更有利的比较基数。随着政府补贴计划效果消退，增长在2025年底开始减弱。商品和大件商品购买可能依然低迷，7月汽车销量下滑21%。

## China’s High-Tech Boom Seen Failing to Halt Slowdown of Economy

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- China's economy is expected to show a slowdown in industrial production growth and a deepening contraction in fixed-asset investment in official July data.
- The country's technology sector, particularly artificial intelligence hardware production, is booming, but this has limited impetus to employment and incomes and does not address underlying deflationary pressures.
- Retail sales are projected to rise, but at a slow pace, with purchases of goods and large-ticket items likely remaining sluggish, including a slump in car sales.
China’s economy likely kicked off the second half of 2026 in much the same way it saw out the first, with a booming technology sector failing to make up for an historic investment slump and sluggish consumption.

Official July data scheduled for release Monday are expected also to reflect extreme weather disruption, as several typhoons disrupted shipping by causing heavy rainfall and flooding.

Industrial production growth slowed and the contraction in fixed-asset investment deepened last month, according to economists surveyed by Bloomberg. Retail sales probably picked up while hovering around the worst pace in recent decades outside of the Covid lockdowns. The official jobless rate is seen inching up, though the value of that gauge has been in question since failing to capture a significant labor deterioration in recent years.

China’s persistent imbalance of recent years — solid export-driven manufacturing alongside weak domestic demand — is now being amplified by a surge in artificial intelligence hardware production. As the government seeks to lift productivity by developing AI and other cutting-edge technology, the risk is it fails to offer a broader lift to the world’s No. 2 economy.

The high degree of automation and capital intensity of AI means limited impetus to employment and incomes, and it does nothing to address China’s underlying [deflationary pressures](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/china-s-inflation-cools-as-oil-shock-of-iran-war-starts-to-ease). In some cities that benefited most from the AI hardware boom, retail sales performed even [worse](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-s-ai-boomtowns-race-ahead-as-rest-of-economy-falls-behind) than elsewhere.

“China may be upgrading successfully in precisely the direction that makes rebalancing harder,” Louise Loo, head of Asia economics at Oxford Economics, wrote in a note Thursday.

On average, the AI, robotics and innovative-pharmaceuticals businesses generate only half the jobs that older industries — including property, furniture, garments and home appliances — once did, according to Loo’s calculations.

For now, policymakers have signaled a [restrained response](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-s-top-officials-to-consider-new-stimulus-after-slowdown). After tightening the fiscal stance in the first half of the year, they’ve accelerated the sales of government bonds, which could lead to stronger spending.

They’ve also signaled a willingness to step up stimulus when needed, though China’s export strength may be keeping the pressure off for now. Shipments abroad climbed 24% in July from a year before after clocking a [27% surge](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/china-s-exports-imports-soar-faster-than-forecast-amid-ai-rush) in June.

“Policy will be data-dependent going forward, with potential new stimulus to materialize in September,” Australia & New Zealand Banking Group Ltd.’s China economists, led by Raymond Yeung, wrote in their data preview.

*“Tech alone isn’t yet strong enough to carry the wider economy. With second-quarter GDP growth slipping below 4.5% amid sluggishness in traditional sectors, continued policy support will be crucial to stem weakness outside the tech space.” — Eric Zhu. ### **Industrial Production**

Monday’s data are expected to show a 4.9% rise in industrial output compared with July last year, weakening from a 5.3% pace in June, the median estimate in Bloomberg’s survey of economists shows.

At least some of that slowdown is likely due to what’s shaped up as one of the [most intense typhoon seasons](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/pacific-typhoon-season-forecast-to-be-among-most-active-ever) for the northwest Pacific Ocean in years. Some of China’s busiest ports suspended operations due to heavy rainfall, while Typhoon Dolphin Causes Mass Flight Cancellations in Shanghai were canceled en masse in cities including Shanghai this month.

Soaring demand for AI and other tech hardware is nevertheless supporting output. High-tech manufacturing now accounts for about 25% to 30% of total industrial production, Bloomberg Economics CHINA INSIGHT: Chips, Robots, Copper - Tracking the New Economy.

### **Investment Spending**

China’s worst slump in fixed-asset investment on record — outside the Covid shock — is forecast to have deepened in July. Economists see a drop of 6.2% for the first seven months of 2026 compared with the same period a year ago, after a 5.7% decline in the first half. A retreat in government spending has dragged on the measure, while private businesses are increasingly reluctant to boost broad capital expenditures with investment returns declining.

The government is expected to accelerate the construction of a major project known as the [Six Networks](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/how-china-plans-to-supercharge-its-ai-ambitions-with-six-networks-program), which includes the build-out and upgrade of power grids, data centers, telecommunications networks and logistics. Faster work on this could cushion the fall in investment, though financing and coordination among departments could pose some challenges.

### **Retail Sales**

Retail sales are projected to rise 1.5% in July from a year ago, a slight improvement compared with June’s 1% gain. Still, that’s in part thanks to a more favorable base of comparison. Growth started to weaken toward the end of 2025 as the effect of a government subsidy program faded. Purchases of goods and large-ticket items likely remained sluggish, with car sales [slumping 21%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/china-s-car-exports-boom-as-domestic-sales-slump-margins-shrink) in July.
