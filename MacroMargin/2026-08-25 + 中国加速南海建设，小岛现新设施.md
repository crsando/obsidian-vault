## 中国加速南海建设，小岛现新设施

![A satellite view of Yagong Reef in the Crescent Group of the Paracel Islands of the South China Sea](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b148103a318f542cee6b01a/3c781cc9-0b14-810d-a798-cec986e39b0f-53e6604484944a791614898782c3213c.jpg)

2026年8月17日，南海西沙群岛永乐群岛鸭公岛卫星视图。Vantor/Handout via REUTERS

**摘要**

- 分析人士称，新建筑可能是早期预警雷达
- 小岛位于羚羊礁建设区东北14公里处
- 中国可能正在加强对南海北部的控制
香港，8月25日（路透社）——根据路透社审阅的卫星图像，在有争议的南海西沙群岛羚羊礁附近的一个小岛上发现了新的设施，分析人士认为中国正在羚羊礁建设其最大的军事基地。

安全分析人士表示，虽然现在要明确判断鸭公岛上三叉形建筑的潜在功能还为时过早，但它可能是早期预警雷达设施的起点，以支持最终在羚羊礁上的设施。

商业卫星图像供应商Vantor表示，最新图像拍摄于8月17日，并在其他图像中指出施工于3月和4月开始。

尽管这些建筑的用途尚不确定，Vantor 公司一位发言人表示，“鸭公礁上的几处建筑与南海其他地方看到的军事雷达/预警设施相似，可能与该礁附近羚羊礁的最终军事功能有关。”

路透社上周报道，中国已完成羚羊礁的第一阶段建设，凸显南海已成为中国与美国及其盟友之间日益军事化的舞台——并且可能在未来任何涉及台湾的冲突中发挥关键作用。

羚羊礁的图像显示，[一个近 6 公里（4 英里）长的人工岛](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-completes-first-stage-work-disputed-south-china-sea-reef-images-show-2026-08-19/)拥有超过 3 公里（2 英里）的直线海岸线，一些分析人士认为这可能是一条潜在跑道。

鸭公礁要小得多，位于羚羊礁东北方向 14 公里处。两者都位于西沙群岛西部的永乐群岛内。

中国国防部没有立即回应路透社的置评请求。

中国尚未承认新军事基地的建设，官方媒体表示，羚羊礁将满足天气预报、渔业保护和科学研究等民用需求。

自 1974 年从前南越海军手中夺取西沙群岛以来，中国一直占据这些岛屿。越南宣称对整个西沙群岛拥有主权，近年来已着手扩大其在更南端的南沙群岛的基地网络。

南沙群岛及其附近海域也被越南、菲律宾、马来西亚、台湾和文莱全部或部分声称拥有主权。

越南外交部未立即回应路透社的置评请求。

地区安全分析人士和武官表示，这些设施的建设更有可能加强中国在这一重要贸易水道北部的军事控制。

开放源情报追踪者达米安·西蒙（Damien Symon）曾监测西沙群岛活动，他表示雷达设施是可能的，但现在下结论还为时过早。

新加坡安全学者科林·许（Collin Koh）表示，鉴于规模有限，该设施可能是某种独立的观察哨，但“如果让我做个有根据的猜测，这很可能是一个雷达站”。

报道：Greg Torode；补充报道：北京新闻编辑室和河内的 Khanh Vu；编辑：Kate Mayberry。

## New installations emerge on islet as China accelerates South China Sea build-up

A satellite view of Yagong Reef in the Crescent Group of the Paracel Islands of the South China Sea, August 17, 2026. Vantor/Handout via REUTERS

**Summary**

- New buildings could be early warning radar, analysts say
- Islet 14 km northeast of build-up on Antelope Reef
- China potentially strengthening hold on northern part of South China Sea
HONG KONG, Aug 25 (Reuters) - New installations have been sighted on an islet near Antelope Reef in the Paracels archipelago of the disputed South China Sea where analysts believe China is building its largest military base, according to satellite imagery reviewed by Reuters.

Security analysts say while it is too early to gauge definitively the likely function of the three-pronged construction on Yagong Island, it could be the start of an early warning radar facility to support eventual installations on Antelope Reef.

Vantor, a commercial provider of satellite images, said it took the latest image on August 17, noting in other images that construction started in March and April.

While the purpose of the buildings was not certain, "several structures on Yagong Reef resemble other military radar/early warning facilities seen elsewhere in the South China Sea and are likely associated with the ultimate military functions of nearby Antelope Reef," a Vantor spokesperson said.

Reuters reported last week that China had completed the first phase of construction at Antelope Reef, highlighting the South China Sea as an increasingly militarised arena between China and the United States and its allies — and one that could prove critical in any future conflict over Taiwan.

The Antelope Reef images showed [an artificial island](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-completes-first-stage-work-disputed-south-china-sea-reef-images-show-2026-08-19/) nearly 6 km (4 miles) long, with a straight line of coast longer than 3 km (2 miles) that some analysts see as a potential runway.

Yagong is far smaller, 14 km to the northeast of Antelope. Both lie within the Crescent Group in the western Paracels.

The Chinese defence ministry did not immediately respond to Reuters' requests for comment.

China has not acknowledged the construction of a new military base, and state media have said Antelope will meet civilian needs such as weather forecasting, fisheries protection and scientific research.

China has occupied the Paracels since 1974 when it seized them from the navy of the former South Vietnam. Vietnam, which claims the entire grouping as its own, has moved in recent years to expand its own network of bases in the Spratlys, which lie further south.

The Spratlys and nearby waters are also claimed, all or in part, by Vietnam, the Philippines, Malaysia, Taiwan and Brunei.

The Vietnamese foreign ministry did not immediately respond to a Reuters' request for comment.

Regional security analysts and military attaches say the construction of features is more likely to strengthen China's military hold on the northern part of the vital trade waterway.

Open source intelligence tracker Damien Symon, who has monitored Paracels activity, said a radar facility was possible but it was too early to make a solid call.

Singapore-based security scholar Collin Koh said the installation could be some kind of standalone observation post given the limited scale but it was "most likely a radar station, if I can make an educated guess".

Reporting By Greg Torode; Additional reporting by Beijing newsroom and Khanh Vu in Hanoi; Editing by Kate Mayberry.
