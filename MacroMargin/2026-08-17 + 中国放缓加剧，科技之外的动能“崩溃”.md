### 中文译文

## 中国放缓加剧，科技之外的动能“崩溃”

疲弱的国内需求和极端天气造成的干扰，正在使本已因数月财政紧缩而疲弱的经济进一步受挫。

中国经济放缓在下半年伊始加剧，科技行业的繁荣未能带动未受全球人工智能建设热潮影响的行业。

根据国家统计局周一发布的数据，7月份工业产值同比增长4.5%，三个月来首次放缓，且低于预期。相比之下，电子设备产出飙升逾19%。

固定资产投资也出现了类似的分化，前七个月总体下降6.7%，降幅大于预期。零售额增速放缓至0.6%，同样不及预期，而调查的城镇失业率升至5.2%。

法国兴业银行包括姚炜在内的经济学家表示，中国的增长动能正在科技行业以外“崩溃”。他们在报告中表示：“中国经济数据继续令人失望，呈现越来越明显的K型分化：科技强劲，其他几乎所有领域疲弱。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b1481a89d28fde155d52a22/3bf81cc9-0b14-812d-b4e6-ed94078a5198-1aec9ecd429a7584b642a8feceb708ca.png)

随着全球人工智能投资超级周期提振对中国出口的需求，先进制造业的繁荣正在掩盖国内消费停滞，经济增长在第二季度滑落至政府4.5%-5%的年度目标下方。

极端天气造成的干扰进一步拖累了本已因数月财政紧缩而疲弱的经济。上个月，暴雨和强风席卷中国大片地区，导致工厂和港口暂时关闭，造成停电，迫使数万人疏散。

虽然这种干扰可能只是暂时影响，但政策制定者可能会密切关注这些数据，评估是否需要更多援助来确保增长目标能够实现。

7月份的数据表明，国内生产总值(GDP)增速可能放缓至4.1%左右，低于北京方面为达成全年增长目标而需在下半年达到的4.3%水平，法国巴黎银行(BNP Paribas SA)首席中国经济学家Jacqueline Rong表示。她称，总体投资和基础设施资本支出的下降速度较6月加快，上月跌幅均达到两位数。

*“第三季度初的经济动能进一步放缓，在7月底中央政治局会议之前，所有7月份主要指标甚至低于我们原本就很低的预期。在需求端，私人消费温和走弱，而投资收缩明显加深。这表明财政支出尚未从上半年的疲弱步伐中回升。”——Chang Shu和David Qu。完整分析，请点击“CHINA REACT：增长乏力，政策真空风险或致2026年目标落空”*

原定上午10点发布的数据，因国家统计局修改日程、一反近期惯例，推迟了五个小时。当天上午举行了纪念已故前国家主席江泽民诞辰100周年的政府仪式，中国领导人习近平发表主旨讲话。

国家统计局表示，尽管今年迄今为止经济保持“稳定”，但外部环境“复杂多变”，国内需求依然疲弱。国家统计局在发布数据时附带的一份声明中称：“一些企业面临经营困难，经济稳定和改善的基础仍需巩固。”

作为世界第二大经济体，中国正日益从依赖房地产的增长模式转向处于美中竞争核心的科技行业。今年前七个月，房地产[投资](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CHRXCINY%20Index)同比下降19.2%，创下历史新低。

虽然水泥等建材产量持续萎缩，但中国7月份工业机器人产量同比增长超过30%，集成电路产量跃升近21%。

尽管经济大部分领域的投资有所回落，但1月至7月，铁路、船舶、航空航天和其他运输设备制造业的资本支出飙升18.7%，为所有行业中最快。计算机、通信和其他电子设备制造商的固定资产投资增长7.8%。

中国当局现在面临着重振企业和家庭支出的艰巨任务，支出的低迷使经济依赖出口来维持增长。7月份，随着伊朗战争引发的石油冲击消退，消费者和生产者[通胀](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/china-s-inflation-cools-as-oil-shock-of-iran-war-starts-to-ease)放缓幅度均超过预期，凸显了国内需求的显著失衡，这引发了对通缩压力可能很快重现的担忧。

上个月，最高领导人在一次关键政策会议上对经济[表达了](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-s-top-officials-to-consider-new-stimulus-after-slowdown)更加支持的态度，但没有宣布新的刺激措施。相反，他们表示政府将及时规划和出台“务实有效”的新措施。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b1481a89d28fde155d52a22/3bf81cc9-0b14-81f1-bc46-f02fb5ba7a77-d0e98c789c8ede62c493f1d9efce766b.png)

据法国巴黎银行（BNP）的Rong称，政策制定者有多种选择，例如新的特别主权债券和地方债券发行额度，以及如果计划中的8000亿元人民币（合1190亿美元）在下月底前用完，他们可通过新的政策性融资工具部署额外资金。如果经济面临增速低于官方目标下限4.5%的“危险”，新措施最早可能在9月底出台。

“整体看来，一连串令人失望的数据，”荷兰国际集团（ING Bank NV）大中华区首席经济学家林恩·宋（Lynn Song）表示，“又一个月的恶化意味着未来几周和几个月内出台一些支持措施以帮助稳定增长的可能性更高。”

### English Original

## China’s Slowdown Deepens With Momentum ‘Collapsing’ Beyond Tech

Lackluster domestic demand and disruptions caused by extreme weather are setting back an economy already weakened by months of fiscal austerity.

China’s economic slowdown worsened at the start of the second half, as a boom in tech fails to lift** **sectors untouched by the global AI buildout.

Industrial production expanded 4.5% in July from a year earlier, easing for the first time in three months and missing estimates, according to data released by the National Bureau of Statistics on Monday. By contrast, output of electronic equipment soared more than 19%.

A similar divide has emerged when it comes to fixed-asset investment, which overall saw a bigger-than-forecast decline of 6.7% in the first seven months. Retail sales growth slowed to 0.6%, also underperforming expectations, while the surveyed [urban jobless rate](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNUESRU%20%20Index) climbed to 5.2%.

Societe Generale SA economists including Wei Yao said the country’s growth momentum is “collapsing” outside tech. “China’s economic data continue to disappoint, with an increasingly K-shaped pattern: strong tech, weakness almost everywhere else,” they said in a report.

As a global investment supercycle in artificial intelligence propels demand for Chinese exports, a boom in advanced manufacturing is helping mask stagnating consumption at home, with economic growth sliding below the government’s annual target of 4.5%-5% in the second quarter.

Disruptions caused by extreme weather are further setting back an economy already weakened by months of fiscal [austerity](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-22/china-steps-up-austerity-by-cutting-spending-most-since-october). Heavy rainfall and strong winds [swept](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-12/typhoon-bavi-weakens-as-heavy-rain-spreads-across-china) through large swathes of China last month, temporarily shutting down [factories](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/china-s-deadly-storms-turbocharged-by-convergence-of-factors) and ports, leading to power outages and forcing tens of thousands of people to [evacuate](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-06/floods-in-southern-china-s-guangxi-kill-two-thousands-evacuated).

While the disruption may have only a temporary impact, policymakers are likely monitoring the data closely as they assess whether more aid is needed for the economy to ensure their growth goal is within reach.

The July figures suggest growth in gross domestic product likely decelerated to around 4.1%, below the 4.3% level Beijing needs in the second half to reach its annual growth target, according to Jacqueline Rong, chief China economist at BNP Paribas SA. Declines in overall investment and capital spending on infrastructure accelerated from June by slumping at double-digit rates last month, she said.

*“Economic momentum slowed further in the early third quarter ahead of the late-July Politburo meeting, with all major July indicators falling short of even our low expectations. On the demand side, private consumption softened modestly, while the contraction in investment deepened sharply. This suggests fiscal spending has yet to pick up from its sluggish pace in the first half.” — Chang Shu and David Qu. Originally slated for publication at 10 a.m., the data release was delayed for five hours after the statistics agency revised its schedule in a break with recent practice. A government ceremony marking the 100th anniversary of late former President Jiang Zemin’s birth was being held in the morning, headlined by a speech from Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

The NBS said that while the economy has remained “stable” so far this year, the external environment is “complicated and volatile” and domestic demand has stayed weak. “Some companies are facing operational difficulties, and the foundation for the economy to stabilize and improve still needs to be consolidated,” it said in a statement accompanying the data release.

The world’s second-biggest economy is increasingly transitioning away from property-fueled growth toward tech sectors that are at the heart of US-China rivalry. Real estate [investment](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CHRXCINY%20Index) plunged 19.2% on year in the first seven months, a fresh record low.

While output of building materials such as cement has continued to shrink, China’s manufacturing of industrial robots grew more than 30% in July from a year earlier and its output of integrated circuits jumped almost 21%.

Despite an investment pullback across most of the economy, capital spending in railway, shipping, aerospace and other transport equipment manufacturing soared 18.7% in January-July, the fastest among all industries. Investment by producers of computers, telecommunications and other electronics equipment gained 7.8%.

Chinese authorities now face a daunting task of reviving business and household spending whose downturn has left the economy reliant on exports to sustain growth. Underscoring a pronounced imbalance in domestic demand, both consumer and producer [inflation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/china-s-inflation-cools-as-oil-shock-of-iran-war-starts-to-ease) slowed more than expected in July as the oil shock from the Iran war subsided, sparking concern that deflationary pressures could re-emerge soon.

Top leaders [struck](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-s-top-officials-to-consider-new-stimulus-after-slowdown) a more supportive tone on the economy at a key policy meeting last month but stopped short of announcing fresh stimulus. Instead, they said the government will plan and introduce “pragmatic and effective” new measures in a timely manner.

Policymakers have options such as new quota for special sovereign and local bond issuance, and the additional funding they can deploy via new policy financing tools if the planned amount of 800 billion yuan ($119 billion) is used up by the end of next month, according to BNP’s Rong. Should the economy be “in danger” of growing less than the 4.5% lower end of the official target, new measures are possible as soon as the end of September.

“It looks like a slate of disappointing data across the board,” said Lynn Song, chief economist for Greater China at ING Bank NV. “Another month of deterioration suggests higher odds for some support in the coming weeks and months to help stabilize growth.”
