### 中文译文

## 伊朗神秘统治者以新领导层向特朗普传递强硬信号

穆杰塔巴·哈梅内伊全面改组国家安全事务负责人，在战争开始后接管的政府中打上自己的印记。

作者：伯努瓦·福孔

2026年8月10日 晚上10:00（美国东部时间）

7月，哀悼者参加伊朗最高领袖阿亚图拉·阿里·哈梅内伊的葬礼游行。图片来源：法新社/盖蒂图片社

当美国和以色列在2月开始对[伊朗](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran)发动空袭时，袭击打死了最高领袖阿里·哈梅内伊及其几乎整个安全事务负责人团队，消灭了一代军事政策决策者。

如今，[新的最高领袖](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/how-iran-is-building-a-religious-mythology-around-its-new-supreme-leader-1a8ec172?mod=article_inline)——哈梅内伊之子穆杰塔巴——正在与美国的一场可能持续数月甚至数年的对抗中，对国家的国家安全政策打上自己的印记。

在周日和周一政府对最高层进行全面改组中，伊朗任命经验丰富的强硬派掌管国家安全政策和镇压机构。

这标志着穆杰塔巴·哈梅内伊领导下最重要的一次政府改组。自战争开始以来，他从未公开露面。美国情报机构称他还活着但伤势严重，而伊朗内阁高级成员表示自他上任以来从未见过他。伊朗官员称他虽受伤但健康状况良好。

分析人士称，这些任命表明哈梅内伊决心在与特朗普总统的对峙中坚持立场，[后者正寻求在伊朗核计划和霍尔木兹海峡问题上获得让步](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-strait-of-hormuz-opening-iran-negotiations-b6a43ad6?mod=article_inline)。

“该政权正在准备在国内和国际上采取更具对抗性的姿态，”反核伊朗联盟（一个反对伊朗政府的政策组织）的伊斯兰革命卫队（一支强大的准军事部队）专家卡斯拉·阿拉比表示。

新领导人中的首要人物是穆赫森·雷扎伊，现任伊朗最高国家安全委员会秘书，曾任革命卫队司令。作为多次竞选总统的全国性人物，雷扎伊是革命卫队任职时间最长的指挥官，并在1980年代两伊战争期间领导了这支准军事部队。

革命卫队的新领导人是准将艾哈迈德·瓦希迪，他坚决反对向美国让步。此外，哈梅内伊任命侯赛因·塔伊布为巴斯基指挥官，该志愿者部队负责镇压抗议和其他反对政权的异见。塔伊布是哈梅内伊的长期门徒，在2009年血腥镇压抗议期间也曾领导巴斯基。

穆赫森·雷扎伊被任命为伊朗最高国家安全委员会秘书——这是该国新任最高领袖任命的新领导人中的首要职位。Mohammed Salem/路透社

艾哈迈德·瓦希迪是伊斯兰革命卫队的新领导人。Alkis Konstantinidis/路透社

雷扎伊、瓦希迪和塔伊布都接替了在战争中阵亡的官员，周一还有四名指挥官被任命，其中包括革命卫队海军司令，该部队在袭击霍尔木兹海峡船只方面发挥了主导作用。

作为雷扎伊新权力的标志，哈梅内伊还任命他为自己在最高国家安全委员会的个人代表，该委员会负责决定最重要的军事和外交事务。在此之前，雷扎伊是最高领袖的高级军事顾问。

现年71岁的雷扎伊在开放霍尔木兹海峡这一石油工业命脉问题上表现出[毫无妥协意向](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-sees-an-opening-to-kick-the-u-s-out-of-the-gulf-b9f9cc53?mod=article_inline)。上周末，他表示伊朗不会同意任何其无法控制的水道走廊。专家和调解人表示，他或许能帮助伊朗以更统一的声音发声。

由于政治领导人与安全部门之间的冲突，伊朗的谈判经历多次反复。最高国家安全委员会数日来一直在权衡该协议，但未能达成共识。

雷扎伊接替72岁的穆罕默德·巴盖尔·佐勒格德尔担任委员会秘书，后者行事低调但长期担任高级情报官员。

“哈梅内伊需要在安全委员会中有一个更强有力的角色，能够在政府和安全机构之间凝聚共识，”华盛顿智库国际政策中心负责伊朗事务的研究员西纳·图西表示。

“作为长期担任伊斯兰革命卫队（IRGC）指挥官的前任和经验丰富的政治人物，雷扎伊能够做到这一点，”图西补充道，他使用了革命卫队的缩写。

雷扎伊1954年出生于伊朗西南部的马斯吉德苏莱曼，受过机械工程师训练。他曾加入al-Mansouroun（曼苏伦）——一个对抗国王政权的游击组织，该组织后来成为伊朗未来安全机构的试验场。他的前任佐勒格德尔和长期担任委员会秘书、于2月28日遇害的阿里·沙姆哈尼，都曾在该组织中崭露头角。

在1979年加入推翻国王的伊斯兰革命后，雷扎伊在伊朗与伊拉克战争期间加入革命卫队，于1981年成为总司令，并领导这支准军事组织长达16年。

在他担任革命卫队最高指挥官期间，阿根廷当局指控他参与了1994年布宜诺斯艾利斯犹太人文化中心自杀式爆炸事件，该事件造成85人死亡。

1997年，改革派总统穆罕默德·哈塔米解除了他对革命卫队的指挥权。根据美国外交电报，他还与哈塔米的继任者、好斗的马哈茂德·艾哈迈迪内贾德发生冲突，不赞成其民粹主义经济政策和对西方的挑衅行为。

根据美国国防部2009年的一份报告，在艾哈迈迪内贾德执政期间，雷扎伊成为务实保守派群体中的关键成员，这些退役革命卫队成员希望维护伊斯兰共和国，但也主张与西方进行谨慎接触。

![Mohsen Rezaei, as seen in 2013, ran for president in Iran.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b981cc90b14818fb222ed009831250b/3b981cc9-0b14-8117-92f8-d619f5aaae6a-62e425bdea92162cffeedf049f1d76ff.jpg)

雷扎伊数十年来在伊朗不断变化的政治风云中历练，养成了随机应变的特点。2012年，他在伦敦通过视频会议向一群支持政府的伊朗学生发表讲话时，被问到如果伊朗遭到以色列攻击会如何回应。

“以色列将至少有10,000人伤亡，”他以激昂的语气对听众说。

随后，《华尔街日报》记者在一次采访中向他问及一项通过引入外国运营商来降低伊朗核计划威胁的提议——这一想法遭到该国强硬派的激烈反对——他表现出了灵活性。

“我们对与外国公司的双边关系感兴趣，”他说，“铀浓缩不一定非要在伊朗境内进行。”

尽管在战前他被视为务实派，但在为莫杰塔巴·哈梅内伊工作期间，他发出了挑衅的语气，称特朗普“精神失常”，并要求华盛顿按伊朗的条件投降。

田纳西大学查塔努加分校的伊朗安全事务专家赛义德·戈尔加表示，他的任命可能表明“该体系将加大力度，以平衡、遏制或边缘化那些主张维持停火或走向某种和解的人”。

民主防御基金会伊朗项目高级主任贝赫纳姆·本·塔勒布卢表示，雷扎伊被任命接替另一位年逾七旬的官员，这表明政府无法使其队伍年轻化。

“伊斯兰共和国只是在泰坦尼克号上摆放甲板椅，”本·塔勒布卢说，他的智库主张对伊朗政府采取强硬政策。

## Iran’s Mysterious Ruler Signals a Hard Line on Trump With New Leadership

Mojtaba Khamenei overhauled the country’s security chiefs, putting his stamp on a government he took over after the war began

By Benoit Faucon

Aug. 10, 2026 10:00 pm ET

Mourners attend the funeral procession in July for Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. AFP/Getty Images

When the U.S. and Israel began airstrikes on [Iran](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran) in February, the attacks killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and almost his entire team of security chiefs, wiping out a generation of decisionmakers on military policy.

Now, [the new supreme leader](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/how-iran-is-building-a-religious-mythology-around-its-new-supreme-leader-1a8ec172?mod=article_inline), Khamenei’s son Mojtaba, is putting his own stamp on the country’s national-security policy amid a confrontation with the U.S. that could last months or even years.

In a sweeping overhaul of the government’s top echelon on Sunday and Monday, Iran named seasoned hard-liners to run the country’s security policies and institutions of repression.

It marked the most significant government reshuffle under Mojtaba Khamenei, who hasn’t been seen in public since the war began. U.S. intelligence agencies say he is alive but severely injured, and top Iranian cabinet members say they have never met him since he took office. Iranian officials say he was injured but in good health.

Analysts said the appointments signaled Khamenei’s determination to hold fast in a showdown with President Trump, [who is seeking concessions](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-strait-of-hormuz-opening-iran-negotiations-b6a43ad6?mod=article_inline) on Iran’s nuclear program and the Strait of Hormuz.

“The regime is preparing for a more confrontational posture at home and abroad,” said Kasra Aarabi, an expert on the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a powerful paramilitary force, at United Against Nuclear Iran, a policy organization that opposes Iran’s government.

Chief among the new leaders is Mohsen Rezaei, now the secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council and former commander of the Revolutionary Guard. A national figure who ran for president multiple times, Rezaei was the Revolutionary Guard’s longest-serving chief and led the paramilitary force through the war with Iraq in the 1980s.

The new leader of the Revolutionary Guard is Brig. Gen. Ahmad Vahidi, a forceful opponent of making concessions to the U.S. And Mojtaba Khamenei named Hossein Taeb to be commander of the Basij, a volunteer force tasked with repressing protests and other dissent against the regime. A longtime protégé of Khamenei, Taeb also led the Basij during a bloody crackdown on protests in 2009.

Mohsen Rezaei was named the secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council—chief among the new leaders named by the country’s new supreme leader. Mohammed Salem/Reuters

Ahmad Vahidi is the new leader of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Alkis Konstantinidis/Reuters

Rezaei, Vahidi and Taeb all succeed officials killed in the war, as did four other commanders named on Monday, including the head of the Revolutionary Guard’s navy, a division that has played a leading role in attacking ships in Hormuz.

In a sign of Rezaei’s new power, Khamenei also appointed him as his personal representative on the Supreme National Security Council, the body tasked with deciding the most important military and diplomatic matters. Until now, Rezaei was a top military adviser to the Supreme Leader.

Rezaei, 71 years old, has shown [no inclination to compromise](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-sees-an-opening-to-kick-the-u-s-out-of-the-gulf-b9f9cc53?mod=article_inline) on opening the Strait of Hormuz, an artery vital to the oil industry. Over the weekend, he said Iran wouldn’t agree to any corridor through the waterway that it doesn’t control. Experts and mediators said he might be able to help Iran speak with a more unified voice.

Iran’s negotiations have whipsawed through repeated reversals as political leaders and security services clashed. The Supreme National Security Council has been weighing the deal for days but has been unable to reach a consensus.

Rezaei replaces Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, 72, a low-key yet longtime high-ranking intelligence operative, as secretary of the council.

“Khamenei needs a stronger figure at the security council who can forge consensus across the government and the security establishment,” said Sina Toossi, an Iran-focused fellow at the Center for International Policy, a Washington think tank.

“Rezaei can deliver that as a former longtime IRGC commander and veteran political operator,” Toossi added, using the acronym for the Revolutionary Guard.

Born in 1954 in Masjed Soleyman in southwestern Iran and trained as a mechanical engineer, Rezaei joined al-Mansouroun, a guerrilla group fighting the Shah’s regime that became a proving ground for Iran’s future security establishment. Zolghadr, his immediate predecessor, and Ali Shamkhani, a long-serving secretary of the council who was killed Feb. 28, all earned their stripes in the group.

After joining the Islamic revolution that toppled the Shah in 1979, Rezaei joined the Revolutionary Guard during Iran’s war with Iraq, becoming its commander in chief in 1981 and leading the paramilitary group for 16 years.

While he was the Revolutionary Guard’s top commander, Argentine authorities alleged he was involved in the 1994 suicide bombing of a Jewish cultural center in Buenos Aires that killed 85.

He was removed from the command of the Revolutionary Guard in 1997 by reformist President Mohammad Khatami. He also clashed with Khatami’s successor, firebrand Mahmoud Ahmadinejad, disapproving of his populist economic policies and aggressive provocations against the West, according to U.S. diplomatic cables.

Under Ahmadinejad, Rezaei emerged as a key member of a group of pragmatic conservatives, retired Revolutionary Guards who wanted to preserve the Islamic Republic but who also promoted cautious engagement with the West, according to a 2009 Defense Department report.

Rezaei’s decades navigating Iran’s changing political waters have given him an adaptive streak. Speaking by videoconference to a gathering of pro-government Iranian students in London in 2012, Rezaei was asked how Iran would respond if it was attacked by Israel.

“There would be at least 10,000 casualties in Israel,” he told his audience, with a fiery tone.

When a Wall Street Journal reporter asked him in an interview afterward about a proposal to make Iran’s nuclear program less threatening by bringing in a foreign operator—an idea fiercely opposed by the country’s hard-liners—he showed flexibility.

“We are interested in bilateral relations with foreign companies,” he said. “Enrichment does not have to be inside Iran.”

Though he was seen as a pragmatist in the days before the war, he has sounded a defiant tone while working for Mojtaba Khamenei, calling Trump “unhinged” and demanding Washington surrender on Iran’s terms.

His appointment may signal “more of an effort by the system to balance, contain, or marginalize those who favor maintaining the ceasefire or moving toward some kind of accommodation,” said Saeid Golgar, an expert on Iran’s security services at the University of Tennessee in Chattanooga.

Behnam Ben Taleblu, a senior director of the Iran program at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, said Rezaei’s appointment to replace another septuagenarian showed the government was unable to rejuvenate its ranks.

“The Islamic Republic is merely arranging deck chairs on the Titanic,” said Ben Taleblu, whose think tank advocates hard-line policies on Iran’s government.
