Aug 3 (Reuters) - China's state-run trading companies bought 14-16 cargoes of U.S. soybeans on Friday, four Asian traders told Reuters on Monday, taking advantage of lower prices in a flurry of purchases ahead of President Xi Jinping's expected U.S. visit next month.

8 月 3 日（路透社）——四位亚洲贸易商周一透露，中国国有贸易企业上周五购买了 14 至 16 船美国大豆，利用价格走低之际集中采购，此举发生在中国国家主席习近平预计下月访美之前。

The unusually large purchases total about one million metric tons, with each cargo ⁠carrying about 65,000 metric tons scheduled for shipment in October, the sources said.

消息人士称，这批规模罕见的采购总量约达 100 万公吨，每船载货约 6.5 万公吨，计划于 10 月装运。

"These deals were mainly done by Sinograin and the main reason is a drop in prices last week. There are plans for President Xi to visit the U.S. in September so China is also trying to buy U.S. beans under its commitment made to Washington," said one Asia-based trader at an international trading company.

The [White House announced](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/what-did-trump-xi-agree-tariffs-export-controls-fentanyl-2025-11-01/) in October that China had agreed to buy 25 million tons of U.S. soybeans annually until the end of 2028. Before Friday's purchases, ‌China ⁠had bought slightly more than 4 million tons this year.

The companies paid a premium of $3.03 per bushel over the November Chicago Board of Trade contract for shipment from the U.S. Gulf and a $3 premium for Pacific Northwest shipments, said one source. The most active soybean contract fell 5.2% last ⁠week.

State-run companies Sinograin and COFCO did not respond to requests for comment or confirmation of the purchases.

China's Sinograin sold about half of the 504,000 tons of imported soybeans on offer at auction on Friday as ⁠the state stockpiler makes room for incoming U.S. cargoes.

In late July, U.S. President Donald Trump said his Chinese counterpart Xi [would visit](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/trump-says-xi-will-visit-us-september-24-2026-07-23/) the country on September 24.

Markets are waiting to ⁠see whether China will remove tariffs on U.S. soybeans, which would allow private crushers to participate in purchases.

It remains unclear, however, whether U.S. soybeans would be competitive enough to attract these price-sensitive buyers.
