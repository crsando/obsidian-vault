### 中文译文

## 贝森特干预未能安抚投资者，美国长期国债下滑

美国长期国债周四遭遇新一轮抛售，因财政部长斯科特·贝森特支撑市场的干预未能缓解投资者的紧张情绪。

30年期美国国债收益率在周四纽约早盘交易中一度上涨0.07个百分点，至5.27%，逆转了周三[美国财政部公告](https://www.ft.com/content/777c9014-2f12-45cf-8224-6bbe808c62cb?syn-25a6b1a6=1)后0.09个百分点跌幅中的大部分，随后回落至5.25%。债券收益率与价格走势相反。

美国财政部周三表示，将“至少加倍”购买期限在10至30年期的[证券](https://www.ft.com/us-treasury-bonds)，将这些操作从20亿美元增加至“至少”40亿美元。

TwentyFour资产管理公司投资组合经理约恩·沃尔什表示：“我们认为这种干预孤立来看不会成功。这样的干预措施看起来像是创可贴。”

其他全球债券也遭到抛售。30年期英国国债收益率在周三下跌0.05个百分点后，周四上涨0.02个百分点至5.81%。

美元在周三公告后大幅下跌，周四继续下滑。美元兑一篮子货币的美元指数在周三下跌0.8%后，周四一度下跌0.3%。随后收复了周四早盘约一半的跌幅，最终下跌0.1%。

周四的抛售凸显了投资者对美国不断攀升的公共债务负担，以及决策者能否遏制美国总统唐纳德·特朗普对伊朗战争引发的通胀爆发的深切担忧。这些担忧近几个月来尤其拖累长期债务，30年期收益率从6月底的4.9%攀升。

华尔街分析师中许多人对此前财政部的回购计划感到意外，他们表示，此举的规模不太可能大到足以抵消投资者的担忧。

专业服务公司RSM US的首席经济学家乔·布鲁苏埃拉斯表示：“如果不转向财政整顿——提高税收、[a]放缓政府支出步伐，或者像20世纪90年代那样政府支出绝对下降——那么回购将被证明只是暂时的。”

美国财政部数据显示，周二美国国债达到创纪录的40万亿美元。近年来，政府持续出现占GDP 6%的赤字，由于特朗普的减税政策和将国防支出提高50%以上至每年1.5万亿美元的承诺，预计短期内不会减少赤字。

三菱日联金融集团（MUFG）的分析师表示，人们感觉美国财政部这次临时公告“缺乏战略计划”。

他们表示：“这一公告发布后的危险在于……它可能适得其反，导致持有美国资产的兴趣下降，或是对美元敞口的兴趣下降，或者两者兼而有之。”

他们补充说，贝森特没有遵循自己的建议——专注于政策而非市场信号——未能通过财政整顿减少美国财政赤字。

Columbia Threadneedle Investments全球利率和非约束固定收益主管基思·巴顿表示，贝森特公布的计划与美联储成功的量化宽松计划相比“微不足道”。

他表示：“此类干预只有在随后推出关键政策举措时才会奏效，尤其是在支出方面。”他补充说，在“可预见的未来”预计不会削减支出。

法国兴业银行（Société Générale）全球外汇策略主管基特·朱克斯表示，美国财政部的公告能否压低收益率“还有待商榷”。

“随着美国公众持有的债务水平达到GDP的100%，且预算赤字仍然很高”，外国投资者购买美国资产的意愿“将成为一个日益严重的问题”，他表示。

他补充说，这“要么迫使美国收紧财政政策，要么接受更高的借贷成本，要么让美元贬值。不用猜也知道市场现在认为哪种解决方案最有可能。”

### English Original

## US long-term bonds slide as Bessent intervention fails to soothe investors

US long-term government bonds were hit with a fresh wave of selling on Thursday as Treasury secretary Scott Bessent’s intervention to prop up the market failed to soothe investor jitters.

The yield on 30-year Treasury bonds was up as much as 0.07 percentage points at 5.27 per cent in New York morning trading on Thursday, reversing most of the 0.09 percentage point fall that followed the [Treasury announcement](https://www.ft.com/content/777c9014-2f12-45cf-8224-6bbe808c62cb?syn-25a6b1a6=1) on Wednesday, before dipping to 5.25 per cent. Bond yields move inversely to prices.

The Treasury said on Wednesday it would “at least double” its purchases of [securities](https://www.ft.com/us-treasury-bonds) maturing in 10 to 30 years, increasing such operations from $2bn to “at least” $4bn.

“We don’t think this [intervention] can succeed, in isolation,” said Eoin Walsh, portfolio manager at TwentyFour Asset Management. “Interventions such as this look like a sticking plaster.”

Other global bonds also sold off. The yield on 30-year UK gilts rose 0.02 percentage points to 5.81 per cent after falling 0.05 percentage points on Wednesday.

The US dollar, which fell sharply following Wednesday’s announcement, continued its slide on Thursday. An index of the dollar’s value against a basket of its peers fell as much as 0.3 per cent after a 0.8 per cent fall on Wednesday. It later recovered about half of Thursday’s early losses to trade down 0.1 per cent.

The selling on Thursday underscores the deep concern among investors over the US’s mounting public debt burden and whether policymakers will be able to contain the eruption of inflation triggered by US President Donald Trump’s war in Iran. Those concerns have particularly weighed on longer-term debt in recent months, with the 30-year yield climbing from 4.9 per cent at the end of June.

Wall Street analysts, many of whom were surprised by the Treasury buyback plan, said the move was unlikely to be large enough to offset investor concerns.

“Without a shift to fiscal consolidation — higher taxes, [a] slower pace of government spending or outright declines in government spending as happened in the 1990s — the buybacks will prove to be only temporary,” said Joe Brusuelas, chief economist at professional services firm RSM US.

The US national debt reached a record $40tn on Tuesday, according to Treasury department data. The government has consistently run deficits of 6 per cent of GDP in recent years and is not expected to reduce them any time soon, owing to Trump’s tax cuts and pledge to raise defence spending by more than 50 per cent to $1.5tn a year.

Analysts at MUFG said there was a sense that the unscheduled announcement by the US Treasury was “lacking a strategic plan”.

“The danger now following this announcement . . . is that it proves counter-productive and leads to reduced appetite for either holding US assets or reduced appetite for exposure to the US dollar or both,” they said.

They added that Bessent was not following his own advice to focus on policy rather than market signalling by failing to reduce the US fiscal deficit through fiscal consolidation.

Keith Patton, head of global rates and unconstrained fixed income at Columbia Threadneedle Investments, said the plan announced by Bessent was “minuscule” compared with the Fed’s successful quantitative easing programmes.

“Such interventions only work if they are followed up with key policy initiatives, especially around spending,” he said. Lower spending was not expected for the “foreseeable future”, he added.

Kit Juckes, global head of foreign exchange strategy at Société Générale, said it was “up for debate” whether the Treasury’s announcement would hold yields down.

“As the US publicly held debt level reaches 100 per cent [of] GDP and budget deficits remain high”, the willingness of foreign investors to buy US assets “will be a growing issue”, he said.

He added that this would “either force the US to tighten fiscal policy, accept higher borrowing costs or let the dollar weaken. No prizes for guessing which solution the market now sees as most likely.”
