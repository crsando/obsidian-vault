### 中文译文

## AI股票抛售加剧，投资者抛售芯片股

![Chip and memory stocks powered global markets higher in the first half of the year as investors bet they would be the biggest beneficiaries of vast AI spending © SeongJoon Cho/Bloomberg](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A28d7aa37-e06c-44e0-a6ef-028f765a2b8a?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

芯片和存储类股在今年上半年推动全球市场走高，因投资者押注它们将是庞大人工智能支出最大的受益者 © SeongJoon Cho/彭博社

全球芯片类股的溃败在周二加剧，对人工智能热潮持久性的担忧在本周硅谷一些最大公司发布财报之前升温。

韩国Kospi指数领跌亚洲股市，下跌逾10%并引发短暂暂停交易，此前投资者抛售了芯片制造商SK海力士和三星电子的股票。

美国芯片和存储类股周二在华尔街开盘时势将延续前一日的跌幅，追踪纳斯达克100指数的期货下跌1%。

芯片和存储类股在今年上半年推动全球市场走高，因投资者押注它们将是庞大人工智能支出最大的受益者，但近几周随着许多押注逆转，它们承受了残酷抛售的重创。

“对融资不确定性、资本支出增加和大型科技公司自由现金流的担忧已成为焦点，”巴克莱美国股票策略主管Venu Krishna表示。

存储巨头闪迪周一暴跌11%，周二盘前交易中再跌逾5%。该股过去一个月市值蒸发逾40%，但自2026年初以来仍上涨逾五倍。

西部数据、美光科技、超微半导体和英特尔在盘前交易中均至少下跌4%。

在欧洲，全球最大的芯片制造设备制造商阿斯麦（ASML）下跌2.6%。

在首尔又一个波动交易日中，[SK海力士](https://www.ft.com/stream/dc958f59-a536-4441-8c31-52b91ac60e31)周一下跌14.7%，而其规模更大的竞争对手三星电子下跌13.4%。两家公司的股价在7月的芯片股抛售中均下跌超过三分之一——尽管年初至今仍分别上涨138%和83%。

对未来供应过剩的担忧也打压了市场情绪，因为领先的芯片制造商最近公布了激进的扩张计划。

SK海力士和三星计划各自在韩国新建两家芯片工厂，作为总计800万亿韩元（约合5480亿美元）投资的一部分，以在五年内将DRam芯片的产能翻倍。

在东京，日经225指数下跌4.4%，[存储芯片制造商铠侠](https://www.ft.com/content/22bc5436-d5a1-4d03-b8c0-5840f1e3a515?syn-25a6b1a6=1)暴跌逾18%。本月抛售导致铠侠股价腰斩。

全球动荡之际，华尔街正处于第二季度财报季中期，科技巨头微软、Meta、苹果和亚马逊均将于本周公布业绩。

迄今公布的结果显示，投资者对AI集团的大规模支出是否可持续感到紧张，巨额支出报告受到交易员惩罚。Alphabet的股价上周一天内下跌7%，此前该公司宣布谷歌在第二季度烧钱以资助AI基础设施支出。

"市场担心额外借贷、额外资本支出以及其可持续性，"道富银行股票研究主管Marija Veitmane表示。她补充道："负面动能正在积聚，"将此次抛售描述为"螺旋式下跌"。

然而，Veitmane表示：“从迄今已公布业绩的公司来看，需求仍然非常强劲……对我们来说，这是一个逢低买入的机会。”

资产管理公司GAM集团首席执行官Albert Saporta将这一走势归因于“人们意识到，这场AI资本支出狂热最终将像所有资本支出狂热一样以崩溃告终”。

Saporta补充称，[信用违约互换](https://www.ft.com/content/ac136522-ecc7-4262-8702-e0d636ea3099?syn-25a6b1a6=1)价格上涨——用于做空与甲骨文(Oracle)、SpaceX、Alphabet、亚马逊(Amazon)、Meta、博通(Broadcom)和英伟达(Nvidia)等公司相关的公司债——表明投资者对美国科技集团的AI支出计划进行“清算”。

中国人工智能公司取得进展的迹象加剧了投资者的焦虑。

德意志银行(Deutsche Bank)分析师Jim Reid指出：“科技方面的担忧今天上午成为亚洲市场的主要驱动力，对AI投资支出以及来自成本较低的中国公司的竞争的再度担忧，引发了全球半导体股票的又一轮抛售。”

中国AI初创公司Moonshot上周发布了一个大语言模型，其能力似乎接近Anthropic等美国前沿实验室的水平，这一发现令投资者感到不安，他们此前一直押注硅谷巨头将维持其庞大的支出计划以保持领先地位。

在中国，存储芯片制造商长鑫存储(CXMT)股价周二下跌逾4%，此前一天该公司在上海[大规模上市](https://www.ft.com/content/8e82e939-908b-42bf-a314-0bb02a3f1b07?syn-25a6b1a6=1)融资85亿美元。其股价周一上涨466%。

## AI stock sell-off deepens as investors dump chipmakers

Chip and memory stocks powered global markets higher in the first half of the year as investors bet they would be the biggest beneficiaries of vast AI spending © SeongJoon Cho/Bloomberg

A rout in global chipmakers deepened on Tuesday, as fears over the durability of the AI boom intensified ahead of results from some of Silicon Valley’s biggest companies this week.

South Korea’s Kospi led declines in Asia, falling more than 10 per cent and prompting a short halt in trading, after investors dumped shares in chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics.

US chip and memory stocks were set to extend the previous day’s losses at the Wall Street open on Tuesday, with futures tracking the Nasdaq 100 index down 1 per cent.

Chip and memory stocks, which powered global markets higher in the first half of the year as investors bet they would be the biggest beneficiaries of vast AI spending, have borne the brunt of a brutal sell-off in recent weeks as many of those bets have unwound.

“Worries around funding uncertainties, capex increases and Big Tech free cash flow have taken centre stage,” said Venu Krishna, head of US equities strategy at Barclays.

Memory giant Sandisk — which plunged 11 per cent on Monday — was down more than 5 per cent in pre-market trading on Tuesday. The stock has lost more than 40 per cent of its value over the past month, but nevertheless remains more than five times higher since the start of 2026.

Western Digital, Micron, Advanced Micro Devices and Intel were all down at least 4 per cent in pre-market trading.

In Europe, ASML, the world’s biggest maker of chip-manufacturing equipment, fell 2.6 per cent.

In another volatile day of trading in Seoul, [SK Hynix](https://www.ft.com/stream/dc958f59-a536-4441-8c31-52b91ac60e31) fell 14.7 per cent on Monday, while its larger rival, Samsung Electronics, dropped 13.4 per cent. Both companies’ share prices have dropped more than a third in July’s chipmaker sell-off — although still remain 138 per cent and 83 per cent higher year-to-date.

Concerns over future oversupply have also weighed on sentiment, as leading chipmakers have recently unveiled aggressive expansion plans.

SK Hynix and Samsung plan to build two new chip plants apiece in South Korea as part of a combined Won800tn ($548bn) investment to double their production capacity for DRam chips over five years.

In Tokyo, the Nikkei 225 fell 4.4 per cent, with [memory-chip maker Kioxia](https://www.ft.com/content/22bc5436-d5a1-4d03-b8c0-5840f1e3a515?syn-25a6b1a6=1) plunging more than 18 per cent. This month’s sell-off has slashed Kioxia’s share price in half.

The global turbulence comes as Wall Street is in the middle of the second-quarter earnings season, with tech giants Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon all set to deliver results this week.

Results so far have revealed investors’ nervousness about whether the big spending by AI groups will be sustainable, with reports of large spending being punished by traders. Alphabet’s share price dropped 7 per cent in one day last week, following the company’s announcement that Google had burned through cash in the second quarter to fund AI infrastructure spending.

“The market is worried about extra borrowing, extra capex, how sustainable it is,” said Marija Veitmane, head of equity research at State Street. “Negative momentum is building up,” she added, describing the sell-off as a “spiral”.

However, Veitmane said: “Reading through the results from the companies that have reported so far, demand is still fantastically strong . . . for us it’s a buy the dip opportunity.”

Albert Saporta, group chief executive of asset manager GAM, attributed the moves to “a realisation that this AI capex frenzy will end up in a bust like all capex frenzies”.

Saporta added that rising prices for [credit default swaps](https://www.ft.com/content/ac136522-ecc7-4262-8702-e0d636ea3099?syn-25a6b1a6=1) — used to bet against corporate debt tied to companies such as Oracle, SpaceX, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom and Nvidia — pointed to “an investor reckoning” over the AI spending plans of US tech groups.

Signs that Chinese AI companies are making advances have added to the anxiety among investors.

“Tech concerns have been the dominant driver in Asia this morning as renewed worries over AI investment spending and competition from cheaper Chinese companies have triggered another sell-off in global semiconductor stocks,” noted Jim Reid, an analyst at Deutsche Bank.

Chinese AI start-up Moonshot last week released a large language model which appeared to have capabilities approaching those of frontier US labs such as Anthropic, a revelation that rattled investors who have been betting that Silicon Valley’s giants will keep up their vast spending plans to stay ahead.

In China, shares in memory-chip maker CXMT fell more than 4 per cent on Tuesday, a day after it raised $8.5bn in a [blockbuster listing](https://www.ft.com/content/8e82e939-908b-42bf-a314-0bb02a3f1b07?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in Shanghai. Its shares rose 466 per cent on Monday.

*Additional reporting by William Sandlund in Hong Kong*
