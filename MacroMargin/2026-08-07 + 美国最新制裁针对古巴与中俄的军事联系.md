### 中文译文

## 美国最新制裁针对古巴与中俄的军事联系

唐纳德·特朗普政府又制裁了八名古巴官员——包括与美国竞争对手合作的高级军事人员——继续向这个共产党领导的加勒比岛国施压。

根据美国财政部外国资产控制办公室周四发布的最新[指引](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260806)，古巴驻华大使馆武官 Waldo Pérez Cortés 和其驻俄罗斯武官 Monica Milián Gómez 被列入美国财政部黑名单。

同样被点名的还有古巴军方经济局局长奥斯卡·恩里克·比奥斯卡·加列戈将军；军事工业联盟（UIM）总干事罗伯托·耶稣·维西亚纳·穆塞特；以及武装力量总参谋部副总参谋长罗伯托·莱格拉·索托隆戈。

美国国务院在一份[情况说明](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/targeting-enablers-of-the-cuban-regimes-arms-imports-and-foreign-military-cooperation-fact-sheet/)中表示，上述被点名者大多负责试图组织从中国和俄罗斯进行的武器转让。

戈麦斯被[国际人权协会](https://ishr.org/cubans-as-cannon-fodder/)等组织单独指认参与招募古巴人赴乌克兰为俄罗斯作战。哈瓦那政府长期以来一直否认其在将雇佣军送往前线方面扮演任何角色。

美国国务卿马可·鲁比奥在一份[声明](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/targeting-enablers-of-the-cuban-regimes-arms-imports-and-foreign-military-cooperation/)中表示，周四的制裁指定旨在“通过制裁那些为哈瓦那军事关系及向古巴政权输送武器的实体和个人，保护我们的国家安全”。

除上述军官外，周四还制裁了国有军事贸易公司 Tecnotex 和 Technoimport，以及维修与修理企业尤里·加加林军事工业企业、贸易集团 Duna SA 和 UIM。

特朗普和鲁比奥几乎每周都对古巴实施制裁，试图胁迫哈瓦那领导层在近七十年的一党统治后放弃权力。

美国的措施——包括自年初以来切断了除一批主要燃料外的所有对岛燃料运输的前所未有的[石油封锁](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-22/can-cuba-s-regime-survive-rising-us-pressure)——加剧了古巴普通民众的痛苦。

这个拥有940万人口的国家在不到一个月内经历了四次[全国性停电](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/cuba-suffers-fourth-nationwide-blackout-in-less-than-a-month)，政府部分归咎于美国的压力。

另外在周四，联合国专家小组表示制裁正在干扰该岛的食物供应。他们呼吁国际社会防止古巴出现“[无声的加沙](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/08/cuba-un-experts-condemn-new-us-sanctions-humanitarian-crisis-deepens)”的局面。

“食物绝不能被用作政治压力的工具，”联合国专家表示。“明知会使民众失去生存手段的措施，冲击了生命权最基本的保障，削弱了人类尊严的核心。”

### English Original

## Fresh US Sanctions Target Cuba’s Military Ties to China and Russia

Donald Trump’s administration sanctioned eight more Cuban officials — including high-ranking military personnel working with US rivals — as it continues to turn the screws on the communist-run Caribbean island.

Cuba’s military attache at its embassy in China, Waldo Pérez Cortés, and his counterpart in Russia, Monica Milián Gómez, were added to the US Treasury Department’s blacklist, according to updated Office of Foreign Assets Control [guidance](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260806) issued Thursday.

Also named are General Oscar Enrique Biosca Gallego, the head of the Cuban military’s economic directorate; Roberto Jesus Viciana Mousset, the director general of the Union de Industria Militar, or UIM; and Roberto Legra Sotolongo, deputy chief of the general staff of the armed forces.

Most of those named were responsible for trying to organize weapon transfers from China and Russia, the State Department said in a [fact sheet.](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/targeting-enablers-of-the-cuban-regimes-arms-imports-and-foreign-military-cooperation-fact-sheet/)

Gómez has been singled out by the [International Society for Human Rights](https://ishr.org/cubans-as-cannon-fodder/) and other groups as being involved in the recruitment of Cubans to fight in Russia’s war against Ukraine. The government in Havana has long denied it has had any role bringing mercenaries to the front lines.

Thursday’s designations are meant “to protect our national security by sanctioning entities and individuals who facilitate Havana’s military relationships and the shipments of arms to the Cuban regime,” Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in a [statement](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/targeting-enablers-of-the-cuban-regimes-arms-imports-and-foreign-military-cooperation/).

In addition to the officers, state-run military trading companies Tecnotex and Technoimport were sanctioned on Thursday, as was maintenance and repair firm Yuri Gagarin Military Industrial Enterprise, trading group Duna SA and the UIM.

*Marco Rubio Photographer: Eric Lee/AFP/Getty Images*

Trump and Rubio have been hammering Cuba with almost weekly sanctions as they try to coerce the Havana’s leadership to relinquish control after nearly seven decades of one-party rule.

US measures — including an unprecedented [oil blockade](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-22/can-cuba-s-regime-survive-rising-us-pressure) that has cut off all but one major fuel shipment to the island since the start of the year — have ramped up pain for everyday Cubans.

The country of 9.4 million people has seen four [nationwide blackouts](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/cuba-suffers-fourth-nationwide-blackout-in-less-than-a-month) in less than a month, which the government blames, in part, on US pressure.

Separately Thursday, a UN panel of experts said the sanctions were interfering with food delivery on the island. They called on the international community to prevent a “[silent Gaza](https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/08/cuba-un-experts-condemn-new-us-sanctions-humanitarian-crisis-deepens)” from unfolding in Cuba.

“Food must never be used as an instrument of political pressure,” the UN experts said. “Measures that knowingly deprive a population of the means to survive strike at the most basic guarantees of the rights to life and diminish the core of human dignity.”
