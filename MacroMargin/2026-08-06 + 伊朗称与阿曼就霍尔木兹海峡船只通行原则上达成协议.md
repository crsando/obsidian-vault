## 伊朗称与阿曼就霍尔木兹海峡船只通行原则上达成协议

### [政治](https://www.bloomberg.com/politics)

作者：[Eltaf Najafizada](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/APyPkzxxuwE/eltaf-najafizada)2026年8月6日下午5:12 GMT+8*更新时间：2026年8月6日晚上10:13 GMT+8*

### **要点** 由彭博人工智能提供

- 伊朗外交部副部长卡齐姆·加里巴巴迪表示，已就几乎所有提出的问题达成原则性谅解，包括海上交通进出路线图。
- 德黑兰表示，与阿曼的双边协议并不意味着霍尔木兹海峡完全重新开放。德黑兰坚称美国不是协议的一部分，并暗示海峡正常化将取决于华盛顿解除对伊朗港口的封锁。
- 任何涉及霍尔木兹海峡的协议都必须得到伊朗最高领袖穆杰塔巴·哈梅内伊的批准。他自今年2月在美以战争初期袭击中受伤以来一直躲藏。
在伊朗伊斯兰共和国表示拟议航道协议已进入最后阶段后，对伊朗与阿曼部分重新开放霍尔木兹海峡协议的等待一直持续到周四。

这两个濒临这条关键水道的国家自上个月以来一直在谈判管理海峡海上交通的计划。近日，伊朗和美国表示解决方案已接近达成。

“几乎所有提出的问题都已达成原则性谅解，包括海上交通进出航线的地图，”伊朗外交部副部长卡齐姆·加里巴巴迪周三对伊斯兰共和国通讯社表示。他补充说，该安排的有效期可能为“两到四个月”。

然而，他警告说，与马斯喀特的双边协议“并不意味着霍尔木兹海峡全面重新开放”。德黑兰坚称美国不是与阿曼协议的一部分，并暗示海峡正常化将取决于华盛顿解除对伊朗港口的封锁。

观看：伊朗表示已与阿曼就通过霍尔木兹海峡的拟议航运路线达成协议，这可能使这条关键能源供应水道重新开放。彭博社的Abeer Abu Omar带来最新报道。来源：彭博社

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普周三晚些时候重申，有关海峡的协议即将达成，他的政府正在与伊朗接触。“我们正在谈判。看看会发生什么，”他在拉斯维加斯的一次集会上说。

总统过去也曾宣称取得外交突破，但持久协议却难以达成。

这种观望态势反映在石油市场上，交易员在评估波斯湾石油供应迅速增加的可能性之际，油价在小幅涨跌之间波动。周四，布伦特原油价格上涨1%，略高于每桶80美元。

胡塞武装是也门境内受伊朗支持的民兵组织，也是持久和平协议道路上的另一个潜在障碍。据美联社援引政府官员的话报道，该组织周四在也门中部和东部发动袭击，造成至少30名国际公认政府的军人死亡。

胡塞武装和沙特领导的多国联军在也门 突发：官员称胡塞反政府武装袭击造成至少30名也门政府军士兵死亡 自2022年以来停火协议，但最近几周爆发敌对行动，包括红海地区。美联社称，胡塞武装很快将就一次重大军事行动发表声明。

任何涉及霍尔木兹海峡的协议都必须得到伊朗最高领袖穆杰塔巴·哈梅内伊的批准。他自2月在美国和以色列发动的战争初期袭击中受伤以来一直躲藏。目前尚不清楚他是否已经同意，伊朗官员表示与他沟通需要时间。

总统马苏德·佩泽什基安周三在接受电视采访时表示，与哈梅内伊的沟通“目前非常困难”。

这些进展之前，华盛顿与德黑兰之间就如何结束战争已陷入数月僵局，其中对航运的控制是一个特别症结。德黑兰要求船只获得其许可才能通过，并可能支付某种费用。近几周来，它继续攻击其认为违规的船只。

双方之间的战斗目前基本暂停，但仍有许多其他问题尚未解决。关于霍尔木兹的协议可能使双方重返谈判桌，以解决伊朗的核计划问题。

特朗普上周末取消了对伊朗的新一轮袭击，他称这将是自二战以来规模最大的袭击。《华盛顿邮报》报道称，关键导弹和防空系统短缺是改变决定的原因之一。

据该报报道，特朗普上周在内阁会议间隙对赫格塞斯大发雷霆，称自己在武器库存问题上被误导了。

特朗普周四在Truth Social上发帖称：“美国拥有大量‘弹药’”，[否认](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-denies-us-munitions-shortage-warns-leakers-of-jail-time)该国弹药短缺。“国防企业正在建设我国历史上最多的工厂。”

虽然通过该海峡的可见交通量有限，但即使在最近几周，每天仍有数百万桶石油继续通过该水道。

中东生产商已通过穿梭运输计划将货物运出波斯湾，该计划涉及美国军方的帮助和关闭应答器的船只。有些国家，如沙特阿拉伯和阿联酋，通过将大量石油输送到波斯湾以外的港口，设法绕过该海峡。

## Iran Says Deal With Oman on Hormuz Ships Agreed in Principle

By [Eltaf Najafizada](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/APyPkzxxuwE/eltaf-najafizada)August 6, 2026 at 5:12 PM GMT+8*Updated on August 6, 2026 at 10:13 PM GMT+8*

The wait for an Iran-Oman deal that partially reopens the Strait of Hormuz continued through Thursday, after the Islamic Republic said an agreement on proposed shipping lanes is in the final stages.

The two countries that border the crucial waterway have been negotiating a plan for the management of maritime traffic through the strait since last month. In recent days, Iran and the US have indicated a resolution is close.

“An understanding in principle has been reached on almost all the raised issues, including the map for maritime traffic entry and exit routes,” Iran’s Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi told the Islamic Republic News Agency on Wednesday. The arrangement may remain valid for “two to four months,” he added.

Yet, he cautioned a bilateral deal with Muscat “does not mean the full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz.” Tehran has insisted the US is not part of the agreement with Oman and hinted that a normalization of the strait will depend on Washington lifting its blockade on Iranian ports.

US President Donald Trump repeated late Wednesday that a deal regarding the strait is imminent and that his administration is in contact with Iran. “We are talking. Let’s see what happens,” he said at a rally in Las Vegas.

The president has claimed diplomatic breakthroughs in the past, only for a lasting agreement to prove elusive.

The waiting pattern was reflected in oil markets, where prices swung between small gains and losses as traders assessed the likelihood of a speedy increase in oil flows from the Persian Gulf. Brent crude traded up 1% at just over $80 a barrel on Thursday.

The Houthis, an Iran-backed militia based in Yemen, are another potential obstacle in the way of a durable peace deal. The group launched attacks in central and eastern Yemen on Thursday, killing at least 30 troops of the internationally recognized government, Associated Press reported, citing officials from the government.

The Houthis will release a statement shortly on a major military operation, AP said.

Any deal for the Strait of Hormuz would have to be approved by Iran’s Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, who has been in hiding since being injured in the initial attacks of the war by the US and Israel, in February. It’s unclear if he’s given his consent yet and Iranian officials have said that communicating with him takes time.

President Masoud Pezeshkian said in a televised interview on Wednesday that communication with Khamenei is “very difficult at the moment.”

The developments follow months of deadlock between Washington and Tehran over how to conclude the war, with control over shipping a particular sticking point. Tehran has demanded that vessels obtain its permission to cross and possibly pay fees of some kind. It has continued to attack ships it deems to be in violation in recent weeks.

Fighting between the sides has largely paused for now, but a number of other issues remain unresolved. The deal on Hormuz may enable them to get back to the negotiating table to address Iran’s nuclear program.

Trump called off a new round of attacks on Iran over the weekend, which he said would have been the largest since World War II. The Washington Post reported that a shortage of key missiles and air-defense systems was among the reasons for the reversal.

According to the newspaper, Trump lashed out at Hegseth on the sidelines of a cabinet meeting last week, saying he had been misled about weaponry stocks.

“The U.S. has massive amounts of ‘munitions,’”, Trump said in a Truth Social post on Thursday, [denying](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/trump-denies-us-munitions-shortage-warns-leakers-of-jail-time) that the country is running low on them. “Defense companies are building the largest number of plants and factories in our country’s history.”

While visible traffic through the strait has been limited, a few millions of barrels a day have continued to cross the waterway even in recent weeks.

Middle Eastern producers have ferried cargoes out of the Gulf as part of a shuttle program involving help from the US military and vessels turning off their transponders. Some, such as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, are managing to bypass the strait by piping a significant amount of their oil to ports outside the Persian Gulf.
