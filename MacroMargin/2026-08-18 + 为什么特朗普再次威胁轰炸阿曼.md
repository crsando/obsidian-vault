### 中文译文

## 为什么特朗普再次威胁轰炸阿曼

一些美国官员认为阿曼偏向伊朗，另一些则称特朗普因未能达成核协议而大发雷霆。

作者：Alexander Ward 和 Robbie Gramer

2026年8月17日 美国东部时间晚上8:00

![President Trump speaking in the Oval Office.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b14815db946c109118c5c05/3c081cc9-0b14-8148-a1e7-cac65cd5447b-9d07858dadec63547156e39768bbd751.jpg)

特朗普总统周一在椭圆形办公室。Samuel Corum/Sipa/Bloomberg News

华盛顿——白宫数周来一直承诺，[伊朗](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran)即将签署核协议，而阿曼可能有助于重新开放霍尔木兹海峡。这个海湾小国突然发现自己处于结束战争努力的中心。

然而，这项协议却被证明难以达成。特朗普总统对这一僵局的挫败感促使他在周一[威胁轰炸阿曼](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-oman-bomb-threats-hormuz-0941134f?mod=article_inline)，因为他声称该国可能阻碍协议的达成。

“我认为他们表现不太好，但我们会像对待其他人一样轻松地对付他们，”特朗普周一在椭圆形办公室对记者表示。他没有具体说明阿曼官员的哪些行为激怒了他。

美国官员表示，阿曼的情况并不明朗。这个与霍尔木兹海峡南端接壤的海湾国家正在与伊朗谈判，允许更多商业船舶通过该水道。美国官员表示，这些谈判正在取得进展，可能缓解全球能源市场和国内汽油价格上涨的压力。但美国政府内部仍存在一种担忧，即阿曼可能帮助伊朗达成一项对德黑兰更有利的核协议，从而加剧特朗普的愤怒。

阿曼只是最新一个因特朗普认为其未尽力帮助结束他与以色列共同发起的伊朗冲突而引发其愤怒的国家。周日，他下令[缩减美韩联合军事演习](https://www.wsj.com/politics/national-security/trump-orders-cuts-to-u-s-south-korea-military-drills-2bc63cec?mod=article_inline)，因为首尔不愿帮助打开霍尔木兹海峡。

“你愿意帮我们一把吗？”特朗普在讲述他据称与韩国总统李在明的对话时说道，随后他强调美国实际上并不需要任何帮助。“他说，‘不了，谢谢，’”特朗普补充道。“我说，‘等一下：我们有39000名士兵在那里，保护你免受隔壁邻居金正恩的威胁，而你却不愿帮我们执行一项非常简单的在伊朗的军事行动？这太奇怪了。’”

特朗普还抱怨英国和西班牙等欧洲盟友没有采取足够措施来对抗伊朗，这是他数十年来一直抱怨盟友从美国获取多于付出的延伸。

政府内部关于如何处理阿曼问题一直存在激烈争论。

![Vessels near the Strait of Hormuz, seen from Musandam, Oman.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b14815db946c109118c5c05/3c081cc9-0b14-8161-b591-dadd4dd389a8-91ea787f53ad77b8dc3c895b22ffa37d.jpg)

周一，从阿曼看到的霍尔木兹海峡附近的船只。路透社

官员们表示，一些高级助手私下指责阿曼是一个两面派角色，在很大程度上偏向伊朗，而不是寻求华盛顿与德黑兰之间的最佳交易。另一些人则表示，阿曼与伊朗的长期关系对调解进程是一种福音。这些官员断言，特朗普在寻找他人作为未能完成核协议的替罪羊时，将阿曼当成了替罪羊。

美国官员还表示，有人担心伊朗-阿曼协议可能巩固德黑兰对海峡的控制权，并指出特朗普更倾向于一项能完全重新开放该通道的协议——战前全球五分之一的石油和能源流经该通道。

外交关系委员会中东问题高级研究员史蒂文·库克表示，伊朗正在向阿曼施压，迫使其加入联合计划，以便让这一跨海峡项目披上合法性的外衣。“特朗普的威胁只会让那些察觉到进一步虚弱的伊朗人更加胆大妄为，”库克说。“海峡没有完全开放，而是基本关闭。”

阿曼驻华盛顿大使馆未回应置评请求。

特朗普在6月设定了60天期限，以结束与伊朗的战争，并推动德黑兰放弃其核计划。该期限于周一到期，双方均未让步，结束冲突的路径依然不明朗。美国一直依赖卡塔尔和巴基斯坦作为调解人，试图结束战争，同时将最初的调解人阿曼推到一边。

特朗普曾告诉助手，如果德黑兰同意[全面开放水道](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-strait-of-hormuz-opening-iran-negotiations-b6a43ad6?mod=article_inline)，他将考虑宣布伊朗通往核武器的道路已被阻断，且战争已获胜，《华尔街日报》此前曾报道。

近几个月来，美国与阿曼的关系有所恶化。

阿曼外交大臣巴德尔·布赛迪（Badr al-Busaidi）在战前曾参与调解美伊会谈，他多次批评美国发动战争的决定，甚至出现在哥伦比亚广播公司（CBS）的《面向全国》（Face the Nation）节目中，试图阻止第一波攻击。

“无论你对伊朗有何看法，这场战争都不是他们挑起的。”他在3月的一篇社交媒体帖子中说。

特朗普政府此前曾[威胁要制裁](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/us-oman-iran-ties-3e056744?mod=article_inline)甚至对阿曼发动打击，此前情报评估得出结论，这个波斯湾国家计划与伊朗一起对穿越霍尔木兹海峡的船只征收通行费，以期看到该海峡重新开放。阿曼一再否认这些评估。

国务卿马尔科·鲁比奥（Marco Rubio）多次表示，伊朗对霍尔木兹海峡征收通行费将是特朗普政府的红线，并将为世界各地其他具有战略意义的国际水道树立一个危险的先例。

## Why Trump Is Threatening Again to Bomb Oman

Some U.S. officials see Oman as favoring Iran, while others say Trump is lashing out over failures to make a nuclear deal

By Alexander Ward and Robbie Gramer

Aug. 17, 2026 8:00 pm ET

President Trump in the Oval Office on Monday. Samuel Corum/Sipa/Bloomberg News

WASHINGTON—The White House for weeks promised that [Iran](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran) was close to signing a nuclear deal and that Oman could be helpful in reopening the Strait of Hormuz. The tiny Gulf nation suddenly found itself at the center of the effort to end the war.

This deal, however, has proved elusive. And President Trump’s frustration with this standoff prompted him to [threaten to bomb Oman](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-oman-bomb-threats-hormuz-0941134f?mod=article_inline) on Monday, as he alleged the country might be standing in the way of an agreement.

“I don’t think they behaved very well, but we’d handle them very easily just like we do others,” Trump told reporters Monday in the Oval Office. He didn’t go into specifics about what Omani officials did that drew his ire.

U.S. officials said the Oman picture was murky. The Gulf country that borders the Strait of Hormuz to the south is in talks with Iran to allow more commercial traffic in the waterway. U.S. officials said those negotiations were progressing and could relieve pressure on the global energy market and higher domestic gas prices. But there is lingering concern within the administration that Oman could aid Iran in brokering a nuclear deal more favorable to Tehran, feeding Trump’s anger.

Oman is just the latest country to draw Trump’s anger for not—in his view—doing more to help end the Iran conflict he started alongside Israel. On Sunday, he ordered a [cut to a joint U.S.-South Korea military drill](https://www.wsj.com/politics/national-security/trump-orders-cuts-to-u-s-south-korea-military-drills-2bc63cec?mod=article_inline) because Seoul wouldn’t help pry open the Strait of Hormuz.

“Would you like to give us a little hand,” Trump said, recounting a conversation he said he had with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung before stressing that the U.S. didn’t actually need any help. “He said, ‘no thanks,’” Trump added. “And I said, ‘Wait a minute: We have 39,000 soldiers over there, guarding you from Kim Jong Un, your next door neighbor, and you’re not going to help us on a very easy military operation in Iran? That’s strange.’”

Trump has also complained that European allies such as the U.K. and Spain haven’t done enough to counter Iran, an extension of his decadeslong gripe that allies take more from the U.S. than they give.

A debate has raged within the administration about how to handle Oman.

Vessels near the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, as seen from Oman. Reuters

Some senior aides privately accuse Oman of being a duplicitous actor that largely sides with Iran instead of seeking the best possible deal between Washington and Tehran, officials said. Others say Oman’s longstanding ties to Iran are a boon to the mediation process. Those officials assert that Trump is scapegoating Oman as he seeks others to blame for struggling to complete a nuclear deal.

There is also concern the Iran-Oman deal could solidify Tehran’s control over the strait, U.S. officials said, noting Trump prefers an agreement that fully reopens the passageway through which a fifth of the world’s oil and energy flowed before the war.

Steven Cook, a senior fellow for Middle East issues at the Council on Foreign Relations, said Iran was pressuring Oman into the joint plan so it has the patina of a legitimate cross-strait project. “Trump’s threats only embolden the Iranians who sense further weakness,” said Cook. “Instead of being totally open, the strait is mostly closed.”

The embassy of Oman in Washington didn’t respond to a request for comment.

Trump in June set a 60-day deadline to end the war with Iran and push Tehran to give up its nuclear program. That deadline expired on Monday with neither side giving ground, leaving an unclear path to ending the conflict. The U.S. has relied on Qatar and Pakistan as mediators to try to bring about an end to the war while it pushed Oman, the initial mediator, to the side.

Trump has told aides that he would consider declaring Iran’s path to a nuclear weapon blocked and the war won if Tehran agreed [to fully open the waterway](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trump-strait-of-hormuz-opening-iran-negotiations-b6a43ad6?mod=article_inline), The Wall Street Journal has previously reported.

Relations between the U.S. and Oman have deteriorated in recent months.

Omani Foreign Minister Badr al-Busaidi, who played a role in mediating U.S.-Iran talks before the war, repeatedly criticized the U.S. decision to launch the war, even appearing on CBS News’s “Face the Nation” to try to prevent the first wave of attacks.

“Whatever your view of Iran, this war is not of their making,” he said in a social-media post in March.

The Trump administration previously [threatened to sanction](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/us-oman-iran-ties-3e056744?mod=article_inline) and even launch strikes against Oman after intelligence assessments concluded that the Persian Gulf country planned to impose tolls on vessels crossing the Strait of Hormuz with Iran in a bid to see the strait reopened. Oman repeatedly denied these assessments.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio has repeatedly said that Iran’s tolling the Strait of Hormuz would be a red line for the Trump administration and set a dangerous precedent for other strategic international waterways around the world.
