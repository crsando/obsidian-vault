### 中文译文

## 长鑫存储跃升为中国最具价值公司，预示新科技时代到来

[长鑫存储（CXMT）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity)攀升至中国市场的顶峰，预示着一种可能具有深远影响的转变：从私营巨头转向与北京战略[雄心](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-09/china-bets-on-ai-stocks-as-it-races-against-us-for-chip-tech-dominance)更紧密结合的新一代硬件公司。

这家存储芯片制造商——被认为是中国AI基础设施建设的关键——上周超过[腾讯控股有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity)，成为该国[市值最高](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/cxmt-overtakes-tencent-to-become-most-valuable-chinese-company)的公司，市值突破5000亿美元。几天前，[宇树机器人](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688836%20CH%20Equity)的首次公开募股[引发](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/unitree-s-shanghai-ipo-5-526-times-subscribed-by-retail-buyers)了散户的狂热需求，面向散户投资者的部分被认购超过5500倍。两者都与北京在可能改变经济的技术领域追赶并超越美国的努力密切相关。

在十年的大部分时间里，腾讯、[阿里巴巴集团控股有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BABA%20US%20Equity)和移动时代的巨头们一直吸引着投资者的目光，而传统国有企业和老牌经济支柱则受到冷落。2020年，北京对日益强大的私营部门进行[打击](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-02-23/why-china-keeps-on-targeting-its-technology-giants-quicktake)，这一局面开始转变，随之而来的衰退最终以长鑫存储的崛起告终。

大型科技公司今年再度大面积低迷，受到长期经济和消费疲软的拖累。阿里巴巴即使转向人工智能，今年也下跌了约16%。腾讯市值已缩水近27%。就连[小米集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1810%20HK%20Equity)也因成功进军电动汽车而失去往日光彩，截至上周已下跌超过三分之一。

彭博汇编的数据显示，今年中国硬件板块（包括长鑫存储）在MSCI中国全指净总回报指数中的权重曾短暂超过零售业和软件业。

阿里巴巴和[京东集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/JD%20US%20Equity)等在线零售商的合计权重已从2020年的超过15%缩减至8月份的约7%，而硬件板块则从六年前的不到3%攀升至超过12%。以芯片为主的[科创50指数](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/STAR50%20Index)今年上涨约28%，而[恒生科技指数](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HSTECH%20Index)——阿里巴巴和腾讯是其权重股——则下跌近15%。

这种分化在周一进一步加深。长鑫存储在上海一度上涨11.5%，远超腾讯和阿里巴巴在香港2%以上的涨幅。

“这是不可避免的，”[Gavekal Capital Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/0335796D:HK)驻香港基金经理Leonid Mironov表示。“看看腾讯的现金流——已经转为负值，其中很大一部分流向了像长鑫存储这样的硬件公司。美国也有同样的动态。平台最终必须证明自己能借此赚钱。而硬件公司现在正在证明这一点。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b14811689b5c8394ee31972/3bf81cc9-0b14-81f0-a05e-ec0abeed6040-c727260eabed58d4e19a1019c54424e4.png)

AI热潮为所需硬件的供应商带来了激增的需求：来自英伟达（Nvidia Corp.）等公司的处理器，以及来自长鑫存储（CXMT）、SK海力士（SK Hynix Inc.）和三星电子（Samsung Electronics Co.）的高速内存。但软件端的市场更难把握。这一动态在中国被放大，中国政府扮演着多个工业领域“造王者”的角色，并将AI基础设施和机器人列为重点优先事项，予以追求、开发和扩展。

在中美争夺AI主导地位日益激烈的背景下，国家主席习近平呼吁取得颠覆性突破。北京至少希望确保中国在关键领域的自给自足。这些领域确实包括阿里巴巴等软件和AI开发者——但该行业仍陷入侵蚀利润的价格战。

在AI模型开发和推理处理芯片等领域，中国已缩小差距，这得益于从DeepSeek到华为技术有限公司（Huawei Technologies Co.）等本土领军企业。但北京仍需要更多先驱者，在数据中心先进芯片制造和下一代人形机器人的认知AI等领域开拓新天地。

“中国的AI建设将日益定义香港和中国内地市场的股票及IPO领导地位，”彭博行业研究高级分析师Francis Chan表示。“IPO管道由AI主导，代表性企业包括月之暗面（Moonshot AI）、长江存储（Yangtze Memory）、希音（Shein）和宇树科技（Unitree Robotics）。”

宇树科技最早可能于本周在上海开始交易，检验投资者对AI硬件的兴趣。长鑫存储上市已有三周，自首日交易以来涨幅超过500%。市场对中国在AI竞赛中前景的信心日益增强，来自Z.AI Co.、阿里巴巴和DeepSeek的新产品支持了这一论点。但多数押注集中于营收和利润可见度更高的硬件供应商。

“纵观科技周期，总是硬件先行，软件随后，”百达财富管理（Pictet Wealth Management）亚洲首席投资官Kelvin Tay表示。“我相当确信，转向软件的趋势终将回归。”

诚然，在AI时代，科技股已被证明波动剧烈，市场情绪可能大幅摇摆。仅阿里巴巴一家就经历了围绕其高额AI支出的乐观与怀疑周期——这种支出时而被视为负担，时而被视为长期增长的基础。

目前更明显的趋势是，符合北京优先事项的公司，尤其是那些制造稀缺硬件或开发现实世界AI应用的公司。

“估值受到这样一种理念的支撑：自给自足将为这些公司创造更大的总可寻址市场，”[Morningstar Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MORN%20US%20Equity)分析师Jing Jie Yu表示，“监管提升或支持成为这些公司持续的估值推动因素。”

### English Original

## CXMT’s Rise to Most Valuable China Firm Heralds New Tech Era

[CXMT Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity)’s ascent to the pinnacle of Chinese markets heralds a potentially consequential shift away from private-sector giants toward a new generation of hardware companies more closely aligned with Beijing’s strategic [ambitions](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-09/china-bets-on-ai-stocks-as-it-races-against-us-for-chip-tech-dominance).

The memory chipmaker — considered central to China’s AI infrastructure buildout — overtook [Tencent Holdings Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity) as the country’s [most valuable](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/cxmt-overtakes-tencent-to-become-most-valuable-chinese-company) company last week, surpassing half a trillion dollars of capitalization. Days earlier, [Unitree Robotics](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688836%20CH%20Equity)’ initial public offering [drew](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/unitree-s-shanghai-ipo-5-526-times-subscribed-by-retail-buyers) frenzied retail demand, with the tranche for mom-and-pop investors subscribed more than 5,500 times. Both are intricately linked to Beijing’s effort to catch and surpass the US in a technology with the potential to transform economies.

For the better part of a decade, Tencent, [Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BABA%20US%20Equity) and the giants of the mobile era drew investor attention at the expense of staid state-backed and old-economy stalwarts. That began to change in 2020 when Beijing [cracked down](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-02-23/why-china-keeps-on-targeting-its-technology-giants-quicktake) on an increasingly powerful private sector — precipitating a decline that culminated with CXMT’s ascent.

Big tech names have again largely languished this year, dinged by prolonged economic and consumer malaise. Alibaba, even with its pivot to AI, was down about 16% this year. Tencent had given up close to 27% in market value. Even [Xiaomi Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1810%20HK%20Equity) has lost the luster from a successful electric-vehicle foray, down more than a third as of last week.

The weighting of China’s hardware sector — including CXMT — briefly overtook both retailers and software in the MSCI China All Shares Net Total Return Index this year, data compiled by Bloomberg show.

Online retailers such as Alibaba and [JD.com Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/JD%20US%20Equity) have seen their combined weight shrink from more than 15% in 2020 to roughly 7% in August, while hardware climbed from under 3% six years ago to more than 12%. China’s chip-heavy [Star50 Index](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/STAR50%20Index) rose about 28% this year, while [the Hang Seng Tech Index](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/HSTECH%20Index) — which counts Alibaba and Tencent among its heavyweights — shed nearly 15%.

That divergence deepened Monday. CXMT rose as much as 11.5% in Shanghai, far outpacing Tencent’s and Alibaba’s 2%-plus rallies in Hong Kong.

“This is inevitable,” said Leonid Mironov, a Hong Kong-based fund manager at [Gavekal Capital Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/0335796D:HK) “Look at Tencent’s cashflow — it’s gone negative, and a lot of it is going to the hardware guys like CXMT. This is the dynamic in the US as well. At some point the platforms will have to show that they can make money off this. For hardware guys, they are showing it right now.”

The AI boom is delivering runaway demand to suppliers of the hardware required: processors from the likes of [Nvidia Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity) as well as fast memory from CXMT, [SK Hynix Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/000660%20KS%20Equity) and [Samsung Electronics Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/005930%20KS%20Equity) But the market is harder to navigate on the software side. This dynamic is amplified in China, where the government acts as kingmaker for various industrial sectors — and has designated AI infrastructure as well as robots key priorities to pursue, develop and expand.

President Xi Jinping has [called for](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/china-s-ai-ascendance-gives-xi-a-stage-and-a-security-dilemma) game-changing breakthroughs, against the backdrop of China’s intensifying rivalry with the US for AI supremacy. At a minimum, Beijing seeks to ensure China’s self-sufficiency in critical areas. Those do include software and AI developers such as Alibaba — but the industry remains locked in a margin-eroding price [war](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/china-s-ai-blitz-creates-death-zone-for-rival-us-model-makers).

China has narrowed the gap in areas such as AI model development and inference-processing chips, helped by homegrown champions from [DeepSeek](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2544189D:CH) to [Huawei Technologies Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/40978Z:CH) But Beijing still needs more pioneers to break new ground in fields including advanced chipmaking for data centers and cognitive AI for the next generation of humanoid machines.

“China’s AI build-out will increasingly define equity and IPO leadership across Hong Kong and mainland Chinese markets,” Bloomberg Intelligence senior analyst Francis Chan said. “IPO pipelines are AI-led, featuring firms like Moonshot AI, [Yangtze Memory](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1577197D:CH), Shein and Unitree Robotics.”

Unitree is expected to begin trading in Shanghai as early as this week, testing the investor community’s appetite for AI hardware. CXMT, now three weeks into its life as a public company, is up more than 500% since its debut. Conviction about China’s prospects in the AI race is growing, and new products from the likes of [Z.AI Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/2513%20HK%20Equity), Alibaba and DeepSeek support that thesis. But most of the bets on that rise are landing on hardware providers with greater immediate visibility on revenue and profits.

“If you look at the tech cycle, it is always the hardware set that goes first before the software set can come in,” said Kelvin Tay, chief investment officer for Asia at [Pictet Wealth Management](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1146Z:SW). “I’m quite sure that at some point this move into software will come back.”

To be sure, tech stocks have proven volatile in the AI age and sentiment can swing dramatically. Alibaba alone has gone through cycles of optimism and skepticism around its high level of AI spending — alternately seen as a burden or a foundation for long-term growth.

The clearer trend for now is companies aligned with Beijing’s priorities, especially if they’re making hard-to-get hardware or developing real-world AI applications.

“Valuations are propped up by the idea that self-sufficiency will create a larger total addressable market for those companies,” [Morningstar Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/MORN%20US%20Equity) analyst Jing Jie Yu said. “Regulatory uplift or support becomes a consistent valuation push factor for these companies.”
