台湾与美国的军事合作“远比你所想象的更为密切”，国防部长威灵顿·顾（Wellington Koo）对一小群记者表示，这是对美国在帮助台湾加强防御和威慑中国方面所扮演的隐秘角色的罕见承认。

美国外交关系委员会亚洲研究研究员戴维·萨克斯表示，特朗普在与中国国家主席习近平的北京峰会后发表的言论——尤其是他不愿“长途跋涉9500英里去打仗”——引起了台湾对美国承诺可信度的担忧。

“事实是，尽管华盛顿和北京之间谈论缓和关系，但自特朗普与习近平会晤以来，中国对台湾的胁迫只增不减，”萨克斯说。“特朗普总统的言论进一步助长了近年来台湾人对美国可信度日益增长的怀疑。”

在特朗普离开北京后播出的一次采访中，美国总统称对台140亿美元军售方案是与中国打交道的“非常好的谈判筹码”。白宫是否会批准以及何时批准这笔军售仍不明朗。

“公开美台安全合作的某些方面，如海岸警卫队联合巡逻，很可能是为了对抗这些说法并恢复对美国的信任，”萨克斯说，他此前曾在美国在台协会（AIT）从事政治军事事务。

《华尔街日报》2021年报道称，一支规模不大的美军分遣队在台湾至少驻扎了一年，秘密[训练台湾军队](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/u-s-troops-have-been-deployed-in-taiwan-for-at-least-a-year-11633614043?mod=article_inline)以防御中国军队。

在台湾的美国军事人员常被称为“美国英语教师”，这一称呼用于避免明确提及美军，因为他们在台湾的存在早已是公开的秘密。

“会有更多的‘美国英语教师’出现在他们需要出现的地方，”Koo说。“但你无法在你们活动的每个地方都发现他们。”

随着台湾为周三开始的年度军事演习做准备，其他高级军事官员也比过去更自由地谈论美国所扮演的角色。

台湾军方发言人 Sun Li-fang 中将周五表示：“这些‘美国英语老师’与我们分享了很多真实的作战经验。”

“如果你想知道我们实际学到了什么，只需看看我们的部队在这些实战化训练中发生了多大的变化，”领导军方战略规划部门的 Huang Wen-chi 中将表示，“但这些都不是我们可以公开谈论的。”

TAIPEI—[Taiwan](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/taiwan) is opening up about long-concealed U.S. military training while Americans note their cooperation with the island, sending a message to China that ties are strong despite comments by President Trump that appeared to put the relationship in jeopardy.

Taiwan’s military collaboration with the U.S. “is far closer than you might imagine,” Defense Minister Wellington Koo told a small group of reporters, a rare recognition of a secretive American role in helping Taiwan strengthen its defense and deter China.

Beijing claims the democratic island as its own and hasn’t ruled out the use of force to seize control. The U.S. is committed by law to provide the island with the means to defend itself, selling billions of dollars in weapons to Taiwan.

“The public sees only the arms sales, but in fact, it’s multifaceted and involves so much more,” Koo said Friday. “Through military exchanges, we have been learning so much practical combat experience from the U.S.”

The Pentagon and U.S. Pacific Command said they had no comment.

Koo added that Taiwan’s planning for potential conflict with China doesn’t include U.S. military intervention.

Training troops in Taiwan is a sensitive issue that both sides have long kept in the shadows to avoid potential retaliation from Beijing.

Even maritime cooperation in law enforcement has been a touchy subject. But the American Institute in Taiwan, the de facto U.S. Embassy in Taipei, released a photo last week of Taiwanese and U.S. Coast Guard cutters sailing in tandem.

Taiwan Defense Minister Wellington Koo Taiwan Ministry of National Defense

“The United States Coast Guard and the Taiwan Coast Guard are busy coordinating on shared maritime objectives,” the post said. It added that the U.S. “appreciates Taiwan’s partnership in maritime security and safety, and supports Taiwan’s meaningful contributions on global issues including maritime law enforcement exchange and international cooperation.”

Kuan Bi-ling, minister of Taiwan’s Ocean Affairs Council, which oversees the Taiwan Coast Guard, responded on her Facebook page to the AIT post: “This announcement is deeply significant and a new step by the U.S. that reflects its commitment to maintaining peace and security in the Western Pacific.”

The U.S. and Taiwan coast guards are “quite different” from their militaries, and Taiwan’s government favors making their military cooperation more public, said Bonnie Glaser, managing director of the Indo-Pacific Program at the German Marshall Fund of the United States, a Washington-based think tank.

These more open stances came after comments from Trump called U.S. support for Taiwan into question, and as Chinese [pressure on Taiwan has steadily mounted](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/how-chinas-navy-is-tightening-the-noose-on-taiwan-7c8ab9bd?mod=article_inline).

On Friday, two Chinese coast guard ships conducted what it said were routine law enforcement patrols in waters east of Taiwan, far from the Chinese mainland. China’s coast guard has become effectively [a military asset](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/china-coast-guard-pacific-sea-disputes-09f535c0?mod=article_inline) and regularly join exercises designed to practice invasion or blockade scenarios and intimidate Taiwan.

There were 85 similar coast guard sightings reported by Taiwanese authorities in May and June—a nearly 90% increase compared with the same period last year.

In May, WSJ joined a Taiwanese Coast Guard patrol in the Taiwan Strait, revealing how ‘gray-zone’ pressure from Beijing is transforming the law-enforcement agency. Photo: Javier Manzano for WSJ

Trump’s remarks following a Beijing summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping—specifically not wanting “to travel 9,500 miles to fight a war”—have raised concerns in Taiwan about the credibility of U.S. commitments, said David Sacks, a Council on Foreign Relations fellow for Asia studies.

“The fact is, despite talk of a detente between Washington and Beijing, Chinese coercion of Taiwan has only increased since Trump’s meeting with Xi,” said Sacks. “President Trump’s statements gave further momentum to the skepticism that has been growing in recent years among Taiwanese regarding the trustworthiness of the United States.”

In an interview that aired after Trump left Beijing, the U.S. president called a $14 billion U.S. arms package for Taiwan “a very good negotiating chip” with China. It remains unclear whether and when the White House will approve the arms sale.

“Making public some aspects of U.S.-Taiwan security cooperation such as joint coast guard patrols is likely an attempt to combat these narratives and restore faith in the United States,” said Sacks, who previously worked at the AIT on political military affairs.

The Wall Street Journal reported in 2021 that a small contingent of U.S. troops had been in Taiwan for at least a year, secretly [training the Taiwanese armed forces](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/u-s-troops-have-been-deployed-in-taiwan-for-at-least-a-year-11633614043?mod=article_inline) to defend against China’s military.

U.S. military personnel in Taiwan are often referred to as “American English teachers,” a term used to avoid mentioning the American military explicitly, as their presence in Taiwan has long been an open secret.

“A lot more ‘American English teachers’ will be showing up where they need to be,” said Koo. “But you won’t be able to spot them everywhere they operate.”

As Taiwan prepares for annual military exercises that start Wednesday, other senior military officials also spoke more freely than in the past about the U.S. role.

“The ‘American English teachers’ share a lot of real combat experience with us,” said Lt. Gen. Sun Li-fang, a spokesman for the Taiwanese military, on Friday.

“If you want to know what we actually learned, you just have to look at how much our units have changed during these realistic combat training,” said Lt. Gen. Huang Wen-chi, who leads the military’s Strategic Planning Department. “But none of this is something we can talk about publicly.”
