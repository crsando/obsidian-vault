### 中文译文

## 为什么美国与加拿大的贸易战是一场“酒吧斗殴”

*经济学家肯·罗格夫解释了自特朗普提出吞并想法以来，紧张局势如何一直暗流涌动。本期节目还涉及钻石和多莉。*

**《人人有份》：美加之间的“酒吧斗殴”（播客）**

美国最新一轮对加拿大商品加征关税，可能意味着美国人即将为从乳制品到卫生纸等各种商品支付高得多的价格。本周，我们调查这场北部边境贸易战可能带来的后果。此外，我们更深入地了解了珠宝行业，史黛西发现了一个价格实际上在下跌的市场。实验室培育钻石是永恒的吗？

### 美国究竟是如何与其北部邻国陷入贸易战的？

自特朗普提出吞并加拿大并开始试图重新谈判一项特朗普本人于2020年签署的区域自由贸易协定以来，特朗普政府与加拿大领导人（最近是总理马克·卡尼）之间的紧张关系一直在酝酿。但在过去一周，情况进一步升温，因为卡尼[退出谈判](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/canada-turned-down-a-us-tariff-deal-now-comes-the-economic-cost)，以避免美国征税，并抱怨特朗普政府试图强加“最后一刻的改动”，他称这些改动“不公平、不经济，并使任何协议的可靠性受到质疑”。特朗普随即宣布新的关税。

加拿大回击，宣布打算实施“等值”报复。（结果是：加拿大胶合板在美国将更贵；美国钢铁和鱼类在加拿大将更贵。）随后是互相侮辱。安大略省省长道格·福特自称是加拿大汽车业就业的捍卫者，多次叫特朗普“滚蛋”。在社交媒体上，特朗普侮辱福特，并称加拿大“显然是最难对付、最不讲理”的国家。（几小时后，他称赞朝鲜“不具威胁且尊重他人”。）8月26日，特朗普签署了一项行政命令，声称将安大略湖改名为“美国湖”。

“感觉像是一场酒吧斗殴，”哈佛经济学家肯·罗格夫在本周的《人人有份》播客中说道。罗格夫曾任国际货币基金组织首席经济学家，最近著有[《我们的美元，你们的问题》](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300275315/our-dollar-your-problem/)。他认为，这场争端在很大程度上归结于自尊心和民族自豪感。由于体量庞大，且美国购买其出口商品的巨大份额，加拿大在贸易战中处于巨大劣势。但吞并威胁以及政府对加拿大主权的更具象征性的攻击，让加拿大人感到深受侮辱，以至于反对特朗普成为普遍情绪。这或许能让卡尼比原本拥有更大的回旋余地。

与此同时，特朗普的关税[并不受美国选民欢迎](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-26/republicans-unusual-midterm-convention-is-a-political-gamble)，总统的低支持率正在加剧共和党人对中期选举的焦虑，民主党有机会重夺众议院甚至参议院。卡尼试图利用这一点，将关税瞄准缅因州和密歇根等州生产的商品，这些州的共和党候选人在11月选情脆弱。罗格夫预计双方最终都会让步，但他表示，这场争吵对美国软实力构成了风险——尤其是在美国军方因与伊朗的战争而捉襟见肘之际。

同样在本周，我们讨论一个至关重要的问题：为你的伴侣购买一枚含有实验室培育钻石的订婚戒指，这是否意味着你对他们的爱也是人工的？正如 Stacey Vanek-Smith 在 *Bloomberg Businessweek* 上撰文所述，实验室培育钻石行业发展迅速，给传统的地采钻石业务带来了价格压力，并[引发了一场狂热的品牌重塑活动](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-18/de-beers-and-the-natural-diamond-industry-face-a-lab-grown-reckoning)。

说到人工，我们回顾了一下 [Stanley Druckenmiller](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/let-the-bond-market-speak-81529d74) 在 *华尔街日报* 上的专栏文章，他在文章中严厉批评了美国财政部长 Scott Bessent（在 [Claude 的一点帮助](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/druckenmillers-surprising-critique-of-bessent-was-delivered-with-the-help-of-ai-9dd0a4fd) 下）。Druckenmiller 认为，使用聊天机器人帮你写文章不仅是可行的，而且他“为使用它感到自豪”。许多记者和媒体伦理学家不同意。

最后，我们为已故创作歌手 Dolly Parton 举杯致敬，欣赏她的商业帝国，以及也许是有史以来最伟大的关于办公室工作的歌曲。

## Why the US Trade War With Canada Is a ‘Bar Fight’

*Economist Ken Rogoff explains how tensions have been simmering ever since Trump raised the idea of annexation. Also on this episode, diamonds and Dolly.*

**Everybody's Business: the “Bar Fight” between the US and Canada (Podcast)**

America's latest round of tariffs on Canadian goods could mean Americans are about to pay way more for everything from dairy to toilet paper. This week, we investigate the possible fallout from a trade war across the northern border. Plus, we take a closer look at the jewelry business, where Stacey found a market whose prices are actually falling. Are lab-grown diamonds forever?

### How exactly did the US end up in a trade war with its northern neighbor?

Tensions between the Trump Administration and Canada’s leaders, most recently Prime Minister Mark Carney, have been simmering ever since Trump raised the idea of annexing Canada and then began trying to renegotiate a regional free trade agreement that Trump himself signed in 2020. But things got even hotter over the past week, after Carney [pulled out of negotiations](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-23/canada-turned-down-a-us-tariff-deal-now-comes-the-economic-cost) to avoid US levies, complaining the Trump administration had attempted to impose “last-minute changes” he said were “unfair, uneconomic and called into question the reliability of any deal.” Trump responded by announcing fresh tariffs.

Canada shot back by announcing its intention to impose a “dollar-for-dollar” retaliation. (The upshot: Canadian plywood will get more expensive in the US; American steel and fish will be more expensive in Canada.) The insults followed. Ontario’s leader, Doug Ford, who has styled himself as a defender of Canadian automotive jobs, repeatedly told Trump to “kiss my ass.” On social media, Trump insulted Ford and called Canada “easily the most difficult and unreasonable” country. (Hours later, he praised North Korea as “unthreatening and respectful.”) On Aug. 26, Trump signed an executive order supposedly renaming Lake Ontario “Lake America.”

“It feels like a bar fight,” Harvard economist Ken Rogoff said on this week’s *Everybody’s Business* podcast. Rogoff, former chief economist at the International Monetary Fund and the author, most recently, of[ Our Dollar, Your Problem](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300275315/our-dollar-your-problem/), argued that much of the dispute comes down to ego and national pride. Canada is at a huge disadvantage in the trade war because of its size and the fact that the US buys a huge share of its exports. But Canadians have felt so insulted by the threats of annexation and the administration’s more symbolic attacks on Canadian sovereignty that opposition to Trump is popular. That could allow Carney more room to maneuver than he might otherwise have.

Meanwhile, Trump’s tariffs [are not well-liked by American voters](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-26/republicans-unusual-midterm-convention-is-a-political-gamble) and the president’s low approval numbers are feeding anxiety among Republicans about a midterm election in which Democrats stand a chance of retaking the House and possibly even the Senate. Carney has sought to exploit this, by aiming his tariffs at goods produced in states, such as Maine and Michigan, where Republican candidates are vulnerable in November. Rogoff expects both sides to eventually back down, but he says the spat creates a risk to American soft power – at a time when the country’s military is stretched by its war with Iran.

Also this week, we discuss the all-important question of whether buying your partner an engagement ring that contains a lab-grown diamond means that your love for them is somehow artificial. As Stacey Vanek-Smith writes for *Bloomberg Businessweek*, the lab-grown diamond industry has been growing rapidly, putting price pressure on the traditional business of mining diamonds from the earth, and[ prompting a frantic rebranding campaign](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-08-18/de-beers-and-the-natural-diamond-industry-face-a-lab-grown-reckoning).

Speaking of artificial, we review [Stanley Druckenmiller](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/let-the-bond-market-speak-81529d74)’s *Wall Street Journal* Op-Ed, in which he sharply criticized US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent (with a[ little help from Claude](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/druckenmillers-surprising-critique-of-bessent-was-delivered-with-the-help-of-ai-9dd0a4fd)). Druckenmiller has argued that using a chatbot to write an essay for you is not only ok, but that he’s “proud of using it.” Many journalists and media ethicists disagree.

And finally, we pour one out for Dolly Parton, the late singer-songwriter, offering an appreciation for her business empire and perhaps the greatest song ever written about office work.
