### 中文译文

## AI繁荣城市暴露中国双速经济更大分歧

在合肥，中国内存芯片产业的核心，工厂几乎无法满足全球对AI硬件的需求。在长春，汽油车制造的铁锈地带老家，官员承认面临“前所未有的”困难——这个措辞后来因引起社交媒体关注而被删除。

这两个城市捕捉了全球AI繁荣催生的双速经济。野村控股公司表示，上半年，少数几个科技中心贡献了中国至少二十年来最大份额的增长，而全国其他地区的增速放缓至北京全年目标的底部边缘。

在中国AI转型的中心是像合肥这样的城市，安徽省省会，内存芯片巨头长鑫存储技术有限公司（CXMT Corp）所在地。但即使在繁荣城市内部，也很少惠及家庭。其中几个城市的零售额正在萎缩，受到推动增长的工厂中的自动化和临时工的削弱。

“AI驱动增长的果实主要被少数‘智慧’城市收获，”野村经济学家Jing Wang和Ting Lu在周三的一份报告中表示。由于这“不太可能抵消全国其他地区日益增长的增长逆风，我们认为北京可能会在下半年加大政策力度。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ad81cc90b14810087cbc736f7efd0d5/3ad81cc9-0b14-8128-b5f7-f95d1e126a7d-58f50f43388578e0677a65ac961c566a.png)

这种火爆的表现显示了AI对增长的前景和风险，尤其是当依赖传统产业的地区快速失去地盘，而长期房地产低迷仍未解决时。对中国而言，少数几个城市在驱动全国经济中承担了过大的负担，也构成了风险，因为主要贸易伙伴如美国限制进口先进技术和设备。

受上半年全球人工智能支出狂潮的推动，野村所称的七个“智慧”城市（包括北京、上海和深圳）的加权平均GDP增长回升至5.6%。

根据野村数据，这组城市还包括苏州、杭州、合肥和武汉，在上半年合计占全国增长的五分之一。中国其他地区增速放缓至4.5%。

日益扩大的分歧可能使中国的政策应对复杂化。在周四的中国共产党政治局会议上，高层官员对整体经济放缓表示谨慎态度，但未宣布新的刺激措施。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ad81cc90b14810087cbc736f7efd0d5/3ad81cc9-0b14-818b-ac78-c65cc8983e5c-e1ea21d31ef99ee7b0dee4d528e9ad40.png)

更令人担忧的是，即使是增长突出的地区也难以重振消费需求，这呼应了中国大部分地区面临的挑战。

自动化的普及以及电子制造业对临时工依赖加重，使得消费经济状况并未比大约五年前房地产价格开始下跌以来有所改善。

亚洲各地也都有类似[情况](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-04/taiwan-s-ai-boom-is-minting-millionaires-and-stoking-inequality)，新财富的惠及范围有限。根据最新官方数据，该行业在中国仅雇佣了[1000万](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202412/t20241226_1957895.html)人，占工业劳动力总数的9%。

牛津经济研究院首席经济学家亚历山德拉·赫尔曼·普拉萨德在一份报告中表示：“亚洲是人工智能热潮的最大受益者，但这些收益在很大程度上绕过了家庭。人工智能热潮带来的收益不成比例地流向了资本而非劳动力。”

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ad81cc90b14810087cbc736f7efd0d5/3ad81cc9-0b14-818b-8d01-dac3fb601a8c-6a92fe31b994bbe6893ed46443248302.png)

与此同时，全球对建设数据中心所需的芯片和其他电子产品的需求飙升，催生了重磅股票上市，并使半导体行业今年的利润实现了[2580%的激增](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-s-industrial-profit-growth-slows-to-weakest-pace-this-year)。

根据本月发布的地区数据，合肥上半年经济同比增长[6.8%](https://tjj.hefei.gov.cn/tjyw/tjgb/15577953.html)，而2025年全年增长率为6.1%。合肥市统计局[表示](https://tjj.hefei.gov.cn/tjyw/tjgb/15577953.html)，前六个月工业产值激增26%，增速为近年罕见，电子产品产量飙升93%。

![Robotic arms operate on the production line of the Fuyao Glass Industry Group Co. manufacturing base in Hefei, China. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3ad81cc90b14810087cbc736f7efd0d5/3ad81cc9-0b14-810b-a657-dbfe3b3b0a32-b6d12079a0baf98896c46bc5e2d7896a.png)

与香港接壤的科技中心深圳GDP增长5.8%。紧随其后的是以生产AI数据中心所用存储芯片和光学元件而闻名的武汉，其经济增速为5.7%，电子产品产量飙升[91%](https://home.wuhan.gov.cn/mtbd/202607/t20260723_2824811.shtml)。在上海附近的工业中心苏州，光模块巨头[中际旭创股份有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300308%20CH%20Equity)的主要业务所在地，GDP增长5.6%，芯片出口激增[188%](https://tjj.suzhou.gov.cn/sztjj/sjfbyjd/202607/f55e9aa08d52467890c350e235d994db.shtml)。

落后者进一步落后。或许没有比长春更鲜明的对比了，这座城市依赖汽油车制造，2025年全年增速从4.9%下滑至仅1.6%。

除长春外，今年还有其他几个区域经济表现疲软。中部省份[湖南](https://baijiahao.baidu.com/s?id=1871279698307086472&wfr=spider&for=pc)因石化等传统行业放缓，GDP增长2.7%，其省会[长沙](http://tjj.changsha.gov.cn/tjxx/tjsj/sjfb/202607/t20260724_12509356.html)仅增长2.5%。西部煤炭开采中心[山西](https://tjj.shanxi.gov.cn/tjsj/jdsj/202607/t20260722_10181955.shtml)在发生自2009年以来最致命的矿难并导致产量[骤降](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/china-coal-mining-output-falls-most-in-decade-after-deadly-blast)后，仅增长2.1%。

随着一批根植于合肥、武汉等地、炙手可热的公司在股市创造数十亿美元收益，经济收益向这些城市集中的趋势可能变得更加极端。

长鑫存储（CXMT）刚刚完成了中国历史上[第二大](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/china-memory-champion-cxmt-set-to-debut-after-9-8-billion-ipo)首次公开募股。据彭博计算，该DRAM芯片制造商的上市为持有约40%股份的安徽省带来了[1920亿美元](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/cxmt-listing-creates-192-billion-windfall-for-chinese-province)的意外之财。还有几家IPO正在筹备中。

经济差距正变得更加极端。以长春为例，这座位于中国东北的汽车制造重镇正陷入困境。

随着全球石油危机冲击其支柱产业、国内汽车销量暴跌，这座城市成为许多地方经济体挣扎的典型代表——它们在向如今驱动中国增长的新兴产业转型方面行动迟缓。

长春工业产值今年前五个月同比[收缩](http://tjj.changchun.gov.cn/tjsj/202607/P020260707546551942750.pdf)4.4%，而2025年全年增长6.2%。汽车产业约占整体工业生产的60%。

随着经济压力加剧，当地政府本月罕见公开承认面临困难。

虽然这一表述——以及多数突出该表述的文章——迅速从中国大陆网站和社交媒体上被删除，但一份总结由该市最高官员主持的会议纪要的报告仍可在香港的[星岛日报](https://www.stheadline.com/zh-hans/realtime-china/3597202/%E9%95%BF%E6%98%A5%E5%B8%82%E5%A7%94%E5%85%A8%E5%B8%82%E7%BB%8F%E6%B5%8E%E5%8F%91%E5%B1%95%E9%9D%A2%E4%B8%B4%E5%89%8D%E6%89%80%E6%9C%AA%E6%9C%89%E5%9B%B0%E9%9A%BE%E5%92%8C%E6%8C%91%E6%88%98)和新加坡的中文[报纸](https://www.zaobao.com/news/china/story20260724-9412767)《联合早报》上找到。彭博致电长春市政府新闻办公室寻求置评时，无人接听电话。

声明中的措辞表明时间所剩无几。

在回应这些“超出预期的挑战”时，官员们表示将“以经济建设为中心，把发展作为第一要务，全力以赴，迎难而上”。

### English Original

## AI Boomtowns Expose Wider Divide in China’s Split-Screen Economy

In Hefei, the heart of China’s memory-chip industry, factories can barely keep up with the world’s appetite for AI hardware. In Changchun, the rust-belt home of gasoline-powered carmaking, officials admitted to “unprecedented” difficulties — a phrase later removed after drawing social media attention.

The two cities capture the split-screen economy emerging from the global AI boom. A handful of tech hubs delivered their biggest share of China’s growth in at least two decades in the first half, according to Nomura Holdings Inc., while the rest of the country slowed to the bottom edge of Beijing’s full-year target.

At the epicenter of the AI transformation in China are cities like Hefei, the capital of the eastern Anhui province and home to memory chip giant [CXMT Corp](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity). But even inside the boomtowns, little of the windfall is reaching households. Retail sales are shrinking in several of them, undercut by automation and temporary labor in the very factories driving the surge.

“The fruits of AI-driven growth are mainly reaped by a few ‘smart’ cities,” Nomura economists Jing Wang and Ting Lu said in a note on Wednesday. As that’s “unlikely to offset mounting growth headwinds across the rest of the country, we believe Beijing will likely step up policy efforts in the second half.”

The blistering performance shows both the promise and peril of AI for growth, especially as regions reliant on older industries quickly cede ground and a prolonged property downturn goes unresolved. For China, the outsize burden borne by a select few in powering the economy of the entire country is also a risk as major trading partners like the US [curb](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs) their imports of advanced technology and equipment.

Propelled by a global spending bonanza on artificial intelligence during the first half of the year, weighted average GDP growth of the seven cities dubbed “smart” by Nomura — which include Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen — picked up to 5.6%.

Together the group, also comprising Suzhou, Hangzhou, Hefei and Wuhan, accounted for a fifth of the national expansion in the first six months, according to Nomura. The rest of China saw a slowdown to 4.5%.

The growing divide could complicate China’s policy response. Top officials signaled a measured approach to the slowdown in the broader economy at a meeting of the Communist Party’s Politburo on Thursday, stopping short of announcing fresh stimulus.

More alarmingly, even the growth standouts struggled to revive consumer demand, echoing the challenges facing most of China.

The spread of automation and heavier reliance on temporary workers in electronics manufacturing are leaving the consumer economy no better off than it’s been since the collapse of property prices began about five years ago.

It’s a similar [story](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-04/taiwan-s-ai-boom-is-minting-millionaires-and-stoking-inequality) across Asia, where the benefits of new wealth are barely trickling down. The sector employs just [10 million](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202412/t20241226_1957895.html) people in China — according to the latest official data — 9% of the overall industrial workforce.

“Asia has been the biggest beneficiary of the AI boom, but the gains have largely bypassed households,” Alexandra Hermann Prasad, lead economist at Oxford Economics, said in a report. “The gains from the AI boom have accrued disproportionately to capital rather than labor.”

Soaring global demand for chips and other electronics needed to build data centers is meanwhile spawning blockbuster stock listings and enabling the semiconductor industry to record a [2,580% surge](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-s-industrial-profit-growth-slows-to-weakest-pace-this-year) in profits this year.

In Hefei, the economy expanded [6.8%](https://tjj.hefei.gov.cn/tjyw/tjgb/15577953.html) in the first half from a year ago, according to regional data published this month, compared with its full-year growth of 6.1% in 2025. Industrial production surged 26% in the first six months, a pace unseen in recent years, the city’s statistics bureau [said](https://tjj.hefei.gov.cn/tjyw/tjgb/15577953.html), and its electronics output soared 93%.

Shenzhen, the tech hub bordering Hong Kong, recorded a 5.8% gain in GDP. Close behind was Wuhan, known for making memory chips and optical components used in AI data centers, which saw the economy rise 5.7% as its output of electronics soared [91%](https://home.wuhan.gov.cn/mtbd/202607/t20260723_2824811.shtml). In Suzhou, an industrial hub near Shanghai that houses the primary operations of optical module giant [Zhongji Innolight Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/300308%20CH%20Equity), GDP grew 5.6% and chip exports surged [188%](https://tjj.suzhou.gov.cn/sztjj/sjfbyjd/202607/f55e9aa08d52467890c350e235d994db.shtml).

The have-nots are falling further behind. Perhaps nowhere is the contrast more stark than in Changchun, a city reliant on manufacturing gasoline-powered cars and whose growth slipped to just 1.6% from 4.9% during the full year of 2025.

Apart from Changchun, several other regional economies are sputtering this year. The central province of [Hunan](https://baijiahao.baidu.com/s?id=1871279698307086472&amp%3Bwfr=spider&amp%3Bfor=pc), which suffered a slowdown in traditional industries like petrochemicals, saw its GDP rise 2.7%, with its capital city [Changsha](http://tjj.changsha.gov.cn/tjxx/tjsj/sjfb/202607/t20260724_12509356.html) expanding just 2.5%. The western coal mining hub of [Shanxi](https://tjj.shanxi.gov.cn/tjsj/jdsj/202607/t20260722_10181955.shtml) grew only 2.1% after the deadliest mine blast since 2009 led to a [plunge](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/china-coal-mining-output-falls-most-in-decade-after-deadly-blast) in output.

The concentration of economic benefits around cities like Hefei and Wuhan can become even more extreme as a slew of red-hot companies with roots in these areas mint billions in the stock market.

CXMT, which just completed China’s [second-biggest](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/china-memory-champion-cxmt-set-to-debut-after-9-8-billion-ipo) initial public offering in history, delivered a [$192 billion windfall](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-28/cxmt-listing-creates-192-billion-windfall-for-chinese-province) to Anhui province, which holds a stake of about 40% in the manufacturer of DRAM chips, according to Bloomberg calculations. Several other IPOs are in the pipeline.

Economic disparities are becoming more extreme. Take Changchun, the embattled car manufacturing hub located in China’s northeast.

As the global oil shock hammered its pillar industry and domestic car sales plunged, the city became a prime example of the struggle faced by many local economies that have been late to transition toward the new sectors now driving China’s growth.

Changchun’s industrial output [contracted](http://tjj.changchun.gov.cn/tjsj/202607/P020260707546551942750.pdf) 4.4% in the first five months of this year from a year ago, after full-year growth of 6.2% in 2025. The auto sector accounts for about 60% of the overall industrial production.

With pressures on its economy intensifying, the local government this month made a rare public admission of facing hardships.

While that phrase — and most articles that highlighted it — was quickly scrubbed from mainland Chinese websites and social media, a report summarizing the readout of a meeting led by the city’s top official is still available from the Hong Kong-based [Sing Tao Daily](https://www.stheadline.com/zh-hans/realtime-china/3597202/%E9%95%BF%E6%98%A5%E5%B8%82%E5%A7%94%E5%85%A8%E5%B8%82%E7%BB%8F%E6%B5%8E%E5%8F%91%E5%B1%95%E9%9D%A2%E4%B8%B4%E5%89%8D%E6%89%80%E6%9C%AA%E6%9C%89%E5%9B%B0%E9%9A%BE%E5%92%8C%E6%8C%91%E6%88%98) and Singapore’s Chinese-language [newspaper](https://www.zaobao.com/news/china/story20260724-9412767) Lianhe Zaobao. No one picked up the phone at the Changchun city government’s press office when Bloomberg called seeking comment.

The language that remained in the statement made clear the time is running short.

In responding to these “greater-than-expected challenges,” officials said they will “make economic development the central task and the top priority, spare no effort and tackle challenges head on.”
