### 中文译文

## 中国对绿色氢能的大胆押注或将决定燃料的未来

赤峰绿色氢工厂。*来源：远景科技集团*

### **要点**（彭博AI）

- 自2024年底以来，中国的年度运营产能已增至三倍以上，目前拥有近25万吨绿色氢能。
- 中国在绿色氢能方面的成功取决于其庞大的工业基础以及尚需消纳的过剩清洁能源。
- 中国清洁电力的惊人扩张产生了丰富的风能和太阳能，将多余电力用于绿色氢能解决了利用率不足的问题。
中国正在对绿色氢能下大赌注，这一赌注可能决定这种长期被视为掌握全球能源转型关键燃料的命运。

风险巨大。据[彭博新能源财经](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/3470094Z:LN)称，自2024年底以来，中国的年度运营产能已增至三倍以上，目前拥有近25万吨——超过世界其他地区总和的两倍。北京最新的五年计划要求到本十年末将产能提高八倍，达到每年200万吨。

中国正在超越那些因成本飙升和需求疲软而搁置重大项目的竞争对手。相对于印度和欧洲等地，中国的成功取决于两个显著优势：一是被证明难以脱碳的庞大工业基础，二是需要找到用武之地的过剩清洁能源。

“中国正在对可再生氢进行战略性押注，将其作为将清洁电力输送到电气化难以覆盖的行业的一种方式，”能源智库Ember驻悉尼分析师杨木易表示。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481c2a109fab4b202dab5/3c981cc9-0b14-812e-bc7b-def02a75dd80-156d50f8074b63e02b9af6a2096b5649.png)

从北京乘高铁向北两小时，内蒙古赤峰横跨中国的过去与未来。这座拥有近四百万人口的城市烟囱林立。在郊区，一平方公里的土地被用于一个旨在清理以采矿为基础、以煤炭为动力的当地经济的项目。

[远景集团](https://www.envision-group.com/)的这座设施——拥有棱角分明的白色外墙、落地玻璃墙和极简内饰——是全球最大的绿氢工厂。该项目耗资26亿美元，于2024年开始运营，利用电解槽通过风能和太阳能将水分解为氢气，支持者称这将改变降低排放的努力。

中国在清洁电力方面的非凡扩张带来了丰富的风能和太阳能，以至于越来越多的电力被白白浪费。将多余电力用于制绿氢解决了利用率不足的问题。当转化为氨等化学品时，它还能让可再生能源像液体电池一样储存以备将来使用。

这个问题对政策制定者来说变得更加紧迫，因为在已经面临产能过剩和激烈价格竞争的情况下，浪费削弱了可再生能源项目的经济效益。

近年来，关于全球将新增多少绿氢产能的预测已被下调。如果像赤峰这样的工厂取得成功，将重申中国作为清洁技术强国的统治地位，并为在最顽固的行业减少排放提供路径。但如果失败，将使绿氢在短期内能否在任何地方实现商业起飞受到质疑。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b1481c2a109fab4b202dab5/3c981cc9-0b14-8190-bbaf-c0e6092c9f25-bce5bcdd2ad9e321dc457ce6ab647f20.png)

电力在中国终端能源消费中的占比稳步上升，2025年达到30%。政府的目标是到2030年达到35%。迄今为止的进展得益于该国庞大的煤炭储量、日益便宜的风能和太阳能以及电动汽车的繁荣。

接下来的五个百分点，还需要考虑该国从煤炭向可再生能源的逐步转型，如果不对方法进行革新将更难实现。借鉴造就其世界领先的风能、太阳能和电动汽车产业的成功经验，北京正专注于先夯实供应。希望需求会随之而来。

“随着中国新五年规划的出台，该行业的增长将越来越取决于中国以及与中国关联的经济体如何培育绿色氢能的需求，”远景科技集团首席产品官兼氢能业务负责人楼益民（Yimin Lou）表示。

但挑战重重。绿色氢能燃料的成本仍然高于由天然气和煤制取的灰氢和黑氢，也高于将化石燃料与碳捕获相结合的蓝氢。

绿色氢能生产商可以获得中央和地方政府的补贴，以及国家碳信用额度的支持。但彭博新能源财经（BNEF）表示，即使将这些补贴加在一起，节省的成本也不足以与灰氢的成本相匹敌。

此外，这些补贴未必直接作用于消费端。彭博新能源财经分析师Kathy Gao表示：“最重要的是，需求侧政策能否落实，以及能否激发真实需求并支撑价格溢价。”

### **领先地位**

尽管如此，中国的优势仍然可观。

据彭博新能源财经称，中国的电解槽成本约为欧洲产品的四分之一。除了在清洁能源领域的领先地位外，中国还在钢铁、水泥和化工等高污染行业的全球生产中占据主导地位，这些行业减排进展缓慢，但如果北京要实现其气候目标，就必须脱碳。其他国家若要达到同样的氢能水平，则需要投入更多资金，并依赖出口来满足未来需求。

“可以公平地说，几乎从各个角度来看，中国都是这一行业发展最有利的市场，”Gao表示。

内蒙古就是这一机遇的例证。它拥有世界上最丰富的风能和太阳能资源，但电力需求集中在遥远的沿海城市。电网基础设施滞后，导致可再生能源弃电现象日益增多。

氢能提供了一种解决方案。在远景科技（Envision）的工厂中，附近的风电场和太阳能电站供应全部电力。该工厂的电解槽专为应对风和太阳发电的波动性而设计。

“关键考虑是如何释放对可再生能源的更大需求，”远景科技的卢（Lou）表示，“从历史上看，市场吸收可再生能源电力（尤其是风能和太阳能）的能力一直受到限制。绿色氢能和绿色氨等产品创造了全新的需求来源。”

## China’s Bold Bet on Green Hydrogen Could Determine Fuel’s Future

Chifeng green hydrogen plant.*Source: Envision Group*

### **Takeaways **by Bloomberg AI

- China has more than tripled its annual operational capacity since the end of 2024 and now hosts nearly 250,000 tons of green hydrogen.
- China's success in green hydrogen hinges on its massive industrial base and surplus of clean energy that needs to find a home.
- China's extraordinary expansion of clean power has created wind and solar power in abundance, and diverting the excess to green hydrogen solves the problem of under-utilization.
China is making a big bet on green hydrogen that could make or break a fuel long-touted as holding the keys to the global energy transition.

The stakes are substantial. China has more than tripled its annual operational capacity since the end of 2024, and now hosts nearly 250,000 tons — over twice that of the rest of the world combined, according to [BloombergNEF](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/3470094Z:LN). Beijing’s latest five-year plan calls for an eightfold increase to 2 million tons a year by the end of the decade.

China is outstripping rivals that have seen major projects shelved due to ballooning costs and anemic demand. Its success hinges on two distinct advantages over places like India and Europe: a massive industrial base that’s proving tough to decarbonize, and a surplus of clean energy that needs to find a home.

“China is making a strategic bet on renewable hydrogen as a way to carry clean electricity into the sectors electrification struggles to reach,” said Muyi Yang, a Sydney-based analyst at energy think tank Ember.

Two hours north of Beijing by bullet train, Chifeng in Inner Mongolia straddles China’s past and future. Home to nearly four million people, the city is dotted with smokestack chimneys. On the outskirts, one square kilometer has been given over to a project that promises to clean up a local economy founded on mining and powered by coal.

The [Envision Group](https://www.envision-group.com/) facility — all sharp-angled white facades, floor-to-ceiling glass walls and minimalist interiors — is the world’s biggest green hydrogen plant. At a cost of $2.6 billion, it began operating in 2024, employing electrolyzers that use wind and solar to split water into an element that its boosters say will transform efforts to lower emissions.

China’s extraordinary expansion of clean power has created wind and solar power in abundance, to the degree that an increasing amount is simply being wasted. Diverting the excess to green hydrogen solves the problem of under-utilization. When converted to chemicals such as ammonia, it also allows renewables to be stored for future use, much like a liquid battery.

The issue has become more pressing for policymakers because wastage undermines the economics of renewables projects at a time when they’re already grappling with excess capacity and fierce price competition.

Projections on how much green hydrogen capacity the world will add have been scaled back in recent years. If plants like Chifeng succeed, it would reaffirm China’s dominance as a clean-tech powerhouse, and offer a path to cutting emissions in the stubbornest industries. But failure would throw into doubt whether green hydrogen can achieve commercial liftoff anywhere in the near term.

The share of electricity in China’s final energy use has risen steadily, reaching 30% in 2025. The government is targeting 35% by 2030. The gains so far have been smoothed by the country’s vast coal reserves, ever-cheaper wind and solar, and the electric vehicle boom.

The next five percentage points, which also need to take account of the country’s gradual shift from coal to renewables, will be harder to capture without a new approach. Adopting the playbook that delivered its world-beating wind, solar and EV industries, Beijing is focused on shoring up supply first. The hope is that demand will follow.

“With the rollout of China’s new five-year plan, the industry’s growth will increasingly depend on how China, and economies linked to China, can foster demand for green hydrogen,” said Yimin Lou, Envision’s chief product officer and head of its hydrogen business.

But challenges abound. The green version of the fuel is still more expensive than the gray and black hydrogen that comes from gas and coal, and the blue hydrogen that combines fossil fuels with carbon capture.

Green hydrogen producers have access to subsidies from both central and local governments and via national carbon credits. But even combined, the savings fall short of matching gray hydrogen’s cost, BNEF says.

Moreover, the subsidies don’t necessarily feed directly into consumption. “The most important thing is whether demand-side policies can be implemented, and whether they can stimulate some real demand and support the price premium,” said BNEF analyst Kathy Gao.

### **Pole Position**

Still, China’s advantages are considerable.

Its electrolyzers cost about four times less than those made in Europe, according to BNEF. In addition to its lead in clean energy, the country dominates global production of highly polluting industries like steel, cement and chemicals, which have been slow to cut emissions but must decarbonize if Beijing is to meet its climate goals. For other countries to get to the same level on hydrogen, they’d need to invest a lot more and rely on exports for future demand.

“It’s fair to say that, from virtually every perspective, China is the most favorable market for the development of this industry,” said Gao.

Inner Mongolia exemplifies the opportunity. It boasts some of the world’s richest wind and solar resources, yet electricity demand is concentrated in faraway coastal cities. Grid infrastructure is lagging, which has led to rising curtailments of renewable power.

Hydrogen offers a remedy. At Envision’s facility, nearby wind and solar farms supply all the electricity. The plant’s electrolyzers are specifically designed for the variable levels of power delivered by the wind and sun.

“The key consideration is how to unlock greater demand for renewable energy,” said Envision’s Lou. “Historically, the ability of the market to absorb renewable power, particularly from wind and solar, has been constrained. Products such as green hydrogen and green ammonia create entirely new sources of demand.”
