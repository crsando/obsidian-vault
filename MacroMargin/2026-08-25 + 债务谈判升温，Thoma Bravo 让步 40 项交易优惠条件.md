## 债务谈判升温，Thoma Bravo 让步 40 项交易优惠条件

作者：[Rachel Graf](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWoaAJWtc5E/rachel-graf) 和 [Aaron Weinman](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXOtkASm1O4/aaron-weinman)，2026年8月25日，晚上8:10 GMT+8

### **要点** 由 Bloomberg AI 提供

- Thoma Bravo 高管向贷款方让步，导致 Proofpoint 交易文件出现 40 项有利于债权人的修改，包括收紧未来借款限制和定期电话沟通。
- 这些修改还包括额外每年 6000 万美元利息，以将 50 亿美元贷款的大部分延长两年，贷款方因市场对 AI 颠覆的担忧而试图索取高昂代价。
- 该交易可能成为其他面临大规模债务到期墙的私募股权公司的模板，行业观察人士表示，Thoma 所做的让步将成为标准，并为其他公司提供可遵循的范本。
[Thoma Bravo](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/129626Z:US) 高管在与贷款方谈判时就知道他们不得不让步——唯一的问题是要让多少。

另一方是华尔街一些最大的资金管理公司——如[贝莱德公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BLK%20US%20Equity)和[景顺有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/IVZ%20US%20Equity)——他们非常清楚力量平衡已向他们倾斜。[Proofpoint](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PFPT%20US%20Equity)是 Thoma 多年前收购的网络安全软件公司，需要更多时间来偿还为收购融资而堆积在资产负债表上的数十亿美元债务。随着市场对任何哪怕稍微容易受到 AI 颠覆影响的事物日益感到不安，贷款方决心收取高昂代价作为回报。

![The building that houses Thoma Bravo’s offices in Miami. Photographer: Scott McIntyre/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b1481d3aab7d09741f9cc96/3c781cc9-0b14-8160-84cf-e326a9c17533-961610df5b5cca1792d686342f47be98.png)

位于迈阿密的 Thoma Bravo 办公室所在大楼。摄影师：Scott McIntyre/Bloomberg

他们得到了想要的，甚至更多。

最终统计：约 40 项对债权人友好的交易文件修改，包括对未来借款的更严格限制和定期电话沟通，以及每年额外 6000 万美元的利息，即 PRICED: Proofpoint 43 亿美元 TLB；S+450，0.50%，98.0，5 亿美元贷款延长两年。

业内观察人士表示，对于这家私募股权巨头来说，这桩交易算是比较容易的之一。

到 2028 年底，约有 90 亿美元的投资组合公司债务到期——其中包括另一家网络安全公司 [Sophos](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2520506D:US) 在 3 月份欠下的 20 亿美元——Thoma 正争分夺秒地说服贷款人继续支持它，以及一个突然失宠的软件行业。

上月底结束的 Proofpoint 谈判（[链接](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/thoma-bravo-cedes-to-lender-revolt-on-5-billion-proofpoint-loan)）让我们初步看到，随着更多贷款到期，Thoma 可能需要做出哪些让步。为了换取更多时间，它给了债权人对公司财务运作大得多的控制权。亚里士多德太平洋资本的高级董事总经理 Michael Marzouk 表示，如果这种方法被证明成功，这笔交易可能成为其他面临巨额到期债务的私募股权公司的模板。

“Thoma 在软件领域树立了标准，”Marzouk 说。“他们做出的让步将成为标准，并为其他公司提供可供遵循的范本。”

Thoma Bravo 的一位代表拒绝就与贷款人的谈判状况置评。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b1481d3aab7d09741f9cc96/3c781cc9-0b14-81bb-8719-c593d1f5ad86-c67a3222a99f97a857f1e87f4887883d.png)

受可预测收入流的吸引，这些收入流可支持高额债务负担，私募股权公司花了十多年时间收购和扩大围绕“软件即服务”（SaaS）模式建立的公司。很少有公司像 Thoma 那样激进，它收购了数十家这样的公司，在此过程中为投资者带来了巨额回报，并建立了全球最大的软件投资组合之一。

现在，其中许多公司正[担忧](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-private-equity-software/)人工智能可能颠覆其商业模式。这使得私募股权公司出售或将这些公司上市变得[更加困难](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/private-market-firms-like-apollo-and-bain-move-into-private-equity-funding-niche)，而通常在贷款到期前几年完成的再融资也放缓至近乎停滞。

“对 SaaS 的需求比几年前低得多，”哈佛商学院金融部门负责人 Victoria Ivashina 说道。

直到最近几周，市场才开始解冻。Thoma 感觉到了机会，迅速抓住。

6 月下旬，这家收购公司为 Imprivata 公司 12 亿美元债务进行了再融资。Imprivata 是一家医疗网络安全公司，被广泛认为是最强大的投资组合公司之一。定价：Imprivata 12 亿美元 TLB；S+375，0.50%，99.0，支付比浮动利率基准高 0.75 个百分点的利率，并包含多项优惠条件。据知情人士透露，除两家贷款方外，所有贷款方均同意参与，并引入新投资者以取代它们。

这笔交易将为一个月后的 Proofpoint 奠定基础。

### **全面障碍**

Proofpoint 的再融资将带来更大的挑战。

这笔贷款规模约为前者的四倍，分布于更广泛的贷款机构群体，且信用评级较低。与此同时，抵押贷款债券（CLO）的经理人——杠杆贷款的最大买家之一——仍在削减对软件公司的敞口。

在与[高盛集团](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GS%20US%20Equity)的银行家磋商后，Thoma 提出了对贷款协议的大约十几项修改，以争取投资者。在接下来九天的谈判中，修改总数增至约 40 项，包括对借款、投资和资产转让的更严格限制，禁止某些私下协商的债务回购，以及针对所谓负债管理操作的新保障措施。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b1481d3aab7d09741f9cc96/3c781cc9-0b14-8148-9e40-fcd7ea0f5dc6-6178871db0be5f861ca6208ccdeb11a6.png)

放贷方表示，赢得的两个最大让步是所谓的“全面阻截条款”（omni blocker）和强制季度电话会议。阻截条款通常对可能将资产移出债权人触及范围、增加优先债务或以其他方式削弱其对公司债权的交易施加限制。同时，季度电话会议给了他们此前未获保证的东西：定期查看公司财务业绩并有机会质询管理层。

其他让步包括所谓的 Pluralsight 和 Xerox 保护条款，旨在防止有价值的知识产权被转移至放贷方掌控范围之外。

这些保障措施意义重大，因为 Thoma 历来回避 LME，部分凸显了放贷方如今预测人工智能对软件公司影响有多困难。

“作为放贷方，终于感觉风向转向对我们有利了，”Barrow Hanley Global Investors 的投资组合经理 Nick Losey 表示。“任何能降低 LME 发生几率的措施，即使不能降为零，我认为也是积极的。”

尽管如此，Thoma 在一个重要问题上坚持立场：债务仍为轻契约（covenant-lite）。这意味着 Proofpoint 无需为保持贷款合规而满足与杠杆或盈利等指标相关的定期财务测试。Thoma 也无需向公司注入任何新的股权。

知情人士称，最终只有一位主要投资者选择不为其在 Proofpoint 交易中的部分进行再融资。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b1481d3aab7d09741f9cc96/3c781cc9-0b14-816d-b7d7-d9fa56da3d02-e0a16ac9593069458005077d56905d91.png)

据彭博汇编的数据，市场观察人士表示，这笔交易可能成为其他私募股权公司的蓝本，这些公司未来几年总计有超过 550 亿美元的大规模银团软件债务到期。

本月早些时候，[Veritas Capital Fund Management](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/3311331Z:US)屈服于投资者的要求，因其垃圾债务贷款人向Veritas支持的软件公司施压，以对医疗保健软件公司[Gainwell Technologies](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/9340321Z:US)的债务进行再融资。

[Clearlake Capital Group](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/8376046Z:US)拥有超过80亿美元的软件债务将于2028年底前到期，其中部分以困境水平交易。该公司以比其他发起人更频繁地使用LME而闻名，如今却面临贷款人占据上风的市场。这可能迫使Clearlake提供保护措施，以防范其曾经利用过的那些操作手法。

5月，随着贷款价值暴跌，软件公司Symplr与贷款人进行谈判；该公司（指Clearlake）也与投资组合公司[Symplr Software](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1800363D:US)的贷款人就一笔将于明年到期、金额12亿美元的贷款进行了接洽。据知情人士透露，任何协议都可能涉及与Proofpoint交易中类似的让步。

Janus Henderson全球多元板块和企业信贷主管John Lloyd表示，面对承压软件债务的贷款人将进入自助模式。他说：“他们持有票据，不希望公司违约，因此也希望能将期限延长，并希望这有助于改善这些证券的交易价格。”

据彭博汇编的数据，Clearlake旗下其他在2028年底前有债务到期的主要公司包括[Precisely Software](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1096258Z:US)、[Cornerstone OnDemand](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CSOD%20US%20Equity)和[FinThrive Software](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1962395D:US)。FinThrive在2024年进行了债务重组，其超过10亿美元的贷款在连续两个季度盈利疲软后，已在困境水平交易数月。

Veritas未回应置评请求，而Clearlake的一位代表拒绝置评。

Thoma Bravo的Conga在交易失败后实现债务延期，其已从一组银行获得超过5亿美元的贷款延期承诺，将提供给企业软件制造商Conga Corp.。该交易于上周浮出水面，此前贷款人今年早些时候在银团市场上难以对该债务进行再融资。

然而，更多考验迫在眉睫。

Sophos 有超过 20 亿美元债务将于 3 月到期，这预计将是 Thoma 迄今最艰难的再融资。该公司今年早些时候被穆迪评级下调至 B3，原因是运营业绩不及预期且杠杆率不断上升。

该公司已开始在广泛银团贷款市场中[试探投资者](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/thoma-bravo-weighs-concessions-in-2-billion-sophos-refinancing)，此前寻求私人信贷支持的尝试受挫。与 Proofpoint 一样，贷款机构正在推动更强的债权人保护。

另一个关键考验将是物业管理软件公司 [RealPage](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/RP%20US%20Equity)，该公司有超过 40 亿美元债务将于 2028 年初到期。

“人工智能颠覆风险已大幅压低估值，与此同时资本成本上升，” Newfleet 资产管理公司高级投资组合经理 Frank Ossino 表示。“这就是挑战所在。”

## Thoma Bravo Conceded 40 Deal Sweeteners as Debt Talks Heat Up

By [Rachel Graf](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AWoaAJWtc5E/rachel-graf) and [Aaron Weinman](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AXOtkASm1O4/aaron-weinman)August 25, 2026 at 8:10 PM GMT+8

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

- Thoma Bravo executives gave ground to lenders, resulting in 40 creditor-friendly changes to the deal documents for Proofpoint, including tighter limits on future borrowing and regular check-in calls.
- The changes also included an extra $60 million in annual interest to extend most of the $5 billion loan by two years, with lenders seeking to extract a steep price due to market skittishness about AI disruption.
- The deal may become a template for other private equity firms facing large maturity walls, with industry watchers saying the concessions Thoma made will become standard and set a playbook for others to follow.
[Thoma Bravo](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/129626Z:US) executives went into talks with lenders knowing they’d have to give ground — the only question was how much.

On the other side were some of Wall Street’s biggest money managers — names like [BlackRock Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BLK%20US%20Equity) and [Invesco Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/IVZ%20US%20Equity) — and they were well aware the balance of power had shifted in their favor. [Proofpoint](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/PFPT%20US%20Equity), the cybersecurity software company Thoma had taken over years earlier, needed more time to repay billions of dollars of debt loaded onto its balance sheet to finance the acquisition. With markets increasingly skittish about anything even remotely vulnerable to AI disruption, the lenders were determined to extract a steep price in return.

The building that houses Thoma Bravo’s offices in Miami. Photographer: Scott McIntyre/Bloomberg

They got what they wanted, and more.

The final tally: some 40 creditor-friendly changes to the deal documents, including tighter limits on future borrowing and regular check-in calls, as well as an extra $60 million in annual interest to PRICED: Proofpoint $4.3b TLB; S+450, 0.50%, 98.0 of the $5 billion loan by two years.

And for the private equity giant, that deal was one of the easy ones, industry watchers say.

With about $9 billion of portfolio company debt maturing by the end of 2028 — including a $2 billion slug in March owed by another cybersecurity firm, [Sophos](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2520506D:US) — Thoma is racing to convince lenders to stick with it and, in turn, a software industry that’s suddenly fallen out of favor.

The Proofpoint negotiations, which [wrapped up](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/thoma-bravo-cedes-to-lender-revolt-on-5-billion-proofpoint-loan) late last month, offer an early glimpse of the concessions Thoma may need to make as more loans come due. In exchange for additional time, it gave creditors far greater control over the company’s financial maneuvering. If the approach proves successful, the deal could become a template for other private equity firms facing large maturity walls, according to Michael Marzouk, a senior managing director at Aristotle Pacific Capital.

“Thoma, they set the standard in software,” Marzouk said. “The concessions they’re making will become standard and set a playbook for others to follow.”

A representative for Thoma Bravo declined to comment on the state of its negotiations with lenders.

Drawn by predictable revenue streams that could support heavy debt loads, private equity firms spent more than a decade buying and scaling up companies built around the “software as a service,” or SaaS, model. Few were as aggressive as Thoma, which acquired scores of them, along the way generating blockbuster returns for investors and building one of the world’s largest software portfolios.

Now, many of those same companies are [confronting fears](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-private-equity-software/) that artificial intelligence could disrupt their business models. That has made it [even harder](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-24/private-market-firms-like-apollo-and-bain-move-into-private-equity-funding-niche) for private equity firms to sell or take those companies public, while refinancings, often completed years before loans mature, slowed to a crawl.

“Appetite for SaaS is way lower than it would’ve been a few years ago,” said Victoria Ivashina, head of the finance unit at Harvard Business School.

Only in recent weeks has the market begun to thaw. Thoma, sensing an opening, moved to seize it.

In late June the buyout shop refinanced $1.2 billion of debt for [Imprivata](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/IMPR%20US%20Equity), a healthcare cybersecurity firm widely considered one of its strongest portfolio companies. The PRICED: Imprivata $1.2b TLB; S+375, 0.50%, 99.0 paid an extra 0.75 percentage point over the floating-rate benchmark and included a number of sweeteners. All but two of its lenders agreed to participate, according to people with knowledge of the situation, and new investors were brought in to replace them.

The deal would help set the stage for Proofpoint a month later.

### **Omni Blocker**

Proofpoint’s refinancing would present a far bigger challenge.

The loan was roughly four times the size, spread across a broader group of lenders and carried a lower credit rating. At the same time, managers of collateralized loan obligations — among the biggest buyers of leveraged loans — were still cutting their exposure to software companies.

After conferring with bankers at [Goldman Sachs Group Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GS%20US%20Equity), Thoma proposed about a dozen changes to the loan agreement in an effort to win over investors. During the next nine days of negotiations, the total rose to roughly 40, including tighter limits on borrowing, investments and asset transfers, a ban on certain privately negotiated debt buybacks and new safeguards against so-called liability-management exercises.

Lenders said that two of the biggest concessions won were a so-called omni blocker and mandatory quarterly calls. Blockers typically impose restrictions on transactions that could shift assets out of creditors’ reach, add new debt ahead of them or otherwise weaken their claims on the company. The quarterly calls, meanwhile, gave them something they hadn’t previously been guaranteed: a regular look at the company’s financial performance and a chance to question management.

Other concessions included so-called Pluralsight and Xerox protections designed to keep valuable intellectual property from being moved beyond lenders’ grasp.

The safeguards were notable given that Thoma has historically avoided LMEs, in part underscoring how difficult it’s become for lenders to predict the impact of AI on software companies.

“It finally does feel like the winds have shifted in our favor as a lender,” said Nick Losey, portfolio manager at Barrow Hanley Global Investors. “Anything that reduces the chances of an LME, even if you can’t reduce it down to zero, I think is positive.”

Still, Thoma held the line on one important point: the debt remained covenant-lite. That means Proofpoint doesn’t have to meet regular financial tests tied to measures such as leverage or earnings in order to remain in compliance with the loan. Thoma also didn’t have to put any new equity into the company.

Ultimately, just one major investor opted not to refinance its portion of the Proofpoint deal, the people said.

Market watchers say the transaction could serve as a blueprint for other private equity firms, which collectively have more than $55 billion of broadly-syndicated software debt coming due over the next couple years, according to data compiled by Bloomberg.

Earlier this month [Veritas Capital Fund Management](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/3311331Z:US) bowed to investor demands for their Junk-Debt Lenders Tighten Screws on Veritas-Backed Software Firm to refinance the debt of healthcare software company [Gainwell Technologies](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/9340321Z:US).

[Clearlake Capital Group](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/8376046Z:US) has more than $8 billion of software debt coming due by the end of 2028, some of it trading at distressed levels. Known for making heavier use of LMEs than other sponsors, the firm is confronting a market in which lenders have gained the upper hand. That could force Clearlake to offer protections against the same maneuvers it once used to its advantage.

In May, it Software Firm Symplr in Talks With Lenders as Loan Values Plunge with lenders to portfolio company [Symplr Software](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1800363D:US) over its $1.2 billion loan maturing next year. Any agreement is likely to involve concessions similar to those in the Proofpoint deal, according to a person with with knowledge of the situation.

“Existing holders are going to be in self-help mode,” said John Lloyd, global head of multi-sector and corporate credit at Janus Henderson, referring to lenders exposed to software debt that’s come under pressure. “They hold the paper and they do not want the company to default, so they’re going to want to push the maturity out as well and hope it helps the trading price of those securities.”
