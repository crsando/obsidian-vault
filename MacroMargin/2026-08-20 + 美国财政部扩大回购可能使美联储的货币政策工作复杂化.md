### 中文译文

## 美国财政部扩大回购可能使美联储的货币政策工作复杂化

![U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent attends a media interview at the White House](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481a68745f2d5fd017bd0/3c281cc9-0b14-81cc-bd7a-ddbef131b9f7-6e955aa46db23fb13156c70c1df8c65f.jpg)

路透社/Kylie Cooper

摘要

- 财政部回购不太可能伴随美联储资产购买
- 财政部购债引发对其市场影响力的质疑
- 分析师认为美联储主席沃什将如何与财政部合作存在不确定性
路透8月20日 - 美联储主席凯文·沃什承诺，美国央行将实现物价稳定，但美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特周三决定将较长期美国国债回购规模加倍，这可能使任何实现物价稳定的努力复杂化。

周三，美国财政部宣布，将其购买期限在10至30年之间的美国国债的回购规模[加倍](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-treasury-double-sizes-some-debt-buyback-operations-least-4-billion-2026-08-19/)，至每次操作40亿美元。美国政府宣布这一决定之际，较长期美国国债借贷成本急剧上升，对全球经济的信贷可负担性造成普遍负面影响。

财政部的这一声明帮助在随后的交易中令收益率[下降](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/view-us-treasury-will-double-some-bond-buy-backs-2026-08-19/)，收益率与价格呈反向变动。

它的行动引发了疑问：在影响整体信贷状况方面，美联储和财政部现在究竟谁的影响力更大。

"鉴于沃什希望少说，加上[财政部长斯科特]贝森特今天的行动，重心可能正从美联储转向财政部。我们必须看看这是否会持续下去，因为这对交易员来说将是一个重大变化，" TradeStation全球市场策略主管戴维·拉塞尔表示。

自二十年前的全球金融危机以来，美联储一直利用资产购买来安抚市场并降低长期借贷成本。美国国债收益率的上升令市场观察人士感到不安，并引发了疑问：当前形势是否已经严重到美联储需要介入的地步，尽管人们对新的财政部回购计划的长期效力也存在诸多疑问。

沃什长期以来一直对将央行资产购买作为政策工具持怀疑态度，并将缩减目前为6.8万亿美元的资产负债表作为基石目标。但他也表示愿意与财政部合作并尽可能协调，这可能会让这位美联储领导人尽管总体上厌恶美联储持有大量资产，却仍更愿意与财政部合作。

沃什可能采取何种行动更加复杂，因为他几乎完全拒绝解释他对货币政策前景的看法，也不愿就他如何分析数据以做出政策决定提供更多指导。

### 高门槛

迄今为止，市场参与者认为美联储没有理由介入。

"目前美联储介入进行稳定市场的购买门槛非常高，我们需要看到流动性严重恶化和市场功能失调的迹象，而目前我们根本没有看到这些迹象，" 道明证券美国利率策略主管根纳季·戈德堡表示。

最重要的是，市场仍以美联储能够管理其利率目标区间的方式运行，美联储7月底联邦公开市场委员会会议纪要（周三发布）确认，这是央行实现其就业和通胀使命的主要工具。

“我不认为这会影响美联储控制短期利率的能力，”摩根大通首席美国经济学家迈克尔·费罗利(Michael Feroli)表示。

财政部扩大回购操作所针对的债务范围，对抵押贷款和企业借贷等现实世界借贷成本影响重大。在通胀担忧、政府借贷需求上升，以及财政部面临来自竞相建设人工智能基础设施的公司的发债竞争之际，长期债券的借贷成本已触及近二十年高位。

按理说，美联储的资产购买可以降低这些收益率或限制其上升，而且美联储在这方面比财政部拥有更多火力。但这将类似于放松货币政策，这与美联储为降低仍远高于2%目标的通胀而持续做出的努力很难相吻合。

PGIM首席全球经济学家达利普·辛格(Daleep Singh)曾在纽约联储和财政部任职，他表示，财政部的做法最终不会改变推高收益率的故事，尽管其行动将聚光灯投向了一个真实问题，但这样做“没有解决该问题的可信策略”。

### 缩表

美联储持有资产的规模对沃什很重要，因为他认为美联储持有过多债券，这些持有扭曲了金融市场定价。他希望美联储减少债券持有，但表示考虑到问题的复杂性，实现这一目标需要时间。

在6月份的联邦公开市场委员会会议上，他宣布了一系列评估美联储问题的特别工作组，其中一个专门致力于解决美联储资产负债表问题。

市场普遍猜测，美联储或许能够通过修改银行规则来缩小其资产持有规模，这些规则将允许银行持有更少的应急流动性，但有人担心这可能加大金融稳定风险，并迫使美联储在压力时期更频繁地干预。

由迈克尔·S·德比、安·萨菲尔和丹·伯恩斯报道；由奥罗拉·埃利斯编辑

### English Original

## Treasury's upsized buybacks may complicate Fed's monetary policy work

REUTERS/Kylie Cooper

Summary

- Treasury buyback unlikely to be joined by Fed asset buying
- Treasury buying raises questions about its influence on markets
- Analysts see uncertainty how Fed Chair Warsh will work with Treasury
Aug 20 (Reuters) - Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh has promised that the U.S. central bank will deliver price stability, but Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's decision on Wednesday to double buybacks of longer-dated U.S. debt may complicate any effort to do so.

On Wednesday, the Treasury Department announced that it was [doubling](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-treasury-double-sizes-some-debt-buyback-operations-least-4-billion-2026-08-19/) the size of its effort to buy back Treasury securities with maturities between 10 and 30 years, to $4 billion per operation. The government announced this as long-dated Treasury borrowing costs have been moving up sharply, negatively impacting credit affordability in the global economy generally.

The Treasury announcement helped bring yields, which move inversely to prices, [down](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/view-us-treasury-will-double-some-bond-buy-backs-2026-08-19/) in trading that followed the announcement.

Its action has generated questions as to whether the Fed or the Treasury is now the bigger influence on general credit conditions.

"Given Warsh’s desire to say less and [Treasury Secretary Scott] Bessent’s actions today, the center of gravity could be moving from the Fed to the Treasury. We’ll have to see if this continues because it would be a big change for traders," said David Russell, global head of market strategy at TradeStation.

Since the global financial crisis two decades ago the central bank has used asset buying to calm markets and to lower long-term borrowing costs. The rise in Treasury bond yields has been jarring to market observers and has raised questions as to whether the situation is extreme enough for the Fed to get involved, even as there are lots of questions about the longer-term potency of the new Treasury buyback schedule.

Warsh has long expressed skepticism over using central bank asset buying as a policy tool, and has made as a cornerstone goal getting what is now a $6.8 trillion balance sheet lower. But he has also signaled a willingness to work with the Treasury and coordinate where possible, which might make the Fed leader more open to link up with the Treasury despite his broader disdain for large Fed holdings.

How Warsh could act is further complicated by his near blanket refusal to explain what he thinks lies ahead for monetary policy and his unwillingness to provide much guidance on how he looks at data to reach policy decisions.

### HIGH BAR

Thus far, market participants don’t see a case for Fed involvement.

“The bar for the Fed to step in with market-stabilizing purchases is very high at the moment and we would need to see signs of liquidity deteriorating enormously and signs of market dysfunction, which we're simply not seeing,” said Gennadiy Goldberg, Head of U.S. Rates Strategy at TD Securities.

Most importantly, the market is functioning in a way that the Fed can still manage its interest rate target range, which meeting minutes from the late July Federal Open Market Committee, released Wednesday, affirmed is the central bank’s main tool to achieve its job and inflation mandates.

“I don’t see any impact on the Fed's ability to control short-term interest rates,” said Michael Feroli, chief U.S. economist at J.P. Morgan.

The range of debt targeted by the Treasury increased buyback operation is very influential to real world borrowing costs for things like mortgages and corporate borrowing. Borrowing costs for the long bond hit nearly a two-decade high amid concerns about inflation, government borrowing appetites, and as the Treasury faces competition from the offerings from companies furiously building out infrastructure for artificial intelligence.

Presumably Fed asset buying could lower those yields or cap their rise, and it would have more firepower to devote to the matter than the Treasury. But as it would be akin to an easing of monetary policy and that would be hard to square against the Fed’s ongoing efforts to lower inflation that still stands well above the 2% target.

Daleep Singh, chief global economist at PGIM, who also served at the New York Fed and Treasury Department, said what the Treasury has done ultimately doesn't change the story driving yields up and while its action is shining a spotlight on a real issue it is doing so "without a credible strategy to solve" the issue.

### SHRINKAGE

The size of Fed holdings matters to Warsh because he believes the Fed owns too many bonds and that those holdings distort financial market pricing. He wants the Fed to own fewer bonds but has said that will take time to achieve that given the complexity of the issue.

At the June FOMC meeting he announced a series of task forces weighing Fed issues and one of them was devoted to tackling Fed balance sheet issues.

There is broad speculation the Fed may be able to get its holdings smaller by changes in banking rules that would permit banks to hold less emergency liquidity, though some fear that could increase financial stability risks and force the Fed to intervene more often in times of stress.

Reporting by Michael S. Derby, Ann Saphir and Dan Burns; Editing by Aurora Ellis
