***The following is the transcript of an interview with Neel Kashkari, President and CEO of the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis that aired on "Face the Nation with Margaret Brennan" on Aug. 23, 2026.***

***以下是2026年8月23日在玛格丽特·布伦南主持的《面向全国》节目中播出的对明尼阿波利斯联邦储备银行总裁兼首席执行官尼尔·卡什卡里的访谈实录。***

MARGARET BRENNAN: For more on what's next for the American economy, we're joined by Neel Kashkari, President and CEO of the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis. Welcome back to Face the Nation.

玛格丽特·布伦南：关于美国经济下一步的更多信息，我们请到了明尼阿波利斯联邦储备银行总裁兼首席执行官尼尔·卡什卡里。欢迎回到《面向全国》节目。

NEEL KASHKARI: Thanks for having me, Margaret. Great to be with you.

尼尔·卡什卡里：谢谢你的邀请，玛格丽特。很高兴与你在一起。

MARGARET BRENNAN: So when we spoke back in May, I asked you about the debt level America has. You said you didn't see an immediate crisis brewing, but at some point, this is going to become a problem. We just hit $40 trillion. Are we at the point where this is a problem?

玛格丽特·布伦南：我们五月份谈话时，我问过你关于美国债务水平的问题。你说你没有看到迫在眉睫的危机正在酝酿，但迟早这将成为一个问题。我们刚刚达到40万亿美元。我们现在是否已经到了这个问题的临界点？

: Well, Margaret, if you look at the Treasury yields, yields are high, 4.7% for example, on the 10-year Treasury. They're high relative to recent history. They're not high relative to more longer American history. In the early 2000s, the 10-year Treasury and the 30-year Treasury were around these levels. In the 90s, they were meaningfully higher than they are now, and there's no sign of Treasury market dysfunction or breaking down in financial markets. There are a lot of different factors that go into those Treasury yields. Inflation, and the outlook for inflation is one of those factors, that's the Fed's job. But there are many other factors, like, it's the AI investment, government borrowing, economic growth. All of those end up going together to set these long-run Treasury yields.

：嗯，玛格丽特，如果你看看国债收益率，收益率很高，例如10年期国债收益率达到4.7%。相对于近年历史，它们处于高位。但相对于更长的美国历史，它们并不算高。在2000年代初期，10年期和30年期国债收益率就在这些水平附近。在90年代，它们显著高于现在，而且没有迹象显示国债市场功能失调或金融市场崩溃。影响这些国债收益率的因素有很多。通货膨胀和通胀前景是其中之一，这是美联储的职责。但还有许多其他因素，比如人工智能投资、政府借贷、经济增长。所有这些最终共同决定了这些长期国债收益率。

MARGARET BRENNAN: But we saw this bond sell-off. We saw extraordinary action by the Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to intervene. He said what was happening was due to traders having bad information. Is that what's happening?

玛格丽特·布伦南：但是我们看到了这次债券抛售。我们看到财政部长斯科特·贝森特采取了非同寻常的行动进行干预。他说正在发生的事情是由于交易员获得了错误信息。事实是这样吗？

: Well, I'm going to leave it to the Treasury Secretary to manage the Treasury debt market. That's the job of the Treasury Department. The Fed's job is really to take care of the inflation piece of it. We are all absolutely committed to getting inflation back down to 2%, but long run, it's going to be fundamentals of debt issuance, of investment, of economic growth and productivity. Long run, that's what sets the Treasury yields, and not just in the U.S. but government bond yields all around the world.

：嗯，我会把管理国债市场的任务留给财政部长。那是财政部的工作。美联储的工作实际上是负责其中与通胀相关的部分。我们所有人都坚定致力于让通胀率回落到2%，但从长远来看，决定国债收益率的是债务发行、投资、经济增长和生产率等基本面因素。从长远来看，不仅美国，全球的政府债券收益率都由这些因素决定。

MARGARET BRENNAN: No, and I appreciate as a central banker, you don't want to get involved in fiscal policy or what the markets are doing day to day, but there is this debate right now about whether something more fundamental is shifting. Investor Mohamed El-Erian wrote in the New York Times, "There's a breathtaking leap in the cost of borrowing. If it persists, it could mark the beginning of a structural economic shift more enduring and more globally consequential than most previous episodes of market volatility." Is that overstating things? Are we in the midst of a huge shift?

玛格丽特·布伦南：不，我理解作为央行行长，你不想介入财政政策或市场的日常运作，但现在存在一场辩论，关于是否有更根本性的东西在转变。投资者穆罕默德·埃尔-埃里安在《纽约时报》上写道：“借贷成本出现了惊人的跃升。如果这种状况持续下去，它可能标志着一种结构性经济转变的开始，这种转变比以往大多数市场动荡事件更持久、更具全球影响。” 这是否言过其实？我们是否正处于一场巨大转变之中？

: Well, there are a lot of different factors going on at the same time. So, the stock market, as you know, has been really bullish for the last several years, really excited about AI and the prospects that that will lead to productivity growth. You know, a more optimistic take on these market moves is that the bond market is catching up to the stock market, and the bond market is seeing a higher growth trajectory. If the- if the global economy is in for higher productivity growth and higher growth, you would also expect to see higher yields all around the world. Now, I'm not endorsing that view. I'm not saying that's right. But, there are many different ways that you could see bond yields go up globally. It is- Mr. El-Erian is right. These moves are happening all around the world at the same time. I think that there are negative views of what could be driving that. There are also more optimistic views. It's hard to know right now which is the- the bigger driver.

：嗯，目前有很多不同的因素同时发生。你知道，股市在过去几年一直非常看涨，大家对人工智能及其带来生产率增长的前景非常兴奋。你知道，对这些市场走势更乐观的看法是，债券市场正在追赶股票市场，债券市场看到更高的增长轨迹。如果全球经济将迎来更高的生产率和更高的增长，你也会预期世界各地的收益率都会上升。当然，我不是在赞同这个观点，也不是说它正确。但，你有很多不同的方式可以看到全球债券收益率上升。确实是埃尔-埃里安先生说得对。这些变动在世界各地同时发生。我认为有一些关于驱动因素的负面看法，也有一些更乐观的看法。目前很难知道哪个是更大的驱动因素。

MARGARET BRENNAN: Well, the vice president J.D. Vance, in a TV interview, said Secretary Bessent has a, quote, "very discreet plan to shrink the nation's debt." Mr. Bessent is holding a press conference tomorrow, or is supposed to, and- and he said he's going to do things about fiscal consolidation. What are the things that people at home should be listening for that say America's debt load is really being dealt with now, that this isn't a problem?

玛格丽特·布伦南：嗯，副总统J.D.万斯在一次电视采访中说，贝森特部长有一个，引用，“非常谨慎的计划来缩减国家债务”。贝森特先生明天将举行新闻发布会，或者应该举行，而且他说他将就财政整顿采取行动。国内民众应该注意哪些迹象表明美国的债务负担真的正在被处理，而不是一个问题？

: Well, I just think you can look to the Congressional Budget Office, which makes forecasts of debt, U.S. debt and deficits for decades out into the future, and many Fed leaders have said for a long time that the debt trajectory is on an unsustainable path. I think ultimately it's up to our fiscal actors. That's the Treasury and the executive branch working with Congress to design a fiscal package that can change that and put it on a sustainable path. But obviously, you know, you said it a moment ago. That's not the Fed's role. The Fed's role is to take whatever they decide to do, put that into our forecast of the economy, and then our job is to take that and get inflation back down to our 2% target and achieve our dual mandate goals. And my colleagues and I are all committed to doing so.

：嗯，我认为你可以看看国会预算办公室，它对未来几十年的债务、美国债务和赤字进行预测，许多美联储领导人长期以来一直表示债务轨迹处于不可持续的道路上。我认为最终这取决于我们的财政行为者。即财政部和行政部门与国会合作，设计一套能够改变这种状况并使其走上可持续道路的财政方案。但很明显，你知道，你刚才说过，这不是美联储的角色。美联储的角色是无论他们决定做什么，都将之纳入我们对经济的预测，然后我们的工作是利用这一点将通胀降回2%的目标，并实现我们的双重使命目标。我和我的同事们都致力于做到这一点。

MARGARET BRENNAN: Right. And you have a meeting at the end of this week, I understand, out in Jackson Hole. But, I'm- I'm wondering how all these dots connect because do you think that this kind of activity and level of concern in the markets is going to make your job harder when it comes to- to making some decisions or cause the Fed to intervene?

玛格丽特·布伦南：没错。而且我了解到，本周末你们在杰克逊霍尔有一个会议。但是，我在想这些点是如何联系起来的，因为你认为这种市场活动和担忧程度会不会让你们的决策工作变得更加困难，或者导致美联储干预呢？

: I don't think so, right. As I mentioned a few moments ago, there's every indication that the U.S. Treasury market is functioning as it should--

：我不这么认为，对吧。正如我刚才提到的，有种种迹象表明美国国债市场正在正常运转——

MARGARET BRENNAN: Okay.

玛格丽特·布伦南：好的。

: --that trades are taking place. That there's liquidity in the market, and so that enables us to focus on the federal funds rate as our primary policy tool to get inflation back down. And so I think we have the freedom to do what we need to do to achieve our goals that Congress has assigned us.

：——交易正在发生。市场有流动性，这使我们能够专注于联邦基金利率作为主要政策工具，让通胀回落。所以我认为我们有自由去做我们需要做的事情，以实现国会赋予我们的目标。

MARGARET BRENNAN: Okay. At that last Fed meeting that was held, you dissented from the broader decision to hold interest rates steady. You said there have now been five years of persistently high inflation, and, sort of like, let's get on with it was my understanding. You were saying let's start to possibly raise rates. You mentioned a quarter of a percentage point. Are you still thinking that that's necessary next month?

玛格丽特·布伦南：好的。在上次美联储会议上，你对维持利率不变的广泛决定投了反对票。你说现在已经有五年持续高通胀，而且，有点像，我的理解是“让我们继续吧”。你在说可能开始加息。你提到了0.25个百分点。你仍然认为下个月有必要这样做吗？

: Well, I want to get- more data has yet to come between now and our next FOMC meeting, so I don't want to prejudge things. But what's happening in the Middle East, the conflict with Iran, is now a big driver of what's happening on inflation because, as you know, energy goes into every facet of the U.S. economy, and the longer that conflict, the more turmoil there is. The longer it goes on, the bigger effect it ends up having on the U.S. economy and on inflation. And even if it's driven by geopolitical factors like that, ultimately it does affect inflation. Ultimately, it does have a bearing on the Federal Reserve's mandate and so the longer that goes on, the less confident I am that inflation is going to return to target. You know, for- for five years we've been saying inflation is going to return to target in the next year or two. It just keeps getting pushed out. Eventually, we're going to have to do more. And I'm not sure if we're there yet, we need to see more data. But I don't want to prejudge the next meeting. But I'm not feeling confident right now that inflation is heading back down to target in a short period of time.

：嗯，我想——从现在到我们下次FOMC会议之间还会有更多数据公布，所以我不想预判。但中东局势、与伊朗的冲突，现在是影响通胀的一大因素，因为你知道，能源贯穿美国经济的方方面面。冲突越久，动荡就越多。冲突持续越久，最终对美国经济和通胀的影响就越大。即使它是由此类地缘政治因素驱动的，最终它确实会影响通胀。最终，它确实与美联储的职责有关。因此，冲突持续越久，我就越不相信通胀会回到目标水平。你知道，五年来我们一直说通胀将在未来一两年内回到目标，但这一预期一直在被推迟。最终，我们将不得不采取更多行动。而且我不确定我们是否已经到了那一步，我们需要看到更多数据。但我不想预判下次会议。但眼下我并不确信通胀会在短期内回落至目标。

MARGARET BRENNAN: And- and we should say that at that press conference, Secretary Bessent is expected to have tomorrow, he's supposed to talk about switching to financial warfare, or I guess returning to where we were and maybe ratcheting it up with financial warfare against Iran. It seems that that's the foot forward rather than military conflict. Does that change anything for you?

玛格丽特·布伦南：而且——而且我们该说明，在贝森特部长明天预计举行的新闻发布会上，他应该会谈论转向金融战，或者说我猜是回到我们以前的状态，也许是对伊朗升级金融战。看来这是前进的方向，而不是军事冲突。这对你有什么改变吗？

: I- I- it's too soon--

：我——我——还太早——

MARGARET BRENNAN: Okay.

玛格丽特·布伦南：好的。

: --to know. I don't know what the secretary will announce, and I don't know what the implication will be for--

：——还不知道。我不知道部长会宣布什么，也不知道这对——会有什么影响。

MARGARET BRENNAN: Yeah.

玛格丽特·布伦南：嗯。

: --energy prices and commodity prices.

：——能源价格和大宗商品价格。

MARGARET BRENNAN: Yeah. Let me ask you as well about what we heard in the last few days about the trade tension with Canada. You are in Minnesota, key border state there. Prime Minister Carney said there will be new retaliatory tariffs rolling out next week on steel, dairy appliances, ag equipment, pulp and paper, electronics. You've got a lot of cross-border trade here. Do you have any indication how significant the impact will be?

玛格丽特·布伦南：嗯。让我也问问你，关于我们过去几天听到的与加拿大的贸易紧张局势。你在明尼苏达州，那里是关键边境州。卡尼总理表示，下周将对钢铁、乳制品、家电、农业设备、纸浆和纸张、电子产品推出新的报复性关税。你这里有大量跨境贸易。你知道影响会有多大吗？

: Well, Canada is an important trading partner for America. You know, we talked about the five years of elevated inflation that has largely been driven by what we call supply shocks. One of those supply shocks is the trade and tariff conflicts. And so to the extent that we can get to a new- a new normal, a- a- a level of- whatever the trade dynamic is going to be, once we can get to that steady state, then businesses can adjust, and the inflationary impact can fade into the background. The longer there's back and forth on the trade front, just like the longer there's back and forth in the conflict of Iran, the imprint in inflation end up being extended and delayed, and so that's another factor to pay close- close attention to.

：嗯，加拿大是美国的重要贸易伙伴。你知道，我们谈到了五年来通胀持续高企，这主要是由我们所说的供给冲击所驱动的。其中一个供给冲击就是贸易和关税冲突。因此，只要我们能够达到一个新的常态，一个某种水平的——无论贸易动态将是什么，一旦我们达到那个稳定状态，那么企业就可以调整，通胀影响就会逐渐消退。贸易领域的反复持续越久，就像伊朗冲突中的反复持续越久一样，通胀的影响最终就会延长和推迟，所以这也是我们需要密切关注的一个因素。

MARGARET BRENNAN: Right. Well, we will follow that, and we appreciate your insight as always. Neel Kashkari, thanks for joining us this morning. Face Nation will be right back.

玛格丽特·布伦南：好的。我们会持续关注，并且一如既往地感谢你的见解。尼尔·卡什卡里，感谢你今天上午接受我们的采访。《面向全国》节目马上回来。
