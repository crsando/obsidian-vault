### 中文译文

## 中国以制裁和收紧无人机出口规则回击美国

![China announced strengthened controls on exports to the US of drones, their key components and related technologies © Liang Xu/Xinhua/Alamy](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3Acb95df22-2311-46ce-b18e-9580534ef8cd?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

中国宣布加强对美国出口的无人机、其关键部件及相关技术的管控 © Liang Xu/Xinhua/Alamy

中国已收紧对美无人机出口管制，并对多家美国公司实施新的制裁，这些报复性举措可能加剧两国之间的紧张关系。

在四份单独声明中，中国商务部周三公布了对美国近期行动的应对措施，这些行动包括限制从[中国](https://www.ft.com/china)进口技术，以及扩大对被指涉及新疆地区强迫劳动相关商品的禁令。

商务部禁止中国企业与美国生物技术公司Applied DNA Sciences及其他五家美国实体进行交易，理由是这些实体"支持美国有关新疆的非法制裁"。

商务部还宣布加强对美国出口无人机、其关键部件及相关技术的管控，这些物项已被列入两用物项管制清单。该部表示，出口将根据"逐案审查"原则进行审查。

这些声明凸显出，在习近平主席预计于9月访问美国之前，北京与华盛顿之间的关系持续紧张。习近平此行是在唐纳德·特朗普5月对北京进行国事访问之后进行的，那是美国总统九年来首次对华国事访问。

华盛顿上周将43家中国企业列入禁止向美国进口的名单，理由是与新疆使用强迫劳动有关。

美国联邦通信委员会还以所谓安全风险为由[限制进口中国科技产品](https://www.ft.com/content/48f0cc68-dfae-48d7-8950-c8c72c2ebe93?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，包括禁止部分无人机和路由器的新型号。6月，FCC将早先对华为、海康威视等公司电信设备的禁令扩大至更新型号。

北京商务部周三提及美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）最近的“消极措施”，并对美国公司Compliance Testing实施制裁，理由是其“协助”该监管机构，北京称此举危害中国“主权、安全和发展利益”。

此前中国对美国公司的报复措施包括[针对Calvin Klein和Tommy Hilfiger母公司PVH的行动](https://www.ft.com/content/a5ce3dc5-c36a-4091-abbb-817ba9d71e7f?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，去年初美国开始对中国加征关税时，北京将PVH列入“不可靠实体清单”。

特朗普和习近平去年10月在韩国会晤时达成脆弱的贸易休战，美国总统5月访华使关系进一步稳定。但两位领导人的讨论未能就两国之间的经济分歧取得明确突破。

中国已经[对无人机出口实施广泛限制](https://www.ft.com/content/82a48953-36b7-40b9-98f2-fd1b984489ee?shareType=nongift&syn-25a6b1a6=1)，其中许多是在2023年实施的，此前一年俄罗斯全面入侵乌克兰。

中国在全球无人机供应链中占据主导地位，大疆、道通智能和极飞科技等公司在美国有大量销售，尽管它们在美国面临越来越复杂的监管。

周三，中际旭创和新易盛这两家在全球人工智能热潮中股价飙升的中国制造商的股价[大幅下跌](https://www.ft.com/content/24bddb7e-9064-4d1b-80b1-d2dc59eed574?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，此前有报道称美国可能禁止其设备进入美国数据中心。

习近平上月提出在人工智能领域与美国一争高下的雄心，并表示将为发展中国家提供培训。

## China hits back at US with sanctions and tighter drone export rules

China announced strengthened controls on exports to the US of drones, their key components and related technologies © Liang Xu/Xinhua/Alamy

China has tightened controls on drone exports to the US and imposed new sanctions on several American companies, retaliatory moves that could escalate tensions between the two powers.

In four separate statements, Beijing’s Ministry of Commerce on Wednesday set out countermeasures to recent US actions that have included restrictions on tech imports from [China](https://www.ft.com/china) and the expansion of a ban on goods connected to alleged forced labour in the western region of [Xinjiang](https://www.ft.com/content/119d8c3a-e10f-4bf7-a198-90507293054b?syn-25a6b1a6=1).

The commerce ministry banned Chinese parties from dealing with US biotech company Applied DNA Sciences and five other American entities for “supporting illegal US sanctions concerning Xinjiang”.

It also announced strengthened controls on exports to the US of drones, their key components and related technologies included on a dual-use control list. Exports would be subject to review on a “case-by-case basis”, the ministry said.

The announcements highlight continuing strains in relations between Beijing and Washington ahead of an expected visit by President Xi Jinping to the US in September. The trip follows Donald Trump’s [state visit to Beijing in May](https://www.ft.com/content/7ae6a01d-df8d-4148-8424-27272e63939d?syn-25a6b1a6=1), the first by a US president in nine years.

Washington last week added 43 Chinese companies to its list of those banned from importing to the US over alleged links to the use of forced labour in Xinjiang.

The US Federal Communications Commission has also [restricted imports of Chinese tech over alleged security risks](https://www.ft.com/content/48f0cc68-dfae-48d7-8950-c8c72c2ebe93?syn-25a6b1a6=1), including banning new models of some drones and routers. In June, the FCC expanded to newer models an earlier ban on telecommunications equipment from companies such as Huawei and Hikvision.

Beijing’s commerce ministry on Wednesday referred to recent “negative measures” by the FCC when it imposed sanctions on US company Compliance Testing for “assisting” the regulator, an action it said endangered China’s “sovereignty, security and development interests”.

Previous Chinese retaliation against US companies has included [action against PVH, the owner of Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger](https://www.ft.com/content/a5ce3dc5-c36a-4091-abbb-817ba9d71e7f?syn-25a6b1a6=1), which Beijing put on an “unreliable entity list” early last year as the US began to increase tariffs on China.

Trump and Xi struck a fragile trade truce at a meeting in South Korea in October, with relations further stabilised by the US president’s trip to China in May. But the two leaders’ discussions yielded no clear breakthroughs on the economic fissures between their countries.

China already has [extensive restrictions on drone exports](https://www.ft.com/content/82a48953-36b7-40b9-98f2-fd1b984489ee?shareType=nongift&syn-25a6b1a6=1), many of them imposed in 2023 after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine a year earlier.

China has a dominant role in the supply chain for drones globally and companies such as DJI, Autel Robotics and XAG have large US sales despite being subject to increasingly complex regulations there.

On Wednesday, shares in Zhongji Innolight and Eoptolink, two Chinese manufacturers that have soared on the global AI boom, [fell sharply](https://www.ft.com/content/24bddb7e-9064-4d1b-80b1-d2dc59eed574?syn-25a6b1a6=1) following a report that the US could ban their equipment from its data centre.

Xi last month set out ambitions to rival the US as a global AI leader, offering training to developing countries.
