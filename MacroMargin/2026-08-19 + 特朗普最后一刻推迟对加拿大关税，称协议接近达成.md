### 中文译文

## 特朗普最后一刻推迟对加拿大关税，称协议接近达成

特朗普政府将对加拿大数十亿美元产品的50%关税推迟三天，称双方已达成初步协议，以解决一场使两国关系跌至新低的更广泛贸易争端。

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普在关税生效前不到两小时宣布了这一决定。他在社交媒体上表示，暂停关税“基于加拿大和美国，在文件最终确定的前提下，已经达成协议！”

白宫随后发布的公告称，在加拿大“承诺消除”对美国汽车、乳制品和酒类的歧视后，关税被暂停。美国贸易代表办公室表示，该协议包括“所有美国商品的全面市场准入、经济安全承诺、数字贸易协调”等因素，但未提供具体细节。

加拿大总理马克·卡尼则没有那么肯定，并未直接表示双方已达成协议。双方均未说明此前的症结，如汽车关税和木材问题，是否会得到解决。

卡尼在一份声明中表示：“虽然取得了实质性进展，但仍需完成重要工作。”他说：“在继续推进这项工作的同时，加拿大仍专注于在国内建设一个更强大、更独立、更具竞争力的经济体。”

伦敦时间上午中段，加元上涨0.2%，报1美元兑1.3876加元。

尽管存在不确定性，这一宣布表明两个长期盟友之间紧张关系取得进展，两国去年相互贸易额近9000亿美元。这也表明，在正在进行的北美贸易协定审查中，有可能向前迈出一步，该协定还包括墨西哥。

特朗普在7月下旬宣布了这些关税，理由是指责加拿大对去年一系列关税的报复，目前尚不清楚他从最新举措中赢得了哪些让步。当谈判取得通常微不足道的让步时，他经常放弃一些最重大的关税威胁。

白宫曾表示，将根据《1930年关税法》第338条对来自加拿大的一系列商品征收关税——包括曲棍球设备、啤酒、牛奶和胶合板——该条款赋予总统对被认为歧视美国商业的国家征收高达50%关税的权力。

包括安大略省和魁北克省在内的大部分加拿大省份已禁止美国酒精饮料进入零售商店，切断了美国葡萄酒和烈酒制造商的重要出口市场。

美国蒸馏酒委员会主席克里斯·斯旺格表示，自2025年以来，美国烈酒对加拿大的出口在[省级抵制](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/canada-must-remove-retaliation-to-avoid-new-tariffs-greer-says)中下降了70%。

### **烈酒抵制**

斯旺格在周二晚些时候的一份声明中表示：“随着未来几天讨论的继续，我们鼓励边界两侧的领导人达成协商解决方案，让美国烈酒重新回到所有加拿大省份的零售货架上，并使烈酒行业回到零对零关税框架。”

加拿大还对特朗普的汽车关税做出回应，对美国制造的汽车和卡车征收类似的反制关税——尽管它建立了一种机制，向包括[本田汽车公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/7267%20JP%20Equity)在内的也在加拿大生产车辆的公司退还这些关税。

这些关税原定于周三生效，为旨在施压加拿大做出让步的谈判创造了30天的窗口期。加拿大首席贸易谈判代表贾尼斯·夏雷特和内阁部长多米尼克·勒布朗周末留在美国首都，试图敲定一项协议。

拟议的《第338条》关税本不适用于美国从加拿大购买的最重要资源，如石油、钾盐和矿产。

卡尼此前表示，加拿大在贸易谈判中的目标是降低对钢铁、铝和汽车等行业的部门关税，这些关税已对该国制造业基础造成巨大不确定性，并导致大量裁员。

在宣布推迟关税的 Truth Social 帖子中，特朗普还表示将重启 Keystone XL 输油管道。

特朗普已签署一份[总统许可](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/terminal/TEBPAUGFLIIS)，授权将加拿大石油输送至怀俄明州的 Bridger 管道扩建项目——该项目是 Keystone XL 输油管道的部分复活，也是特朗普在第一任期内所希望的项目。前总统乔·拜登在2021年撤销对该项目的授权，实际上令其搁浅。

美国每天从加拿大进口超过400万桶原油和石油产品，这一数字数十年来稳步增长。能源是美国从加拿大进口的最大类别。

欣里希基金会贸易政策主管黛博拉·埃尔姆斯周三在[LinkedIn帖子](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/deborah-elms_tariffs-on-hold-as-trump-claims-deal-with-share-7495685627456163840-Czs1/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_ios&rcm=ACoAAAQhrDgB0bYwZGt1gaWdmMK9EIR1HhRFMEU)中表示，尽管最后期限延长增加了试图在两国之间进行贸易的公司的不确定性，但“对大多数加拿大商品征收的关税仍然为零，几十年来符合条件的商品一直为零，而且可能保持为零。”

### English Original

## Trump Delays Canada Tariffs at 11th Hour, Saying Deal Close

The Trump administration delayed 50% tariffs on billions of dollars of Canadian products for three days, saying the two sides had reached a tentative agreement to resolve a broader trade dispute that had plunged relations to a fresh low.

President Donald Trump announced the decision less than two hours before the tariffs were set to take effect. He said on social media he was pausing the tariffs “based on the fact that Canada and the U.S.A., subject to the finalization of documents, have a DEAL!”

A White House proclamation later said the duties were halted after Canada “expressed a commitment to remove the discriminations” on US autos, dairy and alcohol. And the US Trade Representative’s office said the deal includes “comprehensive market access for all American goods, economic security commitments, digital trade alignment” and other factors, without giving specifics.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney wasn’t as definitive, stopping short of saying they had a deal. Neither side said whether earlier sticking points such as auto tariffs and lumber will be resolved.

“Substantial progress has been made, although there is important work still to be done,” Carney said in a statement. “While we continue this work, Canada remains focused on building a stronger, more independent, and more competitive economy at home.”

The Canadian dollar was up 0.2% at 1.3876 per US dollar mid-morning in London.

Despite the uncertainty, the announcement signals progress in the tense relationship between two longstanding allies that conducted almost $900 billion of trade with each other last year. It also indicates the potential for a step forward in the ongoing review of the North American trade agreement, which also includes Mexico.

US President Donald Trump and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney at the White House on Oct. 7. Photographer: Anna Moneymaker/Getty Images

Trump had announced the tariffs in late July, justifying them by pointing to Canada’s retaliation against a barrage of tariffs last year, and it wasn’t immediately clear what concessions he’d won from the latest gambit. He has routinely backed off some of his biggest tariff threats when negotiations yield what are often modest concessions.

The White House had said it would put duties on a range of items from Canada — including hockey equipment, beer, milk and plywood — under Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act, which gives the president the power to impose duties of as much as 50% on countries deemed to discriminate against US commerce.

Most Canadian provinces, including Ontario and Quebec, have barred US alcoholic beverages from retail stores, cutting off an important export market for American makers of wine and spirits.

Chris Swonger, head of the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States, said exports of US spirits to Canada have dropped 70% in [provincial boycotts](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/canada-must-remove-retaliation-to-avoid-new-tariffs-greer-says) since 2025.

### **Spirits Boycott**

“As discussions continue over the next few days, we encourage leaders on both sides of the border to reach a negotiated solution that gets American spirits back on retail shelves in all Canadian provinces and returns the spirits sector to a zero-for-zero tariff framework,” Swonger said in a statement late Tuesday.

Canada also responded to Trump’s tariffs against autos by placing similar counter-tariffs against US-made cars and trucks — though it created a mechanism for refunding them to companies including [Honda Motor Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/7267%20JP%20Equity) that also manufacture vehicles in Canada.

The tariffs were set to go into effect on Wednesday, creating a 30-day window for talks designed to pressure Canada into making concessions. Janice Charette, Canada’s chief trade negotiator, and cabinet minister Dominic LeBlanc stayed in the US capital over the weekend to try to hammer out a deal.

The proposed Section 338 tariffs wouldn’t have applied to the most important resources that the US buys from Canada, such as oil, potash and minerals.

Carney previously said Canada’s goal in trade talks was to reduce the sectoral duties on industries like steel, aluminum and autos, which have created huge uncertainty for the country’s manufacturing base and caused a number of layoffs.

In his Truth Social post announcing the tariff delay, Trump also said the Keystone XL Pipeline would be revived.

Trump has already signed a [presidential permit](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/terminal/TEBPAUGFLIIS) authorizing the Bridger Pipeline expansion project meant to carry Canadian oil to Wyoming — a project that’s a partial revival of the Keystone XL Pipeline, which Trump wanted during his first term. Former President Joe Biden effectively sank that project by withdrawing authorization for it in 2021.

The US imports more than 4 million barrels a day of crude oil and petroleum products from Canada, an amount that has steadily grown for decades. Energy is the US’s largest import from Canada.

Deborah Elms, head of trade policy at the Hinrich Foundation, said in a [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/deborah-elms_tariffs-on-hold-as-trump-claims-deal-with-share-7495685627456163840-Czs1/?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_ios&rcm=ACoAAAQhrDgB0bYwZGt1gaWdmMK9EIR1HhRFMEU) on Wednesday that while the deadline extension raises uncertainty for companies trying to trade between the countries, “tariffs on most Canadian goods are still zero, have been at zero for qualifying goods for decades, and may stay at zero.”
