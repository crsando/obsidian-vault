## 托德·布兰奇：从‘黑马’到特朗普的执行者

### [**凯·威金斯**](https://www.ft.com/kaye-wiggins) 在纽约和 [**埃拉·李**](https://www.ft.com/ella-lee) 在华盛顿

发布于2小时前

### [**16**](https://archive.is/5sDyV#comments-anchor)

就在三年前，托德·布兰奇还是纽约白领法律界的固定人物。他是一名注册民主党人，一位因其对司法部独立性的承诺而备受尊敬的前检察官，也是华尔街历史最悠久律所的合伙人。

如今，他是唐纳德·特朗普总统报复议程的代言人。布兰奇将于下周出席美国司法部长确认听证会，他将掌管一个已经追查特朗普批评者和反对者、并推翻制约总统权力规范的司法部。

作为纽约精英律所卡德瓦拉德、威克舍姆和塔夫特的合伙人，他似乎并不认同Maga运动中的许多观点。该公司曾运营帮助寻求庇护者以及允许跨性别者更改姓名的项目，一位前同事回忆布兰奇曾支持参与这些项目的年轻律师。

特朗普政府此后已对这两个群体采取过措施，一名司法部官员表示，布兰奇在这些努力中支持同事的说法“绝对错误”。

如果布兰奇在7月15日和16日的听证会后获得确认，这将标志着他从特朗普的私人律师迅速晋升为美国政府的首席律师，并巩固他在特朗普政府核心的地位。他将监管一个自水门事件以来以不受政治干预而自豪、但现在却处于政府使国家屈从其意志努力中心的机构。

![Donald Trump and his attorney Todd Blanche, right, during his trial in New York in May 2024. Blanche saw the trial as the biggest and most consequential in America © Mark Peterson/Pool/AP](https://www.ft.com/content/undefined)

唐纳德·特朗普及其律师托德·布兰奇（右）于2024年5月在纽约受审期间。布兰奇将这场审判视为美国最重要、影响最深远的案件 © Mark Peterson/Pool/AP

前任司法部长帕姆·邦迪因特朗普对其对手的起诉进度不满而被解职。时任副手的布兰奇因对总统表现出明显忠诚而引发争议。

他曾支持一项现已废止的18亿美元基金，用于“让法律战和武器化的受害者得到倾听并寻求补救”，该提案被讥讽为总统盟友的贿赂基金。他签署了一份文件，旨在保护特朗普及其部分家族成员免受过往纳税记录调查。他还附和特朗普的言论，在法庭文件中提及“特朗普妄想症”。他甚至表示，若非被提名司法部长，他会对总统说：“我爱您，先生。”

一些曾钦佩布兰奇的前同事如今认为，他和政府中的其他人已经损害了美国司法系统的公信力，影响可能持续一代人乃至更久。

“他从一个广受喜爱和尊敬的人，变成了我们现在都摸不着头脑的人，”一位前同事说。“就像……变了一个人。”

然而，在布兰奇的朋友和盟友看来，他在特朗普政府中的崛起与他多年前形成的观点一致——作为一个在蓝领郊区长大、未就读顶尖大学、相信精英阶层长期忽视普通民众需求的人。

“托德并非身居法律精英顶层，他知道美国人想要的是一个严厉打击犯罪、严厉打击欺诈的司法系统，而不是在政治方便时心安理得地视而不见，”曾在Cadwalader共事、之前担任司法部国家安全检察官的乔·莫雷诺说。

这篇关于布兰奇明显转变的报道基于对20位认识或曾与他共事的人的采访。由于担心遭到报复，许多人要求匿名。

司法部一名官员为布兰奇的任职辩护，称"大量两面派的律师匿名侮辱代理司法部长以编造虚假故事，然后又出现在司法部赞美我们的非凡成就，这令人不齿"。

他们表示，布兰奇"以正直和卓越领导司法部"，"专注于兑现特朗普总统让美国再次安全的承诺"。

布兰奇早期法律生涯的大部分时间在纽约南区联邦检察官办公室度过，这是一个精英律师团队，曾起诉美国一些最臭名昭著的罪犯。

一位认识他的人说，当时布兰奇是一个取悦他人的人，深受同事和辩护律师的喜爱。曾与布兰奇共事的咪咪·罗卡（Mimi Rocah）表示，他是一个有团队精神的人，"胸中燃烧着"积极调查案件的"火焰"。

#### 布兰奇生平简介

![© WILL OLIVER/EPA/Shutterstock](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/39981cc90b14816b956ec9bb7e01beba/39981cc9-0b14-8181-b4da-c05ca843bcd3-18def3d9befd56e60344a5579bcdd3e9.png)

© WILL OLIVER/EPA/Shutterstock

**1996年** 在华盛顿特区的私立大学美利坚大学获得公共事务学士学位，随后在该市美国检察官办公室工作

**2003年** 在纽约南区联邦检察官办公室担任律师助理时从布鲁克林法学院毕业

**2006年10月** 成为纽约南区联邦检察官办公室助理检察官，处理白领犯罪和暴力犯罪案件

**2014年12月** 进入私人执业，在WilmerHale担任法律顾问，后于2017年9月加入Cadwalader担任合伙人

**2019年** 接受特朗普前竞选经理保罗·马纳福特（Paul Manafort）为委托人

**2023年4月** 离开Cadwalader，被任命为唐纳德·特朗普的律师，在三个刑事案件中为其辩护

**2025年3月** 被确认为副总检察长

**2026年4月** 在帕姆·邦迪（Pam Bondi）被解雇后成为代理总检察长

像许多联邦检察官一样，他离开办公室加入了一家大型企业律所，即WilmerHale。但与许多以合伙人身份加入的前高级检察官不同，布兰奇被聘为法律顾问，级别较低。不到三年后，他跳槽到了Cadwalader。

莫雷诺（Moreno）表示，“我们这些非精英人士要打入高端律所世界，需要‘勇气和决心’”。

布兰奇首次涉足Maga圈子是在2019年，当时他同意为特朗普前竞选经理保罗·马纳福特辩护，后者被控抵押贷款欺诈。他告诉同事，此案可能有助于提升他的知名度，并赢得更多客户。他成功地以双重追诉为由驳回了指控。

鲁迪·朱利安尼的前合伙人伊戈尔·弗鲁曼（Igor Fruman）以及特朗普的长期助手鲍里斯·埃普什泰因（Boris Epshteyn）后来聘请了他，使他更接近特朗普的核心圈子。

2023年2月，特朗普要求布兰奇在一桩涉及2016年大选前向色情明星斯托米·丹尼尔斯（Stormy Daniels）支付封口费的纽约案件中代表他。这是美国历史上首次对前总统提起刑事指控。

Cadwalader拒绝参与此案。布兰奇对同事表示，他对律所抵制接手他眼中的美国最重要、最具影响力的审判感到惊讶，随后他离开并创立了自己的律所布兰奇法律（Blanche Law）。

特朗普的政治筹款工具“拯救美国政治行动委员会”（Save America Pac）最终向布兰奇法律支付了超过1000万美元，用于为其辩护。

当布兰奇离开Cadwalader时，“没有人知道（对他而言）接下来的几年会是什么样子”，同事肯德拉·沃顿说，她后来加入布兰奇律师事务所处理封口费案。“这需要勇气和他对自己的信心。没有任何保障。”

莫雷诺表示，“在一个普遍看不起保守派、尤其鄙视唐纳德·特朗普的行业里，托德离开Cadwalader、创办自己的事务所并为前总统辩护，这确实是勇敢之举。”

尽管他接手了一位许多纽约白领律师都回避的客户，但一些同行表示支持，理由是人人都应有权聘请律师，而布兰奇具备为特朗普辩护所需的性格力量。

布兰奇在2024年[对FT表示](https://www.ft.com/content/e3812221-6661-4fa1-8755-fc1d80748d6a)，他认为封口费案根本就不应该提起。一位前同事回忆说，他曾告诉同行，接手此案能让他进入海湖庄园，那里聚集了众多富人，他们将来可能需要律师。

司法部官员表示，布兰奇“从未、也绝不会”在海湖庄园或任何与特朗普相关的地方招揽客户。

接下来的几个月里，随着布兰奇被卷入其他特朗普案件，他的立场发生了变化。据一位接近他的人士透露，他逐渐认同一种观点，即所有针对特朗普的起诉——包括在海湖庄园保留机密文件以及声称2020年选举被操纵——都是拜登政府的报复行为。

特朗普输掉了纽约案，成为重罪犯。但这位人士说，特朗普感激布兰奇在他连任似乎无望之时，仍甘冒职业风险为他辩护。

“他们建立了关系——一种信任关系——他被视为一名合法的律师，”曾在凯威莱德与布兰奇共事的前民主党州检察长道格·甘斯勒说。“我认为总统希望他站在自己一边。”

在2024年接受英国《金融时报》采访时，布兰奇否定了政治任命的说法。但到年底，这位律师——此时已是注册共和党人——被当选总统提名为副司法部长。

2025年1月，布兰奇在纽约南区联邦检察院的100多位前同事联署了一封信，支持他获得该职位的提名。

信中写道：“我们……看到了他如何将司法部的核心价值观——独立、公正、诚实、正直、尊重、卓越以及法律下的公平正义——付诸实践。也许最重要的是，托德本质上是一个善良正派的人。”

特朗普政府上台后，布兰奇开始公开反对法律体制。

2025年2月，在参议院确认听证会前夕，布兰奇以一份讽刺性的文稿阐述了他对法律体系对待特朗普的看法，回应了参议员关于他如何定义“法律战”的问题。

上个月，抗议者在纽瓦克与新泽西州的移民官员发生冲突。布兰奇的前雇主凯威莱德曾运营帮助寻求庇护者的项目 © Andrew Lichtenstein/Corbis/Getty Images

他写道，他的定义将涉及一位前总统被四次起诉，以及一位州检察长“在一位极其偏见的法官面前提起诉讼，其目的是摧毁这位前总统的生意，并让他及其子女在经济上破产”。他冷冷地说，“很难想象这些例子会在这个伟大的国家发生”。

成为司法部第二号官员，对于一位在纽约法律界（Blanche的熟人认为）建立了成功但平凡职业生涯的律师来说，是一个巨大的进步。在纽约，很多前检察官都转行当了辩护律师。

他有一种“败犬心态”，部分原因在于他的教育背景，当时认识他的一位人士说。他对新职位喜爱的一点是“超越那些自认比你强的人”。

特朗普上任后不久，总统针对一系列他认为曾伤害过他的律师事务所，以惩罚性行政命令威胁。其中就有Blanche的前东家凯威莱德（Cadwalader）。据知情人士向英国《金融时报》透露，该事务所被告知，因其拒绝在封口费案中代表特朗普而被单独针对。与其他许多事务所一样，凯威莱德承诺提供数百万美元的无偿法律服务以避免制裁。

一些前同事认为，Blanche可能对政府起到缓和作用。“我认为大多数人，在托德被任命为副司法部长时，都相当希望他能成为房间里那个清醒的人，”其中一人说。

有时，他可能确实如此。据报道，他向白宫建议，没有足够证据就抵押贷款欺诈指控起诉纽约州总检察长莱蒂蒂亚·詹姆斯，并反对任命林赛·哈利根为弗吉尼亚东区联邦检察官，这一任命后来被裁定为非法。

在他就任司法部的早期，一些右翼人士并不确信他是他们中的一员。与埃德·马丁之间出现了紧张关系，马丁是特朗普的忠实拥趸，当时负责一个关于司法“武器化”的工作组，并支持2021年1月6日冲击美国国会大厦的暴乱者。马丁现在是特朗普的赦免律师，紧张关系已大大缓解，以至于他现在公开支持提拔Blanche的计划。

“战士之间有时会互相推搡，但这只是为了击败敌人而进行的练习，”马丁告诉英国《金融时报》。“托德是一名伟大的战士，他选择捍卫公民特朗普，而不是享受高薪的律师事务所合伙人待遇；如今，他选择在司法部为总统服务，而不是躺在功劳簿上。”

在特朗普的第二任期内，司法部将矛头对准了总统的一批批评者和反对者。

司法部起诉了曾对特朗普提起案件的纽约州总检察长詹姆斯（James），以及曾调查总统2016年竞选团队与俄罗斯接触的前联邦调查局局长詹姆斯·科米（James Comey）。司法部还对美联储主席杰伊·鲍威尔（Jay Powell）和州长丽莎·库克（Lisa Cook）展开了刑事调查。特朗普曾批评鲍威尔没有更积极地降息，并试图罢免库克。

作为副手，布兰奇因与杰弗里·爱泼斯坦（Jeffrey Epstein）的被监禁同谋吉斯莱恩·麦克斯韦尔（Ghislaine Maxwell）会面而成为新闻焦点，参议院少数党领袖查克·舒默（Chuck Schumer）称这次会面似乎旨在“提供某种腐败交易，以便她能免除唐纳德·特朗普的责任”。

当联邦法官阻挠政府的一些计划时，布兰奇采取了对抗性态度，称司法部正在与“流氓活动分子”法官“开战”。

特朗普在4月解雇了邦迪（Bondi），并任命布兰奇为代理司法部长。总统在播客中表示，“我想看看他如何被接受”，然后再任命他为邦迪的永久继任者。

布兰奇被任命为代理司法部长后不久，司法部再次起诉科米，这次称他通过发布一张用贝壳拼出“86 47”的照片（‘86’是美国俚语，意为除掉某物，特朗普是第47任总统）来威胁杀害总统。一位熟悉白宫与司法部关系的人士表示，这一举动改变了他们对布兰奇的看法，使之变好。

## Todd Blanche: ‘underdog’ turned Trump enforcer

### [**Kaye Wiggins**](https://www.ft.com/kaye-wiggins) in New York and [**Ella Lee**](https://www.ft.com/ella-lee) in Washington

Published2 hours ago

### [**16**](https://archive.is/5sDyV#comments-anchor)

Just over three years ago, Todd Blanche looked like a fixture of New York’s white-collar legal establishment. He was a registered Democrat, a former prosecutor admired for his commitment to the Department of Justice’s independence and a partner at Wall Street’s oldest law firm.

Today he is the face of President Donald Trump’s retribution agenda. Blanche, who is due to appear for confirmation hearings as US attorney-general next week, is set to run a justice department that has pursued Trump’s critics and opponents and overridden norms that held presidential power in check.

As a partner at the elite New York firm Cadwalader, Wickersham & Taft, he did not appear to share the views of many in the Maga movement. The company ran programmes to help asylum seekers and to enable transgender people to change their names, and a former colleague recalls Blanche supporting younger lawyers as they worked on them.

The Trump administration has since taken measures against both groups and a DoJ official says it is “unequivocally false” that Blanche supported colleagues in those efforts.

If Blanche is confirmed after his July 15 and 16 hearings, it will cap a rapid rise from Trump’s personal attorney to the US government’s top lawyer and cement his status at the heart of the Trump administration. He will oversee an agency that since the Watergate scandal has prided itself on being free from political interference but is now at the centre of the administration’s efforts to bend the state to its will.

Donald Trump and his attorney Todd Blanche, right, during his trial in New York in May 2024. Blanche saw the trial as the biggest and most consequential in America © Mark Peterson/Pool/AP

The last attorney-general, Pam Bondi, was fired after Trump grew frustrated at the pace at which his opponents were prosecuted. Blanche, who was her deputy, has invited controversy with his apparent fealty to the president.

He championed a now scrapped $1.8bn fund for “victims of lawfare and weaponisation to be heard and seek redress”, a proposal derided as a slush fund for the president’s allies. He signed a document that seeks to protect Trump and some family members from having past tax returns investigated. He has echoed Trump’s language, referring to “Trump Derangement Syndrome” in court filings. He even said he would tell the president, “I love you, sir,” if he had not been nominated as attorney-general.

Some former colleagues who once admired Blanche now believe he and others in the administration have tarnished the credibility of the US justice system for a generation or more.

“He went from someone who was universally loved and well regarded to someone we’re all scratching our heads at right now,” a former colleague says. “It’s like . . . a different person.”

To Blanche’s friends and allies, however, his emergence in the Trump administration is consistent with views he formed years ago as a man raised in a blue-collar suburb who did not attend a top university and who believes that elites have long neglected ordinary people’s needs.

“Rather than reside in the upper regions of the legal elite, Todd knows that what Americans want is a tough-on-crime, tough-on-fraud justice system that is not content to look the other way when politically convenient,” says Joe Moreno, a former colleague at Cadwalader who had previously been a DoJ national security prosecutor.

Pam Bondi, then attorney-general, testifies before the House judiciary committee in February. She was fired in April, with Blanche stepping in as acting attorney-general © Win McNamee/Getty Images

This account of Blanche’s apparent transformation is based on conversations with 20 people who know him or have worked with him. Many asked to remain anonymous for fear of retribution.

A DoJ official defends Blanche’s tenure, saying “the sheer number of two-faced lawyers who anonymously insult the acting attorney-general to generate false stories then show up at the department praising our extraordinary achievements is shameful”.

They say Blanche is “leading the justice department with integrity and distinction” as it “focuses on delivering on President Trump’s promise to make America safe again”.

Blanche spent much of his early legal career in the Southern District of New York federal prosecutors’ office, an elite group of lawyers who have taken on some of America’s most high-profile criminals.

At the time, Blanche was a people-pleaser, a person who knew him says, and was well liked by colleagues and defence lawyers. Mimi Rocah, who worked with Blanche in the office, says he was a team player with a “fire in the belly” to aggressively investigate cases.

#### Blanche’s biography, in brief

© WILL OLIVER/EPA/Shutterstock

**1996 **Earns a bachelor’s degree in public affairs from American University, a private college in Washington DC, before working at the US Attorney’s Office in the city

**2003** Graduates from Brooklyn Law School while working as a paralegal at the Southern District of New York

**October 2006** Becomes an assistant US attorney at SDNY, where he works on white-collar cases and violent crimes

**December 2014** Goes into private practice as a counsel at WilmerHale before joining Cadwalader as a partner in September 2017

**2019** Takes on Paul Manafort, Trump’s former campaign manager, as a client

**April 2023** Leaves Cadwalader and is appointed Donald Trump’s attorney. Defends him in three criminal cases

**March 2025** Confirmed as deputy attorney-general

**April 2026** Becomes acting attorney-general after Pam Bondi is fired

Like many federal prosecutors, he left the office for a large corporate law firm, joining WilmerHale. But unlike many former senior prosecutors, who join as partners, Blanche was hired as counsel, a level below. Less than three years later, he moved to Cadwalader.

It takes “grit and determination” for “us non-elites to break into the white-shoe law firm world”, says Moreno.

Blanche made his first foray into the Maga universe in 2019 when he agreed to represent Paul Manafort, the former Trump campaign manager charged with mortgage fraud. He told colleagues the case might help him build a higher profile and win more clients. He successfully got the charges thrown out on double jeopardy grounds.

Rudy Giuliani’s former associate Igor Fruman and longstanding Trump aide Boris Epshteyn later hired him, bringing him more closely into Trump’s circle.

In February 2023, Trump asked Blanche to represent him in a New York case about a pay-off to porn star Stormy Daniels ahead of the 2016 election. It was the first-ever criminal indictment of a former US president.

Cadwalader refused to be involved. Blanche, who expressed astonishment to colleagues at the firm’s resistance to taking on what he saw as the biggest and most consequential trial in America, left and set up his own firm, Blanche Law.

Save America Pac, Trump’s political fundraising vehicle, would ultimately pay Blanche Law more than $10mn to defend him.

When Blanche left Cadwalader, “no one knew what the next few years would look like [for him]”, says Kendra Wharton, a colleague at the firm, who later joined Blanche Law to work on the hush-money case. “It took courage and betting on himself. There were no guarantees of anything.”

Moreno says that “while operating within an industry that looks down on conservatives in general and especially despises Donald Trump, it was a real act of courage for Todd to leave Cadwalader, form his own firm and defend the former president”.

Though he was taking on a client that many New York white-collar lawyers snubbed, some peers were supportive, reasoning that everyone is entitled to a lawyer and Blanche had the strength of character needed to act for Trump.

Blanche arrives for a closed-door meeting with Republican senators in May. Once a registered Democrat, the acting attorney-general has embraced Maga causes © J. Scott Applewhite/AP

Blanche [told the FT](https://www.ft.com/content/e3812221-6661-4fa1-8755-fc1d80748d6a) in 2024 that he had believed the hush-money case should never have been brought. A former colleague recalls him telling peers that taking the case would give him access to Mar-a-Lago, an establishment packed with wealthy people who might one day need a lawyer.

The justice department official says Blanche has “never tried, and never will” solicit a client at Mar-a-Lago or anywhere else associated with Trump.

In the months that followed, Blanche’s position shifted as he was drafted into other Trump cases. Over time he came to espouse the view that all of the Trump prosecutions, including over keeping classified documents at Mar-a-Lago and claiming the 2020 election was rigged, were part of a vendetta from the Biden administration, according to a person close to him.

Trump lost the New York case, becoming a convicted felon. But he appreciated that Blanche had risked his career to defend him at a time when re-election did not appear likely, the person says.

“They developed a relationship — a trusting relationship — and he was seen as a legitimate lawyer,” says Doug Gansler, a former Democratic state attorney-general who worked with Blanche at Cadwalader. “And I think the president wanted him on his side.”

In his 2024 interview with the FT, Blanche dismissed the idea of a political appointment. But by the end of the year the lawyer — by now a registered Republican — was named as deputy attorney-general by the president-elect.

In January 2025, more than 100 of Blanche’s former SDNY colleagues signed a letter to support his nomination for the role.

“We . . . saw how he puts the core values of the DoJ — independence, impartiality, honesty, integrity, respect, excellence and a fierce dedication to fairness under the law — into practice,” it said. “Perhaps most importantly, Todd is a fundamentally good and decent man.”

Once the Trump administration was in power, Blanche began to publicly turn against the legal establishment.

In February 2025, ahead of his Senate confirmation hearings, Blanche articulated his views about the legal system’s treatment of Trump in a sarcastic script responding to senators’ questions about how he would define “lawfare”.

Protesters clash with immigration officers in Newark, New Jersey, last month. Blanche’s former employer, Cadwalader, ran programmes to help asylum seekers © Andrew Lichtenstein/Corbis/Getty Images

His definition would involve a former president being indicted four times, he wrote, and a state attorney-general filing a suit “before an incredibly biased judge where the goal . . . is to destroy the former President’s business and financially ruin him and his children”. He said drily that it “would be hard to imagine these examples happening in this great country”.

Becoming the second-ranking official in the DoJ marked a huge step for a lawyer who, people close to Blanche say, had built a successful if unexceptional career in the context of New York’s legal scene, where many former prosecutors become defence attorneys.

He had an “underdog mentality” in part because of his educational background, a person who knew him then says. One thing he enjoyed about the new role was “surpassing the people who presumed they were better than you”.

Soon after Trump took office, the president targeted a range of law firms seen to have wronged him, with threats of punitive executive orders. Among them was Cadwalader, Blanche’s former firm, which was told it had been singled out for its refusal to represent Trump in the hush-money case, people with knowledge of the matter tell the FT. Along with many others, Cadwalader pledged millions of dollars of pro bono work to avoid sanction.

Some former colleagues believed Blanche could be a moderating influence on the administration. “I think most people, when Todd was appointed to be the deputy attorney-general, were quite hopeful that he would be the adult in the room,” one says.

At times, he might have been. He reportedly advised the White House that there was not enough evidence to indict New York attorney-general Letitia James over mortgage fraud allegations, and opposed the appointment of Lindsey Halligan as US attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia, an appointment that was later ruled unlawful.

In his early days at the DoJ, some on the right were not convinced he was truly one of them. Tensions emerged with Ed Martin, a Trump loyalist who then ran a working group on the “weaponisation” of justice and who has supported rioters who stormed the US Capitol on January 6 2021. Martin is now Trump’s pardon attorney, and the tensions have eased so much that he is now publicly supportive of plans for Blanche’s promotion.

“Warriors sometimes push among themselves but it’s only practice for beating the enemy,” Martin told the FT. “Todd is a great warrior who chose defending Citizen Trump over a cushy law firm partnership and today chooses serving the president in DoJ over resting on his laurels.”

Lindsey Halligan, whose appointment as US attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia was later ruled unlawful. Blanche was reported to have opposed the appointment © Al Drago/Getty Images

During Trump’s second term, the justice department has targeted a range of critics and opponents of the president.

The DoJ indicted James, the New York attorney-general who had brought a case against him, and James Comey, the former FBI director who had investigated contacts between the president’s 2016 campaign and Russia. It opened criminal investigations into Federal Reserve chair Jay Powell, whom Trump had criticised for not cutting interest rates more aggressively, and governor Lisa Cook, whom Trump had tried to remove.

As deputy, Blanche made headlines for sitting down with Ghislaine Maxwell, Jeffrey Epstein’s imprisoned accomplice, a meeting Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer said looked designed “to offer some kind of corrupt deal so that she can exonerate Donald Trump”.

As federal judges thwarted some of the administration’s plans, Blanche took a combative tone, saying the department was “at war” with “rogue activist” judges.

Trump fired Bondi in April and made Blanche acting attorney-general. “I wanted to see how he’s received” before appointing him as Bondi’s permanent replacement, the president said on a podcast.

Shortly after Blanche was made acting attorney-general, the DoJ indicted Comey again, this time saying he made a threat to kill the president by posting a photo of seashells spelling out “86 47” (‘86’ is an American slang term for getting rid of something, and Trump is the 47th president). A person familiar with the dynamic between the White House and DoJ said that move changed their view of Blanche for the better.

The same month, the DoJ charged the liberal-leaning Southern Poverty Law Center with fraud and money laundering. It moved to wipe away the seditious conspiracy convictions of rightwing extremist Proud Boys and Oath Keepers members.
