## 中国的开放权重AI模型正促使美国企业重新考虑战略

*欢迎阅读彭博《AI今日》通讯。每个工作日，我们将剖析人工智能对企业、劳动者、金融和经济构成的威胁与机遇。 *[立即注册](https://www.bloomberg.com/account/newsletters/ai-today)* 如果您尚未订阅。*

一封语气恳切而急迫的信函于7月24日送到华盛顿，签署方阵容强大，公司标识排满一页半。二十多家公司以英伟达、微软、Meta、Hugging Face和Andreessen Horowitz等为首，[警告监管机构不要对开放权重AI模型进行“过早”限制](https://images.nvidia.com/pdf/Open-Weights-and-American-AI-Leadership.pdf)，这类模型可以被下载、修改和再分发，限制相对较少。签署方中明显缺席的——毫不意外——是Anthropic和OpenAI，这两家实验室在严格控制专有模型方面让步的损失最大。

这封信本身并非关于开放优点的论述，而是业界竞争神经的一次闪现——业界目睹中国凭借来自DeepSeek、Alibaba Qwen、Moonshot Kimi和Zhipu的开放模型悄然席卷全球。这些模型不仅是免费下载，而且正在以硅谷或华盛顿任何人都未预料到的速度缩小与美国前沿实验室的差距。

这种焦虑可以理解。中国正在将一套熟悉的工业剧本应用于AI：积极建设、压低成本、安排过剩产能，以期建立全球市场份额。牛津经济研究院（Oxford Economics）上周的一份报告认为，这可能比中国此前在太阳能电池板、电动汽车、钢铁等方面的产能过剩浪潮产生更大的竞争影响。该报告作者认为，与这些产品不同，[AI模型可以以接近零的边际成本通过数字方式轻松跨境流动](https://cdn.roxhillmedia.com/production/email/attachment/1840001_1850000/328190fe4e3e154ec814d1e71fd38120b17323eb.pdf)。

与此同时，美国在AI技术栈中的优势正不平衡地缩小。虽然它在模型层的领先优势正在迅速缩小，但在先进计算方面仍然强大，不过在国内受到电力短缺、许可审批延迟和数据中心建设政治因素的制约。牛津经济研究院表示，其结果是一种明显分叉的秩序：一个以美国为主导、围绕卓越计算构建的AI体系，以及一个以中国为主导、通过定价和广泛分发进行竞争的AI体系。

但现在，美国正在以火攻火。信函发出后几天内，英伟达和[Meta都发布了自己的开放权重模型](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/meta-releases-muse-glimmer-ai-model-people-can-run-on-their-laptop)。这等于默认美国的护城河已经失效。如果中国要免费赠送这项技术，至少一些美国参与者认为他们可能别无选择，只能加入其中。

## China’s open-weight AI models are prompting US players to reconsider their strategy

*Welcome to Bloomberg’s AI Today newsletter. Every weekday we’ll break down artificial intelligence’s threats and opportunities for businesses, workers, finance and economies. *[Sign up now](https://www.bloomberg.com/account/newsletters/ai-today)* if you’re not already on the list.*

An earnest, urgent-sounding letter made its way to Washington dated July 24, signed by an impressive roster of companies, their logos running one-and-a-half pages long. Over two dozen firms led by the likes of Nvidia and Microsoft, as well as Meta, Hugging Face, and Andreessen Horowitz, [warned regulators against “premature” restrictions on open weight AI models](https://images.nvidia.com/pdf/Open-Weights-and-American-AI-Leadership.pdf), the variety that can be downloaded, modified and redistributed with relatively few constraints. Conspicuously absent among the signatories — no surprise — were Anthropic and OpenAI, the two labs with the most to lose from ceding their ground on tightly-controlled proprietary models.

The letter itself is no treatise on the virtues of openness. Rather, it was a flash of competitive nerves from an industry watching China quietly blitz the world with the open models from DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, Moonshot Kimi and Zhipu. These models aren’t just free to download but are closing the gap with US frontier labs faster than anyone in Silicon Valley, or Washington, expected.

The anxiety is understandable. China is applying a familiar industrial playbook to AI: building aggressively, driving down costs, and arranging excess capacity in the hope of establishing global market share. An Oxford Economics report last week argued that this could have an even greater competitive impact than China’s previous waves of overcapacity in solar panels, electric vehicles, steel and so on. Unlike those products, its authors argued, [AI models can easily cross borders digitally at near-zero marginal cost](https://cdn.roxhillmedia.com/production/email/attachment/1840001_1850000/328190fe4e3e154ec814d1e71fd38120b17323eb.pdf).

Meanwhile, the American AI advantage is diminishing unevenly in the AI stack. While its lead in the model layer is narrowing swiftly, it’s strong in advanced computing, but constrained domestically by electricity shortfall, permit delays and the politics of building data centers. The result, Oxford Economics said, is a distinctly bifurcating order: a US-led AI built around superior compute and a China-led one competing through pricing and ubiquitous distribution.

But now, the US is responding by fighting fire with fire. Within days of the letter, both Nvidia and [Meta released open-weight models of their own](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/meta-releases-muse-glimmer-ai-model-people-can-run-on-their-laptop). It’s a tacit admission that the US moat isn’t holding. If China is going to give the technology away, at least some US players feel they may have no choice but to join the party.

### **Banking & Finance**

**The AI borrowing binge risks flooding financial markets **with debt, taxing the market’s ability to absorb so much debt and [driving up the price the US government has to pay.](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything) Nomura Securities estimates that this year’s borrowing by the biggest tech companies alone is equivalent to roughly 25% of the US Treasury’s net issuance of notes and bonds to private investors, five times more than it was in 2025.

**Stripe finalized a deal to acquire AI model-switching startup **OpenRouter for more than $7 billion, according to people familiar with the matter. The deal underscores the [demand from businesses to find the most cost-friendly AI solutions](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-16/stripe-nears-deal-to-buy-ai-firm-openrouter-for-over-7-billion) and could also give payments processing firm Stripe a stronger footing in the technology.

**Alibaba Group is selling its gaming arm **in a deal worth at least $1.5 billion. It’s all part of a pivot to [make artificial intelligence and cloud computing its top strategic priority](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/alibaba-to-sell-gaming-arm-for-1-5-billion-in-boost-to-ai-pivot), with the aim of reaching $100 billion in AI revenue in five years. Alibaba’s open-weight models have already [accumulated more than 3 billion global downloads](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/alibaba-ai-models-hit-3-billion-downloads-passing-meta-google) in the past six months, eclipsing Meta Platforms, Alphabet and domestic peers to become the world’s No. 1 model.

### **Management & Labor**

**A stark cultural divide is shaping how AI adoption is unfolding **globally, with [84% of Chinese citizens expressing excitement about AI](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/why-ai-optimism-is-so-much-higher-in-china-than-the-us) compared to just 38% of Americans. The difference is similar when it comes to trusting the technology: not because Chinese people see fewer risks or Americans use AI less. They differ over whether AI’s gains will reach them — and whether anyone is keeping the technology in check.

**AI has raised the stakes of taking your first step **up the career ladder — and made it tougher and costlier than ever as [bots break down traditional paths into the workforce by taking away the grunt work](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-16/landing-your-first-job-is-getting-more-expensive-in-the-uk). The situation is particularly dire in Britain, where a youth-unemployment rate of 16.4% is the highest in a decade, and is rising faster than in any other G-7 country.

**Lululemon Athletica’s first-ever Chief AI and Technology Officer** Ranju Das has departed after less than a year, raising fresh [concerns about the athleisure retailer’s ability to stabilize its AI strategy](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/lululemon-s-ai-chief-departs-retailer-after-less-than-a-year) amid broader leadership turmoil. Das, who joined last September to develop responsible AI strategies aimed at improving efficiency and driving shareholder value, is just the latest in a string of high-profile exits.

The UK is bracing **for an AI-driven inflation surge after warnings by the Bank of England and British Retail Consortium that higher [memory chip costs that are feeding through to consumer electronics](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-15/ai-chipflation-washes-ashore-in-the-uk-economy) prices — including Apple laptops, tablets, and Xbox gaming consoles.

Memory chipmaker CXMT last week surpassed Tencent to become [China’s most valuable company at over half a trillion dollars in capitalization](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/cxmt-s-rise-to-china-s-most-valuable-firm-heralds-new-tech-era), while Alibaba and Tencent have shed market value amid a prolonged consumer malaise.
