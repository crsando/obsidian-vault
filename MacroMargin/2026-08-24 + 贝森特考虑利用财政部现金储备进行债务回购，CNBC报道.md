### 中文译文

## 贝森特考虑利用财政部现金储备进行债务回购，CNBC报道

美国财政部可能会动用部分现金储备，为回购收益率较高的旧证券提供资金，以应对不断上升的借款成本。CNBC报道称，贝森特可能会动用近1万亿美元的财政部总账户来为债券回购提供资金，消息人士周一援引两位部门高级官员的话称。

财政部长斯科特·贝森特上周在长期限债券收益率触及多年高位后，推出了扩大后的回购计划。债券交易商此前预计，财政部将发行更多短期债务——包括期限在一年以内的国库券——来为这些回购提供资金。

CNBC报道称，财政部可能会动用[财政部总账户](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USCBOPBL%20Index)——即存放在美联储的现金，简称TGA。截至8月20日，该账户余额为9350亿美元。过去，财政部一直维持着可观的余额，以便为政府的预期支出提供缓冲，这些支出涵盖从社会保障金到联邦雇员和承包商付款等各项内容。

受此消息影响，美债价格进一步上涨，10年期国债收益率一度下跌4个基点至4.69%。纽约时间上午9:58时该收益率为4.70%，仍距离贝森特上任以来的最高水平不远。

过去，有关拍卖、回购和现金持有的重大变化只会在财政部每季度的债务管理公告中公布。贝森特上周的举措以及周一CNBC的报道，让一些市场参与者认为，随着11月大选临近，政府反而优先考虑压低收益率。

“这似乎是一个非常草率的试图阻止抛售的尝试，而不是围绕现金余额政策进行深思熟虑的讨论，”加拿大皇家银行资本市场美国利率策略主管布莱克·格温表示。他补充说，他认为使用现金的可能性“非常、非常低”，而且如果要说的话，在网络安全攻击风险上升之际，缓冲资金现在更为重要。

美国财政部没有立即回应置评请求。贝森特预计将于周一晚些时候就政府对伊朗的经济隔离计划举行新闻发布会，届时他可能会面临有关现金余额想法的提问。

CNBC援引的高级官员并未排除用国库券为回购提供资金的可能性——这基本上是用一种债务替代另一种债务。而减少现金可以绕开这一问题。官员们不愿透露如果使用TGA的话，将动用多少资金。

早在2015年，财政部制定了一项政策，在账户中保留至少五天的支出额，或至少1500亿美元，以防意外中断导致其无法进入债务市场。特朗普政府上任时，一些市场参与者猜测该部门的指导方针可能会改变，尽管这种讨论随着时间的推移逐渐消退。

最近，财政部一直在研究使用盈余现金的其他方式，官员们正在考虑将部分资金投入市场进行回购协议的可能性。

财政部有着几十年的传统，只有在内部以及与市场参与者进行广泛协商之后，才会改变管理联邦债务的方式。它所坚持的原则——也是贝森特去年11月在[主题演讲](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0314)中反复强调的——是保持“规律且可预测”的做法。

一些分析师表示，在该计划的季度暂定日历发布仅两周后，就[突然决定](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades)加大回购计划的力度，这可能会削弱“规律且可预测”的形象。风险在于，投资者会要求更高的溢价来购买美国国债，尤其是期限最长的国债，以应对未来拍卖规模意外变化。

据报道，CNBC援引的财政部官员反驳了关于该部门已放弃“规律且可预测”原则的批评。

### **美联储与债务上限**

财政部现金减少可能缓解美联储瞄准的短期利率压力，同时也会缩减中央银行的资产负债表。美联储主席凯文·沃什（Kevin Warsh）呼吁缩小资产负债表，并任命了一个工作组来研究这一问题。

缩减现金储备也可能对即将到来的2027年联邦债务上限之争产生影响。惠誉评级本月早些时候估计，债务上限将在2027年年中触及。届时财政部通常会耗尽现金来支付联邦款项，并采取特殊会计操作。当那一刻到来时，较小的财政部总账户（TGA）意味着灵活性更低。

在周三的公告中，财政部表示，将从9月9日起，将其对10至30年期证券的“流动性支持回购操作”至少增加一倍。其在那之后到11月4日期间的暂定日历显示，对这些期限的债务回购规模最高可达140亿美元。

这种规模的操作不会引起财政部一般账户（TGA）的大幅变动，尽管贝森特周四强调，这些操作可能会加大力度。

“关键是这些金额如此之小，只是财政部的正常现金流变动，”Wrightson ICAP LLC的高级经济学家卢·克兰德尔（Lou Crandall）在接受采访时表示。

自20世纪80年代以来一直关注美国债务管理的克兰德尔在早些时候的一份报告中写道，多年来财政部一直专注于向投资者保证，它“不会为了自身的短期利益而操纵市场。”

“这一承诺上周已经化为泡影，”他写道。

## Bessent Eyes Treasury Cash Pile for Debt Buybacks, CNBC Says

The US Treasury could draw down some of its cash pile to fund buybacks of higher-yielding older securities in a bid to tackle rising borrowing costs, CNBC Bessent could tap near $1 trillion Treasury General Account to fund bond buybacks, sources said Monday, citing two senior departmental officials.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent last week unleashed an expanded buyback program after yields on longer-dated maturities hit the highest levels in years. Bond dealers assumed the Treasury would fund those purchases by issuing more shorter-dated debt — including bills, which mature in up to a year.

CNBC reported that the department could use the [Treasury General Account](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USCBOPBL%20Index) — cash parked at the Federal Reserve, referred to as the TGA. That balance stood at $935 billion as of Aug. 20. The Treasury in the past has maintained a significant balance in order to provide a cushion against expected outlays by the government, ranging from Social Security checks to payments for federal employees and contractors.

Treasuries extended gains on the news, with the yield on 10-year bonds falling as much as four basis points to 4.69%. It was at 4.70% as of 9:58 a.m. in New York, still not far from the highest level since Bessent took office.

In the past, major changes regarding auctions, buybacks and cash holdings were only unveiled in the Treasury’s quarterly announcements on debt management. Bessent’s move last week, and Monday’s CNBC report, suggested to some market participants that the administration is instead prioritizing efforts to pull down yields, as the November election looms.

“This seems like a very slapdash attempt to try to stem the selloff, versus a specifically thought-out conversation around cash balance policy,” said Blake Gwinn, head of US rate strategy at RBC Capital Markets. He added that he thought the chance of using cash was “very, very low,” and that, if anything, a buffer is more important now amid rising risks of cyber attacks.

The Treasury didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment. Bessent is expected to hold a news conference later Monday on the administration’s economic isolation plan for Iran, where he may face questions about the cash-balance idea.

The senior officials cited by CNBC didn’t rule out using bills to fund buybacks — which would essentially replace one type of debt with another. Reducing cash would get around that. The officials wouldn’t say how much, if any, of the TGA would be used.

Back in 2015, the Treasury instituted a policy of keeping at least five days’ worth of expenditures, or a minimum of $150 billion, in the account in case unexpected disruptions locked it out of debt markets. When the Trump administration came to office, some market participants speculated that the department’s guidelines could shift, although such discussion waned over time.

Most recently, the Treasury had been examining other ways to use surplus cash, with officials looking at the potential to park some money in the market for repurchase agreements.

The Treasury has a decades-long tradition of making changes in how it manages the federal debt only after extensive deliberation internally and with market participants. The principle it’s adhered to — and one endorsed repeatedly by Bessent in a [keynote speech](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0314) last November — is to be “regular and predictable” in its approach.

Some analysts said that the [sudden move](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/bessent-becomes-most-interventionist-treasury-chief-in-decades) to ramp up the buyback program, just two weeks after a quarterly tentative calendar for that program had been released, could erode the “regular and predictable” image. The risk is that investors demand a higher premium to buy Treasuries, especially the longest-dated ones, to account for unexpected future changes in auction sizes.

The Treasury officials CNBC cited pushed back against criticism that the department had abandoned its “regular and predictable” principle, the report said.

### **Fed, Debt Limit**

A decline in the Treasury’s cash could potentially ease pressure on short-end rates, which the Fed targets. It would also shrink the central bank’s balance sheet. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh has called for a smaller balance sheet, and appointed a task force to examine the issue.

Shrinking the cash pile could also have implications for a looming 2027 battle over the federal debt limit. Fitch Ratings earlier this month estimated the ceiling will be reached in mid-2027. The Treasury at that point typically then runs down its cash to make federal payments, and deploys special accounting maneuvers. A smaller TGA would suggest less flexibility when that time comes.

In its announcement Wednesday, the Treasury said it would at least double its “liquidity support buyback operations” for 10- to 30-year securities, starting from Sept. 9. Its tentative calendar for the period between then and Nov. 4 indicated up to $14 billion in buybacks for debt of those maturities.

Amounts on that scale wouldn’t involve a massive shift in the TGA, although Bessent on Thursday emphasized that the operations could be ramped up.

“The basic point is the amounts are so small they’re just normal cash flow variations for the Treasury,” Lou Crandall, a senior economist at Wrightson ICAP LLC, said in an interview.

Crandall, who’s been following US debt management since the 1980s, wrote in a note earlier that the Treasury for years has focused on assuring investors it “would not manipulate the market for its own short-term benefit.”

“That promise went out the window last week,” he wrote.
