### 中文译文

## 普京访问争议岛屿加大对日本的压力

东京面临来自北京、莫斯科和平壤的核武邻国的三重威胁

东京——数月来，中国一直在对日本施加外交、军事和经济压力。俄罗斯和朝鲜也越来越加入其中。

俄罗斯总统[弗拉基米尔·普京](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/vladimir-putin)周四首次访问俄罗斯远东偏远的南千岛群岛，这个寒冷的群岛日本宣称属于自己。俄罗斯在二战末期从日本手中夺取这些岛屿，围绕主权的争端从此一直困扰[莫斯科与东京的关系](https://www.wsj.com/articles/japan-russia-see-way-out-of-70-year-squabble-1536752725?mod=article_inline)。

日本首相高市早苗谈到普京访问择捉岛（日语称“択捉岛”）时说：“这伤害了日本人民的感情，绝对不可接受。”

她在[东京](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/tokyo)对记者说：“我希望俄方非常认真地对待此事的严重性。”

这场岛屿争端是[日本](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/japan)一方与[中国、俄罗斯和朝鲜](https://www.wsj.com/world/crink-axis-china-russia-iran-alliance-urkaine-war-3dab9921?mod=article_inline)另一方关系紧张的最新迹象。

分析人士称，这种摩擦凸显出日本拥核邻国对[东京加强防卫能力](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/japan-defense-trump-china-5621e92e?mod=article_inline)及强化与美国军事关系的计划日益不满。

普京在加工厂吃鱼子酱并与员工聊天的照片被拍到，他的这次访问恰逢日本就朝鲜向日本以西海域[发射弹道导弹](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/pyongyang-missile-test-signals-growing-conflict-between-north-korea-and-u-s-allies-a8447ad9?mod=article_inline)提出抗议几天后。发射当天，日本正在纪念美国原子弹轰炸广岛81周年。

而且此前数月与北京的外交摩擦不断，北京对高市早苗（Takaichi）去年关于日本可能被卷入台海冲突的言论感到愤怒。台湾是一个自治民主体，北京称其为中国的一部分，并誓言必要时以武力吸收。作为报复，中国取消了从中国到日本的旅游航班，并限制日本企业获取制造业所需的关键矿产。

数月来，中国官员一直指责日本重新点燃助长其残酷战时行为的日本军国主义精神，朝鲜和俄罗斯此后也提出了这一指控。

据俄罗斯官方媒体报道，俄罗斯太平洋舰队司令维克托·利纳周三表示，日本的“军事政治领导层”已将该国推向“公然军事化”的轨道。

“措辞非常相似，”东京天普大学日俄关系专家詹姆斯·布朗表示。“这似乎确实是在蓄意与中国结盟。”

东京方面表示，其增加军费开支是为了应对日本周边日益恶化的安全环境，而这一恶化被归咎于中国、俄罗斯和朝鲜。

日本最近测试了一枚美国制造的“战斧”巡航导弹，并[参加了今年早些时候由美国领导的军事演习](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/americas-pacific-allies-train-to-face-down-china-together-5671e723?mod=article_inline)。日本一直在[稳步加强其西南岛屿的防御](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/japan-is-fortifying-this-string-of-islands-close-to-china-4b9e7b9d?mod=article_inline)，部署导弹和驻军以抵御攻击。

在本月发布的一份报告中，日本防卫省表示，日本面临80年来最严峻、最复杂的一系列安全挑战。

报告将中国的积极追求广泛海上主张、俄罗斯入侵乌克兰以及朝鲜的核进展列为日本的关切。报告还指出，三国之间日益加强的军事合作构成更高的安全风险。

该报告还描述了俄罗斯如何在普京访问的千岛群岛上部署反舰导弹、战机和其他军事装备。

### English Original

## Putin’s Visit to Disputed Island Adds to Pressure on Japan

Tokyo faces triple threat from nuclear-armed neighbors in Beijing, Moscow and Pyongyang

TOKYO—China has been piling diplomatic, military and economic pressure on Japan for months. Increasingly, Russia and North Korea are joining in, too.

Russian President [Vladimir Putin](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/vladimir-putin) for the first time visited the remote southern Kuril Islands in [Russia](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/russia)’s far east on Thursday, a chilly archipelago that Japan claims as its own. Russia seized the islands from Japan in the dying days of World War II and a dispute over their sovereignty has [bedeviled relations between Moscow and Tokyo](https://www.wsj.com/articles/japan-russia-see-way-out-of-70-year-squabble-1536752725?mod=article_inline) ever since.

“It hurts the feelings of the Japanese people and is absolutely unacceptable,” Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said of Putin’s visit to the island of Iturup, known as Etorofu in Japanese.

She told reporters in [Tokyo](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/tokyo), “I hope the Russian side takes the gravity of this matter very seriously.”

The island quarrel is the latest sign of strained relations between [Japan](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/japan) on one side [and China, Russia and North Korea](https://www.wsj.com/world/crink-axis-china-russia-iran-alliance-urkaine-war-3dab9921?mod=article_inline) on the other.

The friction highlights the growing displeasure among Japan’s nuclear-armed neighbors at [Tokyo’s plans to boost its defense capabilities](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/japan-defense-trump-china-5621e92e?mod=article_inline) and strengthen its military ties with the U.S., analysts say.

The visit by Putin, who was photographed eating fish roe at a processing plant and chatting to employees, comes just days after Japan protested to [North Korea over the firing of a ballistic missile](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/pyongyang-missile-test-signals-growing-conflict-between-north-korea-and-u-s-allies-a8447ad9?mod=article_inline) into waters west of Japan. The launch took place on the day Japan was commemorating the 81st anniversary of the U.S. atomic bombing of Hiroshima.

And it follows months of diplomatic friction with Beijing, which was angered by Takaichi’s remark last year that Japan could get dragged into any conflict over Taiwan, a self-ruled democracy Beijing says is part of China and has pledged to absorb, by force if necessary. In retaliation, China has canceled tourist flights to Japan from China and throttled Japanese firms’ access to critical minerals needed in manufacturing.

Chinese officials have for months accused Japan of rekindling the spirit of Japanese militarism that fueled its brutal wartime conduct, a charge since picked up by North Korea and Russia.

Viktor Liina, commander of Russia’s Pacific Fleet, said on Wednesday the Japanese “military-political leadership” has set the country on course toward “open militarization,” according to Russian state media.

“The language is very similar,” said James Brown, an expert in Japan-Russia relations at Temple University in Tokyo. “It does seem to be about deliberately making common cause with China.”

Tokyo says its increased military spending is in response to a worsening security environment surrounding Japan, a deterioration it blames on China, Russia and North Korea.

Japan recently tested a U.S.-made Tomahawk cruise missile, and it [participated in U.S.-led military exercises earlier this year](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/americas-pacific-allies-train-to-face-down-china-together-5671e723?mod=article_inline). Japan has been [steadily fortifying its southwestern islands](https://www.wsj.com/world/asia/japan-is-fortifying-this-string-of-islands-close-to-china-4b9e7b9d?mod=article_inline) with missiles and garrisons to defend against attack.

In a report published this month, Japan’s defense ministry said Japan faces its most severe and complex set of security challenges in 80 years.

Among Japan’s concerns, the report listed China’s aggressive pursuit of its expansive maritime claims, Russia’s invasion of Ukraine and North Korea’s nuclear advances. The report cited growing military cooperation between the three countries as a heightened security risk.

The report also described how Russia has deployed antiship missiles, warplanes and other military equipment to the Kuril Islands visited by Putin.
