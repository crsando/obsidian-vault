### 中文译文

## 特朗普以“经济D-Day”威胁伊朗

随着战争临近六个月节点，总统表示他将发动“有史以来最具毁灭性的经济行动”。

作者：梅里迪思·麦格劳和菲利普·韦格曼

更新于2026年8月20日上午7:32（美东时间）

华盛顿——随着重新开放霍尔木兹海峡或达成结束近六个月战争的协议缺乏进展，不满情绪日益高涨，特朗普总统誓言要对[伊朗](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran)发动他所谓的“经济D-Day”。

特朗普周三晚间表示，他将对伊朗以及与伊朗政权有商业往来的任何实体发起一场重大经济行动。总统称这将是“有史以来对任何国家采取的最具毁灭性的经济行动！”除了已经对德黑兰实施的严厉制裁之外，特朗普没有说明美国具体还会采取哪些措施。

“我还宣布，任何允许其金融机构、企业、机场或政府实体向伊朗提供任何形式生命线的国家，本身都将面临巨大的经济后果，”特朗普在Truth Social上发文称。“石油走私、互换额度、现金转移、兑换所、船舶登记、空壳公司——所有这些都必须立即停止。你们心里有数。”总统呼吁美国盟友支持他孤立伊朗的努力。

白宫没有立即回应置评请求。

中国是伊朗最大的贸易伙伴，这个伊斯兰共和国与中东邻国也有经济联系，但由于多年制裁和其他限制，与欧洲的贸易规模很小。作为美国对伊朗经济[施压战略](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trumps-plan-to-squeeze-irans-economy-will-live-or-die-in-dubai-8f9f561e?mod=article_inline)的一部分，阿联酋周二晚些时候表示，已暂停与伊朗的金融和经济交易，这可能威胁到德黑兰获得主要进口来源，以及通往世界的金融后门。

人们走过德黑兰瓦利阿斯尔广场的商店。Atta Kenare/AFP/Getty Images

周三早些时候，总统被问及他有什么经济手段来惩罚伊朗，并表示他有“新东西”美国可以制裁。

“我们有非常严厉的制裁，我们将拭目以待，”特朗普对记者说。“要么极其顺利，油价如石头般下跌，要么我们继续做我们正在做的事。”

作为回应，伊朗外交部长表示，特朗普的声明相当于“转移美国自身危机的注意力：前所未有的债务和飙升的利息成本。”

“在失败的政策上加倍下注只会带来进一步的失败——以及伊朗人的敌意。美国的经济恐怖主义威胁全球经济及其主权，”阿巴斯·阿拉格奇周四在X上写道。

上周，财政部长斯科特·贝森特预告了针对伊朗的新措施，旨在让伊朗回到谈判桌，但没有提供细节。

“请密切关注未来一周的更多公告，因为我们将采取在经济孤立一个国家的历史上从未见过的措施，”贝森特周三对[Newsmax](https://www.newsmax.com/newsmax-tv/scott-bessent-iran-economic/2026/08/13/id/1266059/)说。

根据6月[谅解备忘录](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/an-annotated-analysis-of-a-u-s-draft-of-the-iran-deal-6a9ec49f?mod=article_inline)商定的美伊达成协议的60天窗口期于周一到期，但没有任何迹象显示谈判取得进展或完全开放海峡——这条至关重要的水道承载着全球约20%的石油供应。

特朗普政府就任何谈判的状态发出了混乱的信号。特朗普高级顾问贾里德·库什纳周一表示，美国与伊朗通过秘密渠道进行的会谈“积极且活跃”，尽管伊朗否认有任何沟通，特朗普也表示没有正在进行的对话。

“他们想达成协议，但他们不会达成我认为必要的那种协议，”特朗普周一在被问及已过期的谅解备忘录时对记者说。白宫要求以终止伊朗核计划作为永久停火的条件，而德黑兰迄今认为这一要求不可接受。

特朗普此前曾警告，如果伊朗违反协议条款，将面临军事后果，[他签署谅解备忘录](https://www.wsj.com/world/trump-defends-iran-deal-says-he-wants-to-avoid-economic-catastrophe-cdf41846?mod=article_inline)是在两个月前法国凡尔赛宫内。

高级政府官员表示，伊朗的经济绝望可能导致突破，总统表示他认为美国海军针对伊朗航运和港口的封锁已经奏效。但《华尔街日报》报道称，伊朗强硬派领导人已准备好[咬牙坚持](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-plan-escalate-war-cc657664?mod=article_inline)以继续——并升级——战争。

## Trump Threatens Iran With ‘Economic D-Day’

As the war nears its six-month point, the president says he would unleash the ‘most crushing economic operation ever taken’

By Meridith McGraw and Philip Wegmann

Updated Aug. 20, 2026 7:32 am ET

WASHINGTON—President Trump vowed to unleash what he called “ECONOMIC D-DAY” on [Iran](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/iran) as frustrations mount over lack of progress on reopening the Strait of Hormuz or reaching a deal to end the nearly six-month war.

Trump said on Wednesday evening he would launch a major economic campaign against Iran and any entity that does business with the regime. The president said it would be “the MOST CRUSHING ECONOMIC OPERATION EVER TAKEN AGAINST ANY COUNTRY!” Trump didn’t say what specifically the U.S. will do in addition to the heavy sanctions that are already on Tehran.

“I am also announcing that ANY country that allows its financial institutions, businesses, airports, or government entities to provide any type of lifeline to Iran will itself face TREMENDOUS Economic Consequences,” Trump posted on Truth Social. “Oil smuggling, swap lines, cash transfers, exchange houses, ship registries, front companies—It all needs to stop NOW. You know who you are.” The president called on U.S. allies to support his effort to isolate Iran.

The White House didn’t immediately return a request for comment.

China is Iran’s largest trading partner and the Islamic Republic has economic ties with Middle East neighbors, but trade with Europe is small due to years of sanctions and other restrictions. As part of the U.S. [strategy to squeeze](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/trumps-plan-to-squeeze-irans-economy-will-live-or-die-in-dubai-8f9f561e?mod=article_inline) Iran economically, the United Arab Emirates said late Tuesday that it has suspended financial and economic transactions with Iran, potentially threatening Tehran’s access to a major source of imports and a financial back door to the world.

People walk past shops at Valiasr Square in Tehran. Atta Kenare/AFP/Getty Images

Earlier on Wednesday, the president was asked about what kind of economic tools he had to punish Iran and said he had “new things” the U.S. could sanction.

“We have very draconian sanctions and we’ll see what happens,” Trump told reporters. “It’ll either be extremely good and oil prices will drop like a rock, or we’ll continue doing exactly what we’re doing.”

In response, Iran’s foreign minister said that Trump’s announcement amounted to a “diversion from America’s own crisis: unprecedented debt & surging interest costs.”

“Doubling down on failed policies will only bring further defeat—and enmity of Iranians. US economic terrorism threatens global economy and sovereignty worldwide,” Abbas Araghchi wrote on X on Thursday.

Last week, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent previewed new measures against Iran aimed at getting back to the negotiating table but didn’t provide details.

“Watch this space for more announcements coming next week because we are going to apply measures like have never been seen in the history of the economic isolation of a country,” Bessent said on [Newsmax](https://www.newsmax.com/newsmax-tv/scott-bessent-iran-economic/2026/08/13/id/1266059/) Wednesday.

The 60-day window for the U.S. and Iran to reach a deal, as agreed to in a June [memorandum of understanding](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/an-annotated-analysis-of-a-u-s-draft-of-the-iran-deal-6a9ec49f?mod=article_inline), expired on Monday with no sign of any progress on talks or completely opening the strait—the vital waterway through which about 20% of the world’s oil supplies pass.

The Trump administration has sent mixed messages about the status of any negotiations. Senior Trump adviser Jared Kushner said on Monday that talks between the U.S. and Iran through backchannels were “positive and active,” even as Iran denied any communication and Trump said there were no ongoing conversations.

“They want to make a deal, but they’re not going to make the kind of a deal that I feel is necessary,” Trump told reporters Monday when asked about the expired memorandum of understanding. The White House has demanded an end to the Iranian nuclear program as a condition for a permanent ceasefire, a requirement that Tehran has found unacceptable so far.

Trump had previously warned that Iran would face military consequences for breaking the terms of the agreement when [he signed the memorandum](https://www.wsj.com/world/trump-defends-iran-deal-says-he-wants-to-avoid-economic-catastrophe-cdf41846?mod=article_inline) two months ago inside the French palace of Versailles.

Senior administration officials say Iran’s economic desperation could lead to a breakthrough, and the president has said he believes the U.S. Navy blockade targeting Iranian shipping and ports has been effective. But The Wall Street Journal reported Iran’s hard-line leaders are prepared [to dig in their heels](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/iran-plan-escalate-war-cc657664?mod=article_inline) for a continuation—and escalation—of the war.
