### 中文译文

## 美国军费如何推动了中国的机器狗发展

- 摘要
- 公司
- 研究人员表示，宇树科技的 Go 系列与麻省理工学院的迷你猎豹设计高度相似
- 军方支持的美国突破性成果被公开发表，随后在中国得到了更有效的商业化
- 研究人员敦促美国加快商业化进程，而非依靠保密，以应对中国的规模优势
- 经过多年的政策聚焦，中国已在多个高科技行业占据市场份额
华盛顿，8月18日（路透社）——据一位美国前国防技术官员及三位参与该项目的顶级研究人员称，中国的宇树科技（Unitree Robotics）——全球最大的[人形](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-humanoid-robot-maker-unitree-list-shanghai-august-19-2026-08-17/)和四足机器人生产商之一——将其最成功的机器狗设计建立在美国军方资助的创新之上。

宇树科技于2023年推出的售价1600美元的Go2型号，帮助该公司迅速主导了全球四足机器人市场——其上海IPO已[引发狂热需求](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-humanoid-robot-maker-unitree-powers-up-stellar-shanghai-debut-2026-08-14/)。另一款宇树机器人已在[中国国家电视台上展示，打开新标签页](https://www.youtube.com/watch?is=KUCt0IZPeTC-pXFd&v=Xa8Do0kTJUw&feature=youtu.be)，该机器人全副武装，并伴随中国人民解放军（PLA）部队参加演习。

研究人员和这位前官员告诉路透社，这些机器人利用了由美国DEVCOM陆军研究实验室(ARL)和其他军事项目资助的四足运动突破。

参与该项目的宾夕法尼亚大学前研究员Gavin Kenneally表示，这个由陆军资助的项目最终变成了“基本上，第一个具有一定规模的宇树机器人”。

曾在麻省理工学院仿生机器人实验室工作的Ben Katz表示，宇树科技广受欢迎的Go系列机器人尺寸与他参与开发的开创性机器人Mini Cheetah“精确到毫米”几乎完全相同。Ben Katz所在的实验室属于这些军事项目资助的大学联盟之一。

路透社是首家详细报道宇树设计与美国陆军项目之间关联的媒体。

这一事件凸显了美国在包括国家安全关键领域在内的高科技战略产业中与中国政府主导的制造业竞争的能力存在更广泛的问题。在补贴、零部件工厂集群以及对巨额亏损的容忍支持下，中国企业在太阳能电池板、无人机、电动汽车和量子通信等行业夺取了市场份额，其中许多行业最早是在美国政府和军方的支持下发展起来的。

Kenneally通过他的公司Ghost Robotics将美国的这些突破商业化，该公司向美国特种部队提供先进的加固型机器人。但与宇树科技相比，其产量很小，成本也很高。

今年6月，[五角大楼将宇树科技列入](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/pentagon-lists-entities-designated-chinese-military-company-2026-06-08/)中国军事企业名单，称其为“中国国防工业基地的贡献者”。这一认定算不上制裁，但限制了美国军方未来使用宇树科技的技术。宇树科技曾表示其机器人为民用用途。

宇树科技、中国国务院和中国国防部没有回应置评请求。

宇树科技在使用美国陆军研究方面并无不当之处。该项目的研究成果公开发表，以推动该领域的进步，这是常见做法。

美国陆军研究实验室（ARL）发言人阿曼达·利根表示，该研究“强化了美国更广泛的机器人生态系统”，并为政府和私营部门的后续工作提供了参考。

ARL 将有关宇树科技将该技术商业化的问题转交五角大楼，五角大楼拒绝置评。

### 美国公司 vs. 中国产业政策

公司文件显示，宇树科技去年售出超过 5,500 台人形机器人和 18,000 台四足机器人，该公司在周三股市首秀前的估值约为 [90 亿美元](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-robot-maker-unitree-prices-shanghai-ipo-2026-08-06/)。

相比之下，没有一家美国公司大规模生产此类机器人，包括埃隆·马斯克的特斯拉，后者多年来一直展示其 Optimus 人形机器人原型。在 1 月份的财报电话会议上，马斯克称中国在扩大生产规模方面“令人难以置信地擅长”。

美国波士顿动力公司（Boston Dynamics）——其 2019 年的犬形机器人 Spot 采用了不同技术——最近在国会听证会上辩称，中国的低价格将把美国公司挤出市场。

接受路透社采访的美国机器人研究人员中，没有人主张对这类政府资助的研究保密。相反，他们表示，美国政策制定者应着眼于让企业迅速将此类技术进步商业化，以与中国竞争。

ARL还表示，发表论文对科技进步非常重要。

宾夕法尼亚大学工程学学院院长维贾伊·库马尔表示，美国在创新和软件开发方面表现出色，但需要确保资本、工业基础、高技能劳动力和零部件供应链，以扩大突破的规模。

目前，美国当局经常通过对中国创新产品实施贸易限制来保护本国企业。7月，美国联邦通信委员会[禁止进口（打开新标签页）](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-423682A1.pdf)未来型号的国外制造人形和四足机器人，包括宇树科技的产品。中国已[威胁要进行报复](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/china-warns-retaliation-if-us-sticks-with-robot-ban-2026-07-30/)以回应FCC的禁令。

中国驻华盛顿大使馆在一份声明中表示，其企业正在使新技术在国际上变得负担得起且易于获取。它指责美国滥用行政权力，并实施“市场扭曲和单边霸凌”。

接受路透社采访的大多数专家支持美国的禁令，但表示该政策不足以建立一个产业来追赶中国。

Ghost Robotics创始人肯尼利表示：“这不仅仅是美国和中国机器人公司之间的竞争，而是美国私营公司与中国的协调国家战略之间的竞争。”

2015年，中国国家主席习近平制定了一项为期10年的计划，以引领包括绿色能源、电动汽车和机器人在内的产业。此后，中国将资本投入到了对利润率较低的21世纪先进制造业的高风险押注中。

中国机器人制造商对产品的快速逆向工程，得益于中国在精炼机器人应用中磁铁所需关键矿物方面的主导地位，以及电机、齿轮、被称为“肌肉”的执行器和其他零部件供应商在宇树科技杭州总部附近的密集分布。

第1张，共3张 2026年6月22日，在中国北京举办的中国国际供应链促进博览会（CISCE）上，一台宇树科技（Unitree）人形机器人在浙江省展台演示踢拳动作。路透社/Florence Lo/档案照片**[1/3]**2026年6月22日，在中国北京举办的中国国际供应链促进博览会（CISCE）上，一台宇树科技（Unitree）人形机器人在浙江省展台演示踢拳动作。路透社/Florence Lo/档案照片 [购买许可权，打开新标签页](https://www.reutersconnect.com/item/china-international-supply-chain-expo-in-beijing/dGFnOnJldXRlcnMuY29tLDIwMjY6bmV3c21sX1JDMlZZTEFPTVFKNQ%3D%3D/?utm_medium=rcom-article-media&utm_campaign=rcom-rcp-lead)

SemiAnalysis的机器人分析师雷克·努特森（Reyk Knuhtsen）称，美国未能将国内研究成果商业化是一个“失误”，并指出美国风险投资倾向于高回报的软件初创公司。他表示，要推动机器人制造业，仅靠对华贸易壁垒是不够的。

“这对美国来说是一座非常难爬的大山，”他说。

白宫没有回应就美国产业政策发表评论的请求。美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）的一名发言人表示，该委员会正在保护美国人免受外国威胁，并让美国准备好“实现特朗普总统在下一代技术领域引领世界的愿景”。

曾任美国陆军研究实验室主任的贾雷特·里迪克（Jaret Riddick）负责监督军方资助的机器人项目，他表示，该项目确实启动了美国四足机器人产业。但最终，由于缺乏大规模生产支持，中国的宇树科技“基本上占据了那个领域”。

### 教机器狗新把戏

美国陆军在2010年至2020年期间资助了机器人协作技术联盟（RCTA）。

RCTA汇集了来自宾夕法尼亚大学（UPenn）、麻省理工学院（MIT）、波士顿动力公司、美国国家航空航天局（NASA）喷气推进实验室及其他研究机构的政府、学术界和工业界研究人员。该联盟最大的商业合作伙伴是通用动力地面系统公司，这家总部位于密歇根州的国防制造商生产艾布拉姆斯M1主战坦克。

2016年，包括Kenneally在内的宾夕法尼亚大学（UPenn）研究人员取消了笨重的中央变速箱，将电机放置在机器人腿部，增强了感知和响应地形的能力。宾夕法尼亚大学的进展建立在麻省理工学院（MIT）实验室的工作基础上，该工作由国防高级研究计划局（DARPA）资助。

2019年，MIT实验室展示了Mini Cheetah，在UPenn的特性基础上增强了强度，并具备了完成后空翻的能力。

几个月前，Katz发表了他的麻省理工学院硕士论文，详细介绍了Mini Cheetah的驱动器。六个月内，他发现中国公司制造的驱动器仿制品“你可以在AliExpress上买到，”他说，这里指的是中国在线零售巨头。

宇树科技（Unitree）由王兴兴在三年前创立，当时他26岁。在他2016年于上海大学完成的硕士论文中（路透社在线查阅了该论文），王兴兴引用了麻省理工学院的工作，包括早期版本的Cheetah机器人，并阐述了与美国实验室提出的原理一致的原理。他承认该实验室的“开创性”贡献。

在他的论文中，王兴兴探索了加快开发的设计调整，称其发现未来可能应用于“国防、安全、家庭和教育”领域。他引用了Kenneally在ARL资助下的宾夕法尼亚大学论文。

宇树科技在其网站上表示，该公司“独立研发了四足机器人的电机、减速器、控制器，甚至一些传感器。”

Katz告诉路透社，他决定不将他的驱动器投入生产，认为不会有市场。他说，中国快速行动的能力带来了广泛的好处：机器人技术“由于驱动器的可用性而取得了很大进展。”

Joao Ramos曾在麻省理工学院实验室与Katz同时进行研究，他说宇树科技复制了Mini Cheetah的外形、结构和设计。但他称赞该公司做了“扎实的工程”来完善量产机器人。

“拉莫斯说：‘实际实施、将其变为现实，这些都取决于他们。’”

### 持枪机器狗

步行机器人尚未在战场上广泛部署，但中美两国军方都探索了这一前景。10月，另一家中国公司的“机器狼”四足机器人出现在中国媒体对解放军[进行模拟登陆作战，新标签页打开](https://youtu.be/ZGBybUTIwY8?is=72qQIM9ccmYSfVpO)的报道中。

宇树科技在公开声明中表示，其产品旨在民用。2022年，该公司与美国公司共同签署了一封公开信，承诺不将机器人武器化，理由是存在伤害风险和严重的伦理问题。

但中国国家电视台报道了宇树科技的 Go2 型号机器人[与中国军队一同亮相，新标签页打开](https://youtu.be/Xa8Do0kTJUw?is=KUCt0IZPeTC-pXFd)，以及该公司 B1 系列机器人的一种改装版本，后者发射了安装在上面的突击步枪。

根据[采购记录，新标签页打开](https://www.madeinamerica.gov/waivers/nonavailability/64fa58a21734bedbb83b9663)，2023年9月，美国陆军获得了联邦豁免，不受要求政府机构优先采购美国制造产品的法律约束，以购买两台宇树 Go2。采购记录称，在缺乏足够商业数量的、质量令人满意的美国产替代品的情况下，这些设备是至关重要的工具。路透社无法确认这些采购是否进行。

当月晚些时候，美国海军陆战队进行了一次演练，在 Go1 上[安装火箭发射器，新标签页打开](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/456001/marines-test-emerging-technologies-combat-center)以实现远程射击。

Kenneally 的 Ghost Robotics 已交付约 1,000 台 Vision 60 机器人，主要给军事和政府客户，用于恶劣环境中的监视和搜索应用。该公司的第一个客户是负责监管海豹突击队的海军特种作战司令部。

Vision 60s的售价高达16.5万美元以上，其部分组件在中国制造，尽管该公司已寻求转移其供应链。相比之下，宇树科技（Unitree）在美国标价低于10万美元的B系列机器人。

Ghost Robotics两年前将电机供应从中国转移到韩国，但肯尼利（Kenneally）表示，该公司仍难以摆脱对中国的磁铁和一些金属部件的依赖。

他说，要摆脱中国对机器人供应链的钳制，唯一办法是从其他国家获得美国所缺乏的制造能力。2024年，韩国LIG防务与航空航天公司（LIG Defense & Aerospace）以2.4亿美元收购了Ghost Robotics 60%的股份。肯尼利表示，该公司正在探索在台湾生产。

“我们需要与盟友密切合作，”他说，“没有他们，绝对没有前进的道路。”

## How US military funding propelled China’s robot dogs

- Summary
- Companies
- Researchers said Unitree's Go series closely mirrored MIT's Mini Cheetah design
- Army-backed U.S. breakthroughs were published openly, then commercialized more effectively in China
- Researchers urge faster U.S. commercialization, not secrecy, to counter China’s scale advantages
- China has seized market share across high-tech industries after years of policy focus
WASHINGTON, Aug 18 (Reuters) - China’s Unitree Robotics – among the world's biggest producers of [humanoid ](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-humanoid-robot-maker-unitree-list-shanghai-august-19-2026-08-17/)and quadruped robots – based designs for its most successful robot dogs on innovations funded by the U.S. military, according to a former U.S. defense technology official and three top researchers involved in the project.

Unitree’s $1,600 Go2 model, launched in 2023, helped the company rapidly dominate the global quadruped robot market — its Shanghai IPO has [drawn frenzied demand](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-humanoid-robot-maker-unitree-powers-up-stellar-shanghai-debut-2026-08-14/). Another Unitree robot has been shown [on Chinese state television, opens new tab](https://www.youtube.com/watch?is=KUCt0IZPeTC-pXFd&v=Xa8Do0kTJUw&feature=youtu.be), armed and accompanying People’s Liberation Army (PLA) troops on an exercise.

These robots draw on breakthroughs in quadruped movement financed by the U.S. DEVCOM Army Research Laboratory (ARL) and other military programs, the researchers and the former official told Reuters.

The Army-funded project turned into, “basically, the first Unitree robot that had any kind of scale," said Gavin Kenneally, a former University of Pennsylvania researcher involved in the program.

Ben Katz, who worked at Massachusetts Institute of ‌Technology’s Biomimetic Robotics Lab, among a consortium of universities financed by the military programs, said the dimensions of Unitree’s popular Go series were “to the millimeter” almost identical to a groundbreaking robot he helped develop called Mini Cheetah.

Reuters is the first media outlet to detail connections between Unitree designs and the Army program.

The episode underscores broader problems with the United States’ ability to compete with China’s state-directed manufacturing sector in high-tech strategic industries, including those critical to national security. Backed by subsidies, clusters of component factories, and a tolerance for heavy losses, Chinese companies have seized market share across industries such as solar panels, drones, EVs and quantum communications, many of which were first developed in the U.S. with government or military backing.

Kenneally commercialized the U.S. breakthroughs through his company Ghost Robotics, which supplies U.S. special forces with advanced, ruggedized robots. But compared to Unitree, its production is small, and costly.

In June, [the Pentagon added Unitree ](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/pentagon-lists-entities-designated-chinese-military-company-2026-06-08/)to a list of Chinese military companies, calling it a “contributor to the Chinese defense industrial base.” That designation falls short of a sanction but limits the U.S. military’s future use of Unitree’s technology. Unitree has said its robots are for civilian use.

Unitree, China's State Council and China's defense ministry did not respond to requests for comment.

Unitree did nothing underhanded in using the U.S. Army research. The program's findings were published openly to stimulate progress in the field, a common practice.

ARL spokesperson Amanda Ligon said the research “strengthened the broader U.S. robotics ecosystem” and informed subsequent work across government and the private sector.

ARL referred questions about Unitree’s commercialization of the technology to the Pentagon, which declined to comment.

### U.S. COMPANIES VS. CHINA INDUSTRIAL POLICY

Unitree sold more ⁠than 5,500 humanoids and 18,000 quadrupeds last year, company filings show, and it’s now valued at about [$9 billion ](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinese-robot-maker-unitree-prices-shanghai-ipo-2026-08-06/)ahead of its stock market debut on Wednesday.

By contrast, no U.S. company has mass-produced such robots, including Elon Musk’s Tesla, which has displayed prototypes of its Optimus humanoid for years. On a January earnings call, Musk called China “incredibly good” at scaling production.

Boston Dynamics, the U.S. company whose 2019 canine, Spot, used different technology, recently argued in a Congressional hearing that China’s low pricing would drive U.S. firms out of the market.

None of the U.S. robotics researchers who spoke with Reuters advocated for keeping such government-financed research secret. Instead, they said, U.S. policymakers should focus on enabling companies to quickly commercialize such advances to compete with China.

ARL also said publication was important to scientific and technological advancement.

The U.S. excels in innovation and software development but needs to ensure the capital, industrial base, highly skilled workforce and parts supply chains to scale-up breakthroughs, said Vijay Kumar, dean at Penn Engineering.

For now, U.S. authorities often protect companies with trade restrictions on innovative Chinese products. In July, the Federal Communications Commission [banned imports, opens new tab](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-423682A1.pdf) of future models of foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots, including those from Unitree. China has [threatened to retaliate ](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/china-warns-retaliation-if-us-sticks-with-robot-ban-2026-07-30/)to the FCC ban.

China’s Washington embassy said in a statement its companies are making new technologies affordable and accessible internationally. It accused the U.S. of abusing administrative power, and of “market distortion and unilateral bullying.”

Most of the experts who spoke to Reuters supported the U.S. ban but said the policy is not enough to build an industry to catch up with China.

“This isn't just a competition between U.S. and Chinese robotics companies,” said Kenneally, the Ghost Robotics founder. “It's between private U.S. companies and China’s coordinated national strategy.”

In 2015, Chinese President Xi Jinping set out a 10-year plan to lead industries including green energy, electric vehicles and robotics. Since then, China has channeled capital into risky bets on low-margin 21st-century advanced manufacturing.

Chinese robot manufacturers’ rapid reverse-engineering of products draws on the country’s dominance in refining critical minerals needed for magnets in robotics applications and the density of suppliers of motors, gears, ‘muscles’ known as actuators, and other components clustered near Unitree’s base in Hangzhou.

Reyk Knuhtsen, a robotics analyst at SemiAnalysis, called the United States’ failure to commercialize domestic research a “dropped ball,” citing U.S. venture capital’s preference for high-return software startups. It would take more than trade barriers on China to boost robotics manufacturing, he said.

“This is a very big hill for the U.S. to climb,” he said.

The White House did not respond to requests for comment about U.S. industrial policy. An FCC spokesperson said the commission was protecting Americans from foreign threats and preparing the country "to deliver on President ‌Trump’s vision to lead ⁠the world on the next generation of technologies."

Jaret Riddick, who as a former Army Research Laboratory director oversaw the Army-funded robotics project, said it did kick-start the U.S. quadruped industry. But in the end, without support for large-scale production, Unitree in China “just kind of consumed that space.”

### TEACHING ROBOT DOGS NEW TRICKS

The Army funded the Robotics Collaborative Technology Alliance (RCTA) between 2010 and 2020.

The RCTA gathered government, academic and industry researchers from UPenn, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), Boston Dynamics, NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory, and other research institutions. The alliance's top commercial partner was General Dynamics Land Systems, the Michigan-based defense manufacturer that makes Abrams M1 tanks.

In 2016, UPenn researchers, including Kenneally, eliminated heavy central gearboxes and placed motors in robots’ legs, enhancing the ability to sense and respond to terrain. UPenn’s progress built on the MIT lab’s work funded by the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency, DARPA.

In 2019, the MIT lab presented the Mini Cheetah, building on UPenn features with increased strength – and the ability to do backflips.

Months earlier, Katz published his MIT masters thesis, detailing Mini Cheetah’s actuators. Within six months, he found Chinese companies manufacturing actuator copies “you could buy on AliExpress,” he said, referring to the Chinese online retail giant.

Unitree had been founded three years before by Wang Xingxing, then 26 years old. In his 2016 master's thesis from Shanghai University, which Reuters reviewed online, Wang cited the work done at MIT, including on earlier iterations of the Cheetah, and set out principles consistent with those ⁠developed at the U.S. lab. He acknowledged the lab’s "pioneering" contributions.

In his thesis, Wang explored design tweaks to speed up development, saying his findings could have future "defense, security, family and education" applications. He referenced Kenneally's ARL-funded UPenn paper.

On its website, Unitree says the company “independently developed the motors, reducers, controllers, and even some sensors of the quadruped robot.”

Katz told Reuters he decided against taking his actuators into production, thinking there would be no market. He said China’s ability to move fast had broad benefits: Robotics “has advanced a lot because of the availability of actuators.”

Joao Ramos, who did research at the same time as Katz at the MIT lab, said Unitree copied the Mini Cheetah shape, structure and design. But he credited the company with doing “proper engineering” to perfect a mass-produced robot.

“The actual practical implementation, bringing it to real life, that's all on them,” Ramos said.

### GUN-TOTING ROBOT DOGS

Walking robots aren’t yet widely deployed on battlefields, but the United States and Chinese militaries have both explored the prospect. ⁠In October, another Chinese company’s “Robot Wolf” quadrupeds featured in Chinese media coverage of the PLA [conducting mock beach landings, opens new tab](https://youtu.be/ZGBybUTIwY8?is=72qQIM9ccmYSfVpO).

Unitree has said in public statements that its products are intended for civilian use. In 2022, the company signed an open letter along with U.S. companies pledging not to weaponize robots, citing risks of harm and serious ethical issues.

But Chinese state television has featured Unitree’s Go2 model robots [alongside Chinese troops, opens new tab](https://youtu.be/Xa8Do0kTJUw?is=KUCt0IZPeTC-pXFd) and a modified version of the firm’s B1 series robot firing a mounted assault rifle.

In September 2023, the U.S. Army secured a federal waiver from laws requiring government agencies to prioritize American-made products, in order to purchase two Unitree Go2s, according to [procurement records, opens new tab](https://www.madeinamerica.gov/waivers/nonavailability/64fa58a21734bedbb83b9663)that described the devices as crucial tools in the absence of sufficient commercial quantities of U.S.-produced alternatives “of a satisfactory quality." Reuters could not establish whether the purchases went ahead.

Later that month, U.S. Marines conducted an exercise [attaching a rocket launcher, opens new tab](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/456001/marines-test-emerging-technologies-combat-center)to a Go1 ⁠to enable remote firing.

Kenneally’s Ghost Robotics has shipped about 1,000 of its Vision 60 robots, mostly to military and government customers for surveillance and search applications in harsh environments. The company’s first customer was Navy Special Warfare Command, which oversees Navy SEAL teams.

Some components for the Vision 60s, which sell for upwards of $165,000, are built in China, though the company has sought to move its supply chain. By comparison, Unitree lists its B series robot, for below $100,000 in the U.S.

Ghost Robotics shifted its motor supply from China to South Korea two years ago, though it still finds it difficult to eliminate Chinese magnets and some metal components, Kenneally said.

The only way to escape China’s chokehold on the robotics supply chain, he said, is to secure supplies from other countries that have the manufacturing capacity that the United States lacks. In 2024, South Korea’s LIG Defense & Aerospace acquired a 60% stake in Ghost Robotics for $240 million. The company is exploring production in Taiwan, Kenneally said.

“We need to work closely with our allies,” he said. “There's absolutely no path forward without them.”
