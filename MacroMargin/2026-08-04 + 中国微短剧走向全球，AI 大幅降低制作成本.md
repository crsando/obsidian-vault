### 中文译文

## 中国微短剧走向全球，AI 大幅降低制作成本

曾经被斥为低俗肥皂剧的短剧，如今已成为价值110亿美元的全球产业，吸引了各大影视公司的日益关注。

卡西·埃瑟在美国加利福尼亚州格伦代尔为《烈火之恋》的一个场景做准备，2025年 *摄影：阿梅莉亚·乔伊斯*

### **要点**

- 微短剧专为手机观看设计，单集时长仅两到三分钟，现已成为全球增长最快的移动娱乐形式。
- 微短剧的成功得益于其在印度、拉丁美洲和东南亚的迅速普及，到2026年4月，用户在微短剧应用上的日均使用时间升至25分钟。
- 该行业预计到2030年将达到260亿美元，各公司正在探索如何使内容适应当地文化和品味，并尝试利用AI降低成本、创作更具创意的故事。
作为一名在洛杉矶打拼的演员，[卡西·埃瑟](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm6820472/)偶然踏入了微短剧的新世界。他在一部[60集剧集](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt28777411/)中出演狼人主角，原本只希望说服至少几个朋友在一个小众手机应用上观看他的首秀。

该剧在美国上线后几周内就在全球积累了数亿次观看，使他成为这种大多数美国人从未听过的内容形式的首批全球明星之一。微短剧专为手机观看设计，单集时长仅两到三分钟，现已成为全球增长最快的移动娱乐形式。

“人们一直问那是什么，我说，‘其实我也不知道。’你必须下载应用、按集付费——似乎障碍重重，”Kasey 说道。他在申请这个角色前一天被经纪人解约。

*Fated to My Forbidden Alpha* 的成功也让制片人松了一口气，他们曾冒险尝试在世界其他地区复制微短剧在中国的爆炸性成功。这类剧集曾被嗤之以鼻，被视为充斥着夸张亿万富翁爱情和复仇情节的低级内容，但如今已被证明对那些想要随时轻松娱乐的观众具有成瘾性。

全球范围内，微短剧应用的日均使用时长截至2026年4月升至25分钟，较2025年1月大幅增长85%，这得益于印度、拉丁美洲和东南亚的快速普及。根据市场情报公司 Sensor Tower 六月发布的报告，这一数字紧追传统流媒体平台，后者基本持平在约35分钟。

但在这个与其情节一样快节奏的行业，平台已在应对激烈竞争。在美国，由于愿意按集付费的观众减少，许多平台被迫转向广告，世界各地的制片人也在试验 AI 以削减成本。

微短剧的制作成本仅为数万美元，完成拍摄通常不到两周，顶尖演员日薪最高可达2000美元。最受欢迎的作品通过广告、订阅和向观众收费的组合，能够产生数百万美元的销售额。18至35岁的女性观众占比最大，她们在午休、通勤或一天中任何空闲时间如饥似渴地追看这些留下悬念的情节。

“情节很肤浅。但正是这一点让它们如此有趣，”曼谷45岁的全职母亲 Thananan Srisaengchay 说，她每天花约两小时观看微短剧。“我意识到自己对冗长复杂的剧集越来越缺乏耐心。我只想要有趣、快速且易懂的内容。”

![Esser hangs from a wire rig before shooting a stunt scene in Love Under Fire. Photographer: Amelia Joyce](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b281cc90b1481fcaef9cf439c29d2b6/3b281cc9-0b14-8161-bb77-eb7b7e977076-2b34820b5d22f9ffb22f06f72b30d8a6.png)

Esser 在拍摄《Love Under Fire》的特技镜头前悬挂在钢丝装置上。摄影师：Amelia Joyce

微短剧大约在2018年在中国出现，此后飞速崛起，到2024年收入超过中国整个电影票房。随着本土市场近7亿观众趋于饱和，ReelShort 和 [DramaBox](https://www.dramabox.com/) 等中国领先内容公司投入数亿美元将该模式推向全球。根据数字营销平台 DataEye-ADX 的数据，到2025年2月，超过200个短剧应用涌入海外市场——几乎是前一年的四倍——共产出数千部作品。

“同样的故事，不同的演员，不同的地点，”洛杉矶导演 Xavier Serrano 说。他曾为中国顶级平台创作了 11 部微短剧。他说，制片方往往希望保持保守，坚持使用他们知道会受欢迎的相同肥皂剧式剧情。

根据 Sensor Tower 的数据，美国迅速成为营收引擎，在 2026 年初贡献了全球应用内购买收入的 37%，尽管下载量仅占全球的 3%。但美国的增长现在正在降温：第一季度美国用户支出同比下降 11%，而其他地区的同类内容却大幅增长。

Bogdan Nesvit 是 Holywater Tech 的联合创始人兼联席首席执行官。Holywater Tech 是一家由乌克兰人创办的娱乐科技公司，也是少数在美国站稳脚跟的非中国公司之一。他表示，微短剧利用了观众在智能手机上快速获取多巴胺刺激的需求，这种需求建立在 TikTok 和 Instagram 等平台推广的观看习惯之上。

随着市场竞争日益激烈，他预测未来几年平台将迎来一场优胜劣汰的洗牌，同时对犯罪或悬疑微短剧等更广泛内容的需求也将不断增长。“如果这个领域不进化，总有一天它将不再引起观众共鸣，增长也会大幅放缓，”他说。

好莱坞不再旁观微短剧的繁荣。今年 5 月，NBCUniversal 的 Peacock 成为美国首家在移动应用上推出专用竖屏视频信息流的大型流媒体服务，并上线了像 *Fated to My Forbidden Alpha* 这样获得授权的微短剧。TikTok 已经推出了自己的独立应用 PineDrama，而福克斯正在将其黄金时段真人秀 *Farmer Wants a Wife * 一整季切分成不到 2 分钟的剧集，供 Holywater 的 My Drama 应用使用。

观看量的增长促使制片公司和流媒体公司竞相在市场成熟前抢占位置，探索如何让内容适应当地文化和口味。根据分析公司 Omdia 的数据，该行业 2025 年全球收入为 110 亿美元；另一家分析公司 Media Partners Asia 则预测，到 2030 年这一数字将达到 260 亿美元。

在 6 月举行的亚洲最大媒体会议 APOS 上，Story TV 的首席执行官 Saurabh Pandey 说：“在宝莱坞，无论你走进哪个房间，微短剧都是大家谈论的头号话题。”这个平台一年前才推出，如今已是印度领先的短剧平台之一。

“这曾是那种让所有人都觉得尴尬的东西，”他接受采访时说。“过去六个月里，人们感到好奇但无法接受。最近两个月，所有人都完全被它吸引了。这股势头是真实的。”

但推动中国热潮的付费模式——每集收费20至50美分——正显示出放缓迹象，并在亚洲和拉丁美洲市场不受欢迎，那里的观众更喜欢看广告。由于广告支持剧集的利润率低于按集付费，平台正争相削减成本，包括迅速采用人工智能。

ReelShort是打入美国市场受益最大的公司之一，今年有望实现超过10亿美元的收入。其创始人贾毅表示，到2027年，AI制作的内容可能占其内容的90%以上，从而为成本降至目前水平的10%铺平道路。

不过，他并不掩饰对AI可能带来竞争的焦虑：“我感到非常害怕。AI正在彻底改变竞争格局，”他在APOS的一个小组讨论会上表示。“以前，如果你在好莱坞工作，你知道你的竞争对手是高端媒体工作室。”

![Jin Ze and Wu Yi in the hit vertical microdrama Love at Reunion, one of the short form romance series fueling China's booming microdrama industry. Source: Presence Matter](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b281cc90b1481fcaef9cf439c29d2b6/3b281cc9-0b14-814a-979c-f49b09fd0d9b-5a7899da3b5eadb61c14e79dbb660e5f.png)

热门竖屏微短剧《重逢爱情》中的金泽和吴怡，是推动中国蓬勃发展的微短剧行业的浪漫短剧系列之一。来源：Presence Matter

主导海外市场的三家中国巨头——昆仑万维、COL集团和DramaBox——都以某种方式将AI作为其战略核心。

[昆仑万维](https://www.kunlun.com/en/)已将AI引入包括音频和视频在内的所有制作领域，根据公司5月份提交的[投资者披露文件](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-05-06/1225280764.PDF)，成本从每部系列约20万美元降至不足2万美元，降幅达90%。这帮助公司今年第一季度经营现金流转正，但仍报告净亏损约8.87亿元人民币（1.32亿美元）。

摄影师Jo Zhou在中美两地工作，曾拍摄过一些最早爆火的微短剧，2025年每月参与两个项目，但今年减半。她最近接到的一个项目被取消，转而采用完全由AI生成的作品。行业创意人员现在可以在几天内制作出一小时的故事，将其分成数十个短集并发布。

“我相信这能让整个行业在内容本身释放更多想象力，”AI驱动制作公司Presence Matter的首席运营官陈昭灵表示。利用AI降低制作成本可以释放资金，尝试更多创意叙事，并最终超越耸人听闻的肥皂剧式情节。

“我仍然相信 AI 不会完全取代真人拍摄，”她说。“尤其对于某些需要传达人与人之间微妙、敏感联系的主题。”

### English Original

## Chinese Microdramas Go Global as AI Slashes Production Costs

Once dismissed as lowbrow soap operas, the bite-sized shows are now an $11 billion global industry, attracting growing interest from major studios.

Kasey Esser gets ready for a scene in “Love Under Fire” Glendale, CA, USA, 2025 *Photographer: Amelia Joyce*

### **Takeaways**

- Microdramas, designed to be watched on a mobile phone with episodes just two to three minutes long, are now the fastest growing mobile entertainment format worldwide.
- The success of microdramas has been fueled by rapid popularity in India, Latin America, and Southeast Asia, with the average daily time spent on microdrama apps rising to 25 minutes by April 2026.
- The industry is expected to reach $26 billion by 2030, with companies exploring how to adapt content for local cultures and tastes, and experimenting with AI to cut costs and produce more creative storytelling.
As a struggling actor in Los Angeles, [Kasey Esser](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm6820472/) stumbled into the new world of microdramas. Cast as the werewolf lead in a [60-episode series](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt28777411/), he had hoped to convince at least a few friends to watch his debut on an obscure mobile app.

Within weeks of its release in the US it had racked up hundreds of millions of views worldwide, making him one of the first global stars of a format most Americans have never heard of. Designed to be watched on a mobile phone, with episodes just two to three minutes long, microdramas are now the fastest growing mobile entertainment format worldwide.

“People kept asking what it was, and I was like, ‘I actually don’t know.’ You had to download an app, pay per episode — it seemed like a lot of friction,” said Kasey, who was dropped by his agent the day before he applied for the part.

The success of *Fated to My Forbidden Alpha* was also a relief to producers, who had gambled on emulating the explosive success of microdramas in China throughout the rest of the world. Once dismissed as lowbrow content dominated by over-the-top billionaire romances and revenge plots, they have proved addictive to audiences who want light entertainment on-the-go.

Globally, the average daily time spent on microdrama apps rose to 25 minutes by April 2026 — up a massive 85% from January 2025 — fueled by rapid popularity in India, Latin America and Southeast Asia. That’s hot on the heels of traditional streaming platforms, which remained broadly flat at around 35 minutes, according to a report by market intelligence firm Sensor Tower published in June.

But in an industry as fast-paced as its plots, platforms are already grappling with fierce competition. In the US, many have been forced to pivot to advertising after a slump in viewers willing to pay per episode, and producers across the world are experimenting with AI to cut costs.

Microdramas cost just tens of thousands of dollars to make and are often filmed in under two weeks, with top actors earning up to $2,000 a day. The most popular can generate millions of dollars in sales through a mix of advertising, subscriptions and charging viewers. Women aged between 18 and 35 make up the largest share of the audience, devouring the cliffhanger plots on lunch-breaks, work commutes or any spare minute throughout the day.

“The plots are very superficial. But that’s exactly what makes them so entertaining,” said Thananan Srisaengchay, a 45-year-old stay-at-home mother in Bangkok who spends around two hours a day watching microdramas. “I’ve realized I have less patience for long, complicated series. I just want something fun, quick and easy to understand.”

Esser hangs from a wire rig before shooting a stunt scene in Love Under Fire. Photographer: Amelia Joyce

Microdramas first emerged in China around 2018, enjoying a meteoric rise to outearn the country’s entire movie box office by 2024. With the home market nearing saturation with 700 million viewers, leading Chinese content companies like ReelShort and [DramaBox](https://www.dramabox.com/) poured hundreds of millions of dollars into taking the model global. By February 2025, more than 200 short‑drama apps had flooded overseas markets — nearly four times as many as the year before — collectively churning out thousands of titles, according to digital marketing platform DataEye-ADX.

“Same story, different actors, different locations,” said Los Angeles-based director Xavier Serrano, who has created 11 microdramas for top Chinese platforms. He said producers often want to remain conservative, sticking to the same soap opera-style storylines that they know will be popular.

The US quickly became the revenue engine, contributing 37% of global in-app purchases in early 2026 despite accounting for just 3% of downloads, according to Sensor Tower. But that US growth is now cooling: American user spending fell 11% year-on-year in the first quarter, even as the category surged elsewhere.

Bogdan Nesvit, the co-founder and co-chief executive officer of Holywater Tech, a Ukraine-founded entertainment-tech company and one of the few non-Chinese firms to gain a foothold in the US, said microdramas have tapped into audiences’ appetite for quick dopamine hits on their smartphones, building on viewing habits popularized by platforms such as TikTok and Instagram.

With an increasingly crowded market, he predicts a competitive shakeout of platforms in the next few years, and growing demand for broader content, such as crime or mystery microdramas. “If the space doesn’t evolve, it will stop resonating with the audience at some point and the growth will slow a lot,” he said.

Hollywood is no longer watching the microdrama boom from the sidelines. This May, NBCUniversal’s Peacock became the first major US streamer to launch a dedicated vertical-video feed on its mobile app, stocking it with licensed microdramas like *Fated to My Forbidden Alpha*. TikTok has already launched its own standalone app, PineDrama, while Fox is now slicing up a full season of its primetime reality show *Farmer Wants a Wife *into sub-2-minute episodes for Holywater’s My Drama app.

The growth in viewership has sent studios and streaming companies rushing to establish positions before the market matures, exploring how to adapt content for local cultures and tastes. The industry, which analyst group Omdia said generated $11 billion globally in 2025, is on track to reach $26 billion by 2030, according to another analyst firm, Media Partners Asia.

If you go into any room in Bollywood, microdrama will be the number one topic everyone’s talking about, said Saurabh Pandey, chief executive of Story TV, at Asia’s largest media conference APOS in June. Launched only a year ago, the platform is one of India’s leading short-form platforms.

“It was one of those things that everyone found cringe,” he said in an interview. “For the last six months they were curious but couldn’t accept it. For the last two months, everyone’s just fully gravitated to it. The momentum is real.”

But the revenue model that powered the boom in China — charging between 20 and 50 cents per episode — is showing signs of slowing, and has proved unpopular in Asian and Latin American markets where audiences prefer to sit through adverts. With ad-supported series generating thinner margins than pay-per-episode releases, platforms are scrambling to cut costs, including rapid adoption of artificial intelligence.

ReelShort is one of the companies to benefit the most from breaking into the US market and is on track to generate more than $1 billion in revenue this year. By 2027, AI-generated productions could account for more than 90% of its content, paving the way for costs to plummet to 10% of current levels, its founder Joey Jia said.

Still, he doesn’t hide his anxiety about the competition that AI could bring: “I feel really scared. AI is completely changing the competitive landscape,” he told a panel at APOS. “Before, if you worked in Hollywood, you knew your competitors were high-end media studios.”

Jin Ze and Wu Yi in the hit vertical microdrama Love at Reunion, one of the short form romance series fueling China's booming microdrama industry. Source: Presence Matter

All three Chinese giants dominating the overseas market — Kunlun Tech, COL Group, and DramaBox — have in some way made AI central to their strategy.

[Kunlun Tech](https://www.kunlun.com/en/) has introduced AI across all areas of production, including audio and video, slashing costs by 90% from roughly $200,000 per series to under $20,000, according to an [investor disclosure](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-05-06/1225280764.PDF) the company filed in May. It helped turn operating cash flow positive in the first quarter of the year, yet it still reported a net loss of about 887 million yuan ($132 million).

Jo Zhou, a cinematographer who splits her time between China and the US and has shot some of the earliest viral microdramas, was working on two projects a month in 2025, but that’s halved this year. A project she was recently booked for was scrapped in favor of a fully AI-generated production. Industry creatives can now produce hour-long stories, divide them into dozens of short episodes and release them in a matter of days.

“I believe this allows the entire industry to unleash more imagination in the content itself,” said Chen Zhaoling, chief operating officer of AI-driven production company Presence Matter. Using AI to cut production costs could free up cash to experiment with more creative storytelling, and eventually move beyond sensationalized soap-opera style plots.

“I still believe AI won’t completely replace real human filming,” she said. “Especially for certain subjects that require conveying subtle, sensitive connections between people.”
