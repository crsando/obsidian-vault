### 中文译文

## 能源需求飙升，中国批准250亿美元核能扩张计划

中国周五批准了八座新核反应堆，这些项目据[报道](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33691572)价值超过1700亿元人民币（合250亿美元），加速其核能建设，因为该国寻求在不增加碳排放的情况下为经济提供动力。

中国国务院（即内阁）批准了四个省份的项目，国有电力生产商的申报文件显示。其中包括位于广东省的[惠州5号和6号机组](https://www.szse.cn/disclosure/listed/bulletinDetail/index.html?f6e262ed-9193-4568-976f-1d7df0c2f115=)，由[中国广核电力股份有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1816%20HK%20Equity)的子公司运营，该公司是中国最大的核电运营商。每台机组建成后将增加1,217兆瓦的容量——足以为中国百万家庭供电。

据监管文件，[批准](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-08-01/601985_20260801_LY6U.pdf)还授予了中国核能电力股份有限公司位于辽宁的庄河项目和浙江的金七门设施，以及国家电投产业金融控股有限公司位于山东的[莱阳开发项目](https://www.szse.cn/disclosure/listed/bulletinDetail/index.html?c56ea831-963f-450c-81f2-84fc68910cbc=)。

这些批准凸显了北京方面在电力需求持续激增之际确保可靠、全天候供电的努力。尽管中国在太阳能和风电装机方面领先世界，但核能对于提供无碳基荷电力至关重要，以稳定因可再生能源间歇性而脆弱的电网。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b181cc90b14812f9660d00348bbd1b4/3b181cc9-0b14-81c1-8438-cb159cdab07a-40290e9718c49858ac34763272d7e3fd.png)

今年3月，中国政府[设定](https://blinks.bloomberg.com/news/stories/TBGW17T96OSJ)了到2030年核电装机容量达到110吉瓦的目标，比去年年底增长76%。这一快速扩张是中国努力在本十年结束前超越法国和美国成为全球最大核电发电国的一部分。

尽管如此，实现这些雄心可能具有挑战性。由于2011年日本福岛核灾难后多年的审批冻结，以及疫情后的供应链瓶颈，中国未能实现到2020年达到58吉瓦和到2025年达到70吉瓦的先前目标。

## China Approves $25 Billion Nuclear Expansion as Energy Demand Soars

China approved eight new nuclear reactors on Friday, with the projects [reportedly](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33691572) worth more than 170 billion yuan ($25 billion), accelerating its atomic buildout as the country seeks to power its economy without increasing carbon emissions.

The State Council, China’s cabinet, greenlit projects across four provinces, filings from state-backed power producers show. Among them are [Huizhou Units 5 and 6](https://www.szse.cn/disclosure/listed/bulletinDetail/index.html?f6e262ed-9193-4568-976f-1d7df0c2f115=) in Guangdong province, operated by a subsidiary of [CGN Power Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1816%20HK%20Equity), the nation’s top nuclear operator. Each unit will add 1,217 megawatts of capacity once complete — enough to power million of homes in China.

Approvals were also [granted](https://static.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2026-08-01/601985_20260801_LY6U.pdf) for China National Nuclear Power Corp.’s Zhuanghe project in Liaoning and Jinqimen facility in Zhejiang, alongside SPIC Industry-Finance Holdings Co.’s [Laiyang development](https://www.szse.cn/disclosure/listed/bulletinDetail/index.html?c56ea831-963f-450c-81f2-84fc68910cbc=) in Shandong, according to separate regulatory filings.

The approvals underscore Beijing’s drive to secure reliable, around-the-clock power as electricity demand continues to surge. While China leads the world in solar and wind installations, nuclear energy is vital to provide carbon-free baseload power to stabilize a grid vulnerable to the intermittent nature of renewables.

In March, the Chinese government [set](https://blinks.bloomberg.com/news/stories/TBGW17T96OSJ) a target of 110 gigawatts of installed nuclear capacity by 2030 – a 76% surge from the end of last year. The rapid expansion is part of China’s efforts to overtake France and the US as the world’s largest nuclear generator before the decade ends.

Still, hitting those ambitions may prove challenging. China missed its previous targets of reaching 58 gigawatts by 2020 and 70 gigawatts by 2025 following a multi-year approval freeze after Japan’s 2011 Fukushima disaster, as well as post-pandemic supply chain bottlenecks.
