### 中文译文

## 美国电信监管机构负责人表示，限制中国科技产品进口旨在刺激生产并应对安全风险

2026年3月14日，中国北京，一个人形机器人在招聘会的参观者面前跳舞。路透社/Florence Lo

**摘要**

- 美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）表示，限制措施旨在迅速刺激美国机器人和电源逆变器的生产
- 该机构自12月以来已禁止外国新型无人机、路由器、机器人和电源逆变器型号
- 民主党委员戈麦斯批评FCC技术政策变更的随意、混乱推出
华盛顿，8月6日（路透社）——美国联邦通信委员会主席布伦丹·卡尔周四表示，对中国机器人和电源逆变器进口的限制旨在迅速刺激美国生产并应对国家安全风险。

美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）负责监管美国电信，自12月以来已禁止外国新型无人机、路由器、机器人和电源逆变器型号，向非中国供应商授予豁免，同时有效排除了中国制造商。

"我们正在告诉人们，现在是时候将大量投资回流国内，并将其发展成为可能构成国家安全关键要素的产业，"卡尔表示，并补充说："我们不希望五年后出现成千上万甚至数百万台这类设备构成国家安全风险的局面。"

美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）[上月](https://www.reuters.com/world/trump-administration-drafting-ban-chinese-data-center-devices-sources-say-2026-08-04/)禁止从中国进口新型人形机器人和四足机器人，以及联网电力逆变器——这些逆变器可使可再生能源和电池接入电网和数据中心设备。这些措施本周早些时候引发了中国的[报复](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/china-targets-us-firms-drone-exports-after-fcc-xinjiang-actions-2026-08-05/)。

路透社[本周早些时候报道](https://www.reuters.com/world/trump-administration-drafting-ban-chinese-data-center-devices-sources-say-2026-08-04/)称，美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）正起草一项禁令，禁止美国进口中国数据中心组件（即光收发器）的新型型号，以保护支撑[人工智能](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/)热潮的基础设施。

当被问及这篇报道时，卡尔表示相关问题应询问其他机构。

他说：“关于增加哪些项目的决定由行政部门和国家安全机构推动。他们做出决定，我们随后执行他们的决定。因此，如果他们要采取什么下一步行动，请向他们询问。”

民主党籍FCC委员安娜·戈麦斯对卡尔以国家安全为重点的行动表示欢迎，但对该过程提出批评。

她说：“我从许多美国公司和行业领袖那里听到的是，他们担心我们推出FCC技术政策根本性变革时那种杂乱无章、混乱无序的方式。”她补充说：“如果我们想避免看起来偏袒某些参与者而非其他参与者，就需要透明度……否则，这实际上看起来只是产业政策。”

报道：David Shepardson；撰稿：Alexandra Alper 和 David Shepardson；编辑：Mark Porter 和 Sanjeev Miglani

## US telecoms regulator chief says curbs on Chinese tech imports aim to spur production, counter security risks

A humanoid robot dances in front of visitors at a job fair in Beijing, China March 14, 2026. REUTERS/Florence Lo

**Summary**

- FCC says restrictions seek to spur US production of robots and power inverters quickly
- Agency has banned new foreign drone, router, robot and power inverter models since December
- Democratic Commissioner Gomez criticises haphazard, chaotic rollout of FCC tech policy changes
WASHINGTON, Aug 6 (Reuters) - The chair of the U.S. Federal Communications Commission, Brendan Carr, said on Thursday that restrictions on imports of Chinese robots and power inverters are aimed at quickly spurring U.S. production and addressing national security risks.

The FCC, which regulates U.S. telecommunications, has banned new models of foreign drones, routers, robots and power inverters since December, granting waivers to non-Chinese suppliers while effectively shutting out Chinese manufacturers.

"We're telling people that now is the time to onshore a lot of the investment and develop it into what could be critical elements of our national security," Carr said, adding, "We don't want to be in a situation five years from now where you've got many thousands or millions of these types of devices that are a national security risk."

The FCC [last month](https://www.reuters.com/world/trump-administration-drafting-ban-chinese-data-center-devices-sources-say-2026-08-04/) barred Chinese imports of new humanoid and quadruped robots and connected power inverters, which enable renewable energy sources and batteries to connect to grids and data center equipment. The measures triggered Chinese [retaliation](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/china-targets-us-firms-drone-exports-after-fcc-xinjiang-actions-2026-08-05/) earlier this week.

Reuters [reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/trump-administration-drafting-ban-chinese-data-center-devices-sources-say-2026-08-04/) earlier this week that the FCC was drafting a ban on U.S. imports of new models of Chinese data center components, known as optical transceivers, as it seeks to protect the infrastructure that undergirds the [AI](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/) boom.

When asked about the report, Carr deferred to other agencies.

"The decision about what to add is one that's driven by the executive branch, national security agencies. They make the decision. We then implement the decision they make. So I'd refer you to them for any comment on what, if anything, they may have next," he said.

Democratic FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez welcomed Carr's national security-focused actions but took aim at the process.

"What I'm hearing from a lot of American companies and industry leaders is that they're concerned about the haphazard way, the chaotic way, in which we've seen the rollout of basically profound changes" to FCC tech policy, she said. "If we want to avoid the appearance of favoring certain actors from others, we need transparency....Otherwise, this actually looks like is just industrial policy," she added.

Reporting by David Shepardson; Writing by Alexandra Alper and David Shepardson; Editing by Mark Porter and Sanjeev Miglani
