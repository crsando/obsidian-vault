### 中文译文

## 伊朗如何扩大施压行动以迫使美国让步

贝鲁特，8月3日（路透社）——海湾官员和分析人士称，伊朗正押注于通过将中东的贸易路线、航运通道和能源基础设施变为压力点，逐步提高对抗成本，从而在与美国的较量中坚持到底。

德黑兰并非寻求决定性的军事胜利，而是在推行一种经过精心校准的升级战略，旨在[扩大冲突](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/)而不引发全面战争。

他们表示，其目标是让美国及其盟友相信，遏制危机的代价要高于在霍尔木兹海峡问题上接受伊朗的要求。

德黑兰发出的信息是，除非华盛顿接受一种让伊朗在霍尔木兹海峡发挥更大作用的新现状，否则冲突可能蔓延至海湾以外。

伊朗认为，通过将多个海上咽喉要道和能源设施置于风险之中，它可以在未来的任何谈判中增强自己的筹码。

“伊朗从一开始就试图通过不断扩大其升级选项来超越美国，使其每周都有新东西可拿——新的地理区域、新式武器、新目标类型，”华盛顿研究所的迈克尔·奈茨对路透社表示。

奈茨补充说：“他们更大的优势不在于能伤害美国或以色列，而在于能伤害地区国家和全球经济。”

### 威胁扩展至霍尔木兹海峡以外

在展示了其扰乱霍尔木兹海峡交通的能力之后（战前全球约五分之一的石油消费量通过该海峡），德黑兰已表示，任何海上咽喉要道都不是禁区。

对红海和沙特能源基础设施的威胁表明，伊朗正做出更广泛的努力，以提高保护全球商业的成本，同时试探华盛顿对破坏行为的容忍度。

伊朗的做法反映了一位海湾消息人士所称的伊斯兰革命卫队指挥官们的一种信念，即"他们可以得到更多"。

他们将总统[唐纳德·特朗普的](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/donald-trump/)在11月中期选举前不愿深度卷入另一场中东冲突视为一个机会。

"伊朗人……相信通过扩大战争和增加压力，他最终会让步，"该海湾消息人士说。"特朗普认为他可以狠狠打击伊朗人，迫使他们回到谈判桌前，但他们不会屈服。"

### 升级以迫使让步

这一判断支撑着德黑兰的谈判策略。

特朗普表示，[与伊朗的谈判](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/trump-says-iran-talks-take-place-monday-sets-no-deadline-deal-2026-08-02/)将于周一举行，此前他曾表示已取消一次迫在眉睫的袭击，以期达成重新开放霍尔木兹海峡的协议。然而，德黑兰表示目前并未与美国进行谈判。

一位伊朗高级消息人士表示，德黑兰领导层已得出结论，此前展现灵活性的尝试只换来了华盛顿方面更大的压力，这强化了这样一种看法，即必须先建立筹码，才能开始有实质意义的谈判。

该消息人士称，德黑兰方面评估认为，特朗普将让步视为软弱，只对压力作出回应。

华盛顿研究所高级研究员迈克尔·辛格表示，德黑兰认为其仍握有主动权，部分原因在于特朗普政府似乎不确定自己愿意在军事上走多远。

"伊朗人正试图扩大自身优势，"他补充道，"他们有意确立对霍尔木兹海峡的主权，并对这条水道实施选择性控制。"

曾任美国外交官、伊朗问题专家艾伦·艾尔表示，德黑兰正在推行一种威慑战略，旨在迫使华盛顿解除封锁并停止军事打击。"他们不希望美国来主导事态发展的节奏，"他说。

争端的核心在于霍尔木兹海峡未来的治理方式。据地区消息人士称，[阿曼已向伊朗提交](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/gulf-states-back-plan-let-iran-collect-voluntary-fees-use-hormuz-2026-07-28/)一份由海湾国家支持的方案，提议管理这条水道，包括自愿用户付费机制。

然而，德黑兰正寻求获得对霍尔木兹海峡的行政管理权，并能够对船只征收服务费。华盛顿已拒绝任何收费方案，坚持霍尔木兹海峡应保持国际水道地位，不受伊朗控制。

### 耐力博弈

伊朗的施压行动产生了一个反映其战略逻辑的后果。随着威胁扩展到霍尔木兹海峡之外，地区大国和西方大国越来越专注于保护贸易路线和能源基础设施，而不是加大对伊朗的压力。

正在形成的由沙特主导的海上联盟体现了这一转变。海湾消息人士将其描述为一个防御性框架，专注于护送商船、共享情报和维护海上航道，而不是直接对抗伊朗。

奈茨将此努力与欧洲在红海的“阿斯皮德斯”任务相提并论，该任务旨在保护商船通行，而非改变军事平衡。

对德黑兰而言，这种动态代表了一定程度的成功。由于无法直接与美国军力抗衡，伊朗仍可通过迫使各国政府和海军采取防御姿态来施加代价。

每一次新威胁——无论是发生在霍尔木兹海峡、连接红海与亚丁湾的曼德海峡，还是其他地方——都会增加维持全球贸易畅通所需投入的资源。

从核心来看，这场较量是耐力之战。伊朗领导人似乎相信，他们承受经济压力的时间能比美国主导的联盟承受全球商业和能源市场反复中断的时间更长。

这一战略还带有外交目的。通过展示其能在必要时扩大危机，德黑兰希望影响任何最终谈判的条件。

“如果会有谈判，他们将定义条款，”美国外交关系协会的雷·塔基赫表示，他曾在美国国务院担任伊朗事务顾问。“双方都在以升级回应升级。”

然而，双方似乎都不愿让步。随着华盛顿和德黑兰继续试探彼此的决心，风险依然存在：旨在最大化杠杆效应的策略可能演变成一场双方都无法完全控制的冲突。

### English Original

## How Iran is widening its pressure campaign to force US concessions

BEIRUT, Aug 3 (Reuters) - Iran is betting it can outlast Washington by turning the Middle East's trade routes, shipping lanes and energy infrastructure into pressure points that steadily raise the cost of confrontation, according to Gulf officials and analysts.

Rather than seeking a decisive military victory, Tehran is pursuing a strategy of calibrated escalation aimed at [widening the conflict](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran/) without triggering full-scale war.

The goal, they say, is to convince the United States and its allies that containing the crisis is more costly than accommodating Iran's demands over the Strait of Hormuz.

The message from Tehran is that unless Washington accepts a new status quo that gives Iran a greater role in Hormuz, the conflict could spread beyond the Gulf.

By putting multiple maritime chokepoints and energy assets at risk, Iran believes it can strengthen its hand in any future negotiations.

"Iran, from the beginning, has tried to out-escalate the United States by stretching out its escalation options so that it always has something new to bring every week -- a new geography, a new type of weapon, a new type of target," Michael Knights of the Washington Institute told Reuters.

"The bigger advantage they have is not that they can hurt America or Israel," Knights added. "Their big advantage is that they can hurt the regional states and the global economy."

### EXPANDING THREATS BEYOND HORMUZ

Having demonstrated its ability to disrupt traffic through Hormuz, through which about a fifth of global oil consumption flowed before the war, Tehran has signalled that no maritime chokepoint is off limits.

Threats to the Red Sea and Saudi energy infrastructure suggest a broader effort to raise the cost of protecting global commerce while testing Washington's tolerance for disruption.

Iran's approach reflects what one Gulf source described as a belief among Revolutionary Guards commanders that "they can get more".

They see an opportunity in what they regard as President [Donald Trump's](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/donald-trump/) reluctance to become deeply entangled in another Middle East conflict ahead of November's midterm elections.

"The Iranians...believe that by widening the war and increasing the pressure, he will eventually give in," the Gulf source said. "Trump thinks he can hit the Iranians hard and bring them to the negotiating table, but they won't bend."

### ESCALATE TO FORCE CONCESSIONS

That calculation underpins Tehran's negotiating strategy.

Trump said [talks with Iran](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/trump-says-iran-talks-take-place-monday-sets-no-deadline-deal-2026-08-02/) would take place on Monday after earlier saying he had called off an imminent attack in hopes of securing a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran, however, said it was not currently holding talks with the United States.

A senior Iranian source said Tehran's leadership has concluded that earlier attempts at flexibility only yielded greater pressure from Washington, reinforcing the view that leverage must be established before meaningful negotiations can begin.

In Tehran's assessment, the source said, Trump interprets concessions as weakness and responds only to pressure.

Michael Singh, senior fellow at the Washington Institute, said Tehran believes it still holds the initiative, partly because the Trump administration appears uncertain how far it is willing to go militarily.

"The Iranians are trying to press their advantage," he added. "They're interested in establishing sovereignty over the strait and exercising selective control over the waterway."

Alan Eyre, a former U.S. diplomat and Iran expert, said Tehran is pursuing a strategy of deterrence aimed at forcing Washington to lift its blockade and halt military strikes. "They don't want the U.S. to dictate the pace of events," he said.

At the centre of the dispute is the future governance of Hormuz. [Oman has presented Iran](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/gulf-states-back-plan-let-iran-collect-voluntary-fees-use-hormuz-2026-07-28/) with a Gulf-backed proposal for managing the waterway, including voluntary user fees, according to regional sources.

Tehran, however, is seeking administrative authority over the strait and the ability to levy service charges on vessels. Washington has rejected any fees, insisting Hormuz remain an international waterway free from Iranian control.

### THE ENDURANCE GAMBIT

Iran's campaign has produced a consequence that reflects the logic of its strategy. As threats have expanded beyond Hormuz, regional and Western powers have increasingly focused on protecting trade routes and energy infrastructure rather than intensifying pressure on Iran.

The emerging Saudi-led maritime coalition exemplifies that shift. Gulf sources describe it as a defensive framework focused on escorting commercial vessels, sharing intelligence and safeguarding sea lanes rather than confronting Iran directly.

Knights compares the effort to Europe's Aspides mission in the Red Sea, which was created to protect merchant traffic rather than alter the military balance.

For Tehran, that dynamic represents a measure of success. Unable to match U.S. military power directly, Iran can still impose costs by forcing governments and navies into a defensive posture.

Each new threat, whether in Hormuz, Bab al-Mandeb — that connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden — or elsewhere, increases the resources required to keep global trade flowing.

At its core, the contest is one of endurance. Iranian leaders appear to believe they can tolerate economic pressure longer than a U.S.-led coalition can tolerate recurring disruption to global commerce and energy markets.

The strategy also carries a diplomatic objective. By demonstrating it can broaden the crisis when necessary, Tehran hopes to shape the terms of any eventual negotiations.

"If there are going to be negotiations, they will define the terms," said Ray Takeyh of the Council on Foreign Relations and a former advisor on Iran at the U.S. State Department. "Both sides are matching escalation with escalation."

Yet neither side appears willing to back down. As Washington and Tehran continue to test each other's resolve, the risk remains that a strategy designed to maximise leverage could spiral into a conflict neither can fully control.
