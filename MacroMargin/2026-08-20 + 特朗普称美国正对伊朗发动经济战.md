### 中文译文

## 特朗普称美国正对伊朗发动经济战

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普威胁称，将对伊朗及其贸易伙伴实施经济孤立。此前数月军事打击和海上封锁未能迫使德黑兰屈服。

特朗普没有具体说明将采取哪些措施或针对哪些国家，但他的威胁立即将焦点转向中国——中国购买伊朗大部分石油。布伦特原油价格连续第五天上涨，至每桶约94美元，创下本月最高水平。

在对特朗普言论的首次[回应](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/china-says-trump-s-economic-warfare-won-t-solve-iran-crisis)中，北京表示制裁和压力不会奏效，并呼吁通过外交途径解决。与此同时，伊朗表示，特朗普的威胁是在“转移视线”，让人们忽视美国自身前所未有的债务和激增的利息成本危机。

观看：彭博社的斯图尔特·利文斯顿-华莱士讨论战争最新进展。来源：彭博社

"在失败政策上加倍下注只会带来更大失败——以及伊朗人的敌意，"伊朗外长阿巴斯·阿拉格齐在X上表示。"美国的经济恐怖主义威胁全球经济和世界各地的国家主权。"

目前尚不清楚美国还能对德黑兰实施哪些其尚未经历过的新的经济惩罚。针对伊朗石油销售还将冒着与中国发生新对抗的风险——中国几乎买下了伊朗全部原油出口。这将使特朗普试图与习近平主席延长关税休战的努力复杂化，习近平定于下月访问美国。

"我不认为有什么灵丹妙药，"美国前驻波兰大使丹尼尔·弗里德对彭博电视台表示。"我们是否还有更多杠杆可用于伊朗经济？可能有。但我认为他们现在基本已经做好了规避手段。短期内我们的筹码并不大。"

观看：美国前驻波兰大使丹尼尔·弗里德讨论冲突最新进展。来源：彭博社

特朗普的言论表明，在弹药短缺和成本上升的困扰下，美国希望从一场已消灭伊朗大部分最高领导人并重创其经济、但迄今未能使其屈服的军事行动中转向。

中国不承认单边制裁，但其国有实体通常远离被列入黑名单的石油。中国最大的国有银行也有遵守美国对伊朗、朝鲜甚至香港高级官员制裁的历史，以避免失去美元清算系统的准入。

自美国2月下旬对伊朗发动战争以来，华盛顿已制裁了一些独立的“茶壶”炼油厂和企业。但迄今为止，美国尚未针对为这笔贸易提供融资的中国主要银行。

今年5月，中国命令国内企业不要遵守美国对五家炼油厂的制裁，而中国最大的银行则夹在北京的指示与失去美国金融体系准入的风险之间。

“制裁和压力无助于解决问题，”中国外交部发言人林剑周四在北京对记者表示。“中方呼吁有关各方采取负责任的措施，并寻求通过外交和政治途径解决问题。”

特朗普要求所有国家停止让伊朗获得兑换机构、现金转账、货币互换额度、船舶注册、空壳公司以及走私石油的途径。他还试图召集迄今拒绝支持他对伊斯兰共和国发动战争的其他国家，称美国需要所有盟友帮助“孤立并击败伊朗威胁”。

周二，美国得到提振，阿联酋在指责伊朗向其领海水域发射两枚弹道导弹后，[切断了与伊朗的经济联系](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/uae-cuts-economic-ties-with-iran-after-missiles-target-territory)。伊朗否认对这次袭击负责。

去年，阿联酋是伊朗最大的贸易伙伴，德黑兰依靠这一关系获取外国商品和硬通货，其次是中国、土耳其和印度。

特朗普的目标是迫使伊朗重新谈判，以彻底结束战争，迫使德黑兰放弃核计划并解除对霍尔木兹海峡的封锁。这些目标在双方六月签署的一份谅解备忘录中有明确阐述，但后来在一系列针锋相对的打击中破裂。

伊朗几十年来经受住了经济制裁，其回应是提出自己的要求，包括解冻资产、终止制裁和战争赔偿。

解决冲突对特朗普来说日益紧迫，因为他面临高油价、战争日益不得人心，以及共和党对在11月中期选举中失去国会一院或两院的担忧。本周早些时候，他甚至威胁要轰炸阿曼——这个有时扮演调解角色的美国盟友——如果它妨碍美国的话。

伊朗对商业航运的威胁扼杀了通过霍尔木兹海峡的交通，而特朗普下令的反封锁严重限制了伊朗自身的石油出口能力。

这场已持续近六个月的战争和封锁，已在伊朗引发近80%的通胀，其货币今年对美元贬值近30%。经济学家警告说，这场冲突有可能将这个拥有9000万人口的国家拖入历史上最严重的经济危机之一。

## Trump Says US Is Waging Economic Warfare Against Iran

US President Donald Trump threatened Iran and its trading partners with economic isolation after months of military strikes and a maritime blockade failed to force Tehran to capitulate.

Trump didn’t specify what measures he would take or which countries would be targeted, but his threat put an immediate focus on China, which buys the bulk of Iran’s oil. Brent crude prices advanced for a fifth day to about $94 a barrel, reaching the highest this month.

In its first [reaction](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/china-says-trump-s-economic-warfare-won-t-solve-iran-crisis) to Trump’s comments, Beijing said sanctions and pressure wouldn’t work and called for a diplomatic resolution. Iran, meanwhile, said Trump’s threats were a “diversion” from America’s own crises of unprecedented debt and surging interest costs.

“Doubling down on failed policies will only bring further defeat — and enmity of Iranians,” Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on X. “US economic terrorism threatens global economy and sovereignty worldwide.”

It’s unclear what new economic punishment the US could impose, if any, that Tehran hasn’t already endured. Targeting Iranian oil sales would also risk fresh confrontation with China, the buyer of almost all Iranian crude exports. That would complicate Trump’s bid to extend a tariff truce with President Xi Jinping, who is set to visit the US next month.

“I don’t think there’s a magic bullet,” said Daniel Fried, a former US ambassador to Poland, told Bloomberg TV. “Is there more leverage we could apply against the Iranian economy? Probably. But I think they’ve got the workarounds pretty much in place right now. Our leverage is not great in the short run.”

Trump’s comments signal how the US, beset by munitions shortages and rising costs, wants to pivot from a military campaign that’s eliminated most of Iran’s top leaders and battered its economy, but has so far failed to bring about its surrender.

China doesn’t recognize unilateral sanctions, but its state entities generally stay away from blacklisted oil. Its biggest state banks also have a history of complying with US sanctions against Iran, North Korea and even top officials in Hong Kong, in order to avoid losing access to the US dollar-clearing system.

Washington has already sanctioned some independent Chinese teapot refineries and firms since the US launched the war against Iran in late February. But so far, the US has stopped short of targeting the major Chinese banks that finance the trade.

In May, China ordered domestic companies not to comply with US sanctions on five refiners, while its biggest banks were caught between Beijing’s directive and the risk of losing access to the US financial system.

“Sanctions and pressure will not help resolve the issue,” Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman Lin Jian told reporters at in Beijing on Thursday. “China calls on relevant parties to take responsible measures and sought to solve the issue through diplomatic and political means.”

Trump demanded that all countries stop giving Iran access to exchange houses, cash transfers, swap lines, ship registries, front companies and avenues to smuggle out its oil. He also sought to rally other nations that have so far declined to support his war on the Islamic Republic, saying the US needs all its allies to help “isolate, and defeat, the Iran threat.”

The US got a boost on Tuesday, when the United Arab Emirates [severed economic ties](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/uae-cuts-economic-ties-with-iran-after-missiles-target-territory) with Iran after accusing it of firing two ballistic missiles at its territorial waters. Iran denied responsibility for the attack.

The UAE ranked as Iran’s top trade partner last year, with Tehran relying on the relationship for access to foreign goods and hard currency, followed by China, Turkey and India.

Trump’s aim is to force Iran into fresh negotiations meant to end the war for good, force Tehran to abandon its nuclear program and release its chokehold on the Strait of Hormuz. Those goals were spelled out in a memorandum of understanding the two sides signed in June, but later collapsed amid a flurry of tit-for-tat strikes.

Iran has weathered economic sanctions for decades, and its response has been to make its own demands, including the unfreezing of its assets, an end to sanctions and war damages.

Resolving the conflict is increasingly urgent for Trump as he contends with high gasoline prices, the war’s growing unpopularity and Republican concerns about losing one or both houses of Congress in November’s midterm elections. Earlier this week, he even threatened to bomb Oman, an American ally that has sometimes played the role of mediator, if it gets in the way of the US.

Iran’s threats against commercial shipping have stifled traffic through Hormuz, while a counter-blockade ordered by Trump has severely limited Iran’s own ability to export oil.

The war, which is approaching the six-month mark, and the blockade have spurred almost 80% inflation in Iran and its currency has lost almost 30% of its value against the dollar this year. Economists have warned the conflict risks dragging it into one of the worst economic crises in the history of the nation of 90 million people.
