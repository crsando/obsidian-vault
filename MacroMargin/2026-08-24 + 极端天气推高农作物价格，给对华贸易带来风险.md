### 中文译文

## 极端天气推高农作物价格，给对华贸易带来风险

极端天气正在削减美国收成并抬高农民价格，恰逢最大大宗商品进口国中国增加大豆采购，以履行两国领导人达成的承诺。

近日，印第安纳州和俄亥俄州遭遇强降雨，引发供应担忧，大豆和玉米价格上涨。如果大豆植株不能迅速干燥，暴雨可能致其死亡。伊利诺伊州创纪录的龙卷风季节等过度风暴也在热浪给谷物作物带来压力之际来袭。高温限制了作物产量，而此时美国收成本已预计低于去年的丰收水平。

本周的年度Pro Farmer作物考察发现，爱荷华州、伊利诺伊州、内布拉斯加州、印第安纳州、南达科他州和俄亥俄州的玉米产量滞后，数十名农民、交易员和分析师在泥泞中艰难跋涉清点作物。

“总体而言，中西部地区的玉米产量令所有人失望，”考察团作物巡视员Maxence Devillers表示，他是Argus的谷物分析师，同时也是法国农民。

美国的作物担忧出现之际，欧洲预计将迎来其最小的玉米收成——数据显示，欧盟玉米收成或创2007年以来最低——这将收紧全球粮食库存，同时乌克兰与俄罗斯之间的袭击使黑海小麦出口面临风险。

人们越来越希望，全球众多作物问题可能标志着农业市场的转折点。该市场一直面临低谷物和油籽价格与相对较高的种子、机械和设备成本之间的失衡。拖拉机制造商[迪尔公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/DE%20US%20Equity)本周[表示](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/deere-narrows-profit-outlook-as-farm-recovery-seen-in-2027)，形势正在企稳，尽管该公司指出要到2027年才会出现更明显的复苏。

但农作物价格上涨给包括中国在内的国家贸易带来风险。据白宫称，这个亚洲国家已同意每年购买2500万吨美国大豆，并额外增加170亿美元的农产品采购。

![Soybeans are unloaded from a grain cart during harvest in Wyanet, Illinois. Photographer: Daniel Acker/Bloomberg](https://assets.bwbx.io/images/users/iqjWHBFdfxIU/ipmeTD702qb4/v1/-1x-1.webp)

在伊利诺伊州怀内特收获期间，大豆从粮车上卸下。摄影师：Daniel Acker/彭博社

联邦出口数据显示，截至8月中旬，中国已购买约570万吨美国大豆。美国农业部上周五宣布向中国出售71.2万吨大豆，这是今年最大单日成交量，在9月唐纳德·特朗普总统与习近平主席会晤之前，购买步伐加快。

美国农业界希望两位领导人能讨论对华主要农作物出口问题。在特朗普政府加征关税的背景下，中国已越来越多地转向南美。

“他们并不是先找我们，而是去其他地方购买饲料所需，”艾奥瓦州大豆协会董事会候任主席李·布鲁克表示，他指出该协会正在较小国家寻找需求。

不过，他也表示希望特朗普的贸易政策最终能让农民受益。“我们将绕开目前对我们不利的一些因素，继续努力，”他说。

在内布拉斯加州，农场主布莱恩·尼尔森表示，影响今年作物的更广泛问题使他对自身在更高价格环境中的前景感到乐观。

“在农业中，要让我们的作物卖个好价钱，就得有人失败，这真的很可悲，”耕种600英亩的尼尔森说。“但我认为我那里的收成非常好，而且考虑到全国其他地区的情况，也许我们有望获得不错的价格。”

本周，芝加哥[玉米](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/C%201%20COMB%20Comdty)期货价格自2025年初以来首次突破每蒲式耳5美元，交易员正在考量这项已进入第34年的作物考察数据。

![A crop scout measures ear length on three corn samples in Nebraska on Aug. 17. Photographer: Erin Ailworth/Bloomberg](https://assets.bwbx.io/images/users/iqjWHBFdfxIU/ig0H396l9P9E/v1/-1x-1.webp)

8月17日，一名作物考察员在内布拉斯加州测量三个玉米样本的穗长。摄影：Erin Ailworth/彭博

根据作物考察数据，在美国头号玉米产区爱荷华州，单产预计将降至每英亩194蒲式耳。这低于一年前的198.4蒲式耳，而美国农业部（USDA）美国2026-27年各州玉米产量和单产（表格）中为每英亩216蒲式耳。全国来看，Pro Farmer的单产比美国农业部（USDA）的Pro Farmer估算美国玉米收成153.44亿蒲式耳低4.2%。

“我们在整个一周看到的是，作物潜力可观，但同时也存在更多变数，实际蒲式耳数可能不及表象，”作物考察西部路线负责人、电台节目[AgriTalk](https://www.agweb.com/agritalk)主持人奇普·弗洛里（Chip Flory）表示。

“在玉米方面，穗数偏低、籽粒长度不足以及田间表现不一，限制了许多地区的增产空间，”弗洛里补充道。“大豆显示出一些强劲潜力，但仍更依赖晚季天气和植株健康状况。”

对于遭遇洪水的州，可能需要数周时间才能了解损失的严重程度。

“有人遭遇了8到18英寸的降雨，”印第安纳州农民、美国农业部（USDA）部长布鲁克·罗林斯（Brooke Rollins）的特别顾问基普·汤姆（Kip Tom）说。汤姆并未参加此次考察，但一直在举行听取意见会议，以改进美国农业部的作物展望。“损失有多大？单产损失如何？有很多东西需要了解。”

此次考察是在美国农业部（USDA）8月份作物报告之后进行的，该报告在8月12日震惊市场，当时它[预测](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/corn-surges-as-usda-makes-bigger-than-expected-cut-to-us-yield)在夏季酷热天气下，玉米单产将低于预期的每英亩蒲式耳。

Pro Farmer的数据分析师艾米丽·卡罗兰（Emily Carolan）表示，高温只是给因降雨不合时宜而受压的玉米田雪上加霜。根据美国国家海洋和大气管理局（NOAA）的[数据](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/monitoring/us-maps/maps?maps=statewide-prcp-rank--1--202606)，伊利诺伊州经历了有记录以来第六多雨的6月。这些降水恰好在对玉米粒发育最关键的时刻浸透了玉米作物。

“我们在关键时期遭遇了水分饱和，”卡罗兰在考察夜场活动上对听众说，而天气在7月转为干燥，8月目前则成为伊利诺伊州最潮湿的月份之一。“简直是压力一个接一个。”

许多地区的玉米穗比一年前更短，平均粒数也更少。大豆长势较好，明尼苏达州的豆荚数多于一年前，而艾奥瓦州和伊利诺伊州等州的豆荚数虽低于2025年，但仍高于作物考察的三年平均水平。

当考察员们跋涉穿过田地时，他们遇到了正在祈祷的农民，希望天公作美，让庄稼获得最好的收成。

周三，尼克·戈斯拉尔和他的狗特里格开着皮卡车停下来，与正在艾奥瓦州西部检查他玉米和大豆田的考察员交谈。戈斯拉尔与儿子和女婿一起务农，他说预计产量将与去年持平，他形容去年的收成“还不错”。

“我希望今年的收成和去年差不多，我的银行家也是这样希望的。”戈斯拉尔谈到他目前的庄稼时说。

## Wild Weather Sends Crop Prices Rising in Risk to China Trade

Extreme weather is trimming US harvests and boosting prices for farmers just as top commodities importer China is increasing soybean purchases to meet a commitment struck between the two nations’ leaders.

Heavy rains have brought Soybeans and Corn Rise as Indiana Flooding Lifts Supply Concerns across Indiana and Ohio in recent days, which could kill off soy plants if they don’t dry out quickly. Excessive storms, including a record tornado season in Illinois, also come as heat waves stressed grain crops. The hot temperatures limited crop yields at a time when American harvests were already expected to be smaller than bumper crops from a year ago.

The annual Pro Farmer Crop Tour this week found lagging corn yields in Iowa, Illinois, Nebraska, Indiana, South Dakota and Ohio as dozens of farmers, traders and analysts trudged through slippery mud to count plants.

“Overall, corn yields were disappointing for everyone in the Midwest,” said tour crop scout Maxence Devillers, a grains analyst at Argus and also a French farmer.

The crop concerns in the US come as Europe is expected to reap its smallest corn harvest in EU’s Corn Crop Poised to Be Lowest Since 2007, Data Show, tightening global grain stocks even as attacks between Ukraine and Russia put wheat shipments out of the Black Sea at risk.

There’s mounting hope that a myriad of crop issues globally could signal a turning point for an agriculture market that’s been struggling with an imbalance between low grain and oilseed prices and comparatively high costs for seeds, machinery and equipment. Tractor maker [Deere & Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/DE%20US%20Equity) this week [said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/deere-narrows-profit-outlook-as-farm-recovery-seen-in-2027) conditions were stabilizing even as it pointed to 2027 for a more pronounced recovery.

But higher crop prices carry risks for trade with countries including China. According to the White House, the Asian nation has agreed to purchase 25 million tons of US soybeans annually along with $17 billion in additional agriculture purchases.

Soybeans are unloaded from a grain cart during harvest in Wyanet, Illinois. Photographer: Daniel Acker/Bloomberg

Federal export data shows China has purchased roughly 5.7 million tons of American soybeans as of mid-August. The US Department of Agriculture on Friday announced the sale of 712,000 tons to China, the largest daily amount this year as the buying pace ramps up ahead of a September meeting between presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping.

The US agriculture industry hopes the two leaders will discuss exports of key crops to China, which has increasingly turned to South America amid tariffs levied by the Trump administration.

“They’re not looking for us first, they’re going to other places to buy their needs for feed,” said Lee Brooke, president-elect of the Iowa Soybean Association board, who noted his organization is finding demand in smaller countries.

Still, he voiced hope that Trump’s trade policies will ultimately benefit farmers. “We’re going to work around some of the things that are not in our favor right now and just keep plugging away,” he said.

In Nebraska, producer Brian Nilson said the broader issues affecting this year’s crops made him optimistic about his own prospects in a higher-priced environment.

“In farming to get good prices for our crops someone else has to fail, which is really sad,” said Nilson, who farms 600 acres. “But I think I got really good crops out there, and with the way the rest of the country’s looking, maybe we got some hope for some decent prices.”

[Corn](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/C%201%20COMB%20Comdty) futures in Chicago this week topped $5 a bushel for the first time since early 2025 as traders considered data from the crop tour, now in its 34th year.

A crop scout measures ear length on three corn samples in Nebraska on Aug. 17. Photographer: Erin Ailworth/Bloomberg

In the top US corn state Iowa, the yield is estimated to fall to 194 bushels per acre, according to the crop tour data. That’s down from 198.4 a year ago and compares to the USDA’s US 2026-27 Corn Production and Yield by State (Table) for 216 bushels per acre. Nationally, Pro Farmer’s yield is 4.2% Pro Farmer Estimates US Corn Harvest at 15.344 Billion Bushels the USDA.

“What we saw throughout the week was a crop with solid potential, but also more variability and fewer bushels than appearances might suggest,” said Chip Flory, the leader of the western leg of the crop tour and host of the radio show [AgriTalk](https://www.agweb.com/agritalk).

“In corn, lower ear populations, shorter grain length and inconsistent field performance limited upside in a number of areas,” Flory added. “Soybeans showed pockets of strong potential but remain more dependent on late-season weather and plant health.”

For states with flooding, it could take weeks to know the extent of the damage.

“There’s folks that experienced anywhere from eight to 18 inches of rain,” said Kip Tom, an Indiana farmer and a special adviser to USDA Secretary Brooke Rollins. Tom was not on the tour but has been conducting listening sessions to improve the USDA’s crop outlooks. “How big is the loss, what are the yield losses? There’s a lot to learn.”

The tour follows the USDA’s August crop report that shocked the market Aug. 12 when it [projected](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/corn-surges-as-usda-makes-bigger-than-expected-cut-to-us-yield) a lower-than-expected corn yield of bushels per acre amid severe heat this summer.

Pro Farmer data analyst Emily Carolan said the hot temperatures just added to pressure put on corn fields by untimely rains. Illinois experienced its fifth wettest June on record, according to [data](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/monitoring/us-maps/maps?maps=statewide-prcp-rank--1--202606) from the National Oceanographic and Atmospheric Administration. That precipitation soaked corn crops at precisely the wrong moment for kernel development.

“We got saturated during a crucial time,” Carolan told an audience at a nightly tour event, with the weather then drying out in July, and August now shaping up as one of the wettest in Illinois. “It’s just stress after stress after stress.”

Ears of corn in many areas were shorter than a year ago, with fewer kernels on average. Soybean counts fared better, with more pods of beans on plants than a year ago in Minnesota while states such as Iowa and Illinois had lower counts compared with 2025 but still topped the crop tour’s three-year average.

Crop scout Wyatt Rollenhagen records survey data in a corn field in Nebraska on Aug. 17. Photographer: Erin Ailworth/Bloomberg

As scouts waded through fields, they encountered farmers praying for the conditions they need to give their crops the best possible finish.

Nick Goslar and his dog Trigger pulled up in a truck to chat with scouts inspecting his corn and soybean fields in western Iowa on Wednesday. Goslar, who farms with his son and son-in-law, said they are expecting yields to come in on par with their crops last year, which Goslar described as “good enough.”

“I’m hoping about the same, as is my banker,” Goslar said of his current crop.
