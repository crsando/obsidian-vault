## 全球债券暴跌将长期借贷成本推至数十年高位

长期债券正处在投资者对从[通胀](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/us-bond-selloff-drives-30-year-yields-to-the-highest-since-2007)到债台高筑的人工智能[繁荣](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything)等一系列问题担忧的风暴眼——而各国政府正在为此付出代价。

全球主权借贷利率正在飙升。本周，[30年期美国国债](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG30YR%20Index)收益率升至2007年以来最高，法国[借贷成本](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GFRN30%20%20%20Index)则触及2008年以来高位，德国同类债券收益率也达到2011年水平。英国[国债收益率](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GUKG30%20%20%20Index)逼近6%，日本30年期国债收益率也接近其1999年首次发行以来的最高水平。

尽管每个市场都有本国因素的作用，但推高收益率的结构性力量本质上是全球性的。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b1481de99fff65f7b4f8619/3c081cc9-0b14-81ea-8f82-df98a8a88fbb-bf506537b6ada80de4bc09a6c7b3d1fd.png)

一个担忧是，持续的地缘政治动荡将使经济体更容易遭受供给冲击和通胀压力。债券持有人还担心政府无法控制支出，从而给财政健康带来压力。与此同时，[市场结构](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-11/gilts-risk-more-turmoil-as-swift-sellers-replace-steady-buyers)和人口结构的变化导致曾经稳定的买家需求减弱。

圣詹姆斯广场的Justin Onuekwusi表示：“市场在说的是：我们预计未来会有更高的通胀，或至少更大的不确定性，因此我们要求长期债券提供更高的收益率。”

政府融资成本在全球债券收益率曲线上都在上升，但长期债务对财政问题最为敏感，因为其收益率通常最高。

这一担忧已足以让各国[将发债重点从长期债券转向较短期限](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-03/uk-and-japan-lead-global-government-exodus-from-long-bond-sales)。在英国，当局已停止大部分原定的长期债券发行。不过，政府的操作空间有限，因为他们正在适应这样一个世界：无法再以极低的利率锁定数十年的融资成本。

*彭博策略师观点：“有很多理由认为本十年收益率将结构性走高，但一个重要的不同在于不断上升的赤字如何融资。通常情况下，赤字在经济疲软时扩大，伴随政策利率下降，有助于缓冲债券市场。然而，如今的顺周期财政扩张意味着，在利率已经偏高的情况下，更多的政府借贷正推高收益率。”——宏观策略师Skylar Montgomery Koning。更多内容请点击：债务负担正成为债券面临的更大问题*

“很难说收益率达到什么水平才能让长期固定收益的总回报前景变得更好，”法国巴黎银行资产管理公司旗下AXA IM Core首席投资官克里斯·伊戈表示。

“唯一可能改变这一局面的因素是经济数据突然走弱或某种外部冲击。后者似乎比前者更可能出现。”

美国[30年期国债收益率](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG30YR%20Index)自6月底以来已攀升近40个基点，周二触及5.33%，为2007年年中以来的最高水平。在中期选举前，这对总统唐纳德·特朗普和财政部长斯科特·贝森特来说是个头疼的问题，高昂的政府融资成本正传导至企业和消费贷款。

“11月的选举可能带来更多政策风险，并且肯定会在常规预算季之前将市场注意力集中在财政问题上，”安盛的伊戈表示。“理想情况下，人们不希望带着不断上升的抵押贷款利率进入一个重要的选举期，即使利率仍低于2023年的水平。”

美国国债市场只是故事的一部分。一个[基准投资组合](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/I40608US%20Index)中投资级政府债券的平均收益率已飙升至近4.5%，为彭博自2015年汇编数据以来的最高水平。

今年，中东冲突导致的能源价格飙升重创了全球债务市场，这加剧了市场对美联储和其他央行将收紧货币政策的押注。

利率上升将加剧世界主要经济体面临的财政挑战。在美国，公共债务利息仍然是该国不断扩大的预算赤字的关键驱动因素。本财年迄今，利息总额已达1.17万亿美元，同比增长15%，部分原因是国债收益率走高。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b1481de99fff65f7b4f8619/3c081cc9-0b14-819f-a52a-e225daa48893-c9cbf81cdb0784a6b601e814a2b015d9.png)

但固定收益投资者面临的挑战在此之前就已存在，最近的价格走势表明，还有其他因素在推动长期收益率走高。

全球范围内，对长期限政府债券构成阻力的一大因素是[来自企业借款人的竞争](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything)。创纪录的债券发行速度为美国固定收益市场增加了大量久期供给，尤其是那些寻求为人工智能投资融资的科技公司，正试图以更长期限借款。

这些美国公司正越来越多地进入[海外债券市场](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/alphabet-hires-banks-for-inaugural-aussie-dollar-bond-sale)，其中一个例子是Alphabet Inc.决定发售其首笔澳元计价债券，规模为50亿澳元（36亿美元）。

供给挑战来临之际，买家基础正在发生变化。传统上，许多债券市场依赖于养老基金等对长久期资产的需求，这些基金寻求将此类证券与其负债相匹配。如今，许多提供方正逐渐放弃确定给付制，同时监管规定正[鼓励基金](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-05/dutch-pension-overhaul-to-fuel-pivot-from-long-bonds-within-eu)增加对股票的投资。

更广泛地说，随着政府债券发行量增加，各国更加依赖私人投资者。

美联储6月政策会议纪要显示，官员们听取了[简报](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260617.htm)，内容涉及美国国债所有权如何从“对价格相对不敏感的官方部门持有者转向对价格更敏感的私人投资者”，这可能会影响期限溢价——即投资者持有较长期债务所要求的额外收益率。

巴克莱银行(Barclays Plc)美国利率策略主管安舒尔·普拉丹(Anshul Pradhan)表示：“官方需求很大程度上是由政策目标驱动的”，而“私人投资者对回报更敏感”。他表示，过去十年买家结构的变化约贡献了30年期美国国债期限溢价的90个基点。

尽管美国每年近2万亿美元的赤字使国家债务濒临突破40万亿美元，但由埃德·亚德尼(Ed Yardeni)领导的亚德尼研究公司(Yardeni Research)策略师周二表示，目前还没有理由对美国债券市场感到恐慌。

“我们不会按下恐慌按钮，”这些策略师表示，“不过，我们正在密切关注债券义警是否会这么做。”

在欧洲，对政府借贷上升和持续通胀逆风的紧张情绪帮助将长期借贷成本推至多年高点。法国30年期国债收益率触及2008年以来最高，投资者将目光转向2027年预算谈判和明年的总统选举。

与此同时，随着德国融资需求增加，德国周二可能为其30年期债券的银团发行支付15年来最高的成本。在英国，7月底，首相安迪·伯纳姆表示将寻求财政灵活性，令投资者不安，导致英国国债受到冲击。

在日本，名义收益率水平仍低于同类国家，但其近期涨势一直持续。

尽管对物价压力的担忧推动了大部分债券抛售，但衡量市场对未来通胀预期的长期盈亏平衡利率在大多数主要市场中仍保持相对稳定。相反，借贷成本上升是由所谓的实际收益率驱动的，即投资者在通胀补偿之外额外要求的持有债券的补偿。

摩根资产管理投资组合经理凯尔西·贝罗表示，这种重新定价为新增资金提供了一个潜在的有吸引力的切入点。

“我们看到创造更多价值、我们更喜欢一点的领域是长期端，尤其是实际收益率，”她说。

## Global Bond Slump Sends Long-Term Borrowing Costs to Highest in Decades

Longer-maturity bonds are at the epicenter of investor angst about everything from [inflation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/us-bond-selloff-drives-30-year-yields-to-the-highest-since-2007) to the debt-laden artificial-intelligence [boom](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything) — and governments are paying the price.

Sovereign borrowing rates are surging around the world. Yields on [30-year US Treasuries](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG30YR%20Index) rose to the highest since 2007 this week, while French [borrowing costs](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GFRN30%20%20%20Index) hit the loftiest since 2008 and their German peers traded at 2011 levels. Equivalent [gilt yields](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/GUKG30%20%20%20Index) are approaching 6% in the UK and similar-maturity Japanese ones are close to their Japan’s 30-Year Bond Yield Rises to Highest Since 1999 Debut.

While domestic factors have a role in each market, the structural forces driving up yields are global in nature.

One fear is that persistent geopolitical turmoil will make economies more prone to supply shocks and inflationary pressures. Bondholders are also worried that governments will fail to rein in spending, pressuring fiscal health. Meanwhile,[changes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-07-11/gilts-risk-more-turmoil-as-swift-sellers-replace-steady-buyers) in market structure and demographics are resulting in waning demand from once-steady buyers.

“What the market is saying is: we expect greater inflation or at least greater uncertainty in the future and therefore we demand a higher yield for longer bonds,” said Justin Onuekwusi at St James’s Place.

Government costs are rising across global bond curves, but long-maturity debt is most sensitive to fiscal matters given its yield is most often the highest.

The concern is significant enough for nations to [skew sales away](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-03/uk-and-japan-lead-global-government-exodus-from-long-bond-sales) from longer-dated bonds toward shorter tenors. In the UK, authorities have ceased most scheduled long-dated sales. Governments have only limited room for maneuver, though, as they adjust to a world where they can no longer lock in financing costs for decades at rock-bottom rates.

*What Bloomberg Strategists Say: “There are many reasons to think yields will be structurally higher this decade, but one important difference is how rising deficits are being financed. Typically, deficits expand when the economy weakens, accompanied by lower policy rates that help insulate the bond market. However, today’s procyclical fiscal expansion means more government borrowing is pushing yields higher when rates are already elevated.” — Skylar Montgomery Koning, macro strategist. For more, click Debt Loads Are Becoming a Much Bigger Problem for Bonds*

“It is hard to know what level of yield would make the outlook for total returns from long duration fixed income better,” said Chris Iggo, chief investment officer at AXA IM Core at BNP Paribas Asset Management.

“The only thing which might change that is a sudden weakening in economic data or some kind of external shock. The latter appears more likely than the former.”

US [30-year yields](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/USGG30YR%20Index) have climbed almost 40 basis points since the end of June to touch 5.33% on Tuesday, the highest level since mid-2007. That’s a headache for President Donald Trump and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent ahead of midterm elections, with lofty government financing costs feeding through into corporate and consumer loans.

“November’s elections could bring forth more policy risk and will certainly focus market attention on fiscal matters ahead of the usual budget season,” AXA’s Iggo said. “Ideally, one would not want to be going into an important election period with mortgage rates rising, even if they remain lower than they were in 2023.”

The US Treasury market is just one part of the story. The average yield on a [benchmark portfolio](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/I40608US%20Index) of investment-grade government bonds has surged to almost 4.5%, the highest in data compiled by Bloomberg going back to 2015.

Global debt markets have been battered this year by surging energy prices caused by the conflict in the Middle East, which has fueled bets the Federal Reserve and other central banks will tighten monetary policy.

Rising interest rates will add to the challenging fiscal backdrop of the world’s major economies. In the US, interest on the public debt continues to be a key driver of the nation’s growing budget deficit. For the fiscal year to date, the tally is $1.17 trillion — a 15% increase, thanks in part to higher yields on Treasuries.

But the challenge for fixed-income investors predates that, and recent price action suggests other factors are helping to drive long-dated yields higher.

One element acting as a headwind for longer-maturity government debt globally is [competition from corporate borrowers](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/ai-is-driving-up-treasury-yields-it-just-touches-everything). A record pace of bond issuance has added substantial duration supply to US fixed-income markets, especially as tech firms looking to finance AI investment seek to borrow at longer maturities.

These US firms are increasingly tapping [overseas bond markets](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/alphabet-hires-banks-for-inaugural-aussie-dollar-bond-sale), with one example being Alphabet Inc.’s decision to market a debut Australian dollar debt issue of A$5 billion ($3.6 billion).

The supply challenge comes just as the buyer base is shifting. Traditionally, many bond markets were supported by demand for long-dated assets from the likes of pension funds seeking to match such securities against their liabilities. Nowadays, many providers are moving away from defined-benefit systems, while regulations are [encouraging funds](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-05/dutch-pension-overhaul-to-fuel-pivot-from-long-bonds-within-eu) to invest more in stocks.

More broadly, as government bond issuance ramped up, nations have leaned more on private investors.

Minutes from the Fed’s June policy meeting show officials were [briefed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260617.htm) on how ownership of Treasuries was shifting from “relatively price-insensitive official-sector holders to more price-sensitive private investors,” which could impact the term premium — or the extra yield investors demand to hold longer-dated debt.

“Official demand is driven largely by policy objectives” while “private investors are more return-sensitive,” said Anshul Pradhan, head of US rates strategy at Barclays Plc. This change in buyers over the last decade is responsible for about 90 basis points of the term premium on 30-year US Treasuries, he said.

Although the US’s nearly $2 trillion annual deficits leave the national debt on the brink of passing $40 trillion, strategists at Yardeni Research led by Ed Yardeni said Tuesday there’s no reason to panic on the US bond market just yet.

“We aren’t pushing the panic button,” the strategists said. “However, we are closely monitoring whether the bond vigilantes might do so.”

In Europe, nerves over elevated government borrowing and persistent inflation headwinds have helped push long-term borrowing costs to multi-year highs. France’s 30-year bond yield hit the highest since 2008, with investors turning their attention to the 2027 budget negotiations and next year’s presidential election.

Meanwhile Germany could pay the most in 15 years in a syndicated bond sale of 30-year debt on Tuesday, as the nation’s financing needs increase. In the UK, gilts were rattled in late July after Prime Minister Andy Burnham unnerved investors by saying he would seek fiscal flexibility.

In Japan, outright yield levels remain below their peers but their recent increase has been relentless.

While concern over price pressures has driven much of the bond selloff, long-dated break-even rates — which measure market expectations for future inflation — have remained relatively well anchored in most major markets. Instead, the rise in borrowing costs has been driven by so-called real yields, or the extra compensation investors demand on top of inflation to hold bonds.

This repricing offers a potentially attractive entry point for fresh cash, according to Kelsey Berro, a portfolio manager at JPMorgan Asset Management.

“Where we have seen more value created and that we like a little bit better would be the long end, particularly in real yields,” she said.
