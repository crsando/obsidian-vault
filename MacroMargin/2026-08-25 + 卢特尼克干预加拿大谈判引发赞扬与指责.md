> • 谈判破裂源于卢特尼克认为框架协议对加拿大过于慷慨，并维护美国金属生产商利益。
> • 卢特尼克与贸易鹰派纳瓦罗批评美国贸易代表格里尔与加拿大达成的框架，导致谈判陷入僵局。
> • 美国钢铁和铝行业向政府施压，成功说服部分官员收回对加拿大的关税减免承诺。

> • 加拿大总理卡尼称美国要求过分，加拿大立场团结；美国官员则指责加拿大在最后时刻提出额外要求。

### 中文译文

## 卢特尼克干预加拿大谈判引发赞扬与指责

据知情人士透露，在美国商务部长霍华德·卢特尼克最后一刻施压后，美加贸易谈判破裂，总统唐纳德·特朗普随即征收新关税，加拿大总理马克·卡尼则誓言报复。

随着两国迅速陷入全面贸易战，卢特尼克在最后一刻谈判中的角色成为焦点。知情人士称，卢特尼克认为框架协议对加拿大过于慷慨，并成为美国金属生产商的捍卫者，这些生产商希望收回已做出的让步。上述人士因讨论谈判内容而要求匿名。

一些人表示，加方指责他改变谈判条件，而一些商界领袖则称赞卢特尼克破坏了他们认为会对特朗普誓言保护的美国金属产业构成威胁的协议。

上周二，特朗普宣布协议即将达成，并推迟对一系列加拿大商品征收50%的关税。然而，随着卢特尼克和主张关税的贸易鹰派彼得·纳瓦罗批评美国贸易代表贾米森·格里尔与加拿大数周谈判所达成的框架，双方已形成对峙。

卢特尼克在最后一刻的介入使他更深地卷入谈判，这标志着他作为特朗普政府内部破坏性力量的最新例证——他经常抓住那些他认为美国可以榨取更多筹码的贸易议题。

一位白宫官员驳斥了卢特尼克破坏谈判的说法，坚称谈判破裂是因为加拿大在最后时刻提出了超出商定讨论范围的要求。该官员补充说，虽然美国官员之间可能就关税减免幅度存在争论，但卢特尼克和格里尔立场一致，都在特朗普领导下工作。

一位加拿大官员表示，虽然卢特尼克的介入毫无帮助，而且是谈判破裂的主要促成因素，但他的到来并非症结所在。该官员表示，在最后几天，美国的目标存在根本性问题。

美国贸易代表办公室和商务部未回应就谈判发表评论的请求。加拿大总理办公室也未有回应。

卢特尼克去年在汽车和钢铁关税问题上反复变化的公开表态加剧了市场波动。他与日本和韩国达成的协议——两国承诺数千亿美元投资以换取关税减免——最初[遭到谨慎对待](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-28/south-korea-unable-to-pay-350-billion-in-cash-under-trade-pact)，并持续[面临质疑](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-13/us-japan-see-big-gap-over-first-projects-for-550-billion-fund)，尽管一些[重大项目](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-17/how-tariff-threats-got-japan-south-korea-to-promise-billions-in-us-investments)已经公布。

尤其是在加拿大问题上，卢特尼克有时扮演了搅局者的角色，包括推迟戈迪·豪国际大桥的预定开通时间，这些举动在加拿大被视为一种敲诈企图。

美国官员将僵局归咎于加拿大同行。“美国贸易代表办公室在谈判中做得很好。加拿大没有接受协议是非常愚蠢的，”纳瓦罗说。“情况不会好转。卡尼选择了政治而非加拿大人民。”

在上周二特朗普与卡尼通话后，美国总统大肆宣扬重启Keystone XL输油管道的前景，并公开为框架背书，将谈判延长三天以完成收尾。然而，紧张局势仍在加剧。

![Jamieson Greer, center Photographer: Bryon Houlgrave/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b1481219edbc28f74a22dd0/3c781cc9-0b14-81ba-9e21-e9877eee8e2c-7d47953a612c348cbcff57db6ff090e6.png)

美国钢铁和铝行业通常是特朗普的坚定盟友，他们感到愤怒，称他们被一项将削弱其行业和利润率关税壁垒的提议打了个措手不及，知情人士说。

这些行业向格里尔办公室施压。一些人转向卢特尼克和国会中的盟友寻求帮助。知情人士描述，为了警告政府不要采取此举，电话和紧急会议如潮水般涌来。

美国国内铝挤压企业将原铝加工成增值产品，它们告诉政府官员和关键议员，铝关税的大范围减免可能毁掉它们的利润率。美国繁荣联盟强调，铝挤压企业对支持美国国内铝金属生产至关重要。

卢特尼克和格里尔在政府内部展现出截然不同的形象。格里尔被认为本质上是一位克制、注重细节的贸易律师；而卢特尼克则更像特朗普那样，性格鲜明、直言不讳。两人在实施特朗普贸易政策中都扮演关键角色。格里尔主导了与加拿大的谈判，而卢特尼克的商务部职权范围包括针对工业部门的232条款关税。

虽然一些知情人士淡化了这一分歧，称两位官员在特朗普的领导下团结一致，但这种动态导致了某种双轨谈判。在谈判的最后几天，卢特尼克多次直接与卡尼交谈，尽管格里尔一直是加拿大代表团的主要联络人。

![Mark Carney Photographer: Kelly Clark/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b1481219edbc28f74a22dd0/3c781cc9-0b14-814f-b934-ccc8356eca7c-3caf3a3b82e5ecf79bc97a2d0b897053.png)

加拿大驻美国大使马克·怀斯曼拒绝讨论卢特尼克对谈判的影响。“你得问问美国人在幕后是如何运作的，”他在周日接受采访时说。“我们在讨论中的对手是美国贸易代表办公室。”

与此同时，卡尼自己也面临着与各省省长的分歧，他需要这些省长解除对美国酒类销售的禁令才能达成任何协议。尤其是马尼托巴省省长瓦布·基纽，他公开批评了这项临时协议。

格里尔周一在CNBC上承认，加拿大寻求他无法单方面提供的保证，但称他与卢特尼克存在分歧的报道是“障眼法”。不过，他承认“加拿大人可能会因我们的关税权限分属两个不同机构而感到沮丧，但这是国会如此设置的。”

随着讨价还价的继续，条款发生了变化。到了周五上午，对铝和钢实施大幅直接关税削减的前景，已被推动更有限、更细致化减免的努力所取代。行业施压活动已说服一些官员收回计划给予加拿大的让步，该让步本将把一定配额内加拿大金属进口的关税从50%降至25%，同时削减部分衍生产品的关税。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b1481219edbc28f74a22dd0/3c781cc9-0b14-8126-8a11-cb9f3afe33c8-dcecb48ccc43d80f328e205b6b12b6ae.png)

随着谈判继续，美国告诉一些利益相关方，希望他们公开谈论协议的好处，一位知情人士说。截至周五下午，美国和加拿大都发出信号，称官员们相信他们有望敲定一切，一些知情人士说。截至周五晚上，加方认为他们已达成协议。

卢特尼克专注于保留汽车行业让步的范围。尽管加拿大认为两国同意将汽车关税从25%降至15%，但美国表示这一降幅仅适用于轻型车辆，不适用于中型和重型卡车。这一区别本将危及通用汽车公司和福特汽车公司在安大略省的工厂。

目前尚不清楚卢特尼克的目标是破坏该协议还是重塑它。一位人士表示，卢特尼克和纳瓦罗似乎在押注加拿大会接受这些条款而不会退出。但卡尼表示，美国的要求太过分。

美国钢铁和铝业公司私下对谈判破裂感到满意。

“这些谈判的破裂应终结削弱232条款关税的提议，”美国繁荣联盟主席乔恩·图米表示。

包括商业圆桌会议和Autos Drive America在内的其他组织，代表在美国经营的外国公司，呼吁恢复谈判。目前几乎没有立即重启谈判的迹象。

卡尼周六发表讲话，回避了关于勒特尼克是否扼杀了协议的问题。

“这是美国的问题，”他说。卡尼称加拿大的立场“团结一致”，并补充说：“你不能对美国行政当局这么说。”

## Lutnick’s Intervention in Canada Talks Draws Praise, Blame

US-Canada trade talks collapsed after last-minute pressure from Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, according to people familiar with the matter, leaving President Donald Trump to impose new tariffs and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney vowing retaliation.

With the countries spiraling into a full-blown trade war, the spotlight is on Lutnick’s role in the last-minute talks. Lutnick viewed a framework deal as too generous to Canada and emerged as a champion for US metal producers who wanted to claw back concessions, the people said, speaking anonymously to discuss the negotiations.

The Canadians blame him for moving the goalposts, some of the people said, while some business leaders credit Lutnick with derailing what they saw as a threat to US metals industries Trump has vowed to protect.

Trump last Tuesday announced a deal was in sight and delayed imposing 50% tariffs on a range of Canadian goods. Battle lines, however, had formed as Lutnick and Peter Navarro, a pro-tariff trade hawk, criticized the framework from US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer’s weeks of talks with Canada.

Lutnick’s 11th-hour intervention, taking him deeper into the talks, marked the latest example of how he’s functioned as a disruptive force inside Trump’s administration, often seizing on trade files where he senses the US can squeeze more leverage.

A White House official disputed any notion Lutnick derailed the talks, insisting instead that they imploded after Canada made last-minute demands outside the agreed scope of discussions. The official added that while there was likely debate among US officials about the magnitude of tariff relief, Lutnick and Greer are aligned and working under Trump.

While Lutnick was unhelpful and a major contributor to the breakdown of talks, his arrival wasn’t the cause, according to a Canadian official. The official said there were fundamental problems with the US objectives over the final days.

USTR and the Commerce Department didn’t respond to requests for comment on the negotiations. The Canadian prime minister’s office also didn’t respond.

Lutnick’s shifting public comments on auto and steel levies last year fueled market volatility. Deals he helped strike with Japan and South Korea — which saw the countries pledge hundreds of billions in investments in exchange for tariff relief — were initially [met with caution](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-28/south-korea-unable-to-pay-350-billion-in-cash-under-trade-pact) and have continued to [face questions](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-13/us-japan-see-big-gap-over-first-projects-for-550-billion-fund), though some [major projects](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-17/how-tariff-threats-got-japan-south-korea-to-promise-billions-in-us-investments) have been announced.

With Canada in particular, Lutnick has sometimes played the role of spoiler, including helping delay the planned opening of the Gordie Howe International Bridge, actions viewed in Canada as an attempted shakedown.

US officials have blamed Canadian counterparts for the impasse. “The USTR did a fine job negotiating. Canada was very foolish not to take the deal,” Navarro said. “It won’t get better. Carney chose politics over the Canadian people.”

After a conversation between Trump and Carney last Tuesday, the US president touted the prospect of resurrecting the Keystone XL oil pipeline and blessed the framework publicly, extending talks by three days to tie things up. However, tensions continued to mount.

The US steel and aluminum sectors, typically staunch Trump allies, were outraged, saying they were caught off guard by a proposal that would weaken the tariff wall around their businesses and profit margins, the people said.

The industries pressured Greer’s office. Some turned to Lutnick and allies in Congress for help. People familiar with the matter described a torrent of calls and frenzied meetings as they sought to warn the administration against the move.

Domestic aluminum extruders, which shape raw aluminum into value-added goods, told administration officials and key lawmakers that widespread discounts on aluminum tariffs could destroy their profit margin. The Coalition for a Prosperous America emphasized that aluminum extruders are critical to support domestic production of the metal itself.

Lutnick and Greer cut vastly different figures within the administration. Greer is seen as a restrained, details-focused trade lawyer at heart; Lutnick a more colorful, outspoken figure, like Trump. Both have key roles implementing Trump’s trade policy. While Greer led the talks with Canada, Lutnick’s Commerce portfolio includes the Section 232 tariffs targeting industrial sectors.

While some people familiar downplayed the divide, saying the two officials are united under Trump, the dynamic led to something of a dual-track negotiation. Lutnick spoke directly with Carney repeatedly in the final days of negotiations even though Greer had been the Canadian delegation’s point person.

Canadian Ambassador to the US Mark Wiseman declined to discuss Lutnick’s impact on negotiations. “You’d have to ask the Americans how they were operating behind the scenes,” he said in an interview Sunday. “Our counterparty in our discussions was USTR.”

Meanwhile, Carney faced his own divisions with the provincial premiers he would need to lift bans on US alcohol sales to seal any deal. One premier in particular, Manitoba’s Wab Kinew, had publicly criticized the tentative agreement.

Greer acknowledged Monday on CNBC that Canada sought guarantees he couldn’t unilaterally give, but called reports of a divide between him and Lutnick “a red herring.” Still, he admitted “it could be frustrating for Canadians that we have tariff authorities in two different agencies, but that’s how Congress set it up.”

As the haggling continued, the terms shifted. By Friday morning, the prospect of largely straight tariff reductions on aluminum and steel had been replaced by a push for more limited, nuanced relief. The industry pressure campaign had persuaded some officials to dial back a planned concession to Canada that would have lowered the tariff to 25% from 50% on a quota of Canadian metal imports, alongside reductions for some derivative products.

As talks continued, the US told some stakeholders it would appreciate them speaking publicly about the benefits of a deal, one of the people said. Both the US and Canada were signaling as of Friday afternoon that officials believed they were on track to finalize everything, some of the people said. As of Friday night, the Canadians thought they had a deal.

Lutnick focused on preserving the scope of the auto-sector concession. While Canada thought the countries agreed to lower vehicle tariffs to 15% from 25% the US said the reduction applied only to light vehicles, not medium- and heavy-duty trucks. The distinction would have imperiled a General Motors Co. plant and a Ford Motor Co. plant in Ontario.

It’s unclear whether Lutnick’s goal was to derail the pact or reshape it. One person said it appeared Lutnick and Navarro were betting that Canada would accept the terms and not walk away. But Carney said the US demands were too much.

US steel and aluminum companies are privately pleased with the collapse.

“The breakdown of these negotiations should put an end to proposals to weaken” Section 232 tariffs, said Jon Toomey, president of the Coalition for a Prosperous America.

Other groups, including Business Roundtable and Autos Drive America, representing foreign companies that do business in the US, called for a return to talks. There’s little sign of an immediate revival of negotiations.

Carney, speaking Saturday, sidestepped a question on whether Lutnick killed a deal.

“It’s a question for the US,” he said. Carney called the Canadian approach “unified,” adding: “You cannot say that about the United States administration.”
