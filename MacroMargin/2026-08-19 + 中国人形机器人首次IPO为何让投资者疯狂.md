### 中文译文

## 中国人形机器人首次IPO为何让投资者疯狂

### **要点**

- 中国投资者今年已向机器人领域投入超过1000亿元人民币，押注中国能在机器人与人工智能的融合中占据主导地位。
- 宇树科技是中国最大的人形机器人和四足机器人制造商之一，在产量和实际部署方面已领先于美国竞争对手，其股价在中国内地首个人形机器人制造商上市后上涨了629%。
- 中国将人形机器人视为一个未来产业，预计到本十年末将取得革命性突破，北京决心利用这项技术确保自身处于先进制造业的前沿。
为像人类一样移动、工作和行动而制造的机器人。这是一个全球性产业，摩根士丹利[估计](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/humanoid-robot-market-5-trillion-by-2050)，到2050年其价值可能达到5万亿美元。这也是中美争夺技术主导权的前沿领域之一。

从国家支持的基金到中国科技巨头和汽车制造商，中国投资者今年已向该行业[投入](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/china-sends-robots-out-into-the-world-to-learn-how-to-be-human)超过1000亿元人民币（148亿美元）。他们押注中国能在机器人与人工智能的融合中占据主导地位，使机器能够理解并与物理世界互动。像宇树科技这样的中国人形机器人制造商，在产量和实际部署上已领先于美国竞争对手，这些对手包括埃隆·马斯克的[特斯拉公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSLA%20US%20Equity)以及[Figure AI公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2265591D:US)等初创企业。

在周三成为中国大陆首家上市的人形机器人制造商后，投资者争相抢购宇树科技的股票，推动其股价上涨[629%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/unitree-robotics-set-to-debut-after-904-million-shanghai-ipo)。

### **宇树科技是什么？它做什么？**

官方名称为[宇树科技](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688836%20CH%20Equity)，Unitree是中国最大的人形和四足机器人制造商之一，开发能够奔跑、跳跃、穿越复杂地形并完成工业任务的机器。十年前，大学毕业生王兴兴创立这家公司时，专注于开发四足机器人，即机器狗。后来，公司扩展到人形机器人，于2023年发布了其首款型号H1。

目前，宇树将大部分机器人销售给高校、科研院所和企业，主要用于开发和测试。2025年，宇树向国内外客户销售了超过5500台人形机器人和超过33000台四足机器人。其最便宜的机器人[R1 Air](https://www.unitree.com/R1)售价约为4900美元——远低于市场上的其他产品。

最近，这家公司通过在一年一度的中国春节联欢晚会（相当于超级碗中场秀）上的高调亮相，获得了研究界以外的认可。2025年，其16台人形机器人与人类舞者一起表演了同步民间舞蹈。今年，几台宇树机器人[表演](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-20/chinese-startup-s-kung-fu-robots-fire-up-tech-wary-investors)了模仿成龙标志性的*《醉拳》*风格的功夫特技，身体灵活性显著提高。

![An advertisement for Unitree in Shanghai. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c181cc90b1481c2a919e8a466e06037/3c181cc9-0b14-81e0-93c6-eb74128bc8d9-f26c4e89ad73a6443d18d4d61cd22a9f.png)

### **外界对宇树的关注度有多高？**

宇树的上市吸引了散户投资者的巨大需求，他们认购量达到可供发售股份的5500倍以上，订单总额达8.1万亿元人民币（约1.2万亿美元），[据彭博计算](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/unitree-s-shanghai-ipo-5-526-times-subscribed-by-retail-buyers)。这超过了内存芯片制造商[长鑫存储](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity)在7月首次公开募股中创下的7.1万亿元人民币散户订单纪录。

此次IPO还因有战略投资者支持而备受关注。约20%的发行股份分配给了由AI初创公司DeepSeek、[腾讯控股有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity)旗下一家实体以及[中国石油天然气集团有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNPZ%20CH%20Equity)、[中国南方电网有限责任公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SOPOWZ%20CH%20Equity)和[中国电信股份有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/728%20HK%20Equity)等大型央企投资部门组成的集团。

宇树将借助DeepSeek的AI实力，为其人形机器人开发具身智能模型。与腾讯关联的投资者也同意在这类努力上进行合作。这些国有企业将推动宇树的机器人在油田、炼油厂和电力系统等领域获得更多实际应用。没有一家战略投资者提供他们计划购买多少台机器人以及何时购买的具体细节。

### **中国为何大力押注人形机器人？**

北京将仿人机器人视为未来产业之一，预计到本十年末将实现革命性突破。中国和美国的仿人机器人初创公司都认同这一愿景，他们坚信机器人智能模型的发展距离“ChatGPT时刻”只需数年，届时仿人机器人将足够聪明，能够理解物理世界并相应执行人类的日常任务。

北京决心利用这项技术确保其在先进制造业——从汽车到消费电子——的前沿地位，这一领域长期由德国和日本等国家主导。政府已发起一项运动，扩大仿人机器人在制造业供应链中的部署，目标是在今年年底前让超过1万台机器人进入各类工厂工作。

![A woman sits next to a humanoid robot ahead of the World AI Conference in July 2026. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c181cc90b1481c2a919e8a466e06037/3c181cc9-0b14-811f-86f7-c95868f104b3-97bb0921ae1ede11032ae3932714ce1a.png)

中国加倍投入开发此类技术的原因之一是预计未来几十年将出现劳动力短缺。该国生育率持续下降以及Z世代工人对单调乏味的工厂工作日益抵触的态度，增强了仿人机器人在某些工作场所的应用理由。

### **仿人机器人产业有多先进？**

近年来，仿人机器人技术已变得更加精密。最新的机器可以执行拧螺丝、叠衣服和煮咖啡等任务，并且正在物流中心、汽车工厂和电子装配线上进行测试——在某些情况下已有限部署。

然而，尽管市场炒作和展示类人机器人的病毒视频层出不穷，或各种精心设计的演示，该行业仍处于起步阶段。制造具备在工厂装配线上可靠工作、执行家务或在医院协助老年人所需的灵活性、平衡性和可靠性的机器人，仍然是一项艰巨的工程挑战。

该行业最大的障碍之一是复制人手的能力，使其能像人手一样抓握、操纵并有效使用为人类设计的工具。工程师必须将电机、关节和类似肌腱的线缆塞入有限的空间，同时保持机器轻便并防止过热。

另一个挑战是开发控制仿人机器人的“大脑”——即AI模型。这类模型将视觉信息与语言提示相结合，使仿人机器人——甚至例如单个机械臂——能够在没有每一步都明确预编程的情况下执行任务。然而，让这些机器实现真正自主操作而非编排或脚本化演示的机器人大脑，仍处于很大的发展阶段。

### **中国的人形机器人技术与美国相比如何？**

目前，全球约97%的人形机器人销售额来自中国供应商，[据](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/china-humanoid-makers-hold-97-of-global-shipments-report-says) Smart Analytics Global 的数据显示。虽然 Unitree、AgiBot 和 [UBTech Robotics Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9880%20HK%20Equity) 正在加大人形机器人的销售，但特斯拉（Tesla）、Figure AI 和 [Agility Robotics Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1585639D:US) 等美国公司的销量仅为中国竞争对手所报告销量的一小部分。

![A student trains a humanoid robot to pick strawberries in the Future Learning Center at Zhejiang University in Hangzhou. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c181cc90b1481c2a919e8a466e06037/3c181cc9-0b14-819d-bc8c-d9e46e7e7990-0235986259c88d5ee51b126377b9c5b6.png)

中国机器人制造商正借助中国庞大的制造供应链，像 Unitree 这样的公司自己制造许多组件，或在本地采购。在深圳，许多初创公司宣称，组装机器人所需的大部分硬件都可以在附近采购，使他们能够快速开发新技术。另一方面，他们在美国的竞争对手往往需要等待数周甚至数月，才能从海外供应商处获得专用组件。

中国也在更快地推动人形机器人投入使用。今年，领先的中国制造商已将工厂中的机器人部署从几十台扩展到数百台甚至数千台，并利用实际经验改进技术。

## Why China’s First Humanoid Robot IPO Has Investors in a Frenzy

### **Takeaways**

- Chinese investors have poured more than 100 billion yuan into the robot sector this year, betting that China can dominate the convergence of robots and artificial intelligence.
- Unitree, one of China's biggest makers of humanoid and quadruped robots, has taken an early lead over US rivals in production volumes and real-world deployment, with its shares rising 629% after it became mainland China's first publicly traded humanoid robot maker.
- China views humanoid robots as a futuristic industry that will see a revolutionary breakthrough by the end of this decade, with Beijing determined to use this technology to ensure it is at the forefront of advanced manufacturing.
Robots built to move, work and act like humans. It’s a global industry Morgan Stanley [estimates](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/humanoid-robot-market-5-trillion-by-2050) could be worth $5 trillion by 2050. It’s also one of the frontiers in the China-US race for technological supremacy.

Chinese investors, ranging from state-backed funds to the country’s tech titans and automakers, have [poured](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/china-sends-robots-out-into-the-world-to-learn-how-to-be-human) more than 100 billion yuan ($14.8 billion) into the sector this year. They’re betting that China can dominate the** **convergence of robots and artificial intelligence, enabling machines to understand and interact with the physical world. Chinese humanoid makers like Unitree have already taken an early lead over US rivals ranging from Elon Musk’s [Tesla Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/TSLA%20US%20Equity) to startups such as [Figure AI Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/2265591D:US) in production volumes and real-world deployment.

Investors are scrambling for a piece of Unitree after it became mainland China’s first publicly traded humanoid robot maker on Wednesday, sending shares up [629%](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/unitree-robotics-set-to-debut-after-904-million-shanghai-ipo).

### **What is Unitree and what does it do?**

Officially known as [Yushu Technology Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688836%20CH%20Equity), Unitree is one of China’s biggest makers of** **humanoid and quadruped robots, developing machines** **that can run, jump, navigate difficult terrain and complete industrial tasks. When it was founded a decade ago by university graduate Wang Xingxing, its focus was on developing quadrupeds, or robot dogs. It later** **expanded into humanoids, releasing its first model, H1, in 2023.

For now, Unitree sells most of its robots to universities, research institutes and companies, where they are largely used for development and testing purposes. In 2025, Unitree sold more than 5,500 humanoid robots and more than 33,000 quadrupeds to customers in China and abroad. Its cheapest robot, the [R1 Air](https://www.unitree.com/R1), costs about $4,900 — far less than others on the market.

More recently, the company has gained recognition beyond the research world through high-profile appearances at China’s annual Spring Festival Gala, the Chinese equivalent of the Super Bowl halftime show. In 2025, 16 of its humanoid robots performed a synchronized folk dance alongside human dancers. This year, several Unitree robots [performed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-20/chinese-startup-s-kung-fu-robots-fire-up-tech-wary-investors) kung fu stunts mimicking Jackie Chan’s iconic *Drunken Master *style, with noticeable improved physical dexterity.

### **How much interest has there been in Unitree?**

Unitree’s listing drew enormous demand from retail investors, who subscribed for more than 5,500 the number of shares available, placing orders worth 8.1 trillion yuan ($1.2 trillion), [according](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/unitree-s-shanghai-ipo-5-526-times-subscribed-by-retail-buyers) to Bloomberg calculations. That surpassed the 7.1 trillion yuan retail order book for memory-chip maker [CXMT Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity)’s debut in July.

The IPO also drew attention because of the strategic investors backing the company. About 20% of the offering was allocated to a group composed of AI startup DeepSeek, a vehicle affiliated with [Tencent Holdings Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/700%20HK%20Equity) and investment arms of major state-owned enterprises [China National Petroleum Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/CNPZ%20CH%20Equity), [China Southern Power Grid Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/SOPOWZ%20CH%20Equity) and [China Telecom Corp](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/728%20HK%20Equity).

Unitree will draw on DeepSeek’s AI strength to develop embodied-intelligence models for its humanoid robots. Tencent-linked investors also agreed to collaborate on similar endeavors. The state-owned firms will drive more real-world applications of Unitree’s robots across oil fields, refineries and power systems. None of the strategic investors offered details of how many robots they’ll buy or when they’ll make their purchases.

### **Why is China betting big on humanoid robots?**

Beijing views humanoid robots as one of the futuristic industries that will see a revolutionary breakthrough by the end of this decade. It’s a vision shared by humanoid startups in both China and the US, which are adamant that the development of robotic intelligence models are just years away from a “ChatGPT moment,” where humanoid robots will become smart enough to understand the physical world and carry out everyday, human tasks accordingly.

Beijing is determined to use this technology to ensure it is at the forefront of advanced manufacturing — from cars to consumer electronics — that has long been dominated by countries such as Germany and Japan. The government has launched a campaign to scale up humanoid deployment in** **manufacturing supply chains, with a target of sending more than 10,000 robots to work at various factories by the end of this year.

Part of the reason China is doubling down on developing such technology is because of an expected labor shortage in the decades to come. A sustained fall in the country’s fertility rate as well as an increasingly intolerant attitude toward monotonous factory jobs by Gen Z workers has bolstered the case for humanoids in certain workplace settings.

### **How advanced is the humanoid robotics industry?**

Humanoid robot technology has become much more sophisticated in recent years. The latest machines can perform tasks such as screwing nails, folding laundry and making coffee, and they are being tested — and in some cases deployed on a limited basis — in logistic hubs, car factories and electronics assembly lines.

Yet for all the market hype and viral videos showing human-like machines dancing or other elaborate demonstrations, the industry is still in its infancy. Building robots with the dexterity, balance and reliability needed to work dependably on factory assembly lines, perform household chores or assist the elderly in a hospital remains a formidable engineering challenge.

One of the industry’s biggest hurdles is replicating the human hand with the same ability to grasp, manipulate and effectively use tools designed for people. Engineers must squeeze motors, joints and tendon-like cables into a limited space, while keeping the machine lightweight and preventing overheating.

The other challenge is developing the “brains” — or AI models — that control humanoids.** **These are models that combine visual information with language prompts to allow humanoids — or even a single robotic arm, for example — to perform tasks without every move being explicitly preprogrammed. The robot brains that will let these machines operate with real autonomy rather than choreographed or scripted demos, however, are still very much a work in progress.

### **How does China’s humanoid robot technology compare with the US?**

In many ways China is ahead of the US. Currently about 97% of global humanoid robot sales come from Chinese suppliers, [according](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/china-humanoid-makers-hold-97-of-global-shipments-report-says) to Smart Analytics Global. While Unitree, AgiBot and [UBTech Robotics Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/9880%20HK%20Equity) are ramping up sales of their humanoids, American firms like Tesla, Figure AI and [Agility Robotics Inc.](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1585639D:US) are only selling a fraction of the volumes reported by their Chinese rivals.

Chinese robot makers are drawing on the country’s vast manufacturing supply chain, with companies such as Unitree making many components themselves or sourcing them domestically. In Shenzhen, many startups boast that much of the hardware they need to assemble a robot can be sourced nearby, allowing them to develop new technology quickly. Their rivals in the US, on the other hand, often need to wait weeks or even months for specialized components to arrive from overseas suppliers.

China is also moving faster to put humanoids to work. This year, leading Chinese manufacturers have expanded deployments in factories from dozens of robots to hundreds or even thousands, using real-world experience to improve the technology.
