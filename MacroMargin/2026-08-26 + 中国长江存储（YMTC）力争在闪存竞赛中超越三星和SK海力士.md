### 中文译文

## 中国长江存储（YMTC）力争在闪存竞赛中超越三星和SK海力士

长江存储（YMTC）的目标是超越三星和SK海力士，成为全球最大的NAND闪存生产商，这家中国公司正为上市做准备。

这家总部位于武汉的集团在近期的IPO筹备会议上告诉投资者和利益相关方，它希望在2027年底前尽快夺得全球NAND桂冠，据两位知情人士透露。

上周，[长江存储](https://www.ft.com/stream/1fd5ea0b-60b5-4b51-aad6-3067ba04d49e)提交了IPO申请，拟筹资330亿元人民币（合50亿美元），主要用于升级生产设施以及研发。根据上交所网站发布的招股说明书。

这家芯片制造商一季度实现营业收入470亿元人民币（合70亿美元）。根据其IPO招股说明书，净利润为330亿元人民币，是2025年全年总额的两倍多。

长江存储尚未确定上市时间表，预计交易开始后其市值将超过1万亿元人民币（合1500亿美元），使其成为今年中国第二大IPO。然而，据一位参与该过程的银行家称，在监管指导下，股票定价可能会较为保守。

他们补充说，市场共识认为其IPO估值在2000亿至3000亿元人民币之间，而具体数字尚未在上市过程中最终确定。

继[长鑫存储（CXMT）成功上市](https://www.ft.com/content/8e82e939-908b-42bf-a314-0bb02a3f1b07?syn-25a6b1a6=1)之后，长江存储加快了IPO计划。长鑫存储是另一家中国领先的内存芯片制造商，主要生产DRAM芯片。

上月底，该公司在完成今年亚洲规模最大的IPO、筹集90亿美元后，估值飙升超过400%。长鑫存储目前是中国市值最高的上市公司，市值达5800亿美元。

NAND芯片是一种无需电源即可存储数据的硬件，最常见于智能手机和平板电脑等设备。DRAM芯片临时存储数据，可用于计算机以及AI处理任务。

与GPU不同，GPU需要美国制裁积极针对的尖端光刻生产设备，而内存制造在很大程度上依赖于堆叠架构和工艺优化——在这些领域，中国企业迅速缩小了能力差距。

通过利用国家补贴和世界领先的电子供应链，长江存储和长鑫存储都在NAND和DRAM市场中占据更大份额。

这些收益既为他们提供了资助进一步研究所需的现金流，也提供了将中国确立为全球技术生态系统中不可或缺参与者的筹码，无论西方贸易壁垒如何。

据研究和咨询公司TrendForce称，第一季度，长江存储在全球NAND生产商中排名全球第三、中国第一，仅次于[三星](https://www.ft.com/stream/18fac44d-7b87-4341-ab9b-b0cd8ccf498f)和[SK海力士](https://www.ft.com/stream/dc958f59-a536-4441-8c31-52b91ac60e31)。

据TrendForce的数据，去年全球NAND市场规模估计为710亿美元，预计到2026年将飙升至3000亿美元以上。预计到2030年，该市场规模将达到约4900亿美元。

许多投资者担心，长鑫存储和长江存储雄心勃勃的扩张计划将导致存储芯片价格下滑，从而结束该行业股票的牛市行情。它们一直是人工智能热潮的最大赢家之一。

Ninety One的投资组合经理Joanna Yang表示：“对于全球投资者来说，一个问题是这些中国公司的产能扩张将如何影响存储芯片的供需动态。这是一种全球性产品——它具有全球定价。”

摩根大通私人银行（JPMorgan Private Bank）亚洲股票策略师Cameron Chui表示：“中国是否在增加新产能？当然。”

然而，由于训练人工智能模型所需的存储芯片需求上升，他对该行业仍持乐观态度。“供应在增长，但我们认为需求增长更快。我们认为这不足以平衡市场。”

YMTC未回应置评请求。

### English Original

## China’s YMTC aims to overtake Samsung and SK Hynix in flash memory race

YMTC aims to overtake Samsung and SK Hynix to become the world’s largest Nand flash memory producer as the Chinese company gears up for a stock market listing.

The Wuhan-based group told investors and stakeholders in recent IPO preparation meetings that it wants to take the global Nand crown by as soon as the end of 2027, according to two people with knowledge of the matter.

Last week, [YMTC](https://www.ft.com/stream/1fd5ea0b-60b5-4b51-aad6-3067ba04d49e) filed for an IPO to raise Rmb33bn ($5bn), mostly to upgrade production facilities as well as for research and development, according to a prospectus published on the Shanghai Stock Exchange website.

The chipmaker had operating revenue of Rmb47bn ($7bn) in the first quarter. Net profit was Rmb33bn — more than double the total for the whole of 2025, according to its IPO prospectus.

YMTC, which is yet to set a timeline for its market debut, is expected to reach a market valuation above Rmb1tn ($150bn) after trading begins, making it the second-largest IPO in China this year. However, the shares will probably be priced conservatively under regulatory guidance, according to a banker involved in the process.

Market consensus put its IPO valuation at Rmb200bn to Rmb300bn, while the exact figure was yet to be finalised during the listing process, they added.

YMTC has accelerated its IPO plans following the [successful debut of CXMT](https://www.ft.com/content/8e82e939-908b-42bf-a314-0bb02a3f1b07?syn-25a6b1a6=1), another Chinese leading memory-chip maker that primarily makes DRam chips.

Its valuation surged more than 400 per cent late last month after raising $9bn in Asia’s largest IPO this year. CXMT is now China’s most valuable listed company, worth $580bn.

Nand chips are a type of hardware that can store data without a power source and are most commonly found in devices such as smartphones and tablets. DRam chips store data temporarily and can be used in computers as well as for AI processing tasks.

Unlike GPUs, which require leading-edge lithographic production equipment that US sanctions actively target, memory manufacturing relies heavily on stacking architecture and process optimisation — areas where Chinese firms have rapidly narrowed the capability gap.

By leveraging state subsidies and a world-leading electronics supply chain, YMTC and CXMT are both capturing a bigger slice of the Nand and DRam markets.

The gains provide them with both the cash flow needed to fund further research and the leverage to establish China as an indispensable player in the global technology ecosystem, regardless of western trade barriers.

According to TrendForce, a research and consulting company, YMTC ranked third globally and first in China among global Nand producers in the first quarter, behind [Samsung](https://www.ft.com/stream/18fac44d-7b87-4341-ab9b-b0cd8ccf498f) and [SK Hynix](https://www.ft.com/stream/dc958f59-a536-4441-8c31-52b91ac60e31).

The global Nand market size was estimated to be worth $71bn last year, a figure expected to surge to more than $300bn in 2026, according to TrendForce. The market is expected to be worth about $490bn by 2030.

Many investors are concerned that CXMT and YMTC’s ambitious expansion plans will cause memory chip prices to slide, ending a bull run for stocks in the sector. They have been among the biggest winners from the AI frenzy.

Joanna Yang, a portfolio manager at Ninety One, said: “For global investors, one question is how these Chinese companies’ capacity expansion is going to impact the supply-demand dynamics for memory. This is a global product — it has global pricing.”

Cameron Chui, Asia equity strategist at JPMorgan Private Bank, said: “Is China adding new capacity? Absolutely.”

However, he remained optimistic on the sector due to rising demand for memory chips needed for training AI models. “Supply is growing but we think demand grows faster. We think it’s not enough to balance the market.”

YMTC did not respond to a request for comment.
