### 中文译文

## 中国放宽英伟达H200芯片限制，人工智能竞赛升级

北京允许小批量出货，以帮助领先科技集团追赶美国竞争对手。

英伟达的H200芯片。中国希望将这些芯片的大部分保留在中国大陆以外，以支持国内芯片制造商。© 英伟达

[**Zijing Wu**](https://www.ft.com/zijing-wu) 在香港

发布于12小时前

小批量英伟达H200芯片已获准进入中国大陆，北京正试图帮助其领先的人工智能公司追赶美国竞争对手。

据两位知情人士透露，字节跳动和[腾讯](https://www.ft.com/stream/26272ebc-a21c-450e-a743-be89c41d1eb8)最近几周各自收到了约10,000个H200处理器，而其他几家中国科技集团可能很快也会获得类似规模的出货批准。

H200至少比[英伟达](https://www.ft.com/nvidia)最强大的芯片落后两代，而中国客户因美国出口管制被禁止购买后者。

据其中一位知情人士透露，尽管美国已批准这些中国集团各自购买多达100,000个H200，但北京希望它们将这些芯片的大部分保留在中国大陆以外，以支持国内芯片制造商。

除了获准进入中国大陆的少量H200芯片外，中国监管机构已告知企业，可以将此类处理器运往香港——香港作为中国关税边境之外的地区运营——并在那里使用。

美国许可证允许英伟达的H200芯片运往中国大陆和香港。

允许芯片进入香港仍给中国科技企业带来挑战，因为这块前英国殖民地缺乏足够的数据中心来容纳这些芯片。由于电力供应问题，香港的容量不足，这种情况短期内不太可能改变。

“这是一个两难境地。每个人都需要这些芯片，但在香港却苦于找不到使用它们的方法，”一位知情人士表示。“希望控制能逐步放松。”

据多位知情人士透露，英伟达库存中约有50万颗H200芯片，主要面向中国客户。由于北京的限制，销售被搁置。

一位人士称，联想等部分英伟达合作伙伴上周告知中国客户，可以恢复订购其包含H200芯片的产品。这些合作伙伴将英伟达芯片与CPU及其他网络设备集成，作为AI服务器出售。

据一位直接知情人士透露，购买仍需通过由国家规划机构国家发展和改革委员会（NDRC）监管的申请程序进行单独批准。

中国持续的进口管制反映出其支持以华为为首的国内芯片制造商的意愿，这些制造商旨在主导全球第二大半导体市场，而由于出口管制，英伟达在该市场基本被排除在外。

然而，中国缺乏最先进的芯片制造设备，将限制其在短期内满足激增的国内需求的能力，这意味着仍需继续进口部分英伟达芯片——尤其是用于人工智能训练。

中国监管机构最近开始放宽对美国芯片的限制，以确保国内领先的人工智能开发者能够继续训练尖端模型。一位熟悉监管思路的人士表示，中国公司正竞相与Anthropic 的 [Mythos 5](https://www.ft.com/content/4b578b5c-258a-48f5-a5e9-15eef5659573?syn-25a6b1a6=1) 等先进模型展开竞争。

上个月，中国人工智能实验室月之暗面（Moonshot）发布了其 [K3模型](https://www.ft.com/content/c6ecd8ce-c441-4d7c-aea6-fae3e28fb6ff?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，其性能几乎可媲美美国最先进的模型。

[阿里巴巴](https://www.ft.com/content/391c5f14-4bf2-4d24-92f0-65b912574513?syn-25a6b1a6=1)、DeepSeek 和 Z.ai 也相继推出了类似智能水平的模型，这表明中国和美国领先实验室之间的差距已经缩小。

据知情人士透露，中国实验室越来越多地使用国产芯片进行涉及实时响应生成的人工智能推理工作；但大多数仍使用英伟达芯片进行更复杂的训练阶段，以积累知识并寻找规律。

英伟达、字节跳动、腾讯和国家发改委均未回应置评请求。

## China eases limits on Nvidia H200 chips as AI race escalates

Beijing permits small shipments to help leading tech groups in drive to catch up with US rivals

Nvidia’s H200 chip. China wants to keep most of these chips outside the mainland to support domestic chipmakers © Nvidia

[**Zijing Wu**](https://www.ft.com/zijing-wu) in Hong Kong

Published 12 hours ago

Small batches of Nvidia’s H200 chips have been allowed to enter mainland China, as Beijing tries to aid its leading AI companies in the race to catch up with US rivals.

ByteDance and [Tencent](https://www.ft.com/stream/26272ebc-a21c-450e-a743-be89c41d1eb8) have each received about 10,000 H200 processors in recent weeks, while a few other Chinese tech groups could soon obtain approval for shipments of similar size, according to two people with knowledge of the matter.

The H200 is at least two generations behind [Nvidia](https://www.ft.com/nvidia)’s most powerful chips, which Chinese customers are not allowed to buy due to US export controls.

While the Chinese groups have been cleared by the US to buy up to 100,000 H200s apiece, Beijing wants them to keep most of these chips outside the mainland to support domestic chipmakers, one of the people said.

In addition to the small number of H200 chips permitted to enter the mainland, Chinese regulators have told companies that they can ship such processors to Hong Kong, which operates as a territory outside its customs border, and use them there.

The US licences permit shipments of Nvidia’s H200 chips to mainland [China](https://www.ft.com/china) and Hong Kong.

Allowing the chips into Hong Kong still presents a challenge to Chinese tech groups as the former British colony lacks enough data centres to house them. That situation is unlikely to change soon as Hong Kong doesn’t have sufficient capacity due to power supply issues.

“It’s a dilemma. Everyone needs the chips but struggles to find a way to use them in Hong Kong,” said a person familiar with the situation. “The hope is for the control to loosen up gradually.”

Nvidia has about half a million H200 chips in stock mainly for Chinese clients. Sales were held up due to Beijing’s restrictions, according to multiple people with knowledge of the situation.

Some Nvidia partners such as Lenovo told Chinese customers last week that they could resume placing orders for their products containing H200 chips, one person said. These partners integrate Nvidia chips with CPUs and other networking kit to sell as AI servers.

Purchases would still require separate approval through an application process overseen by the state planner National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), according to a person with direct knowledge.

China’s continued import controls reflect its desire to support domestic chipmakers led by Huawei, which aim to dominate the world’s second largest semiconductor market where Nvidia is mostly shut out due to export controls.

However, China’s lack of the most advanced chipmaking equipment will restrict its ability to meet surging domestic demand in the near future, meaning some imports of Nvidia chips will continue to be needed — especially for AI training.

Chinese regulators have recently started loosening restrictions on US chips to ensure that leading domestic AI developers can continue training cutting-edge models. Chinese groups are vying to compete with advanced models such as Anthropic’s [Mythos 5](https://www.ft.com/content/4b578b5c-258a-48f5-a5e9-15eef5659573?syn-25a6b1a6=1), said one of the people familiar with regulatory thinking.

Last month Chinese AI lab Moonshot released its [K3 model](https://www.ft.com/content/c6ecd8ce-c441-4d7c-aea6-fae3e28fb6ff?syn-25a6b1a6=1), whose performance almost rivalled the most advanced US models.

[Alibaba](https://www.ft.com/content/391c5f14-4bf2-4d24-92f0-65b912574513?syn-25a6b1a6=1), DeepSeek and Z.ai all followed with models of similar intelligence level in a sign that the gap between leading Chinese and US labs has narrowed.

Chinese labs are increasingly using domestic chips for AI inference work that involves generating live responses; most still use Nvidia chips for the more complex training phase to build knowledge and look for patterns, according to people with knowledge of the matter.

Nvidia, ByteDance, Tencent and the NDRC did not respond to requests for comment.
