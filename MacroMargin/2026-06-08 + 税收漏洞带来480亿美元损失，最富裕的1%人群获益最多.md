## 税收漏洞带来480亿美元损失，最富裕的1%人群获益最多

曾经是美国税收体系中的一个小漏洞如今已变成一股洪流，每年给政府造成约480亿美元的损失。

根据彭博社的最新估算，这是美国财政部因交易所交易基金利用漏洞递延或避免资本利得税而损失的金额。估算显示，这些节省几乎全部流向了收入最高的美国人，而且近期政策调整后，这一数字可能几乎翻倍。

“我们开始谈论财政部实际损失的税收收入，”宾夕法尼亚州罗伯特莫里斯大学研究该税收减免的教授史蒂文·霍达西说，“其中大部分好处都流向了非常富裕的投资者。”

这种做法是华尔街旨在减少投资者税负的日益复杂的交易和产品组合的一部分。交易所交易基金（ETF）已成为许多此类策略的关键，部分原因在于该漏洞使它们能够发挥某种让税收消失的魔力。

业界表示，对ETF税收减免规模的估计被夸大了，数百万普通投资者受益，并且它鼓励长期储蓄。

然而，关于这项税收减免以及对其利用的快速增长，几乎没有公开讨论或辩论。480亿美元的估算——非党派国会评分机构对2021年计算结果的更新，以反映此后行业的增长——足以让NASA获得两倍的经费。

### 心跳交易的兴起

与个人一样，当基金以高于买入价的价格卖出股票时，需要缴纳资本利得税。对于共同基金，该纳税义务会转嫁给投资者。

但ETF并不总是出售其资产。作为使其价格与持仓价值保持一致机制的一部分，它们经常与银行或其他公司交换股票以换取基金份额。1969年的一项法律规定，此类“实物”交易不会触发纳税。

这意味着增值证券可以轻松脱手而无需产生应税收益，大多数ETF完全避免这些收益。

即使基金没有足够的常规交易活动让经理人抛售增值股票，也有变通办法。经理人可以要求友好公司（通常是银行或做市商）进行一两天的大额投资。然后在资金退出时，股票可以被交换出去。

这类交易被称为“心跳交易”，因为它们在图表上呈现的形态是：突然的大额流入，随后是对称的流出——就像心电图机上的脉冲。这是清除应税收益操作的明显标志。

心跳交易的使用随着ETF行业规模的扩大而激增。事实上，估计显示，这类人为流动的增长速度甚至超过了更广泛的ETF市场，后者在过去十年中增长了五倍，资产规模接近15万亿美元。在2010年代上半年，心跳交易占所有净每日流出的比例不到5%。自2017年以来，平均约占9%。

过去十年中心跳交易激增

来源：彭博估算

总体而言，去年基金利用心跳交易撤出了2930亿美元的资产。随着股市繁荣使得基金经理更难避免税收，以及一项规则变化为其更广泛使用铺平道路，这类交易一直在增加。

要使用心跳交易避税，经理人必须能够挑选他们交换的股票。直到2019年，这需要获得美国证券交易委员会（SEC）的特别许可，只有少数经理人——包括Vanguard Group和BlackRock Inc.等大型指数基金提供商——才有此资格。那一年，委员会改变了规则，为任何ETF使用该技术打开了大门。

这是主动管理型ETF快速增长的原因之一，它们比指数产品更依赖心跳交易。估算显示，去年心跳交易占主动型基金每日净流出量的18%，而被动型基金仅为9%。

在最极端的情况下，一些新基金似乎几乎完全依赖心跳交易。例如，在典型的一天里，Alpha Architect美国股票ETF根本没有资金净流入或流出。但当基金经理想要卖出增值的证券时，投资者会突然出现，注入现金，以便在需要时再次撤出资金。Alpha Architect首席执行官Wesley Gray在一封电子邮件中承认了这些交易，并表示在交易量低的基金中这种情况很常见。

### 帮助顶层1%

在美国证券交易委员会（SEC）的推动下，这一税收漏洞的使用范围即将大幅扩大。去年年底，监管机构开始允许一些大型管理公司将其ETF（交易所交易基金）结构嫁接至现有共同基金——这是Vanguard首创的技术，但其专利已于2023年到期。

这种结构意味着，即使大多数客户持有共同基金份额类别，仍可在ETF份额类别中利用“心跳交易”来冲销整个合并基金的资本利得。一位评论员将其形容为一种税务“透析机”。

随着专利到期临近，其他资产管理公司也向SEC申请使用该结构，但在时任主席Gary Gensler领导下均未获批。特朗普政府上台后，由Paul Atkins领导的SEC开始向美国大多数大型资产管理公司授予许可。首个获批的产品——Dimensional Fund Advisors的美国微型股投资组合——于2月推出了ETF份额类别，并已完成首次心跳交易。Dimensional的代表未回应置评请求。

彭博估算显示，如果整个共同基金行业都利用ETF来冲销资本利得，每年将导致约400亿美元的递延或避税，其中个人约350亿美元，企业约40亿美元。

SEC主席Atkins将这一政策调整描述为对“普通投资者”的奖励。他在《华盛顿邮报》的一篇专栏文章中写道，大多数美国家庭持有共同基金。“这些人是我们的家人、朋友和邻居，他们更可能开一辆小型货车或皮卡，而不是豪华跑车。”

虽然大多数家庭确实持有共同基金，但他们主要通过401(k)或IRA等税收优惠退休账户持有，而这些账户无法从该政策调整中受益。根据美联储消费者金融调查数据，在退休账户之外持有共同基金和ETF的人群高度集中于全国最高收入者。Atkins拒绝就其专栏文章以外内容发表评论。

彭博估算显示，当前ETF税收减免平均每年为美国收入最高的1%家庭节省约1.3万美元，而财富分配中位数家庭仅节省约23美元。如果整个共同基金行业都采用ETF份额类别，最富裕群体每年可再减少1.1万美元税负，中位数群体则可再节省20美元。

最富1%人群获得ETF税收优惠的40%

来源：彭博行业研究估算

宾夕法尼亚州维拉诺瓦大学金融学教授Rabih Moussawi的研究显示，ETF行业的发展很大程度上得益于高净值投资者对税收节省的青睐。Moussawi在接受采访时表示，即使一些中产阶级人士持有ETF，他们也不太可能从这些特性中获得太多好处，因为他们无法将资金锁定那么长时间。

大多数富人从不披露其ETF持仓，但有时会有线索进入公众视野。凯莉·莱夫勒（Kelly Loeffler）和她的丈夫——洲际交易所的亿万富翁创始人——去年持有4700万至2.01亿美元的ETF，这是她在被任命负责美国小企业管理局后披露的。弗吉尼亚州民主党参议员马克·华纳（Mark Warner）通过电信投资发家，他和妻子截至2024年持有3000万至1.5亿美元的ETF。

持有1亿美元ETF投资组合的投资者每年可避免或递延约68.2万美元的资本利得税。

莱夫勒和华纳均未回应向其代表发出的评论请求。

“这种税收递延的概念本身就是为那些储蓄期限非常长的人设计的，”Moussawi说。“他们不是为了拿现金买房或支付子女教育费而储蓄。它本质上是有利于那些能够将资金投入ETF并长期不动的富有的投资者。”

### 政府成本

国会从未明确说明为何在1969年为“实物”基金赎回提供特殊税收减免，但无论原因如何，当时这并非什么大事。共同基金很少利用这一条款。直到后来ETF被发明，才开始被常规使用。

“国会最初通过这项福利时并未充分认识到其规模之大，”纽约大学法学教授丹尼尔·赫梅尔说，“这是一个偶然的税收漏洞。”

2021年，俄勒冈州民主党人、时任参议院财政委员会主席罗恩·怀登传阅了一系列税收提案，其中包括取消ETF漏洞。该想法未获进展，去年他更新改革方案时将其放弃。

“这一被超级富豪滥用的漏洞仍是怀登议员的严重关切，”发言人瑞安·凯里表示，“他正在制定应对措施，将重点放在高净值避税上，同时保护中产阶级不受影响。”

为衡量2021年提案的影响，国会无党派联合税收委员会估计，实施后的九年里联邦收入每年将增加约230亿美元。该估计被视为“非常初步”，此后未再更新。

在此后的几年里，ETF行业规模大约翻了一番，吸引了新投资者并受益于市场上涨。这扩大了可能被征税的资本利得池。以2021年收入估计为起点，如果税收成本与市场规模同步增长，那么目前每年约为480亿美元——彭博估算显示。联合税收委员会拒绝置评。

作为对比，国会预算办公室估计，近期削减《平价医疗法案》健康保险的联邦补贴今年可能节省约230亿美元。据CBO称，削减将使未参保人数增加约200万人。联合税收委员会估计，特朗普总统的“小费不征税”提案（去年通过成为法律，旨在惠及服务员等低薪服务业工人）将使今年联邦收入减少约100亿美元。

主要行业游说组织投资公司协会在一份声明中对怀登办公室传阅的2021年估计表示怀疑，称其“无法核实”归因于联合税收委员会的数字。它还质疑彭博更新分析所用的方法，并称彭博对共同基金政策变化影响的估计“不切实际”。

至于心跳，ICI表示，彭博社研究的资金流入流出模式可能反映了一系列与税收无关的活动，例如常规做市和现金管理。

“ETF绝大多数被中产阶级储蓄者用作建立财务安全的长期工具，约有2000万美国家庭持有ETF。在这些家庭中，收入中位数为15万美元，”ICI表示。ETF税收优惠“是一项长期存在的规则，促进了基金投资的公平和效率。”

ICI长期以来一直认为，将资本利得再投资的投资者在套现之前不应为其缴税。

“税收公平对中产阶级投资者至关重要，政府应保留这一对长期储蓄和投资的激励措施，”ICI表示。

ICI表示，在申报资本利得的美国纳税人中，约73%的收入低于20万美元。

### 闭环

目前尚不清楚ETF规避的税收中有多少是递延到未来年份缴纳，而非完全避免。

从理论上讲，当一只基金避免产生税收时，只会增加投资者最终出售持股时应缴的税款。这就是为什么ICI等行业组织表示ETF只是递延税收而非规避税收。

但ETF有多种方式可帮助完全避税。它们可将短期资本收益转化为长期资本收益，而后者税率更低。如果投资者持有ETF直至去世，继承人出售时无需缴税。

联合委员会2021年的估算表明，即使因该漏洞损失的税收收入并非永久消失，也至少会在很长一段时间内无法收取。委员会发现，即使在漏洞关闭后的第9年，它仍将为税收收入带来220亿美元的净增长。

彭博的估算并未包括ETF带来的几种规模较小但增长迅速的新型避税方式。其中一种基金可复制短期国债的回报，但税率更低且可无限期递延纳税。其他类型的基金（称为351转换）允许投资者在不触发纳税事件的情况下，将自己个人持有的高增值股票变现。还有一些基金则帮助投资者规避股息税。

罗伯特莫里斯大学教授Hodaszy表示，鉴于ETF在金融体系中的根深蒂固及其对免税政策的依赖，政策制定者可能无法完全取消免税。但他指出，或许可以取消诸如心跳交易和351转换等交易中的税收优惠。

Hodaszy说：“我们正处在一个节点上，尤其是对于ETF而言，那些被递延或完全免除的税收——国家无法承受这种为富人提供的不合理税收减免。”

### 方法：

#### 心跳检测

为了识别心跳交易，彭博分析了2010年至2025年期间4683只美国ETF（交易所交易基金）的每日资金流动数据，这些ETF在分析时仍处于活跃状态。对数据进行筛选，找出在八个或更少交易日内出现资金流入后伴随对称流出的情况。要纳入分析，这些事件必须至少是周围20天内任何其他流入或流出（排除其他心跳交易候选者）的三倍，代表基金资产超过1%，并满足其他标准。对称性定义为流出量在流入量的20%以内。同时考虑了一天和两天的流入。两只大型基金VOO和IVV被排除，因为人工检查发现检测标准存在误报。

并非每一种心跳模式都代表为了避税而剥离股票的操作，但抽查显示，几乎所有这些模式都与投资组合的变化有关。筛选仅识别了最显著的心跳交易，并未计入那些不比相邻基金流量大很多的交易。

#### 税收影响估算

为确定ETF漏洞的税收影响，彭博首先采用了美国联合税收委员会（Joint Committee on Taxation）2021年对废除第852(b)(6)条款后可能增加的税收收入的估算。彭博将该影响转化为年化数字，然后根据2021年以来ETF管理的总资产增长进行了缩放。行业增长数据来自美国投资公司协会（Investment Company Institute）。

为确定共同基金采用ETF份额类别后未来的潜在税收影响，彭博根据ICI的数据，统计了自2020年以来分配给应税共同基金持有人的资本利得平均美元价值。根据美联储（Fed）的数据，扣除了外国投资者持有的共同基金份额，以及改革后可能分配的资本利得份额。为估算税收负担，彭博根据美联储消费者金融调查（Survey of Consumer Finances）中关于股票共同基金和ETF（非退休账户）的数据，按收入类别计算了共同基金的所有权比例，然后应用相关的边际税率，假设短期与长期资本利得的分解与Rabih Moussawi在《税收在ETF崛起中的作用》中记录的历史比率一致。

为确定税收节省的分布，彭博依赖消费者金融调查中关于各收入百分位群体在非退休账户中持有股票共同基金和ETF的所有权数据。假设共同基金和ETF在税收优惠退休账户之外的所有权分布相同。

## The Top 1% Reap Most From Tax Loophole Costing $48 Billion

What was once a minor leak in the US tax system is now a torrent that’s costing the government around $48 billion a year.

That’s the amount lost to the Treasury Department from a loophole used by exchange-traded funds to defer or avoid capital gains tax, according to new Bloomberg estimates. The savings go almost exclusively to the highest-earning Americans, the estimates show, and could be set to nearly double following a recent policy shift.

“We’re starting to talk about real money in terms of forgone revenue for the Treasury,” said Steven Hodaszy, a professor at Robert Morris University in Pennsylvania who has studied the tax break. “Much of the benefit of that is going to very high net-worth investors.”

The practice is part of a burgeoning complex of trades and products on Wall Street designed to minimize investor tax bills. ETFs have proved key to a number of such strategies, partly because the loophole allows them to work a kind of magic that makes taxes disappear.

The industry says estimates of the ETF tax break’s size are overblown, that millions of ordinary investors benefit and that it encourages long-term saving.

Yet there’s been little public discussion or debate about the tax break or the rapid growth in its exploitation. The estimate of $48 billion — an update to 2021 calculations by nonpartisan Congressional scorekeepers to reflect industry growth since then — is enough to fund NASA twice over.

### Rise of Heartbeats

As with an individual, when a fund sells a stock for more than it paid, it’s responsible for tax on the capital gain. In the case of a mutual fund, that tax liability is passed on to investors.

But ETFs don’t always sell their assets. As part of the mechanism that keeps their prices in line with the value of their holdings, they often swap them with banks or other firms in exchange for fund shares. A 1969 law says such “in-kind” transactions don’t trigger a tax bill.

That means appreciated securities can be easily offloaded without a taxable gain, and most ETFs avoid them completely.

Even when a fund doesn’t have enough regular trading activity to let a manager get rid of an appreciated stock, there’s a workaround. The manager can ask a friendly firm — typically a bank or market maker — to make a big investment for a day or two. The stock can then be swapped away as the money is withdrawn.

Trades like these are known as heartbeats because of the pattern they make on a chart: a sudden, huge inflow, followed by a symmetrical outflow — like the blip on an EKG machine. They’re a telltale sign of a maneuver to wash away taxable gains.

The use of heartbeats has surged alongside the size of the ETF industry itself. In fact, the estimates show that these types of artificial flows have grown even faster than the broader ETF market, which quintupled in the past decade to almost $15 trillion in assets. Heartbeats made up less than 5% of all net daily outflows during the first half of the 2010s. Since 2017, they’ve averaged about 9%.

Source: Bloomberg estimates

Overall, funds shed $293 billion of assets using heartbeats last year. They have been on the rise as the booming stock market made it harder for fund managers to avoid taxes without them, and after a rule change paved the way for their broader use.

To avoid tax using heartbeats, managers must be able to pick and choose which stocks they swap away. Until 2019, that required getting special permission from the US Securities and Exchange Commission, and only a handful of managers — including big index-fund providers such as Vanguard Group and BlackRock Inc. — had it. That year, the commission changed its rules, opening the door for any ETF to use the technique.

It’s one reason for the rapid growth of actively managed ETFs, which rely more heavily on heartbeats than index products do. Heartbeats comprised 18% of net daily outflows in active vehicles last year, the estimates show, compared with 9% for passive funds.

At the most extreme, some new funds appear to rely almost exclusively on heartbeats. For instance, on a typical day, the Alpha Architect US Equity ETF posts no net inflows or outflows at all. But when the fund manager wants to unload appreciated securities, investors suddenly appear, injecting cash so that they are positioned to withdraw it again when needed. Wesley Gray, the Alpha Architect chief executive officer, acknowledged the transactions in an email and said they’re common for funds with low trading volume.

### Helping the Top 1%

Use of the tax loophole is now set to significantly broaden thanks to the SEC. Late last year, the regulator began granting permission for some of the largest management companies to graft ETFs onto their existing mutual funds — a technique pioneered by Vanguard but protected by patent until 2023.

That structure means that, even if most customers hold the mutual-fund share class, heartbeats can be used in the ETF share class to wash away all of the combined fund’s capital gains. One commentator has described it as a tax “dialysis machine.”

As the patent expiration approached, other money managers asked the SEC for permission to use the structure, but none gained approval under the regulator’s then-Chair Gary Gensler. After the Trump Administration took office, the agency under Paul Atkins began granting permission to most of the largest US money managers. The first fund out of the gate, Dimensional Fund Advisors’ US Micro Cap Portfolio, floated an ETF share class in February and has already completed its first heartbeat trade. Representatives for Dimensional didn’t respond to requests for comment.

If the entire mutual fund industry were to use ETFs to wash out capital gains, it would lead to about $40 billion of additional deferred or avoided taxes each year, including $35 billion for individuals and $4 billion for businesses, the Bloomberg estimates show.

SEC Chairman Atkins pitched the policy change as a reward to “everyday investors.” In an op-ed in the Washington Post, he noted that most American households own mutual funds. “These are our family members, friends and neighbors, and they are more likely to drive a minivan or a pickup truck than a luxury sports car.”

While it’s true that most households own mutual funds, they tend to do so primarily through tax-advantaged retirement accounts, such as 401(k)s or IRAs, which don’t benefit from the policy shift. Ownership of mutual funds and ETFs outside of retirement accounts is highly concentrated among the nation’s top earners, according to data from the Federal Reserve Board’s Survey of Consumer Finances. Atkins declined to comment beyond his op-ed.

The ETF tax break is currently saving each of the highest-earning 1% of American households about $13,000 a year, on average, while those in the middle of the wealth distribution get about $23, the Bloomberg estimates show. If the whole mutual fund industry were to adopt an ETF share class, the richest could see their taxes fall by another $11,000. Those in the middle could save an additional $20.

The Top 1% Get 40% of the ETF Tax Benefit

Source: Bloomberg estimates

Much of the growth of the ETF industry has been driven by high-net-worth investors attracted to the tax savings, according to research by Rabih Moussawi, a finance professor at Villanova University in Pennsylvania. Even if some middle-class people own ETFs, Moussawi said in an interview, they’re less likely to get much benefit from these features because they can’t keep their money tied up for as long.

Most wealthy individuals never have to disclose their ownership of ETFs, but hints sometimes find their way into public view. Kelly Loeffler and her husband, the billionaire founder of Intercontinental Exchange, held between $47 million and $201 million of ETFs last year, she disclosed after being tapped to run the US Small Business Administration. Senator Mark Warner, a Virginia Democrat who made his fortune in telecom investing, and his wife held between $30 million and $150 million as of 2024.

An investor with a $100 million portfolio of ETFs could expect to avoid or defer about $682,000 in capital gains taxes a year.

Loeffler and Warner didn’t respond to requests for comment submitted to their representatives.

“The concept of this tax deferral is built in to favor the guys who are saving for very long horizons,” Moussawi said. “They’re not saving to get the cash to buy a house or finance their kids’ education. It’s built in to favor the wealthy investors who can park their money in these ETFs and not think about them.”

### Cost to Government

Congress never spelled out why it provided the special tax break for “in-kind” fund withdrawals back in 1969, but whatever the reason, it wasn’t a big deal at the time. Mutual funds rarely took advantage of it. Only later, when ETFs were invented, did it start to be used routinely.

“Congress did not fully appreciate the magnitude of this benefit when it was initially enacted,” said Daniel Hemel, a law professor at New York University. “It’s an accidental tax break.”

In 2021, Ron Wyden, the Oregon Democrat and then-chair of the Senate Finance Committee, circulated a raft of tax proposals that included eliminating the ETF loophole. The idea went nowhere, and he dropped it when he updated the reform package last year.

“This loophole being abused by the ultra-wealthy remains a serious concern to Senator Wyden,” said Ryan Carey, a spokesman. “He’s developing ways to address it that will focus on high-net-worth tax avoidance and hold the middle class harmless.”

To gauge the impact of the 2021 proposal, Congress’s nonpartisan Joint Committee on Taxation estimated federal revenue would jump by about $23 billion annually in the nine years following its implementation. The estimate, deemed “very preliminary,” hasn’t been updated since.

In the intervening years, the ETF industry has roughly doubled in size, attracting new investors and benefiting from a rising market. That expanded the pool of capital gains potentially subject to tax. Using the 2021 revenue estimate as a starting point, if the tax cost increased at the same rate as the size of the market, then it currently clocks in at about $48 billion a year, the Bloomberg estimate shows. The Joint Committee declined to comment.

By way of comparison, recent cuts to federal subsidies for Affordable Care Act health insurance will probably save about $23 billion this year, the Congressional Budget Office estimates. The cuts will increase the ranks of the uninsured by about 2 million people, according to the CBO. President Trump’s “no tax on tips” proposal, passed into law last year to benefit waiters and other low-wage service workers, will cut federal revenue by about $10 billion this year, the Joint Committee estimates.

In a statement, the Investment Company Institute, a leading industry lobby, cast doubt on the 2021 estimate circulated by Wyden’s office, saying it “could not verify” the figure attributed to the Joint Committee. It also questioned the methods Bloomberg used to update the analysis, and called Bloomberg’s estimate of the impact of the mutual-fund policy shift “unrealistic.”

As for heartbeats, the ICI said the inflow-outflow patterns Bloomberg examined could reflect a range of activities that aren’t related to tax, such as routine market-making and cash management.

“ETFs are overwhelmingly used by middle-class savers as long-term tools to build financial security, with about 20 million US households owning ETFs. Out of these households, the median income is $150,000,” the ICI said. The ETF tax break “is a long-standing rule that promotes fairness and efficiency in fund investing.”

ICI has long argued that investors who reinvest their capital gains shouldn’t have to pay tax on them until they cash out.

“Tax fairness is vitally important to middle-class investors and the government should preserve this incentive to long-term saving and investing,” the ICI said.

Some 73% of US taxpayers who report capital gains on their returns have income below $200,000, the ICI said.

### Closing the Loop

It’s unclear how much of the tax sidestepped by ETFs is being deferred and paid in future years, rather than being avoided completely.

Theoretically, when a fund avoids incurring tax, that just increases the amount of tax due when investors ultimately sell their holdings. That’s why industry groups like the ICI say ETFs merely defer taxes rather than avoid them.

But there are multiple ways ETFs help avoid tax completely. They can transform short-term capital gains into long-term gains, which are taxed at a lower rate. And if investors hold an ETF until death, heirs can sell without paying the tax.

The 2021 estimate by the Joint Committee suggests that, if the tax revenue lost to the loophole doesn’t disappear permanently, it at least goes missing for a very long time. Even in the 9th year after closing the loophole, it would boost tax revenue by a net $22 billion, the committee found.

Bloomberg’s estimates don’t include several smaller but fast-growing new forms of tax avoidance made possible by ETFs. One type of fund replicates the return from short-term Treasuries but with lower taxes and indefinite deferral of tax payments. Others, known as 351 conversions, allow investors to unload highly appreciated stocks they own personally without triggering a tax event. Others help investors sidestep tax on dividends.

Given how deeply embedded ETFs are in the financial system, and their reliance on the tax exemption, it may be impractical for policymakers to remove it entirely, said Hodaszy, the Robert Morris professor. But it may be possible to take away the tax benefit from trades such as heartbeats and 351 conversions, he said.

“We are getting to a point, with ETFs in particular,” Hodaszy said, “where the tax that is either being deferred or excluded outright — the country just can’t afford that kind of unjustifiable tax break for the wealthy.”

### Methodology:

#### Heartbeat Detection

To identify heartbeat trades, Bloomberg analyzed daily fund flow data from 2010 through 2025 for 4,683 US ETFs that were active at the time of this analysis. The data were screened to find inflows followed by symmetrical outflows over the course of eight or fewer trading days. To be included in the analysis, the events had to be at least three times as large as any other inflow or outflow within the surrounding 20 days (excluding other heartbeat candidates), represent more than 1% of fund assets, and meet other criteria. Symmetry was defined as an outflow within 20% of the size of the inflow. Both one- and two-day inflows were considered. Two large funds, VOO and IVV, were excluded as manual inspection revealed false positives for the detection criteria.

Not every heartbeat pattern represents a maneuver to shed stocks without incurring taxes, but spot checks show almost all of them occurred in connection with portfolio changes. The screen identified only the most pronounced heartbeats and didn’t count those that weren't significantly bigger than adjacent fund flows.

#### Tax Impact Estimates

To determine the tax impact of the ETF loophole, Bloomberg started with the Joint Committee on Taxation’s 2021 estimate of additional revenue collected if Section 852(b)(6) were repealed. Bloomberg converted the impact into an annualized figure. Then it was scaled for the increase in total ETF assets under management since 2021. Data on industry growth came from the Investment Company Institute.

To determine the future potential tax impact of mutual funds adopting an ETF share class, Bloomberg tallied the average dollar value of capital gains distributed to taxable mutual fund holders since 2020, relying on data from the ICI. The amount was reduced to account for the share of mutual funds held by foreign investors, based on Fed data, and the share of capital gains likely to be distributed after the change. To estimate tax liability, Bloomberg calculated the share of ownership of mutual funds by income category, based on data from the Fed’s Survey of Consumer Finances data for stock mutual funds and ETFs outside of retirement accounts, then applied relevant marginal tax rates, assuming a breakdown of short-term versus long-term capital gains consistent with historical ratios as documented by Rabih Moussawi, "The Role of Taxes in the Rise of ETFs."

To determine the distribution of tax savings, Bloomberg relied on the Survey of Consumer Finances’ figures on ownership of stock mutual funds and ETFs outside of retirement accounts across income percentile groups. Mutual funds and ETFs were assumed to have identical ownership distributions outside of tax-advantaged retirement accounts.
