### 中文译文

## 伊朗警告海湾国家：劝特朗普罢手，否则我们将狠狠打击你们

2026年7月30日，伊朗德黑兰阿扎迪广场展示的伊朗导弹系统。Majid Asgaripour/WANA（西亚通讯社）经由路透社

摘要

- 伊朗警告海湾国家，美国发动打击将引发对它们的攻击
- 德黑兰敦促海湾盟友推动特朗普回归外交途径
- 利雅得敦促特朗普推迟军事行动，寻求谈判
- 伊朗押注基础设施威胁可在不引发全面战争的情况下提高压力
8月6日（路透社）——伊朗已对海湾国家展开协调一致的外交努力，明确警告它们：除非德黑兰能让唐纳德·特朗普停止对伊朗的打击，并寻求通过谈判结束战争，否则伊朗将攻击它们的石油、电力和水利设施。

据一位伊朗高级官员称，该警告设想对华盛顿最亲密阿拉伯盟友的能源设施、油田、炼油厂、电网、水利基础设施、交通网络和其他战略资产发动攻击。

消息人士称，这一信息是伊朗为阻止美国采取进一步军事行动而做出的更广泛努力的一部分，其方式是提出海湾地区可能遭受严重经济破坏的前景。在特朗普于7月28日威胁要打击伊朗的能源网络和基础设施之后，伊朗通过一系列高层外交接触传达了这一信息。

两名伊朗高级官员、两名海湾消息人士和一名了解讨论情况的地区高级外交官表示，伊朗外交部长阿巴斯·阿拉格齐（Abbas Araqchi）与沙特、土耳其和卡塔尔外长以及巴基斯坦陆军参谋长进行了通话。由于此事敏感，上述消息人士均要求匿名。

这位高级外交官说，阿拉格齐敦促华盛顿的海湾盟友利用他们对特朗普的影响力，劝阻他不要再次发动打击，并警告称，任何对伊朗的攻击都将引发对美国资产和海湾地区关键基础设施的报复。

这位外交官说，他在每次对话中传递的信息都是一致的：“我们已准备好报复，但找到外交解决方案是避免该地区更大范围升级和破坏的最佳途径。”

一位海湾消息人士说：“伊朗的警告是明确的：如果美国将伊朗的基础设施作为目标，他们将通过打击海湾能源设施和其他地区目标进行报复。”

这场外交攻势凸显了伊朗如何寻求将海湾地区的脆弱性转化为筹码，让地区各国政府面临这样一种前景：任何新的美伊对抗都可能将支撑其经济的基础设施直接置于火线之上。

上述高级外交官和海湾消息人士称，沙特王储穆罕默德·本·萨勒曼随后与特朗普通话，敦促他推迟军事行动、重返谈判、实现停火并寻求外交解决。

另一位海湾消息人士说，这一警告是针对所有海湾国家的，但主要通过沙特阿拉伯和卡塔尔传达。伊朗近年来修复了与海湾邻国的关系，包括曾经的地区竞争对手沙特阿拉伯。

卡塔尔、阿曼和沙特阿拉伯都敦促华盛顿恢复与德黑兰的谈判，避免再次爆发冲突。伊朗方面警告称，美国的又一轮打击可能“将该地区变成火球”，第二位伊朗官员如此表示。

在[8月2日](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-threatens-strike-other-nations-energy-fields-if-us-launches-fresh-attacks-2026-08-01/)，特朗普表示他同意取消对伊朗的打击，“前提是能够迅速达成协议”。

### 伊朗官员称：面临更广泛地区升级的威胁

“沙特阿拉伯认为进一步升级只会带来更多破坏，”第一位海湾消息人士说。“这就是为什么它敦促特朗普给外交一个机会。”

在敦促华盛顿寻求外交途径的同时，利雅得也明确表示，如果受到威胁将自卫。第二位海湾消息人士称，沙特阿拉伯警告说，对其领土的任何攻击都将遭到军事回应。

与王室关系密切的沙特分析人士阿里·希哈比表示，沙特王国的评估是，进一步升级不太可能达到预期结果。“沙特王国认为，相称的军事回应与在霍尔木兹海峡对伊朗持续施压相结合，是为达成切实外交解决方案提供的最有力途径，”希哈比说。

伊朗的教士领导层一再要求关闭美国在海湾的军事基地，并呼吁海湾阿拉伯国家停止与华盛顿共享情报。德黑兰认为这些情报被用来便利对其发动的攻击。

“他们仍然记得沙特的阿美公司，”他补充说，指的是[2019年9月](https://www.reuters.com/article/world/attacks-on-saudi-oil-facilities-knock-out-half-the-kingdoms-supply-idUSKCN1VZ01Q/)对布盖格和胡赖斯设施的无人机和导弹袭击，那次袭击暂时中断了该国超过一半的原油产量，并暴露了海湾能源基础设施的脆弱性。

"敌人继续威胁伊朗的基础设施。如果伊朗遭受损失，将造成更大的损失作为报复，"这位伊朗官员补充说，他提到的目标清单包括电网和炼油厂。

这位伊朗高级官员表示，从德黑兰的角度来看，特朗普现在面临两个不愉快的选择：升级冲突可能席卷海湾并扰乱全球能源供应，或者接受谈判结果，而这达不到华盛顿寻求的决定性胜利。

这位地区高级外交官表示，达成协议的主要障碍之一是华盛顿不愿让德黑兰以宣称胜利的姿态出现。

"美国想要一些可以证明自己赢得战争的东西，"这位外交官说。

## Iran warns Gulf states: tell Trump to desist or we hit you hard

An Iranian missile system is displayed in Azadi Square in Tehran, Iran, July 30, 2026. Majid Asgaripour/WANA (West Asia News Agency) via REUTERS

Summary

- Iran warns Gulf states US strikes will trigger attacks on them
- Tehran presses Gulf allies to steer Trump back to diplomacy
- Riyadh urged Trump to delay military action, pursue negotiations
- Iran bets infrastructure threats can raise pressure without full-scale war
Aug 6 (Reuters) - Iran has made a concerted diplomatic push with Gulf states, warning them explicitly that Tehran will hit their oil, power and water plants unless they convince Donald Trump to end U.S. strikes on Iran and instead seek a negotiated end to the war.

The warning envisaged attacks on the energy facilities, oil fields, refineries, electricity grids, water infrastructure, transport networks and other strategic assets of Washington's closest Arab allies, according to a senior Iranian official.

The message, part of a broader Iranian effort to deter further U.S. military action by raising the prospect of severe economic disruption across the Gulf, was delivered through a flurry of high-level diplomatic contacts after Trump threatened on July 28 to strike Iran's energy network and infrastructure, the sources said.

Two senior Iranian officials, two Gulf sources and a senior regional diplomat with knowledge of the discussions said Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi spoke with his Saudi, Turkish and Qatari counterparts, as well as Pakistan's army chief. All spoke on condition of anonymity because of the sensitivity of the matter.

Araqchi urged Washington's Gulf allies to use their influence with Trump to dissuade him from launching renewed strikes, the senior diplomat said, warning that any attack on Iran would prompt retaliation against U.S. assets and critical infrastructure across the Gulf.

His message was consistent in every conversation, the diplomat said: "We're ready to retaliate, but finding a diplomatic solution is the best way to avoid wider escalation and destruction across the region."

"The Iranian warning was unequivocal: if America targeted Iran's infrastructure, they would retaliate by striking Gulf energy facilities and other regional targets," one Gulf source said.

The diplomatic campaign underscores how Iran is seeking to turn Gulf vulnerability into leverage, confronting regional governments with the prospect that any renewed U.S.-Iran confrontation could place the infrastructure underpinning their economies directly in the firing line.

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman subsequently spoke with Trump, urging him to delay military action, return to negotiations, secure a ceasefire and pursue a diplomatic settlement, the senior diplomat and Gulf sources said.

The warning was intended for every Gulf state, another Gulf source said, but was conveyed primarily through Saudi Arabia and Qatar. Iran has in recent years repaired ties with its Gulf neighbours, including former regional rival Saudi Arabia.

Qatar, Oman and Saudi Arabia have all urged Washington to resume talks with Tehran and avert renewed conflict amid Iranian warnings that another round of U.S. strikes could "turn the region into a fireball", the second Iranian official said.

On [August 2, ](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-threatens-strike-other-nations-energy-fields-if-us-launches-fresh-attacks-2026-08-01/)Trump said he agreed to cancel the attack on Iran "subject to being able to rapidly make a deal".

### THREATS OF WIDER REGIONAL ESCALATION, SAYS IRANIAN OFFICIAL

"Saudi Arabia believes further escalation will only bring more destruction," the first Gulf source said. "That is why it urged Trump to give diplomacy a chance."

While urging Washington to pursue diplomacy, Riyadh also made clear it would defend itself if threatened. The second Gulf source said Saudi Arabia warned that any attack on its territory would be met with a military response.

Ali Shihabi, a Saudi analyst close to the Royal Court, said the kingdom's assessment is that further escalation is unlikely to achieve the desired result. "The Kingdom believes a combination of proportionate military responses and sustained pressure on Iran in the Strait of Hormuz offers the strongest path toward a practical diplomatic settlement," Shihabi said.

Iran's clerical leadership has repeatedly demanded the closure of U.S. military bases in the Gulf and called on Gulf Arab states to stop sharing intelligence with Washington, which Tehran believes has been used to facilitate attacks against it.

"They still remember Saudi Arabia's Aramco," he added, referring to the [September 2019](https://www.reuters.com/article/world/attacks-on-saudi-oil-facilities-knock-out-half-the-kingdoms-supply-idUSKCN1VZ01Q/) drone and missile attacks on the Abqaiq and Khurais facilities that temporarily knocked out more than half the kingdom's crude oil production and exposed the vulnerability of Gulf energy infrastructure.

"The enemy continues to threaten Iranian infrastructure. If Iran suffers damage, it will inflict far greater damage in return," added the Iranian official who cited the target list including power grids and refineries.

From Tehran's perspective, Trump now faces two unpalatable choices, the senior Iranian official said: escalate a conflict that could engulf the Gulf and disrupt global energy supplies, or accept a negotiated outcome that falls short of the decisive victory Washington seeks.

One of the principal obstacles to an agreement, the senior regional diplomat said, is Washington's reluctance to allow Tehran to emerge claiming victory.

"The United States wants something it can present as proof that it won the war," the diplomat said.
