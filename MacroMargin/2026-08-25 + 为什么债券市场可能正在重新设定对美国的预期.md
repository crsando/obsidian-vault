### 中文译文

## 为什么债券市场可能正在重新设定对美国的预期

![U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent speaks outlines further sanctions against Iran, at the Treasury Department in Washington](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b14816abbabe332fe4650c3/3c781cc9-0b14-813f-8d7c-d97fb7586d35-4c2f40872bd3bd7171668e60635ece5b.jpg)

2026年8月24日，美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特在华盛顿美国财政部举行的新闻发布会上举手，记者们也举手提问，该发布会旨在概述对伊朗的进一步制裁。REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein/文件照片

**摘要**

- 美国年度赤字接近GDP的6%
- 美国债务增速超过经济增速
- 债务利息消耗的GDP份额越来越大
- 美国AI超大规模企业今年已发行2200亿美元债券，与全球储蓄竞争
华盛顿，8月25日（路透社）——美国债务尚未被进一步下调评级，通胀保值债券的盈亏平衡利率没有飙升，防范联邦违约的保险成本也没有暴涨，这些迹象表明，尽管投资者要求更高的利率，但大多数投资者仍将美国债券视为基本无风险的押注。

这是好消息。

不太妙的是，近几周的事态发展使美国联邦债务和赤字置于一个新兴时代的更广阔背景中：全球利率走高、对下一美元贷款资金的竞争更加激烈，以及其他国家同样面临的地缘政治和人口结构逆风。明天可能不会出现危机，但未来某个地方存在悬崖的想法似乎变得更加切实，这一点隐含在财政部长[斯科特·贝森特](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-treasury-stick-debt-auction-schedule-despite-bigger-buybacks-bessent-says-2026-08-24/)突然对积极干预以试图限制长期债券收益率持开放态度的举动中。

以下是此刻似乎有所不同的一些原因：

### 经济增长已落后于借贷

从二战到2010年代末，美国债务动态受益于经济总体增长快于联邦债务，这有助于使借贷相对于国家商品和服务产出规模保持克制。这种关系开始转变，因为用于应对2007-2009年金融危机和大约十年后的新冠疫情大流行的支出。唐纳德·特朗普总统推动并由共和党控制的国会在其第一和第二个白宫任期通过的大规模减税措施加剧了赤字，增加了总债务负担。

### 赤字处于衰退水平

政府财政权力的一个典型用途是在危机时支持民众和经济。在经济衰退期间，随着失业救济金和其他"稳定器"增加，赤字扩大。流入家庭的现金有助于提振需求并缩短低迷期。尤其是在新冠疫情大流行期间，向家庭支付的款项达到历史水平，经济迅速反弹，打破了人们对长期崩溃的担忧。

但赤字——政府支出与税收收入之间的差距——即使在经济增长的情况下，仍保持在接近衰退时期的水平。部分原因在于特朗普的减税措施，但也与如今在老龄化人口上的支出增长已成定局有关，以及[最近逆转](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-supreme-court-rejects-trumps-global-tariffs-2026-02-20/)的关税政策——该逆转迫使政府开始向进口商返还资金。尽管美国上周跨过了成为头条的[40万亿美元](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-debt-crosses-40-trillion-threshold-after-doubling-under-trump-biden-2026-08-19/)债务里程碑，但可以说，更高的年度赤字——目前接近国内生产总值的6%——才对债务可持续性构成更大威胁。经济学家通常认为3%的水平是可管理的。

### 40万亿美元是否太多？

在约8万亿美元的未偿债务总额中，有一部分是政府欠自己的钱，例如从包括面向退休人员和残疾人的社会保障计划在内的各种信托基金“借入”的资金。虽然这仍是美国政府的债务，但其地位与欠公共债权人（包括个人、外国政府和美联储）的32万亿美元债务不同。

这些公共债务目前约占年度GDP的100%。其他国家，尤其是日本，曾承受过远高于此的债务与GDP之比。作为世界主要储备货币的发行国，美国在这方面有一定优势，只要该国被视为稳定、守规矩且遵循法治，这种“过高特权”将有助于维持其债务融资。

但没有人能确切知道，市场对可持续性的看法会在何种程度触及临界点。

给定的债务与GDP之比本身并不一定是好是坏。国家可能借款不足和投资不足，从而抑制经济潜力，也可能过度支出。但公司信用利差和计入债券价格的其他溢价表明，美国相对于竞争债务国的优势已经缩小。

### 一些趋势并不乐观

更广泛的经济趋势有助于区分可持续债务与不可持续债务。国际货币基金组织前首席经济学家奥利维尔·布兰查德认为，只要政府能够以低于经济增长速度的利率借款，债务就可以可持续地循环。

这一简单的经验法则在新冠危机期间被证明具有影响力，为疫情引发的大规模借贷奠定了基础。在全球利率结构性走低时，它也显得正确。

如今利率似乎在结构性上升，对于美国而言，借贷成本已不再舒适地锚定在经济增长速度之下。

一项衡量指标——利息支付占GDP的百分比——说明了问题。与总债务与GDP之比不同，利息支付显示了偿还国家债务所需的国民收入流量，类似于一个家庭查看其工资中有多少份额用于偿还抵押贷款。

在[高赤字](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-july-deficit-tops-432-billion-outlays-grow-tariff-receipts-stay-negative-2026-08-12/)、不断增长的债务和更高利率之间，用于支付利息的GDP占比已翻倍至约3%。

### 为何情况可能不会好转

特朗普和贝森特承诺通过更快的经济增长来解决问题，这相当于一种零成本的债务挑战解决方案。

这个赌注并非万无一失。事件可能介入，意外的冲击加上经济衰退将迫使美国寻求财政能力借入更多资金以支持经济。增长的步伐和来源也很重要。想必他们谈论的是通胀调整后的或“实际”增长，而不是通过更廉价的美元贬值来削减相对债务负担。这意味着增长必须处于经济生产商品和服务能力的约束之内，而不推高物价。

目前对非通胀增长率的估计通常在2%左右或略低于2%，远低于即使政府将年度赤字削减至3%所需抑制相对债务负担的水平。

人工智能可能有助于提高生产能力，但这一跃升的时间线尚不确定。对于一项可能削弱就业的技术，税收影响也未知，即使它推高了股价和企业利润，但在特朗普减税后这些利润现在被征税的力度有所降低，可能导致所得税收入下降。

与此同时，人工智能正在极大增加信贷需求，使超级规模企业与美国在争夺全球储蓄池方面展开竞争，并有助于维持较高的利率。

## Why the bond market may be resetting expectations about the US

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent points as reporters raise their hands during a press conference to outline further sanctions against Iran, at the Treasury Department in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 24, 2026. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein/File Photo

**Summary**

- Annual US deficit near 6% of GDP
- Growth in US debt is outpacing the economy
- Interest on the debt is consuming a larger share of GDP
- US AI hyperscalers have issued $220 billion of debt this year, competing for global savings
WASHINGTON, Aug 25 (Reuters) - U.S. debt hasn't been further downgraded, breakeven rates on inflation-protected bonds haven't spiked, and the cost of insuring against a federal default hasn't skyrocketed, signs that most investors still see U.S. bonds as a largely risk-free bet even if they have been demanding higher interest rates.

That's the good news.

Not so great is that developments in recent weeks have put U.S. federal debts and deficits in the broader context of an emerging era of higher global interest rates, stiffer competition for the next dollar of lending, and global geopolitical and demographic headwinds that other countries are also confronting. There may not be a crisis tomorrow, but the idea of a cliff somewhere in the future seems more tangible, a point implicit in Treasury Secretary [Scott Bessent's](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-treasury-stick-debt-auction-schedule-despite-bigger-buybacks-bessent-says-2026-08-24/) sudden openness to active intervention to try to cap yields on long-term bonds.

Here are some reasons why this moment seems different:

### ECONOMIC GROWTH HAS FALLEN BEHIND BORROWING

From World War Two until the late 2010s, U.S. debt dynamics were helped by the fact that the economy in general grew faster than federal debt, which helped keep borrowing contained relative to the size of the nation's output of goods and services. That relationship began to shift due to the spending used to counter the 2007-2009 financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic roughly a decade later. The sweeping tax cuts pushed by President Donald Trump and passed by the Republican-controlled Congress in his first and second terms in the White House exacerbated the deficits, adding to the total debt pile.

### DEFICITS ARE AT RECESSION LEVELS

One typical use of a government's financial power is to support the population and economy in the event of a crisis. Deficits expand during recessions as unemployment payments and other "stabilizers" rise. The cash funneled to households helps boost demand and shorten the downturn. During the COVID-19 pandemic, in particular, payments to households hit historic levels, and the economy rebounded quickly and defied fears of a lingering collapse.

But deficits, the gap between government spending and tax revenue, have remained near such recession levels even as the economy grew. Part of that is due to Trump's tax cuts, but it is also tied to the now hard-wired growth in spending on an aging population, and the [recent reversal](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-supreme-court-rejects-trumps-global-tariffs-2026-02-20/) of tariffs that forced the administration to begin sending checks back to importers. Though the U.S. crossed the headline-making [$40 trillion](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-debt-crosses-40-trillion-threshold-after-doubling-under-trump-biden-2026-08-19/) debt milestone last week, arguably it's the higher year-to-year annual deficit, now close to 6% of gross domestic product, that presents the bigger threat to debt sustainability. Economists generally think of the 3% level as manageable.

### IS $40 TRILLION TOO MUCH?

About $8 trillion of the total outstanding debt is money the government owes itself, representing, for example, money "borrowed" from various trust funds, including the Social Security program for retirees and the disabled. Though still a U.S. government obligation, it holds a different status than the $32 trillion owed to public creditors, including individuals, foreign governments and the Federal Reserve.

Those public debts now amount to roughly 100% of annual GDP. Other nations, notably Japan, have lived with far higher debt-to-GDP ratios. As the issuer of the world's main reserve currency, the U.S. has certain advantages in that regard, and as long as the country is seen as stable, rule-bound, and in line with the rule of law, that "exorbitant privilege" will help keep its debts funded.

But no one can know for certain where the tipping point is in terms of market perceptions of what's sustainable.

A given debt-to-GDP ratio isn't necessarily good or bad on its own. Countries can underborrow and underinvest, crimping their economic potential, just as they can overspend. But corporate credit spreads and other premiums factoring into bond prices have suggested the U.S. advantage over competing debtors has narrowed.

### SOME OF THE TRENDS ARE NOT GREAT

Broader economic trends can help distinguish sustainable from unsustainable debt. Olivier Blanchard, the former chief economist of the International Monetary Fund, has argued that as long as a government can borrow at an interest rate below the pace of economic growth, debts could be sustainably recycled.

That simple rule of thumb proved influential during the COVID-19 crisis, laying a foundation for the massive borrowing triggered by the pandemic. It also rang true when global interest rates seemed structurally low.

Rates now seem structurally higher, and for the U.S. borrowing costs are no longer anchored comfortably below the rate of economic growth.

One measure — interest payments as a percent of GDP — tells the tale. Unlike overall debt-to-GDP, interest payments show the flow of national income needed to service national debt, similar to a household looking at what share of its paychecks goes to service the mortgage.

Between [high deficits](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-july-deficit-tops-432-billion-outlays-grow-tariff-receipts-stay-negative-2026-08-12/), growing debt, and higher interest rates, the share of GDP going to pay interest has doubled to around 3%.

### WHY IT MAY NOT GET BETTER

Trump and Bessent have promised what amounts to a costless fix to the country's debt challenges through faster economic growth.

That bet isn't a sure thing. Events can intervene, and an unexpected shock followed by a recession would force the U.S. to seek the fiscal capacity to borrow more to support the economy. The pace and sources of growth can also matter. Presumably the two are talking about inflation-adjusted or "real" growth, not trimming the relative debt load by deflating it with cheaper dollars. It means growth must be within the constraints of the economy's capacity to produce goods and services without boosting prices.

Current estimates of the non-inflationary rate of growth are generally around or slightly below 2%, well below what would be needed to curb the relative debt load even if the administration trims the annual deficit to 3%.

Artificial intelligence might help boost productive capacity, but the timeline for that jump is uncertain. The tax implications are also unknown for a technology that could undermine employment, resulting in lower income tax receipts even as it boosts stock prices and corporate profits, now taxed less aggressively after the Trump tax cuts.

And, in the meantime, AI is supercharging the demand for credit, putting the hyperscalers in competition with the U.S. for the pool of global savings and helping sustain higher interest rates.
