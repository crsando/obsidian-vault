### 中文译文

## 中国释放28万亿美元资本市场力量，挑战美国AI主导地位

该战略标志着北京方面摆脱了对补贴和国家资金的依赖。

![A CXMT Corp. facility on the outskirts of Shanghai in July. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-8107-8333-d50341a717e6-8b00483b3ebba22f5295b27ea668282e.png)

*7月，长鑫存储（CXMT Corp.）在上海郊区的一处设施。摄影：沈绮来/彭博社*

### **要点**

- 长鑫存储（CXMT Corp.）在上海的上市首日股价飙升逾500%，成为大陆市值最高的股票，超越了长期居于首位的中国工商银行。
- 监管机构为战略性公司开通了首次公开募股（IPO）快速通道，并开辟了更多通过债券销售融资的渠道，以支持科技公司发展的全过程。
- 过去两年，中国科技公司通过首次公开募股和债券发行筹集了约2170亿美元，当局推广绿色和科技类债券，并敦促银行和投资者支持科技发行人。
即便按照AI时代泡沫化的标准，[长鑫存储（CXMT Corp.）](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity)上个月在上海上市首日的表现也非同寻常。

几小时内，这家存储芯片制造商——被视为北京[最有希望](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-07-09/china-s-cxmt-chipmaker-eyes-ipo-to-challenge-samsung-sk-hynix-micron)减少对外国供应商依赖、在AI领域挑战美国的希望——股价飙升逾500%，成为中国大陆市值最高的股票，超越了多年来占据榜首的中国工商银行。

这一狂潮是该国迄今为调动其 28 万亿美元股票和债券市场力量所采取的最积极努力之一。

监管机构为战略性公司快速推进 IPO，并为其通过债券销售融资开辟了更多渠道。当科技股在 7 月暴跌时，当局以异乎寻常的速度[介入](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/china-broadens-market-rescue-with-record-inflows-into-tech-etf)以恢复信心。据一位知情人士透露，此举并非专门针对支持长鑫存储(CXMT)，但该股票发行是决定的一个因素。该人士因讨论私下审议而要求匿名。

长期以来，资金获取一直是美国在科技领域的最大优势之一。如今，北京正试图缩小这一差距，因为人工智能——也许是现代历史上资本最密集的工业事业——正成为经济增长和军事优势的[新引擎](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/ai-is-new-engine-keeping-chinese-economy-from-harder-landing)。

据彭博汇编的数据，过去两年，中国科技公司通过首次公开募股和债券销售筹集了约 2170 亿美元。它们每筹集 1 美元，美国同行则筹集超过 6 美元，以包括 Amazon.com Inc. 和 Alphabet Inc. 在内的公司为首。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-81ac-bc35-e1b312056370-d6304b0c646abb91263a6f75e4ea85aa.png)

这代表着北京为其战略产业融资方式的转变。中国很少将资本市场作为主要的产业政策工具，而是依靠补贴、税收优惠和国家投资。这一转变开启了获取公民持有的 26 万亿美元——全球最大的家庭储蓄池——的途径，同时中国企业还享有全球一些最廉价的融资。

中国已展示了国家支持与制造实力相结合如何打造出世界领先的产业，正如比亚迪(BYD Co.)领导的电动汽车产业那样。但人工智能是一场更艰难的竞赛，华盛顿正寻求限制北京获取世界上最先进的芯片。

“过去几年，美国公司融资渠道更广，但融资成本正在上升，”塔夫茨大学教授、*芯片战争：争夺世界最关键技术之战*一书的作者克里斯·米勒表示。“如果中国公司在资金获取方面获得持久优势，这将带来优势，尽管由于中国人工智能芯片质量较低，中国国内计算能力仍然昂贵得多。”

### **快速通道 IPO**

北京在长鑫存储（CXMT）首次公开发行前数月就为其扫清了障碍。该公司是第一家通过“[预审](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/shanxi/c106408/c7565872/content.shtml)”试点的企业，该试点专为具有重要战略意义的公司设立，允许监管机构在正式IPO申请前解决关键问题。

从提交申请到上市交易耗时不到八个月——这一过程通常可能需要数年，速度异常之快——募集资金约98亿美元，成为该国多年来最大规模的IPO之一。

然而，此次发行也暴露了一个悖论。长鑫存储（CXMT）首日收盘涨幅达466%，表明股票的定价[远低于](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-27/china-memory-giant-cxmt-s-10-billion-ipo-is-a-flop)买家的心理预期。中国在IPO定价上采取保守做法，虽然保护投资者免受损失，但使这家芯片制造商的资金储备少于竞争对手，例如韩国SK海力士公司（SK Hynix Inc.），后者最近在美国[募集了](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/sk-hynix-is-said-to-price-us-share-offering-at-149-apiece-mrdz562z)265亿美元。

![Minimax CEO Yan Junjie, right, and COO Yun Yeyi during the company’s listing ceremony at the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in January. Photographer: Lam Yik/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-819d-b1a0-d20585ba8f7d-aa246799db42826a14d761a429d63f4c.png)

右图为Minimax首席执行官严俊杰（Yan Junjie）和首席运营官云叶一（Yun Yeyi）于1月在香港证券交易所参加公司上市仪式。摄影：林奕（Lam Yik）/彭博（Bloomberg）

在上市前几天，科技股抛售可能阻碍市场势头。北京方面做出了多年来最广泛的[救市努力](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/china-broadens-market-rescue-with-record-inflows-into-tech-etf)之一，监管机构、国家基金和主要投资者迅速采取行动以稳定市场情绪。

此次干预凸显了一项更广泛的战略。自2025年以来，监管机构建立了一套协调的政策框架，在科技公司发展的整个过程中提供支持，结合银行贷款、债券发行、资本市场和长期投资。中国人民银行、中国证券监督管理委员会和财政部是[参与](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1208683&itemId=928)该努力的机构之一。

据一位知情人士透露，如果中国经济增长在下半年放缓，政策制定者更可能倾向于使用有针对性的货币政策工具，以加大对包括科技创新在内的一些关键领域的支持，而不是推出广泛的刺激措施。

中国证监会和中国人民银行没有立即回应就中国转向资本市场融资以及下半年政策措施置评的请求。

北京认为强大的市场对其科技雄心至关重要。只有投资者相信押注会获得回报，家庭储蓄才会流入战略产业。到目前为止，情况似乎确实如此：以芯片为主的科创50指数在6月创下历史新高，今年上涨30%，而沪深300指数上涨1.4%。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-816e-8357-ef8e30ad41d6-9a8de76b903be0998d1e26e85630a1a8.png)

更多科技公司即将上市。Z.AI Co. 和 MiniMax Group Inc. 在完成香港上市后，正谋求A股上市。月之暗面（Moonshot AI）——其 Kimi K3 模型在硅谷引起涟漪——告诉投资者，它正在[准备](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-19/china-s-moonshot-plans-ipo-in-six-months-after-ai-breakthrough)最早在六个月内上市，而 DeepSeek 已经开始为其首次公开募股[奠定基础](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/deepseek-mulls-new-funding-weeks-after-7-billion-round-ft-says)。

“在美国，超大规模云服务商实际上为人工智能建设的很大一部分提供了资金支持，”莲花资产管理有限公司首席投资官洪灏表示。“在中国，如果这些资金不是来自国家，最终就必须来自市场。”

### **债券市场**

债券市场也在讲述相似的故事。当局推动了绿色债券和科技债券，敦促银行和投资者支持科技发行人，并向更多首次借款人开放市场。

中国科技公司今年已在境内外发行至少380亿美元的债券，是自2016年以来同期最高。但这仅相当于美国同行筹集的5780亿美元的约7%，其中三分之一由亚马逊、Alphabet和SpaceX贡献。

这一推动发生在北京试图在多年债务推动的增长之后抑制杠杆之际。随着经济放缓和地方政府债务缠身，政策制定者正在寻找为关键行业融资的新途径，而不只是依赖国家资产负债表。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-81cd-8a33-f8af650ec896-fa5bc7a561ab1f2ba0bef4147d02b75d.png)

“国家已经不再像过去那样有能力刺激经济，甚至资助其希望支持的所有行业，”独立分析师、*红色资本主义：中国非凡崛起的脆弱金融基础*的合著者弗雷泽·豪伊（Fraser Howie）表示。“还要记住，在中国，当资金来自国家（无论是地方还是中央）时，这意味着这笔钱不会流向学校、医院等。至少在美国，不存在这种权衡。”

据知情人士透露，监管机构已告知金融机构要为科技公司提供稳定的融资，但并未设定硬性贷款目标——即便他们追踪贷款增长，并可能召见落后者要求其作出解释。这些人士因讨论私下事项而要求匿名。

但上述人士表示，银行仍然偏好稳定的现金流和盈利能力，而非研发投入大、亏损中的科技初创企业早期阶段。根据中国人民银行数据，科技贷款占第二季度新增企业贷款的22%，但银行家们表示，其中大部分流向了成熟企业，而非高增长公司。这或许可以解释监管机构为何转向资本市场。

国家金融监督管理总局没有立即回应置评请求。

### **更低成本的融资**

中国相对于美国的一个优势在于能够获得一些全球最廉价的融资。

根据彭博汇编的数据，中国大型科技公司今年发债平均票息率为1.9%，比美国同行低逾300个基点，这是至少自2015年以来的最大差距。这一利差也反映了中国低得多的利率和通胀水平。

全球最大电池制造商宁德时代新能源科技股份有限公司发行了票息率为1.58%的五年期人民币债券，而韩国LG新能源解决方案有限公司发行的类似期限美元债券票息率为5.25%。

![Attendees at the CATL Tech Day in Beijing in April 21. China’s technology and battery stocks have been on a blistering rally. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-8165-82c4-e6679e815afa-3a064a625267aab4550a7cfa1a09771b.png)

“这创造了有意义的竞争优势，”富达国际亚洲固定收益主管朱蕾表示。她说，中国科技公司能以比许多西方同行低得多的成本借款，而所谓的耐心资本——不追逐快速回报的长期资金——的可得性，使它们能够更积极地投资于AI、产能和研究。

较小的公司也在受益。[江苏立泰电子股份有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/603629%20CH%20Equity)是中国东部一家鲜为人知的电子元器件制造商，3月份发行了首批公开债券，筹集2亿元人民币用于购买其AI业务的计算设备。这批三年期债券的票面利率为2.5%。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-8150-8cca-c5e33176b724-306cf2b2272f3b575617031e2579ff83.png)

“非国有企业或新兴行业企业之前很难从中国国内债券市场筹集资金，”立泰电子董事会秘书丁阿静在接受采访时表示。“现在出现了自上而下的明显转变。”

### **投资者资金流向**

股票投资者正在跟随北京的指引。资金已从房地产、消费和其他传统增长板块流出，流入芯片制造商和先进制造商。如今，科技股在沪深300指数中的权重已增长到可以与金融股相匹敌——有时甚至超过金融股。

Allspring Global Investments的投资组合经理Gary Tan警告称，这种热情存在风险。他表示，长鑫存储的交易价格较全球内存同行有显著溢价，这表明在短期内，政策驱动的情绪和稀缺性价值比基本面发挥的作用更大。

![A facility on the outskirts of Shanghai operated by CXMT, China’s top producer of memory chips. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-81c9-a43b-c23058561c98-1b0d6490c906ebf9db32d16cd63bd3cc.png)

长鑫存储（中国最大的内存芯片生产商）在上海郊区的设施。摄影师：沈琪来/彭博社

富达的朱蕾表示，向政策支持的行业大量注入资本还可能导致产能过剩、利润率压缩和仓位过于拥挤。她指出太阳能电池板和电动汽车，在这些领域多年的强力政策支持最终加剧了激烈竞争，并削弱了盈利能力。

悬在中国资本市场推动之上的更深层次问题是，单靠资金能否缩小技术差距。

“资本是必要条件，但不是充分条件，”独立智库安邦（Anbound）的高级经济学家 Hongxu Wei 表示。他说，土地、技术和人才也是资本的形式，真正的挑战在于将突破转化为商业应用。

中国最终可能不需要像美国那样多的资金就能实现类似结果。深度求索（DeepSeek）和月之暗面（Moonshot）等公司表示，他们能以许多西方竞争对手声称成本的一小部分来构建有竞争力的模型。

![The Kimi K3 mobile app on a smartphone. In July Moonshot released the more advanced open-weight model. Photographer: Lam Yik/Bloomberg](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b881cc90b1481c5843fd47bc90d1a0a/3b881cc9-0b14-8149-8293-c474be03315a-a7de0e0ccb46ff1c493f2b0399ab3ed3.png)

智能手机上的 Kimi K3 应用。7月，月之暗面发布了更先进的开放权重模型。摄影：Lam Yik/彭博社

瑞银集团（UBS Group AG）估计，中国模型的训练成本不到 OpenAI、Anthropic PBC 等全球领先者的10%，而中国主要模型的平均 API 价格低于全球同类同行者的20%。

这些效率可能成为中国的最大优势之一。北京或许无须试图在创新上超越美国，而是依托其制造业基础、深厚供应链和工程人才，通过大规模工业化和商业化 AI 来缩小差距。

研究中国技术与产业政策的布鲁金斯学会（Brookings Institution）研究员 Kyle Chan 表示：“最终决定美中科技竞争格局的，是对创新和产业能力的大规模、持续投资。同时具备这些要素的国家将拥有持久优势。”

## China Unleashes $28 Trillion Capital Markets to Challenge US in AI

The strategy marks a break from Beijing’s reliance on subsidies and state funding.

*A CXMT Corp. facility on the outskirts of Shanghai in July. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg*

### **Takeaways**

- CXMT Corp.'s trading debut in Shanghai surged more than 500% to become the most valuable stock in mainland China, eclipsing Industrial and Commercial Bank of China Ltd.
- Regulators have fast-tracked IPOs for strategic companies and opened more avenues for them to raise money through bond sales to support tech companies throughout their development.
- Chinese tech firms have raised about $217 billion through initial public offerings and bond sales over the past two years, with authorities promoting green and tech-focused bonds and urging banks and investors to back sci-tech issuers.
Even by the frothy standards of the AI era, [CXMT Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/688825%20CH%20Equity)'s trading debut in Shanghai last month was extraordinary.

Within a few hours, the memory chip maker — seen as Beijing’s [best hope](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-07-09/china-s-cxmt-chipmaker-eyes-ipo-to-challenge-samsung-sk-hynix-micron) of reducing reliance on foreign suppliers and challenging the US in AI — surged more than 500% to become the most valuable stock in mainland China, eclipsing Industrial and Commercial Bank of China Ltd., which held the top spot for years.

The frenzy was the culmination of one of the country’s most aggressive efforts yet to marshal the power of its $28 trillion stock and bond markets.

Regulators have fast-tracked IPOs for strategic companies and opened more avenues for them to raise money through bond sales. When tech stocks tumbled in July, authorities [intervened](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/china-broadens-market-rescue-with-record-inflows-into-tech-etf) with unusual speed to restore confidence. While the move was not aimed specifically at supporting CXMT, the stock launch was a factor in the decision, according to a person with knowledge of the matter, who asked not to be identified discussing private deliberations.

Access to capital has long been one of America’s biggest advantages in technology. Now Beijing is trying to close that gap, as artificial intelligence — perhaps the most capital-intensive industrial undertaking in modern history — emerges as the [next engine](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-20/ai-is-new-engine-keeping-chinese-economy-from-harder-landing) of economic growth and military advantage.

Chinese tech firms raised about $217 billion through initial public offerings and bond sales over the past two years, according to data compiled by Bloomberg. For every $1 they secured, US peers raised more than $6, led by companies including Amazon.com Inc. and Alphabet Inc.

It represents a change in how Beijing finances its strategic industries. China has rarely used capital markets as a major industrial policy tool, relying instead on subsidies, tax incentives and state investment. The shift opens access to the $26 trillion held by citizens — the world’s largest pool of household savings — while Chinese companies also enjoy some of the cheapest funding globally.

China has already shown how a mix of state support and manufacturing prowess can build world-beating industries, as it did with electric vehicles led by BYD Co. But AI is a more difficult race, with Washington seeking to restrict Beijing’s access to the world’s most advanced chips.

“Over the past few years, US firms have had greater access to capital, but financing costs are rising,” said Chris Miller, a professor at Tufts University and author of *Chip War: The Fight for the World’s Most Critical Technology*. “If Chinese firms secure a durable advantage in capital access, this would provide an advantage, though Chinese domestic compute is still substantially more expensive because of the lower quality of Chinese AI chips.”

### **Fast-Tracked IPO**

Beijing cleared the runway for CXMT months before its debut. The company was the first to go through a “[preliminary review](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/shanxi/c106408/c7565872/content.shtml)” pilot reserved for strategically important firms, allowing regulators to resolve key issues before a formal IPO application.

It went from filing to trading in less than eight months — unusually swift for a process that could take years — and raised about $9.8 billion in one of the country’s biggest IPOs in years.

However, the offering also exposed a paradox. CXMT’s shares closed 466% higher on their first day, suggesting the share sale was priced [well below](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-07-27/china-memory-giant-cxmt-s-10-billion-ipo-is-a-flop) what buyers were willing to pay. China’s practice of conservatively pricing IPOs shields investors from losses but leaves the chipmaker with a smaller war chest than rivals such as South Korea’s SK Hynix Inc., which recently [raised](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-09/sk-hynix-is-said-to-price-us-share-offering-at-149-apiece-mrdz562z) $26.5 billion in the US.

Minimax CEO Yan Junjie, right, and COO Yun Yeyi during the company’s listing ceremony at the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in January. Photographer: Lam Yik/Bloomberg

Days before the debut, a selloff in tech shares threatened to derail momentum. Beijing responded with one of its broadest [rescue efforts](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/china-broadens-market-rescue-with-record-inflows-into-tech-etf) in years, with regulators, state funds and major investors moving quickly to stabilize sentiment.

The intervention underscored a broader strategy. Since 2025, regulators have built a coordinated policy framework to support tech companies throughout their development, combining bank lending, bond issuance, capital markets and long-term investment. The People’s Bank of China, China Securities Regulatory Commission and Ministry of Finance are among the agencies [behind](https://www.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1208683&amp%3BitemId=928) the effort.

If China's economic growth falters in the second half, policymakers are more likely to favor targeted monetary tools to increase support for some key areas including tech innovation, rather than roll out broad-based stimulus, according to a person familiar with the matter.

The CSRC and PBOC didn’t immediately respond to requests for comment on China’s shift toward capital market financing and on second-half policy measures.

Beijing sees strong markets as essential to its tech ambitions. Household savings will only flow into strategic industries if investors believe the bets will pay off. So far, it appears they do: the chip-heavy STAR 50 Index hit a record high in June and is up 30% this year, compared with 1.4% for the CSI 300.

More tech listings are on the way. Z.AI Co. and MiniMax Group Inc. are pursuing A-share listings after their Hong Kong debuts. Moonshot AI — whose Kimi K3 model sent ripples through Silicon Valley — told investors it is [preparing](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-19/china-s-moonshot-plans-ipo-in-six-months-after-ai-breakthrough) to go public in as early as six months, while DeepSeek has begun [laying the groundwork](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/deepseek-mulls-new-funding-weeks-after-7-billion-round-ft-says) for its own IPO.

“In the US, hyperscalers are effectively underwriting much of the AI buildout,” said Hong Hao, chief investment officer at Lotus Asset Management Ltd. “In China, if that doesn’t come from the state, the capital ultimately has to come from the market.”

### **Bond Market**

The bond market tells a similar story. Authorities have promoted green and tech-focused bonds, urged banks and investors to back sci-tech issuers, and opened the market to more first-time borrowers.

Chinese tech companies have sold at least $38 billion of onshore and offshore bonds this year, the most for the same period since 2016. But that’s only about 7% of the $578 billion raised by their US counterparts, a third of it by Amazon, Alphabet and SpaceX.

The push comes as Beijing tries to rein in leverage after years of debt-fueled growth. With the economy slowing and local governments saddled with debt, policymakers are looking for new ways to fund critical industries without depending solely on the state’s balance sheet.

“The state simply no longer has the same capacity as it once did to stimulate the economy or even fund all the sectors it wants,” said Fraser Howie, an independent analyst and co-author of *Red Capitalism: The Fragile Financial Foundation of China’s Extraordinary Rise*. “Remember as well that funding in China when coming from the state, local or central, means that is money not going to schools, hospitals etc. At least in the US, that is not the tradeoff.”

Regulators have told financial institutions to provide stable financing to tech companies but stopped short of setting hard lending targets — even as they track loan growth and can call in laggards to explain themselves, according to people with knowledge of the matter, who asked not to be identified discussing private matters.

But banks still prefer stable cash flows and profitability to the R&D-heavy, loss-making early stages of tech startups, the people said. While tech loans made up 22% of new corporate lending in the second quarter, according to PBOC data, bankers said most went to mature companies rather than high-growth firms. That may explain why regulators are turning to capital markets.

The National Financial Regulatory Administration didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment.

### **Cheaper Funding**

One edge China holds over the US is access to some of the world’s cheapest funding.

Major Chinese tech companies are borrowing at an average bond coupon of 1.9% this year, more than 300 basis points below their US peers, according to data compiled by Bloomberg — the widest gap since at least 2015. The spread also reflects China's much lower interest rates and inflation.

Contemporary Amperex Technology Co., the world’s largest battery maker, issued five-year yuan [notes](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/DN600660%40CHBE%20Corp) with a 1.58% coupon, compared with the 5.25% paid by South Korea’s LG Energy Solution Ltd. on a dollar [bond](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/LGENSO%205%C2%BC%2004%7C02%7C2031%20REGS%20Corp) of similar tenor.

Attendees at the CATL Tech Day in Beijing in April 21. China’s technology and battery stocks have been on a blistering rally. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg

“This creates a meaningful competitive advantage,” said Zhu Lei, head of Asian fixed income at Fidelity International. Chinese tech firms can borrow far more cheaply than many Western peers, she said, while the availability of so-called patient capital — long-term money not chasing quick returns — allows them to invest more aggressively in AI, capacity and research.

Smaller companies are also benefiting. [Jiangsu Lettall Electronic Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/603629%20CH%20Equity), a little-known electrical components maker in eastern China, sold its first public bonds in March, raising 200 million yuan to buy computing equipment for its AI business. The three-year notes carried a 2.5% coupon.

“It was quite difficult for non-state owned enterprises or those in emerging sectors to raise funds from China’s domestic bond market,” Ajing Ding, Lettall’s board secretary, said in an interview. “There’s a clear shift from the top down.”

### **Investor Flows**

Equity investors are following Beijing’s lead. Money has flowed out of property, consumer and other traditional growth sectors into chipmakers and advanced manufacturers. Today, tech’s weighting in the CSI 300 has grown to rival — and at times surpass — that of financials.

There are risks to the enthusiasm, warned Gary Tan, a portfolio manager at Allspring Global Investments. CXMT, he said, is trading at a significant premium to global memory peers, suggesting policy-driven sentiment and scarcity value are playing a larger role than fundamentals in the near term.

A facility on the outskirts of Shanghai operated by CXMT, China’s top producer of memory chips. Photographer: Qilai Shen/Bloomberg

Flooding policy-backed sectors with capital can also result in excess capacity, margin compression and overcrowded positioning, according to Fidelity’s Zhu, who flagged solar panels and electric vehicles, where years of strong policy support ultimately fueled intense competition and weaker profitability.

The deeper question hanging over China’s capital markets push is whether money alone can close the tech gap.

“Capital is a necessary condition, but it is not a sufficient one,” said Hongxu Wei, a senior economist at Anbound, an independent think tank. Land, technology and talent are also forms of capital, and the real challenge is in turning breakthroughs into commercial applications, he said.

China may ultimately require less money than the US to achieve similar outcomes. Companies such as DeepSeek and Moonshot said they can build competitive models at a fraction of the cost claimed by many Western rivals.

The Kimi K3 mobile app on a smartphone. In July Moonshot released the more advanced open-weight model. Photographer: Lam Yik/Bloomberg

UBS Group AG estimates the training costs for China’s models are less than 10% of those of global leaders such as OpenAI and Anthropic PBC, while the average API price for major China models is below 20% of comparable global peers.

Those efficiencies could prove to be one of China’s biggest advantages. Instead of trying to out-innovate the US, Beijing may be able to narrow the gap by industrializing and commercializing AI at scale, drawing on its manufacturing base, deep supply chains and engineering talent.

“What will ultimately shape US-China tech competition is large-scale, sustained investment in both innovation and industrial capacity,” said Kyle Chan, a fellow at the Brookings Institution who researches China’s technology and industrial policy. “The country with all of these ingredients will have an enduring advantage.”
