### 中文译文

## 消息人士称，香港交易所探索将GEM与主板合并

## ***独家***香港交易所探索将GEM与主板合并，消息人士称

#### 交易所运营商正探索制定一章上市规则，以吸纳GEM板块300多家公司

![Exchange Square in Central, where the Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing is located. Photo: Nora Tam](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b14810b844edb595cf6d626/3c981cc9-0b14-8123-8648-e0d43552b2ab-6cae33c100185fef4eba896a2a43fa3a.jpg)

[**姚志仁**](https://www.scmp.com/author/enoch-yiu)

发布时间：2026年8月27日下午3:22 更新时间：2026年8月27日晚上7:05

据知情人士透露，交易所运营商香港交易及结算所有限公司（港交所）正探索通过增设上市规则新章节的方式，将GEM板块与主板合并。

该消息人士向《南华早报》表示，设立第18D章的提议将成为上市制度第二阶段审查的核心部分，并将在年底前进行公众咨询。

消息人士称，将GEM与主板合并是重振表现不佳的GEM板块的一个选项，因为该二板市场成交低迷，新上市数量极少。

“关于改革GEM的研究已进行很长时间，因为该二板市场在允许小型公司上市方面效果不佳，”消息人士说。“允许这些较小规模企业在特定章节上市……可能是一个更好的选择，”该消息人士称。

自2018年改革以来，港交所针对无法满足一般要求的公司，推出了针对不同公司需求量身定制的上市规则新章节。

这些章节包括针对未有收入的生物科技公司的18A章、针对特殊目的收购公司的18B章，以及针对未有收入的大型科技公司的18C章。

![Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing is exploring merging its GEM board with its main board. Photo: Nora Tam](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c981cc90b14810b844edb595cf6d626/3c981cc9-0b14-81d8-896b-c7926f6019dd-df38e96f486dde760fa9cc57287e58ca.jpg)

香港交易所正在探讨将其创业板与主板合并。图片来源：Nora Tam

消息人士称，拟议中的第18D章将允许无法满足盈利要求的新成立的小型公司上市。根据新章节，创业板现有的约300家公司可能获准转入主板。

消息人士表示：“取代创业板的新章节将扩大港交所上市公司的范围，同时也有助于小型企业获得融资。该改革将有助于提高整体市场多样性，并惠及实体经济。”

港交所发言人表示，该交易所正在探讨与其上市制度相关的进一步新措施。

发言人在一份声明中表示：“港交所很高兴已落实上市框架竞争力检讨的第一阶段，该检讨获得了市场的大力支持，并提升了香港作为顶级融资目的地的吸引力。”

他说：“在取得这一进展的基础上，我们将继续探讨其他措施，以增强上市框架的吸引力，确保其仍然适用，并将在适当时候适时公布最新进展。”

创业板（GEM）前称为“增长型企业市场”，于1999年设立。创业板不设盈利要求，而主板则要求公司在上市前三年内至少盈利8000万港元。

截至周四，创业板共有306家公司，总市值780亿港元（约99亿美元），远低于香港主板的数据——主板由2455家公司组成，总市值达47.05万亿港元。

周四，创业板成交额仅1.25亿港元，约为主板成交额的0.05%。

今年上半年，创业板仅有一家公司新上市，筹资962万美元。相比之下，主板有79宗新上市，合共筹资260亿美元——为全球第二高，仅次于纳斯达克。

“每年只有一两家创业板IPO，而主板市场非常活跃，这表明创业板未能充分服务初创企业和其他较小的成长型公司，”香港会计师公会（HKICPA）会长、私募股权投资者罗卓坚（Stephen Law Cheuk-kin）表示。

“香港需要进行真正的创业板改革，而不是表面上的重新品牌化，”他补充称，并指出此前对创业板进行的几次改革都未奏效。

罗卓坚认为，任何创业板改革要想成功，都需要为该板块设定更清晰的目标，包括相称的上市标准、恰当的披露与合规体制、更好的流动性安排，以及为企业转入主板提供明确路径。

“如果主板内的新板块能够实现这些成果，那将比保留一个已失去市场势头的独立创业板标签更好，”罗卓坚说。

证券商协会名誉主席陈柏霖（Tom Chan Pak-lam）也支持该提议。

“这是一个明智的演进举措，承认创业板作为中小企业融资的独立板块并未运作良好，”陈柏霖表示。

“创业板的问题从来都不只是结构性的。流动性一直不佳，许多创业板上市公司在盈利能力上挣扎，零售和机构投资者的兴趣也有限。”

但陈柏霖表示，简单地将现有创业板公司移至上市规则的新章节不足以吸引投资者并鼓励研究分析师覆盖该板块。他补充说，需要适当的监管和推广，以吸引初创公司通过新机制筹集资金。

陈柏霖表示，交易所需要确保新的上市规则第18D章有一个平衡的监管方式，以吸引优质小公司上市。

### English Original

## Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing explores merging GEM with main board, source says

## ***Exclusive*****Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing explores merging GEM with main board, source says**

#### The bourse operator is exploring creating a chapter listing rule to absorb 300-plus firms from the GEM board

[**Enoch Yiu**](https://www.scmp.com/author/enoch-yiu)

Published: 3:22pm, 27 Aug 2026Updated: 7:05pm, 27 Aug 2026

Bourse operator Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing (HKEX) is exploring merging the GEM board with its main board by creating a new chapter of its listing rules, according to a source familiar with the discussions.

The proposal to create Chapter 18D, which would be a core part of the second phase of the review of the listing regime, would be subject to a public consultation by the end of the year, the source told the South China Morning Post.

Merging it with the main board was an option to reboot the poorly performing GEM, the source said, as the second board was seeing minimal turnover and fewer new listings.

“There have been studies on reforming the GEM for a long time, as the second board did not really work well in terms of allowing smaller-sized companies to list,” the source said. “Allowing these smaller players to list in a specific chapter … may be a better option,” the source said.

Since its 2018 reform, HKEX has introduced new specific chapters of listing rules, tailored to different companies’ needs, for firms that could not meet general requirements.

The chapters include 18A, for pre-revenue biotechnology, 18B for special purpose acquisition companies and 18C for large technology companies without revenue.

Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing is exploring merging its GEM board with its main board. Photo: Nora Tam

The proposal for a Chapter 18D would allow newly established, small-sized companies that could not meet the profit requirement to list, the source said. The existing 300 or so firms on GEM may be grandfathered in to join the main board under the new chapter.

“The new chapter to replace the GEM would widen the scope of the listed companies at the HKEX, while it would also help the smaller firms to get funding. The reform would help to improve the overall market diversity and also benefit the real economy,” the source said.

A spokesman for HKEX said the exchange was exploring further new measures related to its listing regime.

“HKEX is pleased to have implemented the first phase of the listing framework competitiveness review, which drew strong market support and enhanced Hong Kong’s attractiveness as a premier fundraising destination,” the spokesman said in a statement.

“Building on this progress, we will continue to explore additional measures to strengthen the attractiveness of our listing framework to ensure it remains fit for purpose, and will share updates as appropriate in due course,” he said.

The GEM, formerly known as the Growth Enterprise Market, was established in 1999. GEM does not have a profit requirement, while the main board requires a company to make at least HK$80 million in the three years before its listing.

As of Thursday, the GEM included 306 firms with a combined market capitalisation of HK$78 billion (US$9.9 billion) – far below the figures for Hong Kong’s main board, which comprises 2,455 companies with a market capitalisation of HK$47.05 trillion.

On Thursday, turnover on the GEM was just HK$125 million, about 0.05 per cent of the turnover on the main board.

In the first half of the year, the GEM only saw one new company listing, which raised US$9.62 million. The main board, by contrast, hosted 79 new listings that raised a combined US$26 billion – the second-highest total in the world, behind only the Nasdaq.

“With only one or two GEM IPOs every year, against a very active main board market, it shows the GEM is not adequately serving start-ups and other smaller growth companies,” said Stephen Law Cheuk-kin, president of the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA) and a private-equity investor.

“Hong Kong needs a genuine GEM board reform rather than a cosmetic rebranding,” he added, noting that several previous reforms to the GEM had not worked.

To be successful, any reforms to the GEM would need to give the board a clearer purpose, with proportionate admission criteria, a proper disclosure and compliance regime, better liquidity arrangements, and a clear pathway for companies to graduate to the main board, according to Law.

“If a new chapter within the main board can achieve those outcomes, it would be better than preserving a separate GEM label that has lost market momentum,” Law said.

Tom Chan Pak-lam, honorary president of the Institute of Securities Dealers, also supports the proposal.

“It is a sensible evolutionary move that acknowledges GEM has not worked well as a stand-alone board for small and medium-sized enterprise fundraising,” Chan said.

“GEM’s problems were never purely structural. Liquidity has been poor, while many GEM-listed firms have struggled with profitability and interest from both retail and institutional investors has been limited.”

But Chan said simply relocating existing GEM companies to a new chapter of the listing rules would not be enough to attract investors and encourage research analysts to cover the board. Proper regulation and promotion would be needed to attract start-ups to raise funds via the new mechanism, he added.

Chan said the exchange would need to make sure the new listing chapter 18D had a balanced regulatory approach to attract listings from quality small companies.
