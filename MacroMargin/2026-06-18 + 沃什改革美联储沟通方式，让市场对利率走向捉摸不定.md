## 沃什改革美联储沟通方式，让市场对利率走向捉摸不定

### 在首次以主席身份亮相时，凯文·沃什剥离了央行的政策暗示，承诺降低通胀——但不愿说明需要付出什么代价

Nick Timiraos

2026年6月17日晚上9:42更新

![Kevin Warsh speaking at a news conference following a Federal Open Market Committee meeting.](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/38381cc90b14811a9fcaefb5dd1582e2/38381cc9-0b14-81b0-9d29-fc9e5407471b-dbf68fdc3e69b413f9b6f715fa4bd796.jpg)

美联储主席凯文·沃什拒绝提供自己的利率预测。Al Drago/Bloomberg News

[凯文·沃什](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/kevin-warsh)利用[他的第一次会议](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/federal-reserve-interest-rate-decision-bd700510?mod=article_inline)作为[美联储](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/federal-reserve)主席，为央行的运作和沟通方式打上了自己的印记。

他精简了政策声明，拒绝提交自己的利率预测，并成立了五个工作组，研究从美联储如何沟通到如何分析经济等一系列问题——这是对央行运作方式的全面介入。

关于市场最想了解的事情——新主席如何解读经济并将其转化为政策——他几乎只字未提。随后在[通胀](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/inflation)、政策是否具有限制性以及所谓利率点阵图的未来等问题上受到追问时，沃什一再回避。“我们有一个工作组负责这个。”

有一点他态度坚定：委员会团结一致，决心降低通胀。“我们已经错过了五年，我们将解决这个问题，”他说，并承诺委员会将“明确且一致地”实现物价稳定。

但他拒绝说明这是否意味着加息。“好消息是我们将在六周后开会。”

通过取消所谓的前瞻性指引（即口头或书面暗示），并且[不参与预测](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/fed-meeting-warsh-interest-rate-06-17-2026?mod=article_inline)，沃什搁置了前几任用来引导预期的工具。这使得委员会的走向成为一个悬而未决的问题，而此时其成员意见分歧。近一半人认为利率应在年底前维持当前水平，而另一半则认为应该更高。

投资者从沃什关于降低通胀的承诺以及分歧的点阵图中得出了显而易见的结论。根据芝加哥商品交易所（CME Group）追踪的期货定价，在决策公布后，美联储9月会议加息的可能性从周二约30%跃升至50%以上。

但他们无法读懂的是沃什本人。他[长期主张](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/fed-warsh-chair-communication-d2f2d226?mod=article_inline)美联储说得太多，而周三他开始践行这一点。但在几位美联储观察人士看来，他将一个合理的反对意见扩大化了，不仅抛弃了对下一步行动的预测，也抛弃了委员会将如何推理出决策的任何说明。

摩根大通（JPMorgan）首席美国经济学家迈克尔·费罗利（Michael Feroli）表示，告诉市场美联储在下次会议上可能做什么是一回事，而解释其应对经济过热或物价上涨的框架则是另一回事。“这不是前瞻指引，而是拥有一个框架，”他说。

他认为，沃什利用对前者的反对来悄悄回避后者。费罗利表示，在新闻发布会结束时，他仍然不清楚委员会现在如何推理政策。“了解这个委员会如何思考其业务方式很重要，”他说。

这种解释在主要央行中很常见。英国央行和欧洲央行都会发布详细的决策理由说明，而沃什的三位前任在会后新闻发布会上也主要以此为用途。

在一些市场老手看来，改革的部分内容早该进行了。一些人称赞沃什重申美联储将把通胀率降至2%的目标，并称赞其声明措辞朴实无华，而在多年充斥着行话且往往只是重申显而易见之事（美联储将观察数据并适时调整）的表述后，朴实成了一种优点。

贝莱德固定收益首席投资官里克·里德表示，此次会议“开启了美国货币政策的新时代”。特朗普今年曾就沃什获得的职位面试过里德。里德表示，如果减少沟通能建立信心，那么这可能会降低市场波动。

不过，沃什仍然像一个黑匣子，周三的会议几乎未能打开它。他是他在美联储首任期间及之后所表现出的通胀鹰派，还是如他在去年获得主席职位之前所表现出的那样同情低利率？“我仍然不知道他是鹰派还是鸽派，”费罗利说。

法国巴黎银行首席美国经济学家詹姆斯·埃格尔霍夫表示，未来几周真正的意外可能是沃什在抗击通胀方面比美联储前主席杰罗姆·鲍威尔更积极。他预计美联储将从12月开始三次加息，扭转去年所有的降息。

《华尔街日报》的尼克·蒂米劳斯解读新任美联储主席凯文·沃什的首次新闻发布会。图片来源：Brendan Smialowski/AFP/Getty Images

其他人则认为，考虑到他接手的委员会决心在又一年中意外冲击威胁再次推高物价后消除通胀，沃什的意愿不那么重要。他们将工作组视为争取时间、重建与委员会信任的一种方式，而他在主席之路上多年来一直批评该委员会的决定，并希望尽早获得流程上的胜利。

沃什“现在必须以某种方式将一系列旨在获得这份工作的竞选承诺转化为说服委员会，使所有这些承诺与一个技术官僚、独立的美联储的所作所为相一致，”前美国银行全球经济研究主管伊桑·哈里斯（Ethan Harris）表示。

美联储前高级顾问威廉·英格利希（William English）表示，委员会通过共识运作，“主席必须哄着每个人一起行动。”如果沃什想转向他去年倡导的较低利率，他预计沃什会提出自己的观点，接受这些观点目前还无法在会议中获得支持，并继续努力“逐步让委员会加入自己的阵营”。

## Warsh Overhauls How the Fed Talks and Keeps Markets Guessing on Rates

## Warsh Overhauls How the Fed Talks and Keeps Markets Guessing on Rates

### In debut as chairman, Kevin Warsh stripped away the central bank’s policy hints and committed to bringing inflation down—but wouldn’t say what that will take

By

Nick Timiraos

Updated June 17, 2026 9:42 pm ET

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh declined to offer his own interest-rate forecast. Al Drago/Bloomberg News

[Kevin Warsh](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/kevin-warsh) used [his first meeting](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/federal-reserve-interest-rate-decision-bd700510?mod=article_inline) as [Federal Reserve](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/federal-reserve) chairman to put his stamp on how the central bank operates and communicates.

He trimmed its policy statement, declined to submit a rate forecast of his own, and launched five task forces to study everything from how the Fed communicates to how it analyzes the economy—a sweeping reach into how the central bank operates.

About the things markets most wanted to understand—how the new chairman reads the economy and translates it into policy—he said almost nothing. Pressed later on [inflation](https://www.wsj.com/topics/subject/inflation), on whether policy was restrictive and on the future of the so-called interest-rate dot plot, Warsh repeatedly demurred. “We have a task force for that.”

On one point he was emphatic: The committee was united and determined to bring inflation down. “We’ve missed for five years, and we’re going to fix that,” he said, pledging that the committee would “unambiguously and unanimously” deliver price stability.

But he declined to say whether that meant raising rates. “The good news is we’ll be meeting in six weeks.”

By removing the so-called forward guidance (i.e., verbal or written hints) and [sitting out the projections](https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/fed-meeting-warsh-interest-rate-06-17-2026?mod=article_inline), Warsh put aside the tools his predecessors used to steer expectations. That left the committee’s direction an open question at a moment when its members are split down the middle. Nearly half thought rates should be at the current level by year-end, while the other half thought they should be higher.

Investors read Warsh’s pledge to bring inflation down and the divided dot plot and drew the obvious inference. The probability of a rate increase by the Fed’s September meeting jumped above 50% after the decision, from about 30% on Tuesday, according to futures pricing tracked by CME Group.

What they couldn’t read was Warsh himself. He [has long argued](https://www.wsj.com/economy/central-banking/fed-warsh-chair-communication-d2f2d226?mod=article_inline) the Fed says too much, and on Wednesday he began making good on it. But to several Fed watchers, he had stretched a defensible objection into something broader, discarding not just predictions about the next move but any account of how the committee will reason its way to a decision.

Telling markets what the Fed might do at its next meeting is one thing, said Michael Feroli, chief U.S. economist at JPMorgan. Explaining the framework by which it responds to a hotter economy or rising prices is another. “That’s not forward guidance. That’s having a framework,” he said.

Warsh, he argued, had used objections to the former to quietly sidestep the latter. By the end of the news conference, Feroli said, he was no closer to knowing how the committee now reasoned about policy. “It matters to have some sense of how this committee is thinking about how it goes about conducting this business,” he said.

That kind of explanation is standard among major central banks. The Bank of England and the European Central Bank both publish detailed accounts of their reasoning, and Warsh’s three immediate predecessors used the postmeeting news conference largely for that purpose.

To some market veterans, parts of the overhaul were overdue. Several credited Warsh for reasserting that the Fed would bring inflation down to its 2% target and for a statement whose plainness was a virtue after years of jargon-laden prose that often did little more than restate the obvious: that the Fed would watch the incoming data and adjust as needed.

The meeting “ushered in a new era of monetary policy” in the U.S., said Rick Rieder, [BlackRock’s](https://www.wsj.com/market-data/quotes/BLK) chief investment officer for fixed income, whom President Trump interviewed this year for the job that went to Warsh. Rieder said less communication could reduce volatility if it builds confidence.

Still, Warsh remained something of a black box that Wednesday did little to open. Is he the inflation hawk he was during and after his first tour at the Fed or the sympathizer of lower rates, as he presented himself as last year before winning the chairman’s job? “I still don’t know if he’s a hawk or a dove,” Feroli said.

The real surprise in the weeks ahead might be that Warsh proves himself more willing to fight inflation than the Fed had under former Chair Jerome Powell, said James Egelhof, chief U.S. economist at BNP Paribas. He expects the Fed to lift rates three times beginning in December, reversing all of last year’s cuts.

WSJ’s Nick Timiraos breaks down the first press conference from new Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh. Photo: Brendan Smialowski/AFP/Getty Images

Others say that it matters less what Warsh wants given the committee he is inheriting is determined to stamp out inflation after another year in which an unexpected shock is threatening [to push prices up again](https://www.wsj.com/economy/cpi-inflation-report-may-2026-60db25fe?mod=article_inline). They see the task forces as a way to buy time and rebuild trust with a committee whose decisions he had spent years criticizing on his way to the chairmanship and to bank early process wins.

Warsh “has to now somehow get a set of campaign promises that were aimed at getting the job and switch to convincing the committee that all of those promises are consistent with what a technocratic, independent Fed would do,” said Ethan Harris, the former head of global economics research at Bank of America.

The committee works by consensus, and “the chair’s got to jolly everybody along,” said William English, a former senior Fed adviser. If Warsh wanted to steer toward the lower rates he advocated for last year, he expects Warsh to make his arguments, accept that they won’t carry the room yet and keep working “to get the committee on board over time.”
