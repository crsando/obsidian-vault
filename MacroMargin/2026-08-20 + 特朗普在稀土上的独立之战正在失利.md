> 📌 **核心摘要**
>
> 特朗普政府在减少对中国稀土等关键矿产依赖方面的努力因效率低下、信誉缺失而失败。
> •美国以撤回关税威胁换取中国稀土出口管制停火一年，但实际成效有限。
> •美国政府投入约300亿美元支持相关企业，但存在任人唯亲和效率问题。
> •美国试图通过双边胁迫协议和组建国际联盟，如与印尼、刚果（金）等，但缺乏法律和经济可信度。
> •中国通过多年集中整合，形成严格控制的关键矿产供应链，优势明显。

**🔑 关键词**：稀土、特朗普、关键矿产

### 中文译文

## 特朗普在稀土上的独立之战正在失利

![Workers dispose of nickel waste at a site in Indonesia. Donald Trump’s shambolic attempt to coerce the country into supplying the US will not override Indonesia’s relationship with China © Muchtamir Zaide/AFP/Getty Images](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A2a45710f-bb46-4828-9953-a7a911b80a81?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

工人在印度尼西亚的一个地点处理镍废料。唐纳德·特朗普试图胁迫该国向美国供应镍的混乱做法，不会压倒印度尼西亚与中国的关系。© Muchtamir Zaide/AFP/Getty Images

[Alan Beattie](https://www.ft.com/alan-beattie)

在即使按唐纳德·特朗普的标准也堪称羞辱的退让中，美国总统去年11月撤回了对中国的关税威胁，以换取北京同意在稀土和其他矿物的出口管制上停火一年。其政府表示，此后一直利用这段时间来提高自身的矿物产量，并从盟友那里确保供应，试图削弱北京在关键原材料方面极其强大的影响力。

这种说法被极度夸大了。与中国对抗不仅需要巨大的财力来支持企业并向生产商提供有保证的价格，还需要对生产效率——以及建立一个持久的国际联盟来获取美国没有的矿物——做出无情承诺。相反，这届政府展示了其一贯的任人唯亲、笨拙的胁迫企图以及反复无常的美国优先外交政策。这粉碎了它在主导国际矿物协议方面的可信度。

当然，确实出现了一大批活动，既有金融方面的，也有官僚方面的。这届政府采取了非同寻常的举措，入股多家公司，并提供一系列令人眼花缭乱的采购担保、信贷支持、贷款和赠款，[据称合计达300亿美元](https://www.cfr.org/articles/u-s-allies-aim-to-break-chinas-critical-minerals-dominance?utm_source=chatgpt.com)。最强有力的海外举措是与澳大利亚去年10月签署的一项[协议](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/united-states-australia-framework-securing-supply-mining-and-processing-critical-minerals-and-rare-earths)，该协议促成了美国对那里的[采矿项目投资](https://www.ft.com/content/d88b7e46-6806-4286-94d7-3bb16facee41?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。

但除了澳大利亚和一两个容易受到压力的国家（如刚果民主共和国）外，美国通常只部署了强制性的单边双边贸易协定，并寻求召集一个自愿联盟。这两者似乎都不能对中国的主导地位构成严重威胁。

这些“炮舰协议”被塞进了条款，试图迫使贸易伙伴将其他国家排除在客户之外，并将矿物出口到美国，而不是供本国产业使用。这些协议缺乏成功的法律和经济可信度。

以印度尼西亚为例，该国生产世界三分之二的镍，镍用于电池、太阳能技术和风力涡轮机。过去十年，中国企业在该国[大量投资](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2023/04/how-indonesia-used-chinese-industrial-investments-to-turn-nickel-into-the-new-gold)，建立了从采矿到成品的完整镍供应链。特朗普试图通过一项胁迫印度尼西亚向美国供应的协议与之竞争，但当美国最高法院今年2月宣布其威胁加征的关税违宪时，该协议受到了严重削弱。

特朗普政府匆忙征收替代性关税，但这些关税在法律和政治上同样不确定，而印尼尚未批准该协议。如此混乱的局面不会动摇印尼与中国基于真实投资和坚实成果的关系。

特朗普政府更广泛的多边雄心也存在严重的可信度问题。2月，特朗普召集54个国家以确保关键矿产供应链。其首次尝试将这一目标具体化的努力是在6月于法国举行的七国集团（G7）会议上。但要真正挑战中国的生产，任何计划都需要包含[需求侧](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/g7-progress-on-critical-minerals-diversification-overlooks-demand-side/)战略，并设定价格下限，即使面对中国的倾销也能保证生产商的收入。欧洲各国政府尤其[高度怀疑](https://euobserver.com/222087/g7-expected-to-deliver-minerals-pact-but-eu-resists-trumps-minimum-price-plan/)：他们不太相信美国会制定一个符合除自身以外任何一方利益的定价公式。

欧盟没有忘记特朗普1月试图吞并格陵兰以获取其矿产的举动。过去一个月里，这些雄心得到了一个及时的提醒：格陵兰政府[不得不警告](https://www.ft.com/content/c14d737a-a148-49da-8cb5-ba223c8a9fc5?syn-25a6b1a6=1)一个与特朗普结盟的电视主持人“菲尔博士”有关联的矿业财团，不要未经许可开始钻探石油。更广泛的向矿业公司拨款的进程涉及数十亿美元[流向](https://www.ft.com/content/4ba88e38-fdfd-447c-9250-163cd85293c4?syn-25a6b1a6=1)那些往往在该领域没有多少业绩记录、并与特朗普政府内部人物有财务联系的公司。

这场杂乱无章的行动与中国政府数十年来高度专注的做法相比相形见绌。2010年代初，中国曾笨拙地试图用稀土管制来胁迫日本，但因非法采矿者的普遍走私而受到削弱，如今已被[建立](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/china/how-china-found-most-potent-weapon-xiong-weiss)一条严格控制的供应链所取代，并将生产[整合](https://static.rusi.org/china-and-rare-earths-supply-chain-june-2026.pdf)为两大集团（中国稀土集团和中国北方稀土）。这些企业在严格的国家控制下运营，包括限制相关技术的国际传播，以及对走私[非常严重的刑事后果](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/rare-earths-under-lock-and-key-chinas-supreme-court-elevates-smuggling-to-a-national-security-threat/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)。

中国出口管制造成的供应中断始终是一个持续的威胁。最近，[铒的价格突然飙升](https://www.ft.com/content/395650e6-7963-428b-9d86-b39c36453f5d?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，这是一种非常小众的稀土，原因是人们担心一旦11月出口限制停火期结束，铒将受到限制。

美国的人均经济规模仍远高于中国。但在主导和控制关键矿产等经济咽喉要道方面，美国无能领导层经常使其落于下风。到停火期结束时，美国几乎未能削弱中国的贸易武器。

### English Original

## Trump is losing his war of independence on rare earths

Workers dispose of nickel waste at a site in Indonesia. Donald Trump’s shambolic attempt to coerce the country into supplying the US will not override Indonesia’s relationship with China © Muchtamir Zaide/AFP/Getty Images

[**](https://www.ft.com/alan-beattie)[**Alan Beattie**](https://www.ft.com/alan-beattie)[**](https://www.ft.com/alan-beattie)

In a retreat humiliating even by Donald Trump’s standards, the US president last November withdrew threats of tariffs against China in return for Beijing agreeing a year-long ceasefire on export controls of rare earths and other minerals. His administration says it’s used the time since to boost its own production of minerals and secure supplies from allies, trying to reduce Beijing’s hugely powerful leverage from critical raw materials.

That claim is wildly overblown. Going up against China would not just require huge financial firepower to support companies and provide guaranteed prices to producers but a ruthless commitment to productive efficiency — and to building a lasting international coalition to secure minerals not found in the US. Instead, the administration has displayed its familiar cronyism, inept attempts at coercion and a capricious America-first foreign policy. This shreds its credibility in anchoring an international mineral agreement.

Certainly there’s been a big flurry of activity, both financial and bureaucratic. The administration has made the extraordinary step of taking equity stakes in several companies and a bewildering array of purchase guarantees, credit support, loans and grants which [it claims total $30bn](https://www.cfr.org/articles/u-s-allies-aim-to-break-chinas-critical-minerals-dominance?utm_source=chatgpt.com). The overseas initiative with the most teeth is a [deal](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/united-states-australia-framework-securing-supply-mining-and-processing-critical-minerals-and-rare-earths)signed with Australia last October which has led to US [investment in mining projects](https://www.ft.com/content/d88b7e46-6806-4286-94d7-3bb16facee41?syn-25a6b1a6=1) there.

But beyond Australia and one or two countries vulnerable to pressure such as the Democratic Republic of Congo, the US has typically deployed only coercive one-sided bilateral trade agreements, along with seeking to convene an international coalition of the willing. Neither seems a serious threat to Chinese dominance.

These “gunboat deals” have had provisions inserted to try to force trading partners to exclude other countries as customers and export their minerals to the US rather than to their own industries. These lack the legal and economic credibility to succeed.

Take, for example, Indonesia, which produces two-thirds of the world’s nickel, used in batteries, solar technology and wind turbines. Over the past decade Chinese companies have [invested heavily](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2023/04/how-indonesia-used-chinese-industrial-investments-to-turn-nickel-into-the-new-gold) in the country to create a complete nickel supply chain from mining to finished product. Trump attempted to rival this with a deal coercing Indonesia into supplying the US, but it was heavily undercut when his threatened tariffs were declared unconstitutional by the US Supreme Court in February.

The administration has rushed to impose replacement tariffs, but those too are legally and politically uncertain, and Indonesia has yet to ratify the agreement. Such a shambles will not override Indonesia’s relationship with China, based on genuine investment and solid results.

The administration’s broader plurilateral ambitions also have serious credibility issues. In February, Trump convened 54 countries to secure critical minerals supply chains. One of its first attempts to make this concrete was within the G7, which met in France in June. But to create a genuine challenge to Chinese production, any programme would need to include a [demand-side](https://www.energypolicy.columbia.edu/g7-progress-on-critical-minerals-diversification-overlooks-demand-side/) strategy, and to set up price floors that would guarantee producers’ revenues even in the face of Chinese dumping. European governments in particular were [highly suspicious](https://euobserver.com/222087/g7-expected-to-deliver-minerals-pact-but-eu-resists-trumps-minimum-price-plan/): they had little trust that the US would set a pricing formula in anyone’s interest other than its own.

The EU has not forgotten Trump’s attempts to annex Greenland for its minerals in January. There was a convenient reminder of those ambitions over the past month when the Greenland government [had to warn off](https://www.ft.com/content/c14d737a-a148-49da-8cb5-ba223c8a9fc5?syn-25a6b1a6=1) a mining consortium linked to the Trump-aligned TV host “Dr Phil” from starting to drill for oil without permission. The wider process of handing out money to mining companies has involved billions of dollars being[given to companies](https://www.ft.com/content/4ba88e38-fdfd-447c-9250-163cd85293c4?syn-25a6b1a6=1) often with little record in the area and with financial ties to figures within the Trump administration.

This jumble of a campaign compares very badly with the fierce focus of the Chinese government over decades. A clumsy attempt to coerce Japan with rare earth controls in the early 2010s, undercut by widespread smuggling by wildcat miners, has been supplanted by the [creation](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/china/how-china-found-most-potent-weapon-xiong-weiss)of a tightly controlled supply chain, [consolidating production](https://static.rusi.org/china-and-rare-earths-supply-chain-june-2026.pdf) into two main groups (China Rare Earth Group and China Northern Rare Earth). The companies operate under strict state control including limits on the international dissemination of related technology and [very serious criminal consequences](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/rare-earths-under-lock-and-key-chinas-supreme-court-elevates-smuggling-to-a-national-security-threat/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) for smuggling.

Supply interruptions from Chinese export controls remain a constant threat. There was a recent [sudden leap in the price of erbium](https://www.ft.com/content/395650e6-7963-428b-9d86-b39c36453f5d?syn-25a6b1a6=1), a particularly niche rare earth, following fears that it would be restricted once the export restriction ceasefire ends in November.

The US remains a far richer economy per head than China’s. But when it comes to dominating and controlling economic chokepoints like critical minerals, America’s inept leadership frequently leaves it trailing. By the end of the ceasefire, the US will have done little to render China’s trade weapons any less powerful than before.
