### **要点**

- 阿联酋在指控伊朗向其领土发射弹道导弹后，已切断与德黑兰的所有经济联系。
- 阿联酋已暂停贸易和金融交易，直至另行通知，原因是"破坏地区和国际和平与安全的升级行为"。

阿联酋在指控伊朗向其领土[发射弹道导弹](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/uae-says-iran-fired-two-missiles-at-country-as-war-drags-on)后，切断了与德黑兰的所有经济联系，加剧了地区紧张局势。

这个波斯湾国家是伊朗的主要商业伙伴之一，该国表示，鉴于“破坏地区和国际和平与安全的升级行为”，已暂停贸易和金融交易，直至另行通知。伊朗否认对这次袭击负责，这将是自5月以来对阿联酋领土的首次袭击。周三，两国之间的航班仍在继续。

阿联酋的疏远将加剧对伊朗的压力，此时与美国永久结束战争的谈判陷入僵局，华盛顿正准备采取新措施进一步挤压伊斯兰共和国的经济。伊朗已经承受了数十年的制裁，但近六个月的战斗损害了其工业能力，削减了原油出口并推高了通胀。

“目前没有与伊朗伊斯兰共和国进行或安排任何谈判或对话，”美国总统唐纳德·特朗普周二在其社交媒体平台上发帖称。

美国国务卿马尔科·鲁比奥周二与阿联酋国家安全顾问谢赫·塔赫农·本·扎耶德·阿勒纳哈扬就地区安全和霍尔木兹海峡航行自由进行了通话。鲁比奥的副发言人汤米·皮戈特在一份[声明](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/secretary-rubios-call-with-uae-national-security-advisor-sheikh-tahnoon-bin-zayed-al-nahyan/)中表示，两人还讨论了“美阿联酋继续协调，追究伊朗及其恐怖代理人对持续袭击的责任”。

布伦特原油周三连续第四天上涨，交易价格约为每桶92美元。随着霍尔木兹海峡迅速重新开放的前景变得暗淡，这一全球基准油价飙升；在2月28日战争爆发前，全球五分之一的石油和液化天然气通过该海峡运输。

周二晚上，迪拜响起导弹威胁警报，提醒居民寻找避难所。国防部后来表示，两枚针对海上交通的射弹落入海中，只有一枚进入阿联酋水域。

阿联酋近年来一直是伊朗最大的贸易伙伴，德黑兰依赖这种关系获取外国商品和硬通货。两国之间的密切关系可以追溯到几代人之前，其基础是地理上的邻近和深厚的历史联系。

然而，过去十年的大部分时间里，两国关系一直紧张，尤其是在阿联酋支持特朗普在首个任期内加强对伊朗的制裁之后。阿布扎比与以色列签署具有里程碑意义的正常化协议，进一步加剧了摩擦。

在战争最激烈的时候，阿联酋遭到猛烈攻击，伊朗用无人机和导弹瞄准美国在中东的盟友。该国约有13人丧生，能源设施、港口和酒店遭受了数十亿美元的损失。

尽管如此，阿布扎比在整个过程中与德黑兰保持了外交关系。据彭博社当时报道，两国高级安全官员于6月举行面对面会晤，试图缓和紧张局势。

## UAE Cuts Economic Ties With Iran After Missiles Target Territory

WATCH: The UAE says it’s cutting all economic ties with Tehran. Abeer Abu Omar reports from Dubai.*Source: Bloomberg*

By [Golnar Motevalli](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ARwAJnhPlhE/golnar-motevalli) and [Patrick Sykes](https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AUpqNrs7eAk/patrick-sykes)August 19, 2026 at 3:44 PM GMT+8*Updated on August 19, 2026 at 6:35 PM GMT+8*

### **Takeaways ** by Bloomberg AI

### **Takeaways**

- The United Arab Emirates has cut all economic ties with Tehran after accusing Iran of firing ballistic missiles at its territory.
- The UAE halted trade and financial transactions until further notice due to "escalations that undermine regional and international peace and security".
- The move will compound pressure on Iran at a time when negotiations with the US to permanently end the war are deadlocked and Washington is readying new measures to further squeeze the Islamic Republic's economy.
The United Arab Emirates has cut all economic ties with Tehran after accusing Iran of [firing ballistic missiles](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/uae-says-iran-fired-two-missiles-at-country-as-war-drags-on) at its territory, ramping up regional tensions.

The Persian Gulf nation, one of Iran’s main commercial partners, said it halted trade and financial transactions until further notice in light of “escalations that undermine regional and international peace and security.” Iran denied responsibility for the attack, which would be the first on UAE territory since May. Flights between the two countries continued Wednesday.

The UAE’s distancing will compound pressure on Iran at a time when negotiations with the US to permanently end the war are deadlocked and Washington is readying new measures to further squeeze the Islamic Republic’s economy. Iran has withstood decades of sanctions, but almost six months of fighting has damaged its industrial capacity, curtailed crude exports and driven up inflation.

“There are no talks or conversations going on, or scheduled, with the Islamic Republic of Iran,” US President Donald Trump posted on his social media platform on Tuesday.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio spoke Tuesday with the UAE’s national security adviser, Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan, about regional security and freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz. The two also discussed “continued US-UAE coordination to hold Iran and its terrorist proxies accountable for ongoing attacks,” Rubio’s deputy spokesperson, Tommy Pigott, said in a [statement](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/secretary-rubios-call-with-uae-national-security-advisor-sheikh-tahnoon-bin-zayed-al-nahyan/).

Brent crude rose for a fourth straight day on Wednesday, trading at around $92 a barrel. The global benchmark has surged as prospects dimmed for a swift reopening of Hormuz, through which a fifth of the world’s oil and liquefied natural gas transited before the war erupted on Feb. 28.

A missile-threat alert sounded in Dubai on Tuesday evening, telling residents to seek shelter. The Defense Ministry later said two projectiles that targeted maritime traffic fell into the sea, with only one reaching UAE waters.

The UAE has been Iran’s top trade partner in recent years, with Tehran relying on the relationship for access to foreign goods and hard currency. The close ties between the two nations date back generations and are underpinned by their geographic proximity and deep historical links.

The relationship has been strained for much of the past decade though, particularly after the UAE backed Trump’s strengthening of sanctions on the Islamic Republic during his first term. Abu Dhabi’s signing of a landmark normalization agreement with Israel caused further friction.

The UAE came under heavy attack during the height of the war as Iran targeted US allies across the Middle East with drones and missiles. Some 13 people were killed in the country and its energy facilities, ports and hotels suffered billions of dollars in damage.

Still, Abu Dhabi maintained diplomatic ties with Tehran throughout. Top security officials from both nations met face-to-face in June in an effort to ease tensions, Bloomberg reported at the time.

On Wednesday, Ali Abdollahi, the head of Iran’s Armed Forces General Staff, cautioned countries against aiding America.

Gulf states “should know that nothing escapes our attention,” he said in a statement cited by the state-run Islamic Republic News Agency. “We warn that any assistance or facilitation provided to the aggressor US military constitutes participation with US military forces.”

Thousands of people died in the war, most of them in Iran, before the US and Iran reached a ceasefire in June and agreed to negotiate a comprehensive peace deal within 60 days. But the pact was repeatedly violated and both sides had declared it defunct before it expired this week.
