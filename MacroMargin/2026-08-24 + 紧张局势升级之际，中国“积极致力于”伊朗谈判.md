### 中文译文

## 紧张局势升级之际，中国“积极致力于”伊朗谈判

斯科特·贝森特将公布一项计划，旨在将冲突从中东地区针锋相对的打击转变为全面的经济孤立。

中国表示，在华盛顿即将公布措施惩罚德黑兰经济伙伴之际，中国在一份延迟发布的声明中表示继续支持以外交方式结束美伊战争。

据周日发布的一份[声明](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/wjbxw_new/202608/t20260823_12008663.shtml)，中国外交部副部长苗得雨8月17日在北京与伊朗副外长卡泽姆·加里巴迪会谈时表示，“中国正密切关注中东局势，并积极致力于推动和平谈判”。

中国外交部此前未公开此次会晤，也未对近一周后才公布的原因作出解释。

这一时机值得注意，因为该声明发布一天后，美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特将公布一项计划，旨在将冲突从中东地区针锋相对的打击转变为全面的经济孤立。

美国总统唐纳德·特朗普[此前威胁](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/trump-says-us-starting-economic-warfare-operation-against-iran)伊朗及其贸易伙伴，此前数月的军事打击和海上封锁未能迫使德黑兰屈服。中国购买伊朗大部分石油，并且是伊朗2025年的第二大贸易伙伴，仅次于阿联酋。阿联酋称在指控伊朗向其领土发射弹道导弹后，已切断与德黑兰的所有经济联系。

北京[回应](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/china-says-trump-s-economic-warfare-won-t-solve-iran-crisis)特朗普的言论，称制裁和施压不会奏效，并呼吁通过外交途径解决。伊朗表示，特朗普的威胁是对美国前所未有的债务和飙升的利息成本危机的“转移视线”。

周一在北京的新闻发布会上被问及制裁威胁时，中国外交部发言人林剑表示，制裁无助于解决问题，双方应进行对话。

林表示，他的国家将“采取必要措施维护其合法权利和利益”。

特朗普因伊朗问题对中国加大经济压力的任何举动，都可能在计划中的中国领导人习近平访问华盛顿前几周进一步加剧双边关系的紧张。

复旦大学美国研究中心主任、中国外交部前顾问吴心伯表示，这场战争对峰会产生了间接影响。他说，活动的筹备工作并没有像中方所希望的那样顺利推进。

吴表示：“我认为它受到了伊朗问题的太多干扰。”

外交部周日发布的声明称，加里巴巴迪向中方官员通报了海湾地区的事态发展，并阐述了伊朗的立场。德黑兰“愿意继续通过外交谈判解决问题”，同时伊朗将“坚定”捍卫其主权和安全利益。

声明援引他的话说：“伊朗期待与中方在多个领域深化合作，推动双边关系发展。” “伊朗赞赏中国秉持公道正义，期待中国在维护地区和平稳定方面发挥更大作用。”

北京不承认单方面制裁，但其国有企业通常避免购买被列入黑名单的石油。中国最大的国有银行也有遵守美国对伊朗、朝鲜甚至香港高级官员制裁的记录，以避免失去美元清算系统的准入。

自2月下旬美国对伊朗发动战争以来，华盛顿已制裁了一些中国“茶壶”炼油厂和企业。但迄今为止，华盛顿尚未针对为贸易提供资金的中国主要银行。

5月，中国命令国内企业不要遵守美国对五家炼油厂的制裁，而其最大的银行则夹在北京的指令与失去进入美国金融体系的风险之间。

### English Original

## China ‘Actively Committed’ to Iran Talks as Tensions Rise

Scott Bessent is set to unveil a plan designed to shift the conflict from tit-for-tat strikes across the Middle East to full-blown economic isolation.

China said it continues to support a diplomatic end to the US-Iran war, in a delayed statement released shortly before Washington is set to unveil measures to punish Tehran’s economic partners.

“China is closely monitoring the situation in the Middle East and is actively committed to promoting peace talks,” Vice Foreign Minister Miao Deyu said at talks with Iran’s Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi in Beijing on Aug. 17, according to a [statement](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/wjbxw_new/202608/t20260823_12008663.shtml) published Sunday.

China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs didn’t previously disclose the meeting and provided no explanation for the time lag in making it public almost a week later.

The timing is notable because it comes a day before US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is set to unveil a plan designed to shift the conflict from tit-for-tat strikes across the Middle East to full-blown economic isolation.

US President Donald Trump [earlier threatened](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/trump-says-us-starting-economic-warfare-operation-against-iran) Iran and its trading partners after months of military strikes and a maritime blockade failed to force Tehran to capitulate. China buys the bulk of Iran’s oil and was its second-largest trading partner in 2025, behind the United Arab Emirates, which has said it cut all economic ties with Tehran after accusing it of firing ballistic missiles at its territory.

Beijing [responded](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/china-says-trump-s-economic-warfare-won-t-solve-iran-crisis) to Trump’s comments by saying sanctions and pressure wouldn’t work and called for a diplomatic resolution. Iran said Trump’s threats were a “diversion” from America’s crises of unprecedented debt and surging interest costs.

When asked about the sanctions threat at a press briefing in Beijing on Monday, Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman Lin Jian said they won’t help resolve the matter and both sides should talk.

His nation would “take necessary measures to safeguard its legitimate rights and interests,” Lin said.

Any move by Trump to increase economic pressure on China over Iran could put further strain on bilateral ties just weeks ahead of a planned visit to Washington by Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

The war is having an indirect impact on the summit, said Wu Xinbo, director of Fudan University’s Center for American Studies and a former adviser to China’s Foreign Ministry. Planning for the event wasn’t progressing as smoothly as the Chinese side might like, he said.

“I think it has been too much disrupted by the Iran issue,” Wu said.

The Foreign Ministry statement released Sunday said Gharibabadi briefed Chinese officials on developments in the Gulf and presented Iran’s position. While Tehran is “willing to continue resolving issues through diplomatic negotiations,” he said Iran will act “firmly” in defending its sovereignty and security interests.

“Iran looks forward to deepening cooperation with China in various fields and advancing bilateral relations,” the statement cited him as saying. “Iran appreciates China’s upholding of justice and fairness and looks forward to China playing a greater role in maintaining regional peace and stability.”

Beijing does not recognize unilateral sanctions, but its state-owned entities generally avoid blacklisted oil. China’s biggest state banks also have a history of complying with US sanctions against Iran, North Korea and even top officials in Hong Kong to avoid losing access to the US dollar-clearing system.

Washington has sanctioned some Chinese teapot refineries and firms since the US launched the war against Iran in late February. But so far, Washington has stopped short of targeting the major Chinese banks that finance the trade.

In May, China ordered domestic companies not to comply with US sanctions on five refiners, while its biggest banks were caught between Beijing’s directive and the risk of losing access to the US financial system.
