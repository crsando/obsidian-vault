### 中文译文

## 中国空调制造商在酷热夏季中获利

今年夏天，一轮又一轮的热浪使欧洲各地[气温飙升](https://wmo.int/media/news/western-europe-has-hottest-june-record)，野火正在[吞噬森林](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-31/wildfires-in-spain-threaten-madrid-vacation-homes-and-farms)、田野和农场。这些灾难提高了人们对气候变化现实的[认识和担忧](https://english.cctv.com/2026/07/06/VIDE9cT57TSkdX4ciawW699Y260706.shtml)，从塞维利亚到斯德哥尔摩，人们终于开始接受[空调的概念](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/europe-and-uk-heat-waves-boost-demand-for-air-conditioning)。这五家中国制造商正眼盯着丰厚的利润。

但这些品牌首先必须让欧洲人认识它们。中国空调生产商格力、海尔、海信、美的和TCL在欧洲大陆并不是家喻户晓的名字，尽管它们的产品十多年来越来越多地出现在那里。为了提高知名度，这些公司正在根据当地口味定制产品，并建立深入的服务提供商和零售商网络。它们聘请了欧洲内容创作者在社交媒体上推广产品。并且，效仿索尼、三星和LG先前的做法，它们赞助体育赛事，签约体育明星代言。例如，海信是今年夏天[世界杯](https://en.cheaa.org/contents/468/13439.html)的官方支持者，TCL与西班牙国家足球队合作，美的与挪威足球运动员埃尔林·哈兰德签订了合同。

根据研究机构欧睿国际的数据，中国企业控制着全球空调市场三分之二的份额。它们正在快速增长，尤其是在欧洲。海关数据显示，2026年上半年，中国对欧盟的空调出口额达到38亿美元，同比增长43%。TCL表示，上半年来自欧洲的订单比去年同期增长超过三分之二，便携式机型的销量激增90%以上。海尔报告称，6月份其在法国、德国和英国的销售额比去年同期增长了一倍多。美的表示，上半年其在四个关键欧洲国家的空调收入增长了[超过70%](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33492822)。美的欧洲区负责人赵阿里7月对中国媒体表示：‘我们的本地团队专注于每个市场用户的真正需求，然后为该市场创造产品。’

法国一家大型商场的空调。摄影师：Nicolas Guyonnet/Hans Lucas/AFP via Getty Images

中国空调制造商的崛起与整个中国制造业的崛起如出一辙。十年前，中国制造商主要专注于国内市场，或者生产由大型国际品牌销售的零部件或成品——在空调领域，如开利、大金和东芝。但中国国内市场已经饱和：1995年，每100户城镇家庭只有8台空调。如今这一数字为176台。

如今，中国制造商正在利用为全球老牌企业服务期间获得的技术、专业知识和资本，从昔日的客户手中夺取业务。‘他们在考虑如何向价值链上游移动，让自己成为品牌企业，’为拓展海外的中国企业提供咨询的顾问公司LabaTech创始人Eva Fan表示。

政府的鼓励推动了它们的扩张，鼓励企业聚焦新能源、智能制造和数字技术向海外扩张。对空调制造商来说，这意味着可以预测用户习惯（如温度偏好和日常作息）的算法、检测房间无人后自动关闭制冷的传感器，以及自2022年以来将能效提高近三分之一的技术。欧睿国际分析师Veronika Kandusova表示，中国空调制造商正‘从简单的出口模式转向复杂的本地化品牌建设运营’。

![Manufacturing the PortaSplit at a Midea factory in Guangzhou. Photographer: Liang Xu/Xinhua/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b14810eae6dc3f54e5501b4/3c081cc9-0b14-8145-8e4b-e128c6096a62-a1aaf5b04c6431b86c6cfbd624914293.png)

美的广州工厂正在生产PortaSplit。摄影师：梁旭/新华社/Getty Images

欧洲尤其具有挑战性，因为这项技术难以适应许多建筑。历史建筑保护法规往往禁止在外墙钻孔或在室外安装压缩机，规划审批可能需要数月时间。为此，美的开发了PortaSplit，这是一种空调机组，配备小型室外压缩机，通过柔性管线与室内风机模块相连；它无需在墙上永久钻孔，无需专业安装，而且在大多数地方无需官僚审批。

欧洲的另一重复杂性在于空调周围的政治包袱。环保主义者认为，空调消耗的能源从长远来看只会使气候变化恶化，并加剧不平等。欧盟明年将收紧[对氟化制冷剂的限制](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/fluorinated-greenhouse-gases/climate-friendly-alternatives-f-gases/air-conditioning_en)——这些气体已知会加剧气候变化——而大多数中国制冷设备都使用这种气体。

今年夏天的热浪导致电价飙升，欧洲的配电网有时难以提供运行制冷系统所需的电力。国际能源署分析师法比安·沃斯温克尔（Fabian Voswinkel）表示：“如果这些设备被大量采用，可能会成为一些国家电网的重大问题。国家有动力加强监管。”

![PortaSplits ready for shipping at the factory in Guangzhou. Photographer: Liang Xu/Xinhua/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c081cc90b14810eae6dc3f54e5501b4/3c081cc9-0b14-813d-bf07-df47c14090f9-854638ec4edbbfbcdb9ea3eb12ccae62.png)

广州工厂里待发货的PortaSplit。摄影：Liang Xu/新华社/盖蒂图片社

背景是欧盟希望平衡与中国的贸易。欧盟今年第一季度对华贸易逆差[达到980亿欧元](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=EU_trade_with_China_-_latest_developments#Recent_developments)（合1130亿美元），为2022年以来最高水平，多个团体正在提议对中国商品征收[惩罚性关税](https://www.pulse-z.eu/30-per-cent-tariffs-on-chinese-imports-france-urges-caution/)。美国提供了一个警示：在特朗普政府对空调加征超30%关税后，2025年上半年中国对美国的空调出货量[下降了近四分之一](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-08/europe-s-heat-waves-fuel-new-market-for-chinese-ac-units)。

不过，这些中国新入局者表示，欧洲也提供了一个更深入了解全球市场和提高产品质量的机会。最终目标是：让消费者认可并接受中国品牌，尤其是高端产品。负责海尔国际空调销售业务的于世鹏（Yu Shipeng）表示：“要成为重要的全球玩家，我们必须在国际舞台上竞争。小池塘养不出大鱼。”

### English Original

## Chinese AC Makers Cash In on a Sweltering Summer

Successive heat waves have sent [temperatures soaring](https://wmo.int/media/news/western-europe-has-hottest-june-record) across Europe this summer, and wildfires are [consuming forests](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-31/wildfires-in-spain-threaten-madrid-vacation-homes-and-farms), fields and farms. As those calamities boost awareness of — and [concern about](https://english.cctv.com/2026/07/06/VIDE9cT57TSkdX4ciawW699Y260706.shtml) — the realities of a changing climate, people from Seville to Stockholm are finally warming to the [idea of air conditioning](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/europe-and-uk-heat-waves-boost-demand-for-air-conditioning). And that has a quintet of Chinese manufacturers eyeing big profits.

But first the brands will have to get Europeans to recognize them. Chinese AC producers Gree, Haier, Hisense, Midea and TCL are hardly household names on the continent, even though their products have been a growing presence for more than a decade. To raise their profile, the companies are tailoring products to local tastes and building deep networks of service providers and retailers. They’ve hired European content creators to promote their wares on social media. And, adopting a playbook from the likes of Sony, Samsung and LG before them, they’re sponsoring sporting events and signing star athletes to endorsement deals. Hisense, for instance, was an official backer of this summer’s [World Cup](https://en.cheaa.org/contents/468/13439.html), TCL has partnered with the Spanish national football team, and Midea has a contract with [Norwegian footballer Erling Haaland](https://www.facebook.com/MideaPhilippines/videos/2519694251803917/).

Chinese companies control two-thirds of the global market for air conditioning, according to researcher Euromonitor International. And they’re growing fast, particularly in Europe. Exports of AC units from China to the European Union hit $3.8 billion in the first half of 2026, up 43% from a year earlier, customs data show. TCL says orders from Europe climbed more than two-thirds in the first half from the same period last year, with sales of portable units surging by more than 90%. Haier reports its sales in France, Germany and the UK more than doubled in June from the same month last year. And Midea says AC revenue in four key European countries rose [more than 70%](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33492822) in the first half. “Our local teams focus on what users in each market really need,” Zhao Ali, Midea’s Europe chief, told Chinese media in July, “then create products for that market.”

Air conditioners at a big-box store in France. Photographer: Nicolas Guyonnet/Hans Lucas/AFP via Getty Images

The ascent of the AC makers mirrors that of industries across China. A decade ago, Chinese manufacturers focused largely on their home market or on producing parts or finished products sold by big international brands — in air conditioning, names such as Carrier, Daikin and Toshiba. But the Chinese domestic market has become saturated: In 1995 there were only 8 AC units per 100 urban households. Today there are 176.

Now, Chinese manufacturers are leveraging the technology, expertise and capital they acquired while serving the global incumbents to take business from their former clients. “They’re thinking about how to move up the value chain to become branded businesses themselves,” says [Eva Fan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fanxianeva/), founder of LabaTech, a consultant that advises Chinese companies expanding overseas.

Their push has been boosted by government encouragement of companies to expand abroad by focusing on new energy, smart manufacturing and digital technologies. For AC manufacturers, that means algorithms that predict user habits such as temperature preferences and daily routines, sensors that detect when a room is empty and automatically shut down cooling, and technology that has increased energy [efficiency by almost a third](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629620301468#bib0016) since 2022. Chinese air conditioner makers are “moving from simple export models to sophisticated, localized brand-building operations,” says Euromonitor analyst Veronika Kandusova.

Manufacturing the PortaSplit at a Midea factory in Guangzhou. Photographer: Liang Xu/Xinhua/Getty Images

Europe is particularly challenging, because it’s hard to adapt the technology to many buildings. Historic preservation regulations often prohibit drilling in exterior walls or installing compressors outdoors, and planning approvals can take months. In response, Midea has developed the PortaSplit, an AC unit featuring a small outside compressor that’s connected to an indoor fan-module with a flexible line; it requires no permanent wall drilling, no professional installation and — in most places — no bureaucratic approval.

A further complication in Europe is the political baggage surrounding air conditioning. Environmentalists argue that the energy it consumes only makes climate change worse in the long run, and that it deepens inequality. The EU next year will tighten [restrictions on fluorinated refrigerants](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/fluorinated-greenhouse-gases/climate-friendly-alternatives-f-gases/air-conditioning_en) — gases known to aggravate climate change — which most of the Chinese cooling units use.

And this summer’s heat waves have caused electricity prices to jump, with Europe’s distribution grid sometimes struggling to supply the power needed to run cooling systems. “If we have a massive uptake of these devices, it has the potential to become a major problem for the grids in some countries,” says Fabian Voswinkel, an analyst at the International Energy Agency. “The state has an incentive to regulate more strongly.”

PortaSplits ready for shipping at the factory in Guangzhou. Photographer: Liang Xu/Xinhua/Getty Images

Lurking in the background is the EU’s desire to balance trade with China. The bloc’s trade deficit with the country [reached €98 billion](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=EU_trade_with_China_-_latest_developments#Recent_developments) ($113 billion) in the first quarter of this year, its highest level since 2022, and various groups are pitching the idea of [punitive tariffs](https://www.pulse-z.eu/30-per-cent-tariffs-on-chinese-imports-france-urges-caution/) on Chinese goods. The US offers a warning: Chinese AC shipments to the US [fell almost a quarter](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-08/europe-s-heat-waves-fuel-new-market-for-chinese-ac-units) in the first half of 2025 after the Trump administration imposed duties that topped 30% for air conditioners.

The Chinese newcomers, though, say Europe also offers an opportunity to better understand global markets and improve the quality of their offerings. The ultimate goal: to persuade consumers to recognize and embrace Chinese brands, particularly for premium products. “To become a major global player, we must compete on the international stage,” says Yu Shipeng, who oversees Haier’s international AC sales. “A small pond can’t produce big fish.”
