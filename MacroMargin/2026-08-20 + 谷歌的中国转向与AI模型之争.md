## 谷歌的中国转向与AI模型之争

谷歌计划明年停止在中国生产Pixel硬件，中美紧张局势持续之际。© Feng Li/Getty Images

[**Lauly Li**](https://www.ft.com/lauly-li), [**Zijing Wu**](https://www.ft.com/zijing-wu), [**Yifan Yu**](https://www.ft.com/stream/570d3577-8f77-4f90-a22a-850009a570e1), [**Cissy Zhou**](https://www.ft.com/stream/998feb59-ab7e-4a24-b52d-450078a5ee91) and [**Cheng Ting-Fang**](https://www.ft.com/cheng-ting-fang)

发布于9小时前

大家好！我是Lauly，本周的#techAsia主持人，从潮湿多雨的台北向你们致以温暖的问候。

昨晚，我和一位在苹果供应商工作的主管聊了聊他所在行业的动态。他告诉我，早在2000年代他就认识到台积电对全球科技业的重要性，他甚至曾与一位不看好这家芯片代工商价值的知名媒体人物公开争论。

他说，一时赌气，他买了2000万新台币（627,274美元）的台积电股票，平均每股55新台币，此后一股也没卖。如今台积电股价已超过2,300新台币，他持股的价值已飙升至约8.4亿新台币。

“我所享受的所有美食美酒，都要感谢Morris、Mark和CC。”这位主管说，他指的是台积电的前任和现任董事长。

这让我想起一位台积电芯片设备供应商的主管，他曾和我分享他带全家去欧洲度一周暑假的计划。他们将乘坐商务舱，住在五星级酒店每晚6,300美元的豪华套房里，他称这个价格“合理”。

我只能说，我的想象力受到了贫穷的限制。

玩笑归玩笑，根据我近期对多位不同行业高管的采访，以及富士康、和硕、广达和仁宝等服务器供应商的财报电话会议，人工智能基础设施的需求依然强劲。

以广达为例，该公司今年将资本支出从此前计划的300亿新台币提高至400亿新台币，以扩大在加州、泰国和台湾的产能。到今年年底，广达预计其全球AI服务器产能将是去年的两倍，而其订单“已经排到2028年”。

AI服务器电源解决方案领军企业光宝科技和全球最大芯片基板供应商欣兴电子也将资本支出提高到创纪录水平，而富士康预计其支出将比去年创纪录的1738亿新台币增长30%。

然而，业内人士表示，如此大规模的产能扩张已导致生产设备成为瓶颈。电源测试设备和高档玻璃布织机等工具的交付周期已长达30至50周。中国对某些原材料和稀土元素的出口管制，加上本已昂贵的金属价格上涨，也使生产设备的交付变得更加复杂。

此外，还有中美关系的不确定性。

我的一位朋友告诉我，他的公司担心不断升级的政治紧张局势可能危及供应链的连续性。他说，公司高管们陷入了一场争论：是否应该将所有为其消费电子硬件供应工具的中国供应商全部更换为非中国供应商。

“我不禁想问：我们真的需要把与中国的脱钩细化到这种程度吗？真的有必要吗？”我的朋友问道。

我也一直在思考这类问题。

#### Pixel 迁移

据日经亚洲**Lauly Li**的[这篇独家报道](https://url3563.namail.nikkei.com/ls/click?upn=u001.M0mxR54htVwmfgL9HhtimSxgqTE4QRItjPHDax3pwRpGrMUzc3PQo48Os47OFpKbPvhfvU3gzCyxZsWsNx4eubiDM-2FIJ-2BAF1Thh8rKrlx020S53Gkvb8nxLp4-2FuO5VwduHQlysPZjoxjfNPv4hLoD6RopACQPVP61pYu9SFWwfzaUnO4cjrLfiSe7k15kGq1R25E-2B8oev2NZjIxqaIV6BM1BNFwDSMzKqbqKEMmkz0hxuIqiNK-2BNP7UYj1v-2FTXAVi36-2FRN-2FOat-2BJexGg0ag4xLURdPDSAAiZka8QYxvUVup76Bvty7ur94eMvJ3v7Xa2J6RLhacCxB8WCza5hWjMBehLPnnuX-2BhdqWcOi5pxycKL78ZG-2FB5EgneVPQSupGlgEP6y0zGDwlhbr4I1itBocA-3D-3DhOKD_Nh1pf1G0mH5ql3UFVpr8HYgTYPsL5cgxfwZjsg2NQ2BuzuGn-2BGAYrUQK6ZancxSjVNQ36UnUbq0yySPAeFPIBPiD0t-2Fkb7t5TQ0gEIxVDcapNLzs4gg3qpHDXTNgDM0PXVRNHECmcXMwjJG3EY0XBv9o6LynlCKKnVYFzX9FcJ0X6rfLPKQvTzgRh3eHkEewP-2FS53rvn8arIE6W4gNXzR1eCch7wasx8K12fiCGc25TCgcTG-2F03zidh9BzSR8DRz26zO0eBcywn6WXnkTU6cjepOeEdd9hDmgifTIDzpzHGZmlicrqSIvXV0MjVMIQ2L)，谷歌已告知供应商，由于持续的中美紧张局势，计划明年停止在中国生产其所有Pixel硬件。

知情人士表示，这一决定涵盖智能手机、智能手表和无线耳机，此前这家美国科技巨头在越南开发并量产其高端Pixel智能手机的尝试已被证明成功。

这一举措一旦实施，将使谷歌成为继三星电子之后第二家将生产迁出中国的智能手机公司。这家韩国科技公司已在越南建立了成熟的智能手机供应链生态系统。

谷歌还告知供应商，其目标是今年将Pixel手机出货量提高8%至10%，因为在供应紧张导致内存芯片价格飙升的情况下，该公司仍希望积极扩大市场份额。

#### 一点一点

小批量的英伟达H200芯片已被允许进入中国大陆，北京正试图帮助其领先的AI公司赶超美国竞争对手，英国《金融时报》的**Zijing Wu**写道。

最近几周，字节跳动和腾讯各自收到了约[10,000块H200处理器](https://url3563.namail.nikkei.com/ls/click?upn=u001.M0mxR54htVwmfgL9HhtimeRWRYwvKixZqCR03vHmun36tZJA5Kdoda-2FcF5w7kcDePl1CWJJQAv4D30mRKqE6O-2B3JLIDmpB9fDqbMaoedzAkJ-2BMqaUB1Ajs05afFgU1KDcOqyFJsbzICq8yxcB8-2BcCWLXl4tyWwPhRKnQzHZsFMFYQh5paJbIhWwzErDMtiFyjgl-2Bac7-2BONj3Wrmt4GVQEDg9ueJFF9my7ythznGtb6RO0mF2xYsPcnNiYi5dcFUqxsP-2BkgBqaawsJdihu-2FAPwZXPzTD9GA7O4NCh43gF1LZWXZpUo6k09ngMOwTH0T-2FPlU9IE335-2BA1yAYEfMxuVmL3Ab4NGZFA7cVatU1BzNKc-3DA8Ur_Nh1pf1G0mH5ql3UFVpr8HYgTYPsL5cgxfwZjsg2NQ2BuzuGn-2BGAYrUQK6ZancxSjVNQ36UnUbq0yySPAeFPIBPiD0t-2Fkb7t5TQ0gEIxVDcbBtDAQM29F-2BgaIwgPwd9eZh-2FIPLUram3Ub7w-2Bg-2FNV2i0aJbkJxlBlDz64SDfZCDriDr4IJdJdrwMPEnI0ueco9-2FtaE0d8WEDSKLY8lFkmx6ZtvakwTRzdkHMiiXAFX5zvM62t55z9XbXdTBVTRoPTtIHfSP3D3rBWd1ADxExKNocdPfeJ-2FmobeUJQkrPSP-2BzQeAK5qT-2FmmENV-2Bw6WwRODN)，据两位知情人士透露，其他几家中国科技集团可能很快获得类似规模的出货批准。

H200至少落后于英伟达最强大的芯片两代，由于美国出口管制，中国客户不被允许购买这些芯片。

其中一位知情人士表示，尽管美国已批准这些中国集团各自购买多达10万块H200芯片，但北京希望它们将大部分芯片保留在中国大陆以外，以支持国内芯片制造商。

除了获准进入中国大陆的少量H200芯片外，中国监管机构已告知企业，它们可以将此类处理器运往作为其关境之外地区的香港，并在那里使用。

美国的许可证允许向中国大陆和香港运送英伟达H200芯片。

允许芯片进入香港仍对中国科技集团构成挑战，因为这个前英国殖民地缺乏足够的数据中心来容纳它们。这种情况不太可能很快改变，因为香港由于电力供应问题而缺乏足够的能力。

#### 公开竞争

![图片](https://www.ft.com/content/undefined)

开放权重AI已成为持续的中美科技战争的最新战线，华盛顿就是否以及如何限制中国模型展开激烈辩论，尽管硅谷反对，日经亚洲的**Yifan Yu**和**Cissy Zhou**报道。

当中国的 DeepSeek 发布其开放权重的 V4 Pro 人工智能模型时，这场辩论被推向高潮。

开放权重模型可免费下载，任何人都可以运行、微调并在此基础上构建产品。然而，它们不允许访问模型背后的训练数据，这是它们与开源模型之间的关键区别。

批评者对中国开放权重模型中可能存在偏见或“后门”表示担忧，这些后门可能让中国政府进行隐蔽访问。然而，许多美国科技公司强烈反对禁止中国开放权重模型。

#### 关键材料问题

根据日经亚洲的 **程庭芳** 和 **Lauly Li** 的独家报道，北京正在限制或推迟对台湾出口关键航空航天和光学材料，导致这个亚洲重要科技经济体的多个行业出现供应链瓶颈。

多位消息人士称，多家光学技术制造商和芯片设备供应商在从中国供应商处采购材料时遇到了障碍和延误，原因是海关检查时间延长且没有明确理由。

消息人士称，因此，供应商被迫取消订单，或者生产周期被延长而没有明确的时间表。

受影响的材料包括锗基和石英基材料，这两种材料对于航空航天、国防和半导体制造等广泛行业所使用的镜头至关重要。中国是全球此类材料的主要供应国，在地缘政治不确定性的背景下，中国一直在将出口管制武器化。

## Google’s China shift and the battle over AI models

Google plans to end Pixel hardware production in China next year amid ongoing US-China tensions © Feng Li/Getty Images

[**Lauly Li**](https://www.ft.com/lauly-li), [**Zijing Wu**](https://www.ft.com/zijing-wu), [**Yifan Yu**](https://www.ft.com/stream/570d3577-8f77-4f90-a22a-850009a570e1), [**Cissy Zhou**](https://www.ft.com/stream/998feb59-ab7e-4a24-b52d-450078a5ee91) and [**Cheng Ting-Fang**](https://www.ft.com/cheng-ting-fang)

Published9 hours ago

Hi everyone! This is Lauly, your #techAsia host this week, sending warm greetings from humid and rainy Taipei.

I was chatting with an executive working for an Apple supplier last night about the dynamics in his industry. He told me he recognised Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co’s importance to the global tech industry as early as the 2000s and that he even publicly argued with a well-known media figure who didn’t appreciate the contract chipmaker’s value.

In a fit of pique, he said, he bought 20mn New Taiwan dollars ($627,274) worth of TSMC shares at an average price of NT$55 each and hasn’t sold a single share since. With TSMC’s stock price currently above NT$2,300, the value of his holdings has ballooned to around NT$840mn.

“All the fine dining and good wine I’ve had are thanks to Morris, Mark and CC,” the executive said, referring to TSMC’s former and current chairmen.

That reminded me of an executive with a TSMC chip equipment supplier who shared with me his plan to take his entire family to Europe for a week for their summer vacation. They would be flying business class and staying in a luxury suite at a five-star hotel that costs $6,300 a night, a rate he described as “reasonable.”

All I can say is that my imagination has been limited by my poverty.

Jokes aside, demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure remains strong, according to my recent interviews with executives from different sectors and earnings calls by server suppliers like Foxconn, Pegatron, Quanta and Compal.

Quanta, for example, increased its capital expenditure this year to NT$40bn from a previous plan of NT$30bn in order to expand capacity in California, Thailand and Taiwan. By the end of this year, Quanta expects its global AI server capacity to be double what it was last year, while its orders are “already full until 2028.”

Lite-On Technology, a leading provider of AI server power supply solutions, and Unimicron, the world’s largest chip substrate supplier, also hiked their capital expenditure to record levels, while Foxconn expects its spending to increase 30 per cent from last year’s record of NT$173.8bn.

Such massive capacity expansions, however, have resulted in production equipment becoming a bottleneck, industry people say. Lead times for tools such as testing equipment for power supplies and weaving machines for high-end glass cloth have reached as long as 30 to 50 weeks. China’s export controls on certain raw materials and rare earths, plus increasing prices for already expensive metals, have also complicated the delivery of production equipment.

On top of that is uncertainty over US-China relations.

A friend of mine told me that his company is worried that escalating political tensions could jeopardise its supply chain continuity. Company executives, he says, have been caught in a debate over whether to switch all of the Chinese vendors supplying tools for its consumer electronics hardware to non-Chinese ones.

“I couldn’t help but wonder: Do we really need to decouple China down to this level? Is it really necessary?” my friend asked.

I’ve been thinking about such questions too.

#### Pixel shift

Google has told suppliers that it plans to end production in China of all its Pixel hardware next year amid ongoing US-China tensions, according to [this exclusive story](https://url3563.namail.nikkei.com/ls/click?upn=u001.M0mxR54htVwmfgL9HhtimSxgqTE4QRItjPHDax3pwRpGrMUzc3PQo48Os47OFpKbPvhfvU3gzCyxZsWsNx4eubiDM-2FIJ-2BAF1Thh8rKrlx020S53Gkvb8nxLp4-2FuO5VwduHQlysPZjoxjfNPv4hLoD6RopACQPVP61pYu9SFWwfzaUnO4cjrLfiSe7k15kGq1R25E-2B8oev2NZjIxqaIV6BM1BNFwDSMzKqbqKEMmkz0hxuIqiNK-2BNP7UYj1v-2FTXAVi36-2FRN-2FOat-2BJexGg0ag4xLURdPDSAAiZka8QYxvUVup76Bvty7ur94eMvJ3v7Xa2J6RLhacCxB8WCza5hWjMBehLPnnuX-2BhdqWcOi5pxycKL78ZG-2FB5EgneVPQSupGlgEP6y0zGDwlhbr4I1itBocA-3D-3DhOKD_Nh1pf1G0mH5ql3UFVpr8HYgTYPsL5cgxfwZjsg2NQ2BuzuGn-2BGAYrUQK6ZancxSjVNQ36UnUbq0yySPAeFPIBPiD0t-2Fkb7t5TQ0gEIxVDcapNLzs4gg3qpHDXTNgDM0PXVRNHECmcXMwjJG3EY0XBv9o6LynlCKKnVYFzX9FcJ0X6rfLPKQvTzgRh3eHkEewP-2FS53rvn8arIE6W4gNXzR1eCch7wasx8K12fiCGc25TCgcTG-2F03zidh9BzSR8DRz26zO0eBcywn6WXnkTU6cjepOeEdd9hDmgifTIDzpzHGZmlicrqSIvXV0MjVMIQ2L) by Nikkei Asia’s **Lauly Li**.

The decision covers smartphones, smartwatches and wireless earbuds and comes after the US tech giant’s attempt to develop and mass-produce its higher-end Pixel smartphones in Vietnam proved successful, sources familiar with the matter said.

This move, once it materialises, will make Google the second smartphone company after Samsung Electronics to shift production outside China. The South Korean tech company has built a mature smartphone supply-chain ecosystem in Vietnam.

Google also told suppliers it aims to raise Pixel phone shipments by 8 per cent to 10 per cent this year as the company hopes to aggressively expand its market share despite the skyrocketing prices of memory chips amid a supply crunch.

#### Bit by bit

Small batches of Nvidia’s H200 chips have been allowed to enter mainland China, as Beijing tries to aid its leading AI companies in the race to catch up with US rivals, writes the FT’s **Zijing Wu**.

ByteDance and Tencent have each received about [10,000 H200 processors in recent weeks](https://url3563.namail.nikkei.com/ls/click?upn=u001.M0mxR54htVwmfgL9HhtimeRWRYwvKixZqCR03vHmun36tZJA5Kdoda-2FcF5w7kcDePl1CWJJQAv4D30mRKqE6O-2B3JLIDmpB9fDqbMaoedzAkJ-2BMqaUB1Ajs05afFgU1KDcOqyFJsbzICq8yxcB8-2BcCWLXl4tyWwPhRKnQzHZsFMFYQh5paJbIhWwzErDMtiFyjgl-2Bac7-2BONj3Wrmt4GVQEDg9ueJFF9my7ythznGtb6RO0mF2xYsPcnNiYi5dcFUqxsP-2BkgBqaawsJdihu-2FAPwZXPzTD9GA7O4NCh43gF1LZWXZpUo6k09ngMOwTH0T-2FPlU9IE335-2BA1yAYEfMxuVmL3Ab4NGZFA7cVatU1BzNKc-3DA8Ur_Nh1pf1G0mH5ql3UFVpr8HYgTYPsL5cgxfwZjsg2NQ2BuzuGn-2BGAYrUQK6ZancxSjVNQ36UnUbq0yySPAeFPIBPiD0t-2Fkb7t5TQ0gEIxVDcbBtDAQM29F-2BgaIwgPwd9eZh-2FIPLUram3Ub7w-2Bg-2FNV2i0aJbkJxlBlDz64SDfZCDriDr4IJdJdrwMPEnI0ueco9-2FtaE0d8WEDSKLY8lFkmx6ZtvakwTRzdkHMiiXAFX5zvM62t55z9XbXdTBVTRoPTtIHfSP3D3rBWd1ADxExKNocdPfeJ-2FmobeUJQkrPSP-2BzQeAK5qT-2FmmENV-2Bw6WwRODN), while a few other Chinese tech groups could soon obtain approval for shipments of similar size, according to two people with knowledge of the matter.

The H200 is at least two generations behind Nvidia’s most powerful chips, which Chinese customers are not allowed to buy due to US export controls.

While the Chinese groups have been cleared by the US to buy up to 100,000 H200s apiece, Beijing wants them to keep most of these chips outside the mainland to support domestic chipmakers, one of the people said.

In addition to the small number of H200 chips permitted to enter the mainland, Chinese regulators have told companies that they can ship such processors to Hong Kong, which operates as a territory outside its customs border, and use them there.

The US licences permit shipments of Nvidia’s H200 chips to mainland China and Hong Kong.

Allowing the chips into Hong Kong still presents a challenge to Chinese tech groups as the former British colony lacks enough data centres to house them. That situation is unlikely to change soon, as Hong Kong doesn’t have sufficient capacity due to power supply issues.

#### Open rivalry

Open-weight AI has become the latest front in the ongoing US-China tech war, with heated debate in Washington over whether and how to restrict Chinese models despite Silicon Valley’s pushback, Nikkei Asia’s **Yifan Yu **and **Cissy Zhou **report.

The debate was [kicked into high gear](https://url3563.namail.nikkei.com/ls/click?upn=u001.M0mxR54htVwmfgL9HhtimSxgqTE4QRItjPHDax3pwRpRrFKhbxWpTAXdMzteZfJ8iD1eSnyRI1l4xdXlVhbu5ZZRJ0pB5UMoT-2FXSos-2FlMt5vjPCxjfz20Pn0amnec-2BcxToYxrkOpBOq-2BBgltpTdPkAEPa9HxXcrMHUZgM1YrKevTgVYgVMcgKWvQv6MLdQsqlhOJCCyNEYVG4nsR15skTt8-2FW1Radws5ViBNsRH9UcmXy9Ad3WdzMh8lwiSD0AWEk0BTvmPTTRiTuKBLPjBMv-2FYoHNWIDqFEmE0jrGZUezrGU7X9V9isDpOJ39R110lxl8U7BTztqezDXrARg6SXFr3ks3zpJT1Nc2X9PVDMsG-2Bv-2BrHjiaoYKMpGxPhxuFr7g-2BgxjXbQMnJ1OBOtEMAyIw-3D-3D8YGo_Nh1pf1G0mH5ql3UFVpr8HYgTYPsL5cgxfwZjsg2NQ2BuzuGn-2BGAYrUQK6ZancxSjVNQ36UnUbq0yySPAeFPIBPiD0t-2Fkb7t5TQ0gEIxVDcZGGXisGy1QM7Cr6esHyl989V6gwUH-2BBCcLNfKiIHnZ-2B6fKtAJBrXXcQ-2Fgw0x6aiSPRvDL6029zlZdYzvcRxoHQ3W-2FBBM-2BRc-2BvwqpFb8gcmGH1orLgobi3V5z3QC0TzwFgUV7QjK-2BUH1qiIxvrz6U7X48J4kPdGktUIbxPAGHCM0zBoGXomYxoc1LH1SINeNPxHePCzcIC5BRZYN1oXGQHI) when China’s DeepSeek released its V4 Pro open-weight artificial intelligence model.

Open-weight models are freely available to download, and anyone can run, fine-tune and build products on top of them. They do not, however, allow access to the training data behind the model, which is the key difference between them and open source models.

Critics have raised concerns over the possibility of potential bias in Chinese open-weight models or “backdoors” that could give the Chinese government covert access. Many US tech companies, however, have voiced strong opposition to a ban on Chinese open-weight models.

#### Material concerns

Beijing is restricting or delaying exports of key aerospace and optical materials to Taiwan, causing supply chain bottlenecks for several industries at the key Asian tech economy, according to this scoop by Nikkei Asia’s **Cheng Ting-Fang **and **Lauly Li**.

Several optical technology manufacturers and chip equipment suppliers have encountered [hurdles and delays](https://url3563.namail.nikkei.com/ls/click?upn=u001.M0mxR54htVwmfgL9HhtimSxgqTE4QRItjPHDax3pwRpGrMUzc3PQo48Os47OFpKbPvhfvU3gzCyxZsWsNx4euYWscSetwBkdCrrf6mNrsXjMHWGPcIAtrwNVE88Ug9NwTF8FHqyXbCbMi5blRUxt5977c72iRYQDGXaB4T0es5EC3eVoYdM5gxW8Ze4V3hM-2B2ukV7Umx9qc8JTUI3KNio-2BY-2BJNVF-2FG1uz1SiKR3doGi3OwCyNmH-2BKV7a9wUxB6l50of4ePkoArpznY9lghQGv5Vyci4N2xJmZXL2Ay-2BDwGLNQGWFHbkG-2BvbnGGlYmxytqb5Z4mF2XbtyiDNNZVp8r85M3b9xoxrDJRoijY6QG4xWynPKonHO9lyfolz0zkGlhB3s8dQ5EOuFzUCel06bLQ-3D-3D_buR_Nh1pf1G0mH5ql3UFVpr8HYgTYPsL5cgxfwZjsg2NQ2BuzuGn-2BGAYrUQK6ZancxSjVNQ36UnUbq0yySPAeFPIBPiD0t-2Fkb7t5TQ0gEIxVDcY7trvasa6xRAkBUuksn-2FoXiQDkEjpRdKeJ9EkRwELjyuiEuZYPDoMZvM6QLIeNhJcJfI-2B-2FhjxA0hasaANpbQ20cA6Mm-2BdDO-2FP2WkTwi2Gh6fmsQgqQBRIU-2BIhHemjSu7G6haF3aRR98KdqaKuLgh4j3ib9-2B0V4wgAv1WsdTavJaby74ZHBUtO2cJ5gKoSqAibXIODVof5j9JmjINWvka1D) in securing materials from suppliers in China due to prolonged customs checks without clear reasons, according to multiple sources.
