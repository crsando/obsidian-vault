### 中文译文

## 人工智能为何对共产党中国构成风险

*它利用这项技术的条件并不像许多人以为的那样有利*

![Illustration: Robert Carter/IISH](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b481cc90b1481d984f7cfe07f95d051/3b481cc9-0b14-815c-9116-da6bc561b55b-ff93c836fd0cb5bc940dabe8c8e7a0b8.jpg)

正如前沿人工智能模型相互之间进行基准测试一样，AI 本身也将对中美这两种相互竞争的政治体制进行基准测试。这项技术将检验哪一方更擅长创造 AI 工具并将其推广到整个经济中。而这反过来又将部分取决于它们对社会应如何运作的截然不同的理念。

即使在一个像 AI 这样日新月异的领域，这场竞争的结果也可能要等上数年才会明朗。[但正如我们从中国国内报道的](https://www.economist.com/briefing/2026/08/06/chinas-ai-drive-threatens-the-worlds-largest-workforce)，中国已经在快速采用 AI——而其政府正准备迎接由此预示的剧烈经济和社会变革。作为一个威权国家，中国在驾驭这项 21 世纪最有前途的技术方面具有一些优势，但也存在大量问题。

先从中国模型的水平说起。普遍的认知是它们廉价，而且即使最优秀的模型也始终落后于美国。事实上，两国的 AI 模型正在趋同。具有创新性的中国模型正变得更大、更聪明。就在过去几周，中国实验室发布了 Qwen3.8-Max 和 Kimi K3，它们几乎能媲美最先进的美国 AI，如 Anthropic 的 Fable 5。中国模型与美国模型一样，都有可能[挣脱测试环境的束缚](https://www.economist.com/science-and-technology/2026/08/06/should-ai-labs-be-treated-like-the-owners-of-dangerous-animals)。

尽管中国模型更廉价，但美国模型往往性价比更高。美国公司 Artificial Analysis 发现，在成本与智能之间的大多数权衡中，美国模型的表现优于中国模型。

这种趋同可能会持续。尽管中国正在建设的数据中心容量[仅为美国的十分之一](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2026/08/03/how-china-gets-better-bang-for-its-buck-than-america-in-ai)，但这种差距不太可能持续。作为一个半计划经济体，它擅长建设基础设施。当美国舆论开始反对数据中心时，东南亚却在承接中国的大型设施。中国的开放权重模型可以在任何人的服务器上运行，这意味着计算能力问题并不像醒目数字所暗示的那么严重。中国的发电装机容量几乎是美国的三倍，而且领先优势还在扩大。

中国正在学习应对制裁。它偷运英伟达（Nvidia）的尖端芯片，并在外国数据中心租用这些芯片。而其最优秀的芯片设计公司华为正迅速提高其（性能较次的）芯片产量。中国制造的光刻工具现在使用深紫外光来制造芯片，但使用极紫外光的最佳技术仍然无法企及。

构建模型只是故事的一半。要使经济充满活力，还必须部署AI——这是中国AI政策的重点。在中国模型制造落后于美国的时期，领导人将推广视为竞争的最佳方式。但他们可能也相信，与电力等其他宽泛技术一样，采用带来的回报大于发明。中国的劳动年龄人口到 2050 年预计将缩减 25%；要让机器取代人，也需要推广。

迄今为止，人工智能的使用尚未出现在美国或中国的经济统计数据中。但某些行业的变革已经席卷而来。根据一家大型公司的经验，人工智能辅助的卡车将需要减少 30% 的司机，因为车队人员从两名司机减至一名，从四名减至三名。2026 年第一季度发布的 12.8 万部广受欢迎的一两分钟微短剧中，有 95% 是使用人工智能制作的，取代了许多演员和摄制人员。

中国也在迅速增加机器人的使用。6 月，领导人要求地方政府和国有企业在 2026 年底前让 1 万台人形机器人从事实际工作。投行摩根士丹利预计，到 2030 年中国人形机器人销量将达到 44.6 万台，是今年总量的九倍。工人们受雇在巨大的仓库里通过反复折叠衣物、分类和堆放物品来训练机器人。

中国的白领工人占比低于美国，因此人工智能很可能在消费市场和蓝领工作中产生最大影响。这很容易引发不满。青年失业率已经达到 15% 左右，超过 3 亿人从事零工经济。工作岗位的消失速度可能远快于中国的人口下降。

你可能会认为中国共产党对权力的掌控如此牢固，以至于可以无视反弹。然而，独裁者害怕脆弱。尽管中国企业希望快速推出人工智能，但政府将极力避免不稳定。

这就是为什么人工智能将是对这一制度的考验，也是为什么中国的选择有限。与美国一样，关于再培训的讨论很多。然而，[再培训的记录不佳](https://www.economist.com/united-states/2026/08/02/can-america-retrain-workers-before-ai-leaves-them-behind)，即便是丹麦和新加坡这样最成功的国家，也难以让工人为全新的职业做好准备。没有理由认为中国会更为成功，尤其是它已经拥有过多的毕业生。

知名经济学家和顾问建议扩大社会契约，例如对人工智能公司征税或建立全民基本收入。他们的文章已出现在党刊上，这体现了官方的兴趣。但国家主席习近平长期以来反对施舍，称这会导致懒惰。

政治领导人正在敦促企业重新部署员工，而不是解雇他们。他们还可能要求国有企业吸纳过剩劳动力。然而，尽管这在政治上可行，但在国内增长已经疲软之际，这将抵消快速扩散的某些好处。

因此，政府可能会临时应变，让人工智能自由发展，直到出现问题再进行干预。课外辅导和游戏行业就是如此，当党认定它们造成太大危害时，这些行业一夜之间陷入混乱。谨慎的官员最近减缓了无人出租车的试验，此前有超过 100 辆无人驾驶出租车在武汉出现故障，导致乘客滞留。然而，断断续续的人工智能可能会损害创新能力。如果是这样，中国领导人或许不得不在社会不稳定的风险与落后于美国的风险之间进行权衡。

人工智能给美国和中国带来了类似的挑战。两个社会都将不得不重塑自我。相比独裁政权，民主国家通常更容易做到这一点。■

### English Original

## Why AI is a risk to Communist China

*It is not as well placed to take advantage of the technology as many people think*

Just as frontier artificial-intelligence models are benchmarked against each other, so AI will itself benchmark the rival political systems of America and China. The technology will test which is better at creating AI tools and diffusing them through the economy. And that will in turn depend partly on their very different ideas of how societies should be run.

Even in a field changing as rapidly as AI, the results of that contest may not become clear for some years. [But as we report](https://www.economist.com/briefing/2026/08/06/chinas-ai-drive-threatens-the-worlds-largest-workforce) from inside China, the country is already adopting AI fast—and its government is preparing for the dramatic economic and social change this heralds. As an autocracy, China has some advantages in harnessing the 21st century’s most promising technology. It also has plenty of problems.

Start with the calibre of China’s models. The common perception is that they are cheap and that even the best persistently lag behind America’s. In fact, the two countries’ AI models are converging. Innovative Chinese models are getting bigger and cleverer. Within the past few weeks Chinese labs have released Qwen3.8-Max and Kimi K3, which almost match the most advanced American AIs such as Anthropic’s Fable 5. Chinese models will be just as likely to [break loose from their test beds](https://www.economist.com/science-and-technology/2026/08/06/should-ai-labs-be-treated-like-the-owners-of-dangerous-animals) as American ones.

Although Chinese models are cheaper, American models are often better value. Artificial Analysis, an American firm, finds that for most trade-offs between cost and intelligence, an American model outperforms Chinese ones.

The convergence is likely to continue. Although China is building [only a tenth as much data-centre capacity](https://www.economist.com/finance-and-economics/2026/08/03/how-china-gets-better-bang-for-its-buck-than-america-in-ai) as America, that deficit is unlikely to last. As a semi-planned economy, it is good at creating infrastructure. While American opinion turns against data centres, South-East Asia is hosting huge Chinese facilities. China’s open-weight models can run on anyone’s servers, meaning that compute is less of a problem than the headline numbers suggest. China also has almost three times as much installed electricity-generating capacity as America, and its lead is growing.

China is learning to cope with sanctions. It smuggles in cutting-edge Nvidia chips and rents them in foreign data centres. And Huawei, its best chip-designer, is rapidly increasing the output of its (inferior) chips. Chinese-made lithography tools are now using deep UV light to print chips, though the best technology, using extreme UV, remains out of reach.

Model-building is only half the story. To make the economy dynamic, AI must also be deployed—the focus of Chinese AI policy. For as long as China was behind America in model-making, leaders saw diffusion as the best way to compete. But they may also believe that, as with other broad technologies such as electricity, adoption brings greater rewards than discovery. China’s working-age population is due to shrink by 25% by 2050; for machines to replace people requires diffusion, too.

So far, the use of AI has yet to show up in the economic statistics of either America or China. But change in some industries has already been sweeping. AI-assisted lorries will need 30% fewer drivers, going by one large firm’s experience, as crews slim from two drivers to one and from four to three. AI was used to generate 95% of the 128,000 wildly popular one- or two-minute-long microdramas released in the first quarter of 2026, replacing many actors and film crews.

China is also rapidly increasing the use of robots. In June leaders told local governments and state-owned enterprises to have 10,000 humanoid robots doing real work by the end of 2026. Morgan Stanley, a bank, expects humanoid sales in China to reach 446,000 by 2030, nine times this year’s total. Workers are being paid to train robots in huge warehouses by repeatedly folding clothes and sorting and stacking objects.

China has a lower share of white-collar workers than America, so AI is likely to have the greatest effect in consumer markets and blue-collar work. That could easily lead to discontent. Youth unemployment is already around 15%, and over 300m people work in the gig economy. Job destruction could be much faster than China’s demographic decline.

You might think that the Communist Party’s grip on power is so strong that it can ignore the blowback. However, dictators fear being brittle. Although Chinese firms want to roll out AI fast, the government will be desperate to avoid instability.

That is why AI will be a test of the system—and why China’s options are limited. As in America, there is plenty of talk about retraining. However, [retraining has a poor record](https://www.economist.com/united-states/2026/08/02/can-america-retrain-workers-before-ai-leaves-them-behind) and even the most successful countries, like Denmark and Singapore, struggle to equip workers for entirely new careers. There is no reason to think that China will be any more successful, especially as it already has too many graduates.

Prominent economists and advisers suggest extending the social contract by, say, taxing AI firms or creating a universal basic income. Their work has appeared in party journals, an official expression of interest. But President Xi Jinping has long been against handouts, saying they can lead to laziness.

Political leaders are pressing companies to redeploy staff rather than sack them. They may also ask state-owned companies to mop up surplus labour. Yet, although that is politically possible, it will cancel out some of the benefits of rapid diffusion at a time when domestic growth is already anaemic.

So the government may improvise, letting AI rip until there is trouble, when it will step in. That is what happened with tutoring and gaming, industries thrown into confusion overnight when the party decided that they were causing too much harm. Cautious officials recently slowed robotaxi experiments after more than 100 malfunctioned in Wuhan, leaving passengers stranded. However, stop-start AI may impair the ability to innovate. If so, China’s leaders may have to balance the risk of social instability with the risk of falling behind America.

AI poses similar challenges to America and China. Both societies will have to reinvent themselves. That is something democracies tend to find easier than dictatorships. ■
