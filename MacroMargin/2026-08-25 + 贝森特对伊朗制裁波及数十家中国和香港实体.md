### 中文译文

## 贝森特对伊朗制裁波及数十家中国和香港实体

特朗普政府最新对伊朗的制裁打击了位于中国和香港的企业，但避免了对中国主要金融机构实施更具深远影响的措施。

周一，美国财政部公布了一项[方案](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0613)，针对数十名个人和实体，这是财政部长斯科特·贝森特所称的“经济驱逐行动”的一部分，该更广泛行动旨在切断伊朗剩余的金融生命线。

将总部位于香港的实体纳入其中，遵循了美国在俄罗斯对乌克兰战争中实施制裁的模式。在那场行动中，华盛顿已[针对](https://research.hktdc.com/en/article/MTcyMzc5ODk1Mg)被指控协助[运输](https://2021-2025.state.gov/office-of-the-spokesperson/releases/2025/01/sanctions-to-disrupt-russias-military-industrial-base-and-sanctions-evasion/)包括敏感技术在内的货物的香港贸易中介和航运公司。今年5月，美国以帮助伊朗军方为由制裁了9家中国企业和个人，就在北京举行领导人峰会的前几天。

最新的对伊朗措施表明，华盛顿正寻求提高与德黑兰做生意的成本，但尚未直面制裁中国大型银行可能带来的更广泛经济与外交后果。尽管如此，鉴于这些公司多为私营且对美国的敞口鲜为人知，针对这些公司的措施效果如何尚不明朗。

“针对特定实体的制裁毫无意义，因为针对实体的制裁无法足够快地实施，以匹配替代实体被创建的速度，”美国企业研究所高级研究员、追踪中国海外投资的德里克·西索斯表示。

西索斯说，一些公司可能一开始是空壳公司，如果存活下来，就会处理更多活动。他补充说，美国点名的数十个实体“存在于数万个实体的宇宙中”。

中国外交部发言人林剑周二在北京例行记者会上表示，中国将保持与伊朗的经济关系。“中国与伊朗的合作始终在国际框架内进行，不应受到干涉或破坏，”他补充道。

香港政府表示，完全执行联合国安理会的制裁，但不执行国家实施的单方面措施。一位官方发言人补充说，香港维持着健全的贸易管制制度，监督战略物资的运输，以防止非法转移。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c781cc90b148154ab85f3dcaa4b4dc5/3c781cc9-0b14-815e-ad8e-dae3860d3b0a-6585745f7db3b1fbfee5d8ea75289302.png)

这些措施的一个关键焦点是围绕总部位于香港的 Sweet Ocean Industrial Ltd. 的科技采购网络。美国财政部称，该公司在获取激光光学设备和其他敏感货物方面充当中间人，这些货物最终运往伊朗的马利克·阿什塔尔科技大学（Malek Ashtar University of Technology）——一所受到联合国制裁、与国防有关的研究机构。

个人李娜（Li Na）、田建柏（Tian Jianbai）和张丽梅（Zhang Limei）——均在中国境内——也被列入制裁名单，理由是他们涉嫌通过该网络协助协调采购活动。财政部称，该网络支持伊朗采购用于核研究和导弹开发的关键技术。

美国声明称，此类网络使伊朗能够通过“遍布东亚的幌子公司、隐蔽金融渠道和物流中介体系”获取两用技术。

制裁还涉及与同一供应链相关的多家企业，包括 Shenzhen Sweet Ocean Technology Ltd.、RPT Technology Ltd.、Tiany Technology Ltd. 和 MT Trading and Logistics HK Ltd.。

美国财政部还表示，总部位于香港的 DEC Photonics Ltd. 多次向 Shenzhen Sweet Ocean 转移资金。该公司在其[网站](https://www.decphotonics.com/gywm)上宣传激光和光学产品。

财政部还指控，总部位于香港的 Feili Co.、Minvur Ltd.、Feisu Ltd. 和 Guska Co. 各自向 Sweet Ocean 及其网络转移了数万美元，以帮助伊朗最终用户采购产品。财政部称，其中多家实体充当幌子公司，为伊朗金融网络提供支付便利。

华盛顿方面还分别针对多家据称与伊朗有联系的物流运营商。其中包括深圳华美联运国际物流有限公司（Shenzhen Huamei Lianyun International Logistics Co.）、深圳博世通物流有限公司（Shenzhen Bositong Logistics Co.）和博世通供应链（深圳）有限公司（Bositong Supply Chain Shenzhen Co.）。

DEC Photonics、Tiany 和深圳博世通物流在网上提供了联系方式，但未立即回应寻求置评的请求。

## Dozens of China and Hong Kong Entities Hit by Bessent’s Iran Sanctions

The Trump administration’s latest sanctions salvo against Iran has swept up businesses in China and Hong Kong, but avoided the far more consequential step of targeting major Chinese financial institutions.

On Monday, the US Treasury Department unveiled a [package](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0613) targeting dozens of individuals and entities as part of what Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described as Operation Economic Outcast, a broader campaign aimed at severing Iran’s remaining financial lifelines.

The inclusion of Hong Kong-based entities follows a pattern seen in US enforcement of sanctions imposed over Russia’s war in Ukraine. In that campaign, Washington has [targeted](https://research.hktdc.com/en/article/MTcyMzc5ODk1Mg) trading intermediaries and shipping companies in the city accused of helping to [move goods](https://2021-2025.state.gov/office-of-the-spokesperson/releases/2025/01/sanctions-to-disrupt-russias-military-industrial-base-and-sanctions-evasion/), including sensitive technology. In May, the US sanctioned nine Chinese firms and individuals over claims they were helping Iran’s military, just days before a leaders’ summit in Beijing.

The latest Iran measures suggest Washington is seeking to raise the costs of doing business with Tehran without yet confronting the broader economic and diplomatic fallout that could come from sanctioning large Chinese banks. Still, it is unclear how effective the measures on the firms will be given they are private with little known exposure to the US.

“Sanctions against specific entities are meaningless as entity-specific sanctions can’t be applied quickly enough to match the speed at which substitute entities can be created,” said Derek Scissors, a senior fellow at the American Enterprise Institute who tracks Chinese overseas investment.

Some firms could start out as shell companies and then handle more activity if they survive, Scissors said, adding that the dozens of entities the US named “exist in a universe of tens of thousands.”

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman Lin Jian indicated his country would maintain its economic relationship with Iran on Tuesday at a regular press briefing in Beijing. “China’s cooperation with Iran has always been conducted within the international framework and should not be interfered with or undermined,” he added.

The Hong Kong government said it fully enforces UN Security Council sanctions, but not unilateral measures imposed by countries. An official spokesperson added that the city maintains a robust trade control system monitoring shipments of strategic commodities to prevent illegal diversion.

A key focus of the measures was a tech-procurement network centered on Hong Kong-based Sweet Ocean Industrial Ltd. Treasury alleged the company acted as an intermediary in acquiring laser-optics equipment and other sensitives goods destined for Iran’s [Malek Ashtar University of Technology](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/US%20Equity), a UN-sanctioned defense-linked research institution.

The individuals Li Na, Tian Jianbai and Zhang Limei — all based in China — were also designated for allegedly helping coordinate procurement activities through the network, which Treasury says supports Iran’s procurement of critical technology for nuclear research and missile development.

Such networks allow Iran to obtain dual‑use technology through a “sprawling system of front companies, covert financial channels, and logistics intermediaries across East Asia,” according to the US statement.

The sanctions also reached several firms linked to the same supply chain, including Shenzhen Sweet Ocean Technology Ltd., RPT Technology Ltd., Tiany Technology Ltd. and MT Trading and Logistics HK Ltd.

The Treasury Department also said Hong Kong-based DEC Photonics Ltd. repeatedly transferred funds to Shenzhen Sweet Ocean. The company advertises laser and optical products on its [website](https://www.decphotonics.com/gywm).

Treasury also alleged that Hong Kong-based Feili Co., Minvur Ltd., Feisu Ltd. and Guska Co. each transferred tens of thousands of dollars to Sweet Ocean and its network to help Iranian end-users procure products. Treasury alleged that several of these entities served as front companies that facilitated payments for Iranian financial networks.

Washington separately targeted a number of logistics operators that it said had links to Iran. Among them were Shenzhen Huamei Lianyun International Logistics Co., Shenzhen Bositong Logistics Co. and Bositong Supply Chain Shenzhen Co.

DEC Photonics, Tiany and Shenzhen Bositong Logistics, which provided contact information online, didn’t immediately respond to attempts to reach them for comment.
