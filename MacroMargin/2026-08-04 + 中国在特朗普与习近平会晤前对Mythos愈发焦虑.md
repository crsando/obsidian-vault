### 中文译文

## 中国在特朗普与习近平会晤前对Mythos愈发焦虑

中国正日益担忧，Anthropic PBC的Mythos可能被用来对付全球第二大经济体，在习近平与唐纳德·特朗普计划举行的峰会之前，给本已紧张的局势增添一个不稳定的新议题。

据知情人士透露（因讨论敏感事宜而要求匿名），北京官员对Mythos以及美国公司开发的其他前沿AI模型的网络能力感到担忧。他们认为Mythos等模型有被用作攻击性武器的潜力，并对Anthropic为何拒绝中国出于正常目的使用这些系统提出质疑。

知情人士称，中国希望在习近平计划于9月24日访美之前保持相对友好的氛围，并寻求在领导人会晤前预计举行的AI会谈中达成‘双赢’方案。尽管如此，两国政府之间的猜疑依然严重，美国对中国企业利用美国前沿模型训练自己的版本表示担忧。

美国财政部长斯科特·贝森特上个月提出，如果模型开发者被发现窃取知识产权，将实施未具体说明的制裁。此前，总部位于北京的月之暗面（Moonshot AI）发布了一个据称能与部分美国顶尖模型相媲美的系统。负责出口管制的中国商务部上周警告称，将采取‘一切必要措施’回应美国的威胁。

知情人士表示，北京方面并未准备立即对Anthropic或其他美国AI开发商采取行动，但官员们正在拟定若美国对该领域中国企业采取措施时可能作出的回应。这些人士称，中国的选项可能包括实施制裁或将美国AI公司列入国家的受限实体清单之一。

中国外交部将问题转交给相关部门。商务部未回应传真发出的置评请求。

特朗普已表示，其政府正在权衡对AI实施额外管控，同时对可能削弱美国公司相对于中国竞争对手的措施保持警惕。‘我们两方面都必须小心，’他上周三在椭圆形办公室告诉记者。‘我们不希望限制他们，结果突然之间我们落到中国之后。’

白宫发言人利兹·赫斯顿表示，政府正‘加大创新力度，以扩大美国与世界其他地区的差距。’

这些紧张局势表明，人工智能正日益成为美中关系中一个难以预测的前沿领域。尽管两国去年十月就关税和稀土材料供应达成了为期12个月的休战，但它们仍是战略竞争对手。尽管外界普遍预计该协议将得到延长，但双方都在继续寻求关键产业的自给自足，并维持对尖端技术的广泛限制。

人工智能日益成为美国国家安全限制措施的焦点，尤其是限制中国获取驱动该技术的尖端[英伟达公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity)芯片。对外国制造的机器人和电源逆变器（这两个行业由中国主导）的新限制，将争端扩大到北京认为对[先进制造业](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs)和[经济增长](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/china-s-gdp-growth-weakens-to-4-3-below-official-target-range)至关重要的领域。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3b281cc90b14810fa015fcad2ebfd71a/3b281cc9-0b14-81d1-8a78-d3b37f6c71bf-a462525682e34a69e889db822887cf5f.png)

复旦大学美国研究中心主任吴心伯在上海表示，中国对 Anthropic 的担忧符合对美国人工智能公司更广泛的国家安全焦虑，理由是这些公司能够收集客户数据并与美国政府或军方分享。

吴心伯说：“这就是为什么人们对这些美国人工智能公司带来的国家安全挑战越发担忧。” “这完全可以理解。你必须保持谨慎。”

Anthropic 已成为美国收紧中国获取先进人工智能限制的重要倡导者。首席执行官达里奥·阿莫德伊（Dario Amodei）敦促华盛顿将高端芯片拒之于中国门外，并要求对强大模型进行安全测试。此外，该公司还指责包括[阿里巴巴集团控股有限公司](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BABA%20US%20Equity)的 Qwen 团队在内的中国开发者，不当使用其 Claude 系统的输出结果来训练竞争模型。

这位 Anthropic 首席执行官上周还[警告](https://www.anthropic.com/news/position-open-weights-models)称，先进的中国人工智能可能威胁美国的军事优势或加深国内镇压，并称中国的执政党共产党是威权政府中“最有能力的威胁”。去年，该公司以美国国家安全担忧为由，[阻止](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-05/anthropic-clamps-down-on-ai-services-for-chinese-owned-firms)其服务面向中资控股企业。由于 Anthropic 不在中国运营，北京实施的任何制裁可能只会产生有限的直接商业影响。

Anthropic 拒绝置评。该公司让彭博新闻社参阅一份[5月声明](https://www.anthropic.com/research/2028-ai-leadership)，该声明阐述了对中国的警告以及对芯片管制的支持。

华盛顿自己对 Anthropic 的政策也一直不稳定。6月，美国商务部长霍华德·卢特尼克（Howard Lutnick）下令该公司在向世界任何地方的外国实体提供其前沿模型 Mythos 5 和 Fable 5 之前，须[获得许可证](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-16/read-the-lutnick-letter-that-led-anthropic-to-disable-mythos)。Anthropic 在与政府达成解决方案之前暂时禁用了这些模型。

该公司还因美国军方使用其人工智能而与特朗普政府陷入纷争，起因是该公司坚持为其工具提供额外保障。官员们已禁止联邦政府使用该公司的技术，Anthropic 正在法庭上对这一决定[提出异议](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/judge-voices-doubt-us-has-justified-its-ban-on-anthropic-ai)。

北京在6月展示了其对美国针对其科技公司举措的[反击](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/china-places-two-us-rare-earths-producers-on-export-control-list)能力，当时它对 MP Materials Corp. 和 USA Rare Earth Inc. 实施了出口管制。此前，五角大楼已将包括阿里巴巴和[比亚迪](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1211:HK)在内的中国大型企业[列入](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/pentagon-accuses-alibaba-baidu-byd-of-aiding-china-s-military)其声称支持中国军方的企业名单。

美中高层官员在为9月峰会做准备的同时，也[提出](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-shares-concern-over-restrictive-us-measures-with-bessent)了两国之间的摩擦点。中国国务院副总理何立峰在与贝森特和美国贸易代表贾米森·格里尔的通话中，对美方近期经贸限制措施表示“严重关切”，但没有具体说明是哪些措施。双方的通话摘要均未提及人工智能。

与此同时，中国的开放权重模型——可以被[自由下载](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-s-moonshot-to-release-breakthrough-ai-model-for-download)和修改——为北京提供了一个在国际上将自己塑造成广泛共享人工智能倡导者的机会。中国推广开放模型，向发展中经济体提供技术和培训，并帮助[启动](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/china-led-ai-body-enlists-global-south-states-to-rival-us)了一个由29个成员组成的合作小组，这可能使北京在未来的标准制定中获得更大影响力。

尽管包括 OpenAI 在内的一些美国开发商提供开放权重模型，但美国行业的大部分焦点一直在专有系统上，即企业向客户出售的同时保留对底层技术的控制。包括 DeepSeek 在内的中国企业发布了更实惠的开放模型，这些模型通常在性能上落后于美国竞争对手最尖端的软件，但以价格而言仍具有吸引力。

中国对中国企业发布的人工智能也有自己的安全担忧。官员们已与包括阿里巴巴在内的人工智能公司[讨论](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/china-s-ai-ascendance-gives-xi-a-stage-and-a-security-dilemma)了如何降低其日益强大的模型所带来的风险。

玉渊潭天，一个与中国国家广播电视台关联、经常用于传递官方政策意图的社交媒体账号，上周[提议](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-state-media-says-support-for-open-ai-models-has-limits)建立未来人工智能模型的分级发布制度，根据模型能力的强弱和潜在风险来决定其使用权限。

## China Is Getting More Anxious About Mythos Before Trump Meets Xi

China is growing anxious that [Anthropic PBC](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/1892140D:US)’s Mythos could be wielded against the world’s second-biggest economy, adding a volatile new issue to already heightened tensions before a planned summit between Xi Jinping and Donald Trump.

Officials in Beijing are concerned about the cyber capabilities of Mythos and other AI frontier models developed by US companies, according to people familiar with the matter, who asked not to be identified discussing sensitive matters. They see the potential for models like Mythos to be used as an offensive weapon, and question why Anthropic is denying China access to the systems for normal purposes, the people said.

China wants to preserve a relatively warm atmosphere before Xi’s planned Sept. 24 visit to the US and is seeking a “win-win” approach at AI talks expected before the leaders meet, the people said. Even so, suspicion remains high between the governments, with the US expressing concern that Chinese companies are using American frontier models to train their own versions.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent last month floated unspecified [sanctions](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-21/bessent-says-us-will-scrutinize-chinese-ai-models-for-ip-theft) if model developers are found to have stolen intellectual property after Beijing-based Moonshot AI released a system said to [rival](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/what-is-moonshot-ai-s-kimi-k3-model-and-why-is-it-making-waves) some of the best American models. China’s Commerce Ministry, which oversees export controls, [warned](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/syxwfb/art/2026/art_7f1622463a7c48ef9fad600ce0ef702f.html) last week that it would take “all necessary measures” in response to the US threats.

The people familiar with the matter said Beijing isn’t preparing any immediate action against Anthropic or other US AI developers, but officials are drawing up potential responses should the US take measures against Chinese companies in the sector. China’s options could include imposing sanctions or adding American AI companies to one of the nation’s restricted-entity lists, those people said.

China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs referred questions to the relevant authorities. The Ministry of Commerce didn’t respond to a faxed request for comment.

Trump has signaled that his administration is weighing additional controls on AI while remaining wary of measures that could weaken US companies against Chinese competitors. “We have to be careful in both ways,” he told reporters in the Oval Office last Wednesday. “We don’t want to restrict them where all of a sudden, we come in second to China.”

White House spokeswoman Liz Huston said the administration was “doubling down on innovation to widen the gap between America and the rest of the world.”

The tensions illustrate how AI is becoming an increasingly unpredictable front in the US-China relationship. The two countries remain strategic rivals despite reaching a 12-month truce last October over tariffs and supplies of rare earth materials. While that deal is widely expected to be extended, both sides continue to pursue self-sufficiency in key industries and maintain broad restrictions on cutting-edge technology.

AI has increasingly been the focus of US national security curbs, most notably the restrictions on China’s ability to acquire cutting-edge [Nvidia Corp.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/NVDA%20US%20Equity) chips that power the technology. New limits on foreign-made robots and power inverters, industries dominated by China, have [widened](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-29/us-risks-broadening-china-tech-clash-with-robot-inverter-curbs) the dispute into sectors Beijing considers central to [advanced manufacturing](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-23/china-deepens-bid-for-tech-self-reliance-in-new-development-plan) and [economic growth](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-15/china-s-gdp-growth-weakens-to-4-3-below-official-target-range).

Wu Xinbo, director of Fudan University’s Center for American Studies in Shanghai, said China’s concern about Anthropic fits a broader national security anxiety over US AI companies, citing their ability to collect customer data and share it with the US government or military.

“That is why there is a growing concern over the national security challenge posed by those US AI companies,” Wu said. “That’s quite understandable. You have to be cautious.”

Anthropic has emerged as a prominent advocate of tighter US restrictions on China’s access to advanced AI. Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei has urged Washington to keep high-end chips out of China and require safety testing for powerful models. Separately, the company has accused Chinese developers, including [Alibaba Group Holding Ltd.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/BABA%20US%20Equity)’s Qwen team, of improperly using outputs from its Claude systems to train competing models.

The Anthropic chief also [warned](https://www.anthropic.com/news/position-open-weights-models) last week that advanced Chinese AI could threaten US military superiority or deepen domestic repression, calling China’s ruling Communist Party “the most capable threat” among authoritarian governments. Last year, the company [blocked](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-05/anthropic-clamps-down-on-ai-services-for-chinese-owned-firms) its services from Chinese-controlled companies, citing US national security concerns. Because Anthropic doesn’t operate in China, any sanctions imposed by Beijing would probably have limited immediate commercial impact.

Anthropic declined to comment. It referred Bloomberg News to a [May statement](https://www.anthropic.com/research/2028-ai-leadership) laying out its warnings about China and support for chip controls.

Washington’s own policies toward Anthropic have been unsettled. In June, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick ordered the company to [obtain licenses](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-16/read-the-lutnick-letter-that-led-anthropic-to-disable-mythos) before providing its frontier Mythos 5 and Fable 5 models to foreign entities anywhere in the world. Anthropic temporarily disabled the models before reaching a resolution with the administration.

The company is also mired in a feud with the Trump administration over the use of its AI by the US military, touched off by the company’s insistence on extra safeguards for its tools. Officials have banned the federal government from using the company’s technology, a decision Anthropic is [challenging](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/judge-voices-doubt-us-has-justified-its-ban-on-anthropic-ai) in court.

Beijing demonstrated its ability to [hit back](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/china-places-two-us-rare-earths-producers-on-export-control-list) at US moves against its tech companies in June, when it placed export controls on MP Materials Corp. and USA Rare Earth Inc. That came after the Pentagon [added](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-08/pentagon-accuses-alibaba-baidu-byd-of-aiding-china-s-military) major Chinese firms, including Alibaba and [BYD Co.](https://www.bloomberg.com/quote/1211:HK), to a list of firms it says support the Chinese military.

Top American and Chinese officials have [raised](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/china-shares-concern-over-restrictive-us-measures-with-bessent) points of friction between their countries even as they lay the groundwork for the September summit. Vice Premier He Lifeng expressed “serious concern” over the recent US economic and trade restrictions in a phone call with Bessent and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, without specifying the measures. Summaries of the call from both sides made no mention of AI.

At the same time, Chinese open-weight models — which can be [freely downloaded](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-s-moonshot-to-release-breakthrough-ai-model-for-download) and modified — have given Beijing an opportunity to cast itself internationally as a champion of broadly shared AI. It has promoted open models, offered technology and training to developing economies and helped [launch](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/china-led-ai-body-enlists-global-south-states-to-rival-us) a 29-member cooperation group that could give Beijing greater influence over future standards.

While some US developers, including OpenAI, offer open-weight models, much of the American industry’s focus has been on proprietary systems that companies sell to customers while retaining control over the underlying technology. Chinese companies, including DeepSeek, have released more affordable, open models that typically lag behind the most cutting-edge software from US rivals but nonetheless offer a compelling product for the price.

China has its own security concerns with AI released by Chinese companies. Officials have [discussed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-16/china-s-ai-ascendance-gives-xi-a-stage-and-a-security-dilemma) with AI companies, including Alibaba, how to mitigate the risks posed by their increasingly powerful models.

Yuyuantantian, a social-media account linked to China’s state broadcaster and frequently used to signal official policy thinking, [proposed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/china-state-media-says-support-for-open-ai-models-has-limits) last week a tiered system for releasing future AI models, with access determined by the power and potential risks of their capabilities.
