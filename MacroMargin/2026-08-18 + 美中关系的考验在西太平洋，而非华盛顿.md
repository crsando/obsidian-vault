### 中文译文

## 美中关系的考验在西太平洋，而非华盛顿

黄岩岛的不速之客

*摄影：Jam Sta Rosa/AFP/Getty Images*

9月中旬，全世界的目光将聚焦华盛顿，美国总统唐纳德·特朗普与中国国家主席习近平将举行下一次峰会。分析人士将仔细审视会议进程，寻找双边关系走向的线索。但要理解正在发生什么以及什么最为重要，请关注西太平洋。这个至关重要且充满争议的地区，是中国和美国地缘政治野心正面交锋之地，也是争夺主导权的冲突日益激烈之处。

美国长期以来一直将西太平洋视为其通往亚洲的门户，并试图阻止任何敌对力量主导该地区或将美国排除在其商业之外。然而，中国将西太平洋视为其正当势力范围，同时也是通往更广阔世界的门户。华盛顿和北京应该干脆在夏威夷把太平洋一分为二，一位中国海军上将多年前曾开玩笑说——随着中国积聚起争夺地区主导权的军事力量，这个笑话变得不那么好笑了。

相比之下，由于中东和欧洲接连不断的危机，华盛顿多年来一直难以集中精力关注这一地区。特朗普政府最初曾宣扬其太平洋导向，却因加勒比海和波斯湾的争斗而分心。在一个问题丛生的世界里——以及一场夭折的关税战之后——特朗普试图通过高层外交稳定美中关系。但这些会谈并未减缓中国争取影响力的步伐：北京正大力施压美国的朋友和盟友，以试探特朗普是否会开始反击。

仅7月，中国就在太平洋进行了一次洲际弹道导弹试射——这提醒人们其大规模核武建设。中国海军在日本专属经济区进行了实弹演习；并与俄罗斯军舰和潜艇在冲绳和北海道附近进行了联合巡逻。

与此同时，中国对台湾的钳制越来越紧：这个岛屿民主国家经常被中国海军或海警船只包围。中国飞机和船只不断探测台湾的防御；习近平的军队演练入侵或封锁。就在本月，有消息披露，中国黑客用人工智能驱动的网络攻击袭击了台湾——这让人尝到革命性技术将如何强化北京的全面施压。

南海的危险也在加剧。上个月，中国人员在南沙群岛第二托马斯浅滩（仁爱礁附近）袭击了菲律宾水手，那里经常是冲突爆发点。北京同样加强了对黄岩岛的控制，这是另一个争议地点。局势升级的可能性切实存在：7月，美国国务卿马尔科·卢比奥透露，一艘美国船只曾插入中国和菲律宾船只之间。

幸运的是，这表明美国及其关键盟友*正在*进行反击。

美国正在派遣海岸警卫队快艇[协助](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2026/08/10/2548346/4-us-fast-response-cutters-deployed-philippine-eez)菲律宾巡逻其专属经济区。五角大楼已在菲律宾和日本部署先进导弹；并将飞机、舰艇和部队从美国本土基地调往西太平洋地区。

6月，[美国陆军](https://news.usni.org/2026/07/22-u-s-army-deploys-hypersonic-missiles-in-range-of-china-during-exercise)向关岛部署了“暗鹰”高超音速导弹——这是该导弹首次部署到可打击中国的射程内。与[盟友和伙伴](https://www.pacom.mil/Media/News/News-Articles/Article/4483964/philippines-us-conclude-balikatan-2026/)的演习正变得规模更大、更贴近实战。

当然，这些盟友和伙伴是中国胁迫的主要目标，他们也在为可能出现的问题做准备。日本新版防卫白皮书直言不讳地指出，东亚[已进入](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/08/10/japans-2026-defense-white-paper-highlights-new-era-of-crisis-in-indo-pacific/)一个“危机新时代”。不能排除破坏地区的侵略行为。

作为回应，东京[正推动](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/features/2026-06-07/japan-is-taking-on-china-and-doubting-the-us)国防开支超过GDP的2%，并强化其战略西南岛屿的防御。它通过与菲律宾缔结准联盟以及与亚洲其他面临风险的国家深化防务产业关系，来加强前线。

日本前首相安倍晋三[创造](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Line_of_Advantage/xRtzzgEACAAJ?hl=en)了“自由开放的印太”这一说法——一个不受任何敌对势力支配的地区——他的继任者正在努力实现这一愿景。澳大利亚也在该区域的南端与中国的影响力作斗争，今年春天与巴布亚新几内亚签署了防务协议。

然而，美国自身承诺的严肃性受到质疑。特朗普与伊朗的战争消耗了大量原本在对华任何冲突中所需的弹药。由于对习近平的错误顺从，总统[对台军售](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-05-28/a-14-billion-taiwan-arms-sale-is-trump-s-litmus-test-on-china)行动迟缓。

此外，尽管台湾的防务开支激增，但在无人机和其他低成本、丰富能力方面的关键投资[停滞不前](https://thediplomat.com/2026/08/as-taiwans-drone-budget-debate-drags-on-time-is-running-out/)。菲律宾政治的剧烈波动引发人们对其2028年下次大选后政策走向的质疑。尽管多边合作在增加，西太平洋仍然[缺乏](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/china/case-pacific-defense-pact-ely-ratner)一个统一的威慑与防御制度框架。

该地区尚未遭受大规模战争。但在相互对抗的演习、紧急的军事集结以及有时暴力的冲突中，西太平洋并非真正和平。下月在华盛顿举行的峰会可能会有祝酒词和对中美建设性关系的呼吁。但中美竞争的真正节奏将在决定世界格局的地缘政治斗争战场中显现。

## The Test of US-China Ties Is in the Western Pacific, Not Washington

Unwelcome visitors at Scarborough Reef

*Photographer: Jam Sta Rosa/AFP/Getty Images*

Come mid-September, the eyes of the world will be on Washington, when President Donald Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping meet for their next summit. Analysts will scour the proceedings for clues about the course of the relationship. But to grasp what’s happening and what’s most significant, watch the Western Pacific. That crucial, contested region is where the geopolitical ambitions of China and the US meet head-on, and where the clash for primacy grows ever more intense.

The US has long viewed the Western Pacific as its gateway to Asia and sought to prevent any hostile power from dominating the area or excluding America from its commerce. China, however, views the Western Pacific as its rightful sphere of influence, as well as its gateway to the wider world. Washington and Beijing should simply split the Pacific Ocean at Hawaii, one Chinese [admiral quipped](https://www.google.com/books/edition/A_Contest_for_Supremacy/9E-_A9IAHecC?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=friedberg%20contest%20for%20supremacy&printsec=frontcover) years ago — a joke that became less funny as China amassed the military power to make a bid for regional dominance.

Washington, in contrast, has had trouble focusing on this area for years, amid serial crises in the Middle East and Europe. The Trump administration originally touted its Pacific orientation, only to be consumed by fights in the Caribbean and the Persian Gulf. Amid a world full of problems — and following an abortive tariff war — Trump sought to stabilize the US-China relationship through high-level diplomacy. But these talks haven’t slowed China’s drive for influence: Beijing is pushing hard against US friends and allies to see if Trump will start pushing back.

In July alone, China conducted an intercontinental ballistic missile test over the Pacific — a reminder of its massive nuclear buildup. The Chinese Navy conducted live-fire [exercises in](https://www.newsweek.com/china-and-russia-challenge-us-allies-with-drills-in-asia-12222161) Japan’s exclusive economic zone; it carried out joint patrols with Russian warships and submarines near Okinawa and Hokkaido.

Meanwhile, China’s vise around Taiwan gets tighter: That island democracy is [regularly encircled](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/how-chinas-navy-is-tightening-the-noose-on-taiwan-7c8ab9bd) by Chinese navy or coast guard vessels. Chinese aircraft and ships continually probe Taiwan’s defenses; Xi’s military rehearses an invasion or blockade. Just this month came revelations that Chinese [hackers hit](https://www.ft.com/content/7d2ab3e0-9085-48f6-b38a-d90260d58795?syn-25a6b1a6=1) Taiwan with AI-enabled cyberattacks — a taste of how a revolutionary technology will intensify Beijing’s full-spectrum pressure.

Danger is also mounting in the South China Sea. Last month, Chinese [personnel assaulted](https://news.usni.org/2026/07/20/philippine-sailor-injured-in-violent-clash-with-china-coast-guard-off-second-thomas-shoal) Filipino sailors near Second Thomas Shoal, one frequent flashpoint. Beijing, likewise, has been strengthening its hold on Scarborough Reef, another contested feature. The potential for escalation is real: In July, US Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2026/07/24/2544357/rubio-cites-us-coast-guard-role-after-ayungin-baton-attack-filipino-sailor) revealed that a US vessel had interposed itself between Chinese and Philippine ships.

Fortunately, that’s a sign that the US and key allies *are *pushing back.

The US is sending Coast Guard cutters [to help](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2026/08/10/2548346/4-us-fast-response-cutters-deployed-philippine-eez) the Philippines patrol its EEZ. The Pentagon deployed advanced missiles to the Philippines and Japan; it has shifted aircraft, ships and troops from bases in the US to locations in the Western Pacific.

In June, the [US Army](https://news.usni.org/2026/07/22/u-s-army-deploys-hypersonic-missiles-in-range-of-china-during-exercise) sent Dark Eagle hypersonic missiles to Guam — the first time those missiles were deployed within striking range of China. Drills with [allies and partners](https://www.pacom.mil/Media/News/News-Articles/Article/4483964/philippines-us-conclude-balikatan-2026/) are becoming bigger and more realistic.

Those allies and partners, of course, are the primary targets of Chinese coercion, and they preparing for trouble, as well. East Asia [has entered](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/08/10/japans-2026-defense-white-paper-highlights-new-era-of-crisis-in-indo-pacific/) a “new era of crisis,” Japan’s new defense white paper states bluntly. Region-shattering aggression cannot be ruled out.

In response, Tokyo [is pushing](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/features/2026-06-07/japan-is-taking-on-china-and-doubting-the-us) defense spending beyond 2% of GDP and fortifying its strategic southwestern islands. It is bolstering the front line by concluding a quasi-alliance with the Philippines and deepening defense-industrial ties with other exposed Asian states.

Former prime minister Shinzo Abe [coined the](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Line_of_Advantage/xRtzzgEACAAJ?hl=en) phrase “Free and Open Indo-Pacific” — a region safe from domination by any hostile power — and his successors are striving to fulfill that vision. Australia, too, has been battling Chinese influence in the southern end of that theater, by signing a defense pact with Papua New Guinea this spring.

Still, the US has cast doubt about the seriousness of its own commitment. Trump’s war with Iran expended huge quantities of munitions that would be needed in any conflict with China. The president has slow-rolled [arms sales](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-05-28/a-14-billion-taiwan-arms-sale-is-trump-s-litmus-test-on-china) to Taiwan out of misplaced deference to Xi.

Moreover, while Taiwan’s defense spending is surging, crucial investments in drones and other cheap, plentiful capabilities [have stalled](https://thediplomat.com/2026/08/as-taiwans-drone-budget-debate-drags-on-time-is-running-out/). The vicious swings of politics in the Philippines raise questions about what policy it will follow after its next election, in 2028. Although multilateral cooperation is increasing, the Western Pacific still [lacks](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/china/case-pacific-defense-pact-ely-ratner) a single institutional framework for deterrence and defense.

That region isn’t yet wracked by large-scale war. But amid dueling exercises, urgent buildups, and sometimes-violent confrontations, the Western Pacific isn’t really at peace. Next month’s summit in Washington may feature toasts and calls for productive US-China relations. But the real rhythms of the Sino-American contest will be revealed in the battlespace where the world’s defining geopolitical struggle is playing out.
