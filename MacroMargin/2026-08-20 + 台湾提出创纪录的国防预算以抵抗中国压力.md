### 中文译文

## 台湾提出创纪录的国防预算以抵抗中国压力

![Taiwan concluded its annual Han Kuang military drills last week, during which it simulated efforts to counter a Chinese maritime blockade © Ann Wang/Reuters](https://images.ft.com/v3/image/raw/ftcms%3A176291db-680a-4e95-a0ba-467a13f27d1a?source=next-article&fit=scale-down&quality=highest&width=1440&dpr=1)

台湾上周结束了年度汉光军事演习，期间模拟了反制中国海上封锁的努力 © Ann Wang/Reuters

[**乔·莱希**](https://www.ft.com/joe-leahy) 于北京

发布于7小时前

台湾已为明年提出创纪录的国防预算，总统赖清德政府寻求加强台湾的军事准备，以应对来自中国日益增加的压力。

这一18%的增长于周四作为2027年预算的一部分公布，将首次推动[台湾](https://www.ft.com/taiwan)的计划国防支出超过1.1万亿新台币（345亿美元）。它还将超过GDP的3%，符合美国对该地区盟友增加军事开支的要求。

赖清德政府面临反对党对其计划的阻力，他在预算公布前表示，此举将展示“台湾加强自卫的决心”。

“投资国防就是投资和平，”他说。

台北正试图使其军队现代化，并计划[研发自己的潜艇](https://www.ft.com/content/90e61099-4c36-428f-b1c6-3d302ae95638?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，因为中国空军和海军的演习正对台湾收紧包围圈。北京声称对台湾拥有主权，并威胁如果台北无限期抵抗，将用武力夺取台湾。

近期中国在台湾周边的演习，[包括在台湾东部水域的演习](https://www.ft.com/content/fd7c69e5-b3cf-4b4f-927a-fb7f0c2cdc93?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，引发了对北京正在为潜在封锁及扰乱重要航运通道进行预演的担忧。

### 播放 | 00:14

中国本月在台湾周边进行了海军演习 © 路透社

台湾上周结束了年度“汉光”军事演习，期间模拟了对抗中国海上封锁的行动。

赖清德政府还寻求发展台湾的[本土无人机产业](https://www.ft.com/content/23069435-ada0-4494-9ec0-b4730be8a124?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。该预算旨在将包括无人机在内的非对称作战能力支出增加91%，达到1445亿元新台币。

该预算已获赖清德内阁批准，现在将提交立法院（即议会），该机构由台湾两大主要反对党控制。反对党一直抵制赖清德的军费增长，并在经过数月有关资金分配方式的拖延和僵局后，直到周五[才通过2026年度预算](https://www.ft.com/content/48e56592-e5b5-4335-8b40-361411f950df?syn-25a6b1a6=1)。

这种国内政治斗争以及中国日益强硬的立场，恰逢美国总统唐纳德·特朗普提出可能将对台军售作为与北京谈判的筹码，引发了对华盛顿对台安全保证承诺的担忧。

特朗普政府去年宣布了一项创纪录的111亿美元对台军售，但另一项[140亿美元的一揽子军售](https://www.ft.com/content/aecf40c9-fb61-4958-ae6b-14a471cad680?syn-25a6b1a6=1)仍在等待批准。

中国国防部上周宣布在台湾东部海域举行[与印度尼西亚的联合“航行演习”](https://www.ft.com/content/e0ca4cab-c8ba-47d6-9465-13e2d73285fc?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，但没有说明具体地点。

这次演习以及印度尼西亚海军的罕见参与，引发了台北方面的担忧。与此同时，北京正加大对地区国家的压力，要求它们承认其对台湾的主张。马来西亚总理安瓦尔·易卜拉欣周日接受采访时表示，他认为“台湾是中国的一部分，就这样”。

这些言论受到中共媒体的热烈欢迎，但遭到台北方面的强烈驳斥。

美国在该地区的盟友本周也[感到不安](https://www.ft.com/content/1d713822-e68f-4476-be94-ffab3418bfe4?syn-25a6b1a6=1)，因特朗普在最后一刻决定缩减与韩国的联合演习，他表示这些演习对朝鲜领导人金正恩具有“敌意”。此举进一步引发了人们对特朗普政府亚太安全承诺的担忧。

## Taiwan proposes record defence budget to resist China pressure

Taiwan concluded its annual Han Kuang military drills last week, during which it simulated efforts to counter a Chinese maritime blockade © Ann Wang/Reuters

[**Joe Leahy**](https://www.ft.com/joe-leahy) in Beijing

Published7 hours ago

Taiwan has proposed a record defence budget for next year as President Lai Ching-te’s government seeks to strengthen the country’s military preparedness to respond to increasing pressure from China.

The 18 per cent increase, unveiled on Thursday as part of the 2027 budget, would push [Taiwan](https://www.ft.com/taiwan)’s planned defence spending above NT$1.1tn (US$34.5bn) for the first time. It would also exceed 3 per cent of GDP, in line with demands from the US for its allies in the region to increase their military outlay.

Lai, whose administration has faced resistance to his plans from opposition parties, said ahead of the budget announcement that the move would demonstrate “Taiwan’s determination to strengthen its self-defence”.

“Investing in defence is investing in peace,” he said.

Taipei is trying to modernise its military, with plans to [develop its own submarines](https://www.ft.com/content/90e61099-4c36-428f-b1c6-3d302ae95638?syn-25a6b1a6=1), as Chinese air and naval drills draw a tighter noose around the country. Beijing claims sovereignty over Taiwan and has threatened to take it by force if Taipei resists indefinitely.

Recent Chinese exercises around Taiwan, [including in its eastern waters](https://www.ft.com/content/fd7c69e5-b3cf-4b4f-927a-fb7f0c2cdc93?syn-25a6b1a6=1), have raised concerns that Beijing is conducting rehearsals for a potential blockade as well as manoeuvres to disrupt important shipping lanes.

Taiwan concluded its annual Han Kuang military drills last week, during which it simulated efforts to counter a Chinese maritime blockade.

Lai’s government has also sought to build up Taiwan’s [domestic drone industry](https://www.ft.com/content/23069435-ada0-4494-9ec0-b4730be8a124?syn-25a6b1a6=1). The budget aims to increase spending on asymmetric combat capabilities including drones by 91 per cent, to NT$144.5bn.

The budget, which was approved by Lai’s cabinet, will now go to the Legislative Yuan, or parliament, which is controlled by Taiwan’s two main opposition parties. The opposition has resisted Lai’s military spending increases and [only passed the 2026 budget](https://www.ft.com/content/48e56592-e5b5-4335-8b40-361411f950df?syn-25a6b1a6=1) on Friday after months of delays and deadlock over how the funds would be allocated.

That domestic political fight, as well as China’s increasingly assertive stance, has also come as US President Donald Trump has raised the possibility of using arms sales to Taipei as a bargaining chip in negotiations with Beijing, sparking concerns over Washington’s commitment to its security guarantees to Taiwan.

The Trump administration last year announced a record $11.1bn arms sale to Taiwan, but a further [$14bn package is still awaiting approval](https://www.ft.com/content/aecf40c9-fb61-4958-ae6b-14a471cad680?syn-25a6b1a6=1).

China’s defence ministry last week announced a [joint “navigation exercise” with Indonesia](https://www.ft.com/content/e0ca4cab-c8ba-47d6-9465-13e2d73285fc?syn-25a6b1a6=1) in waters east of Taiwan, without specifying an exact location.

The exercise, and the rare involvement of the Indonesian navy, sparked concern in Taipei. It also comes as Beijing steps up pressure on countries in the region to recognise its claims over Taiwan. Malaysia’s Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said in an interview on Sunday that he saw “Taiwan as part of China, period”.

The comments were greeted with enthusiasm by Chinese Communist Party media but were fiercely rebuked by Taipei.

US allies in the region were [also rattled](https://www.ft.com/content/1d713822-e68f-4476-be94-ffab3418bfe4?syn-25a6b1a6=1) this week by Trump’s last-minute move to scale back joint exercises with South Korea, which he said were “hostile” to North Korean leader Kim Jong Un. The move raised further concerns about the administration’s commitments to Asia-Pacific security.
