### 中文译文

## 美伊和平前景黯淡，特朗普不急于达成协议

中东和平前景遭遇新的挫折，据报道，总统唐纳德·特朗普表示他不急于结束与伊朗的战争，而黎巴嫩的战斗再度燃起。

在接受福克斯新闻的电话采访时，特朗普表示，美国对伊朗港口的海上封锁正在对该国施加压力，他没有解决冲突的时间表。

他还警告说，如果“阿曼挡路，我们就炸他个稀巴烂”，但没有提供更多细节。他在5月曾发出[类似威胁](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-27/trump-not-satisfied-in-iran-talks-casting-doubt-on-quick-deal)。阿曼正在与伊朗就霍尔木兹海峡的管理方式进行谈判，但美国并未参与这些讨论。

观看：据福克斯新闻报道，总统唐纳德·特朗普威胁称，如果阿曼阻碍与伊朗的事态发展，将对其发动轰炸。泰勒·肯德尔报道。来源：彭博

伊朗战争已拖延近六个月，看不到解决的曙光。6月签署的谅解备忘录——技术上于周一到期——本意是给华盛顿和德黑兰一个窗口，在60天内达成更持久的和平协议。但围绕重新开放霍尔木兹海峡的紧张局势迅速重新引发敌对行动，导致多次军事行动。双方均表示该协议已失效。

伊朗和阿曼一直在就霍尔木兹问题进行平行讨论，德黑兰警告称，协议不一定意味着这条关键海上航线的重新开放。伊朗表示，作为海峡交通治理更广泛协议的一部分，“航运地图”已经敲定，但密集的谈判仍在继续。

伊朗外交部发言人伊斯梅尔·巴加埃周一告诉记者，由于安全复杂性以及“破坏性因素的阻挠行为”，达成协议耗时颇长。德黑兰表示，美国必须解除封锁。

![图片](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3bf81cc90b1481b5b8fbe334ce97296b/3bf81cc9-0b14-8113-bf68-d3e2217cf17a-96ccfae2c72ee12275cb7fef9a45920d.png)

特朗普告诉福克斯新闻，与伊斯兰革命卫队官员的幕后渠道仍然畅通，但据国家通讯社伊通社报道，一名发言人后来否认这一点，称其为“妄想”。

自上周初以来，布伦特原油价格已上涨超过7%，因为霍尔木兹海峡迅速重新开放的前景进一步暗淡，战前全球五分之一的石油和天然气通过该海峡运输。全球基准油价周一上涨多达1.3%，至每桶89.68美元。

中东局势本已在周末升级，当时以色列军方表示在黎巴嫩南部打死了真主党高级指挥官阿布·哈桑·阿拉。以色列在周六的空袭造成11人死亡，以色列总理本雅明·内塔尼亚胡称这是对该激进组织袭击导致三名士兵重伤的报复。

近日，以色列还打击了加沙地带受伊朗支持的哈马斯，也门胡塞武装袭击了红海船只，多艘船只也在霍尔木兹海峡遭到炮弹袭击。与此同时，以色列定居者围困了巴勒斯坦民居，招致国际谴责。内塔尼亚胡淡化了这一暴力行为，并将其归咎于少数激进分子。

战斗可能使恢复美国与伊朗之间陷入僵局的谈判的努力复杂化，华盛顿与德黑兰之间的[14点谅解备忘录](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-16/read-the-14-point-draft-memorandum-between-the-us-and-iran)规定在“包括黎巴嫩在内的所有战线”暂停敌对行动，并保证霍尔木兹海峡的自由通行。

特朗普一直在艰难寻找摆脱这场战争的出路，这场战争在美国越来越不受欢迎，并可能威胁共和党在11月中期选举中的选情。上周，财政部长斯科特·贝森特表示，美国准备宣布对德黑兰的“前所未见”的新经济措施。

观看：中东记者丹·威廉姆斯在彭博电视上讨论美伊谅解备忘录到期的影响。来源：彭博

尽管伊朗的大部分工业能力已遭破坏，原油出口也因美国封锁而受到严重限制，但数十年的制裁未能迫使它在核计划上让步或放弃对霍尔木兹海峡的控制。

伊朗利用战斗的相对平静期为可能更广泛的对抗做准备，《华尔街日报》的《伊朗秘密升级战争计划》——WSJ援引伊朗和阿拉伯官员的话说。该报称，德黑兰已在关键安全岗位安插强硬派老将，并加速导弹和无人机生产。

与此同时，特朗普的女婿兼和平特使贾里德·库什纳本周正在访问中东，试图加速美国斡旋的加沙停火。他与英国前首相托尼·布莱尔以及和平委员会首席特使尼克莱·姆拉德诺夫一道，周一在耶路撒冷与内塔尼亚胡举行会谈。

据美联社援引一名地区官员和一名哈马斯官员的话报道，库什纳周日已在埃及会晤哈马斯领导人哈利勒·哈亚。

该通讯社称，哈马斯在一份声明中呼吁调解方以及美国创建的负责监督停火的和平委员会“迫使”以色列批准一项设定停火后续步骤的路线图。

## US-Iran Peace Prospects Dim With Trump in No Hurry for Deal

The prospects for peace in the Middle East suffered a fresh setback, with President Donald Trump reportedly saying he is in no hurry to end the war with Iran and fighting flaring anew in Lebanon.

In a phone interview with Fox News, Trump said a US naval blockade on Iranian ports is putting pressure on the country and that he has no timeline for resolving the conflict.

He also warned that if “Oman gets in the way we’ll bomb the s—— out of them,” without give any further details. He made a [similar threat](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-27/trump-not-satisfied-in-iran-talks-casting-doubt-on-quick-deal) in May. Oman is negotiating with Iran on how the Strait of Hormuz should be managed, but the US isn’t part of those discussions.

WATCH: President Donald Trump threatened to bomb Oman if it gets in the way of developments with Iran, according to reports from Fox News. Tyler Kendall reports. Source: Bloomberg

The Iran war has dragged on for almost six months with no resolution in sight. A memorandum of understanding signed in June — which technically expires Monday — was meant to give Washington and Tehran a window to reach a more lasting peace deal within 60 days. But tensions over the re-opening of the Strait of Hormuz were quick to resurface hostilities, leading to several instances of military action. Both sides have said the agreement is defunct.

Iran and Oman have been involved in parallel discussions over Hormuz, with Tehran warning a deal won’t necessarily translate into a reopening of the crucial maritime route. Iran said a “shipping map” has been finalized as part of a broader agreement for the governance of traffic in the strait, but intensive talks are continuing.

Reaching a deal has been time-consuming due to security complexities and the “obstructionist behavior of destructive elements,” Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei told reporters on Monday. Tehran has said the US must remove its blockade.

Trump told Fox News that back channels with officials from the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps remain open, something a spokesman later denied as a “delusion,” according to the state-run IRNA.

The price of Brent crude has gained more than 7% since the start of last week as the prospects further dimmed for a swift reopening of Hormuz, through which a fifth of the world’s oil and gas transited before the war. The global benchmark rose at much as 1.3% on Monday to $89.68 a barrel.

Tensions in the Middle East were already on the rise over the weekend, when Israel’s military said it killed senior Hezbollah commander Abu Hassan Alaa in southern Lebanon. Eleven people died in the Israeli strikes on Saturday, which Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said were in retaliation for an attack by the militant group that seriously injured three soldiers.

In recent days, Israel has also struck Iran-backed Hamas in Gaza, the Yemen-based Houthis have attacked ships in the Red Sea, and several vessels have been hit by projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz. Israeli settlers have meanwhile laid siege to Palestinian homes, drawing international condemnation. Netanyahu has downplayed that violence and blamed a small number of radicals.

Fighting is likely to complicate efforts to revive the deadlocked negotiations between the US and Iran, with the [14-point memorandum of understanding](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-16/read-the-14-point-draft-memorandum-between-the-us-and-iran) between Washington and Tehran providing for a pause in hostilities “on all fronts, including Lebanon” and free passage through Hormuz.

Trump has struggled to find an off-ramp to a war that’s increasingly unpopular in the US and threatens to penalize Republicans in November’s midterm elections. Last week, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US is preparing to announce [new economic measures](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-to-roll-out-economic-isolation-plan-for-iran-next-week) on Tehran “like have never been seen.”

While much of Iran’s industrial capacity has been damaged and crude exports severely curtailed by the US blockade, decades of sanctions have failed to force it to bend on its nuclear program or relinquish control over Hormuz.

Iran has used the relative lull in fighting to prepare for a potentially broader confrontation, the Wall Street Journal Iran's Secret Plan to Escalate the War -- WSJ, citing Iranian and Arab officials. Tehran has installed hard-line veterans in key security posts and accelerated missile and drone production, it said.

Meanwhile, Trump’s son-in-law and peace envoy Jared Kushner is visiting the Middle East this week to try and accelerate a US-brokered ceasefire in Gaza. He, along with former UK Prime Minister Tony Blair and Nickolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace’s top envoy, are holding talks with Netanyahu in Jerusalem on Monday.

Kushner met with Hamas chief Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt on Sunday, the Associated Press said, citing a regional official and a Hamas official.

In a statement, Hamas called on mediators and the US-created Board of Peace overseeing the ceasefire to “compel” Israel to approve a road map setting out the next steps in the truce, the news agency said.
