### 中文译文

## 厄瓜多尔在联手美国军方之际争取中国资金

唐纳德·特朗普在拉丁美洲的主要盟友之一本周正在访问中国，试图从美国的对手那里获得长期投资。

厄瓜多尔总统丹尼尔·诺沃亚周三会见了中国国务院总理李强，表示他希望看到来自这个亚洲强国的资金继续流入这个安第斯国家经济的关键领域，如能源、矿业和基础设施。

前一天，诺沃亚在一年多内第二次对北京进行国事访问期间，与习近平主席签署了一项中国支持厄瓜多尔太阳能和数字化转型的协议。这些协议包括扩大清洁能源、利用中国人工智能加强气候预测以及深化自由贸易的承诺。

此次访问凸显了中国在厄瓜多尔经济中日益增长的作用，中国公司正在大量收购矿业资产，而消费者纷纷购买中国制造的电动汽车。但这似乎也削弱了特朗普政府重新确立美国在美洲主导地位的努力。

分析人士认为这是一种微妙的平衡。基多国际大学国际关系学院院长阿图罗·莫斯利科表示：“厄瓜多尔政府正在两边下注，因为它显然不想疏远任何一方，因为它从双方获得的合作都很重要。”

虽然诺沃亚属于与特朗普结盟的拉丁美洲领导人群体，但这位38岁的香蕉大亨之子尚未在白宫会晤美国总统。然而，他采用军事手段应对厄瓜多尔安全危机的方式与华盛顿的战略相吻合。在厄瓜多尔总统与习近平会晤数小时后，美国南方司令部司令会见了厄瓜多尔国防部长，讨论针对美国南方司令部的联合努力。厄瓜多尔讨论打击贩毒集团的努力。

“如果被迫在两者之间做选择，我认为诺沃亚显然站在美国阵营，安全合作对他来说在政治上是大事。”华盛顿欧亚集团拉丁美洲总监丽莎·格莱斯-塔尔戈表示。

![Soldiers patrol a metro station in northern Quito on Aug. 5. Photographer: Rodrigo Buendia/AFP/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481fb864cde97598db2b3/3c281cc9-0b14-8135-ba9e-dc675e89dfa5-f99edaefbea9551285d1b428526c9b69.png)

数十年的经济管理不善使厄瓜多尔这个位于南美洲西端附近、虾和香蕉出口居世界前列的战略要地依赖外国资金。这一生命线的关键部分来自国际货币基金组织，这个安第斯国家是第四大借款国，欠款71亿美元。

厄瓜多尔拥有丰富的石油和矿产资源以及丰富的生物多样性，但由于历史上对私营企业的敌意，常年难以吸引外国投资。与此同时，暴力贩毒团伙使其凶杀率成为拉丁美洲最高，每10万人中超过50人。

特朗普在该地区的最高军事指挥官弗朗西斯·多诺万将军称厄瓜多尔为地区领导者，也是政府“美洲反卡特尔联盟”的“关键成员”。尽管如此，联合军事行动仍饱受争议，有人权侵犯的指控，包括年初一艘厄瓜多尔渔船在太平洋上不明原因失踪的事件。

美国已明确表示希望参与厄瓜多尔陷入困境的石油业和新兴采矿业的发展，甚至在今年的贸易协定中将该国最大油田Sacha列为感兴趣资产。

![The Petroecuador oil refinery in Esmeraldas, Ecuador. Photographer: Antony Quintero/AFP/Getty Images](https://pub-b62e8da959b74c1495a20ed03df4741e.r2.dev/notion-images/china/3c281cc90b1481fb864cde97598db2b3/3c281cc9-0b14-81d2-a240-eeab36294802-d7c550e36ca61cd11492577f2d927fe1.png)

但在诺沃亚总统任期内，正是中国建立了一条未来从该安第斯国家进口金属的管道，中国企业收购了一系列最初由西方公司开发的大型项目。

但与中国的商业关系并非毫无摩擦。为应对结构性电力短缺，诺沃亚政府正迫使矿业公司自行采购电力，这阻碍了该国首座铜矿大规模扩建的审批。对中资建设的水电站Coca Codo Sinclair的接管计划也仍未完成。

与此同时，中国不时阻止厄瓜多尔虾的进口，而中国是其虾的主要买家。厄瓜多尔希望还能出口牛油果、蓝莓和家禽等更多商品。诺沃亚在与李会面时，寻求明确的期限以增强双边关系。

在格雷斯-塔戈看来，诺沃亚继续争取北京方面是有道理的，因为“尽管美国对中国在厄瓜多尔关键领域的投资进行审查或表示担忧，我们未必会看到美国企业填补这一空缺。”

## Ecuador Courts Chinese Money While Teaming Up With US Military

One of Donald Trump’s top allies in Latin America is visiting China this week, trying to secure long-term investments from the US rival.

Ecuadorian President Daniel Noboa met Chinese Premier Li Qiang on Wednesday, saying he’d like to see money from the Asian powerhouse keep flowing into key parts of the Andean nation’s economy like energy, mining and infrastructure.

A day earlier, Noboa signed a China to Support Ecuador in Solar Power, Digital Transformation with President Xi Jinping during his second state visit to Beijing in just over a year. The deals included commitments to expand clean energy, to use Chinese artificial intelligence to bolster climate forecasting and to deepen free trade.

The trip underscores Beijing’s growing role in Ecuador’s economy, where Chinese companies are snapping up vast mining assets while consumers flock to buy Chinese-made electric cars. But it also appears to undermine the Trump administration’s push to reassert US dominance in the Americas.

Analysts see it as a delicate balancing act. “The Ecuadorian government is playing both sides as it obviously doesn’t want to distance itself from either because the cooperation it receives from both is important,” said Arturo Moscoso, dean of international relations at Universidad Internacional in Quito.

While Noboa is part of a cohort of Trump-aligned Latin American leaders, the 38-year-old son of a banana tycoon has yet to meet the US president at the White House. His military-led approach to Ecuador’s security crisis, however, dovetails with Washington’s strategy. And hours after the Ecuadorian president’s summit with Xi, the head of US Southern Command met with Ecuador’s defense minister to discuss joint efforts against US’s SouthCom, Ecuador Discuss Efforts to Combat Drug Cartels.

“If forced to choose between the two I think Noboa is clearly in the US camp and the security cooperation is a huge deal for him politically,” said Risa Grais-Targow, Latin America director at the Eurasia Group in Washington.

Decades of economic mismanagement have left Ecuador, strategically located near the western tip of South America and a world leader in shrimp and banana exports, dependent on foreign funding. A key part of that lifeline comes from the International Monetary Fund, where the Andean nation is number-four borrower, owing $7.1 billion.

Home to abundant oil and mining resources and rich biodiversity, Ecuador perennially struggles to attract foreign investment because of a historical record of hostility toward private corporations. At the same time, violent drug gangs have left it with Latin America’s highest homicide rates at more than 50 per 100,000 people.

General Francis Donovan, Trump’s top military commander in the region, called Ecuador a regional leader and a “critical member” of the administration’s Americas Counter Cartel Coalition. Joint military operations have nonetheless been controversial, with allegations of human rights violations including the unexplained disappearance of an Ecuadorian fishing boat in the Pacific Ocean at the start of the year.

The US has made it clear that it wants to participate in the development of Ecuador’s struggling oil and budding mining industries, even specifying the nation’s biggest oil field, Sacha, as an asset of interest in a trade agreement this year.

But it’s China that during Noboa’s presidency has built a pipeline of future metals imports from the Andean nation, with Chinese companies buying up a series of major projects initially developed by Western companies.

But the business relationship with China isn’t frictionless. To deal with a structural energy deficit, Noboa’s government is forcing mining companies to source their own electricity, which is holding up approval of a major expansion of the nation’s first copper mine. The planned takeover of Chinese-built hydroelectric plant Coca Codo Sinclair also remains incomplete.

China, meanwhile, has sporadically blocked deliveries of Ecuadorian shrimp, of which it is the main buyer, and Ecuador wants to be able to export additional goods like avocados, blueberries and poultry. Noboa, in his meeting with Li, sought hard deadlines to help reinforce the bilateral relationship.

To Grais-Targow, it makes sense for Noboa to continue trying to woo Beijing since “despite US scrutiny or concerns over Chinese investment into key sectors in Ecuador, we aren’t necessarily seeing US firms filling that void.”
